# Is the geometry→plaque association expected to differ by artery (LAD/LCx/RCA) and by proximal/mid/distal position?

Scope note: 15 tool calls. PMC full texts were blocked by a browser check, so most findings below are
from abstracts (via Europe PMC metadata) or search-engine summaries. Where a number comes only from a
search summary whose primary source I could not open, this is flagged. Nothing here is from a
plaque-free general-population cohort with a ~10-year incident-plaque outcome; no such study with
segment-specific geometry effects was found.

## 1. Hemodynamics/WSS: do the low/oscillatory WSS effects of curvature, taper and bifurcation geometry differ by position and artery?

### Takeaway
Mechanistically, geometry acts on plaque through *local* low/oscillatory WSS, and the WSS a given
curvature/taper/angle produces depends on local flow, diameter and nearby branches, which differ by
artery and position. So the *size* of the geometry effect is plausibly not identical across segments,
but no study quantifies how a per-SD change in curvature, taper or LM angle shifts plaque risk
differently in LAD vs LCx vs RCA or proximal vs distal. The evidence is small CFD samples and
idealized models; the direction (more curvature/wider angle → more low-WSS area) is broadly
consistent, the magnitude by segment is unknown.

### Cited Findings
- Low ESS combined with large plaque burden independently predicted lumen narrowing at 6–10 months in 506 ACS patients (374 re-imaged) with 3-vessel angiography + IVUS; PPV 41%, NPV 92% for progression requiring PCI. Abstract reports no artery- or position-specific ESS effect. Referral ACS population with existing plaque, short follow-up. — Stone PH et al., PREDICTION, Circulation 2012;126:172–181, [doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.112.096438](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.112.096438), PMID 22723305
- Serial VH-IVUS + CFD in 20 patients (2,249 segments): low-WSS segments showed plaque-area progression (p=0.027) and necrotic-core growth (p<0.001); high-WSS segments showed necrotic core and dense-calcium progression. No vessel-specific results or interaction tests in the abstract. Tiny n, diseased patients. — Samady H et al., Circulation 2011;124:779–788, [doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.021824](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.021824), PMID 21788584
- In vivo 3D IVUS (48 segments): plaque thickness correlated more strongly with local curvature than with WSS; the inverse WSS–plaque relation was stronger in sections with compensatory enlargement (p<0.025) and disappeared once luminal narrowing was extensive. I.e., the WSS–plaque relation itself depends on disease stage. — Wahle A et al., Med Image Anal 2006;10:615–631, [doi:10.1016/j.media.2006.03.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2006.03.002), PMID 16644262
- Steady-state CFD of LM bifurcations from CT in 8 patients without significant atherosclerosis: the LM–LAD tortuosity index was the dominant geometric determinant of low WSS at the *proximal LAD*; high WSS concentrated at the flow divider. Very small n, steady flow. — Malvè M et al., Ann Biomed Eng 2015;43:82–93, [doi:10.1007/s10439-014-1056-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10439-014-1056-y), PMID 24986333
- Idealized CFD of LAD/diagonal bifurcations: bifurcation angle had a *minor* effect on hemodynamics; the cardiac curvature radius mattered more. — Chiastra C et al., J Biomech 2017, [doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2017.04.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2017.04.016), PMID 28457603
- Cast-based computational model: Murray-type Y-bifurcations decreased WSS by 10–40% vs other branching geometries, with higher OSI in atherosclerosis-prone regions (diameter ratios matter for WSS). — Huo Y et al., J Biomech 2012, [doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2012.01.033](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2012.01.033), PMID 22365499
- Left coronary trees (20 non-stenosed, 12 moderate, 7 severe): only the topological shear variation index consistently separated non-stenosed from stenosed trees (AUC 0.876); curvature differed only for whole trees and *non-bifurcating* segments (p<0.024, p<0.027). Analysis split bifurcating vs non-bifurcating segments, not LAD/LCx/RCA; cross-sectional, small. — Zhang M et al., R Soc Open Sci 2024;11:241267, [doi:10.1098/rsos.241267](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.241267), PMID 39309260
- Idealized CFD: taper itself contributes to low-WSS regions, not only curvature; realistic models need taper and out-of-plane bends. — Johnston BM et al., MODSIM 2009 conference paper, [PDF](https://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim09/B4/johnston_bm.pdf) (conference proceeding; idealized geometry)
- Review of how coronary anatomy shapes haemodynamics (Shen, Beier, Wentzel, Morbiducci, Chatzizisis, Serruys et al.); abstract states anatomy varies considerably among patients and populations; journal version DOI 10.1007/s11831-026-10530-w. I could not read the body. — [arXiv:2507.17109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17109) (preprint version)
- A search summary reported a CTA-based CFD study (22 subjects, 28 arteries: LAD 14, LCx 5, RCA 9) with mean ESS 13.76 Pa in LAD vs 3.49 Pa LCx and 3.76 Pa RCA (p<0.001) and highest curvature in LCx (0.1447 vs 0.1229 mm⁻¹). **Primary source not verified**; likely one of [med.auth.gr](https://med.auth.gr/node/5382) or [ISMRM 2007](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/07MProceedings/PDFfiles/03134.pdf). Treat as unconfirmed; LCx n=5.
- Normal-artery CCTA ESS values for LAD, LCx and RCA exist (Schultz et al., Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2023) — [Leiden repository](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3575926/view); I did not read the results.

### Inferences
- The WSS a given geometry creates scales with flow rate and diameter (WSS ∝ Q/r³ for Poiseuille flow, a textbook relation), both of which fall from proximal to distal and differ between arteries. A 1-SD curvature in a distal, low-flow segment need not produce the same WSS change as 1 SD in the proximal LAD. This is a reason to expect *some* heterogeneity, but the direction of the per-SD difference is not predictable from the literature.
- Bifurcation-related geometry (LM angle, LM→LAD/LCx diameter ratio) acts locally at the carina; the clinical and CFD evidence locates the effect at the ostial/proximal LAD and LCx. The thesis model already restricts the LM angle to pLAD/pLCx rows, which matches this.
- The RCA lacks a main bifurcation at its origin and moves most with the cardiac cycle (dynamic curvature); static CCTA curvature may be a noisier proxy of disturbed flow there. This is a mechanistic argument, not a sourced quantitative finding.

### Gaps
- No study found that quantifies curvature→WSS or taper→WSS sensitivity separately for LAD, LCx and RCA, or proximal vs distal, in a reasonably sized cohort.
- Could not read Gijsen F et al., "Expert recommendations on the assessment of wall shear stress in human coronary arteries", Eur Heart J 2019;40:3421–3433, [doi:10.1093/eurheartj/ehz551](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehz551) (open access) for any statement on segment differences.

## 2. Do imaging/CFD or clinical studies report segment- or artery-specific geometry–plaque associations, and has heterogeneity been formally tested?

### Takeaway
Artery-specific reports exist but are almost all single-artery studies (LAD only, RCA only) or
small cross-sectional samples, and none found formally tested an artery × geometry or position ×
geometry interaction. The tortuosity literature even disagrees on direction (protective in
angiographic studies, risk in CTA/CFD studies). There is therefore no empirical basis for prespecified
segment-specific coefficients, and no evidence against a shared one either.

### Cited Findings
- **Tortuosity, RCA only, large:** ML-derived RCA tortuosity in 38,691 angiograms (22,334 patients): OR 1.05 per SD for CAD and 1.09 per SD for severe CAD after adjustment; tortuosity higher in women and hypertension, lower with diabetes. Angiographic referral population, cross-sectional, RCA only, no LAD/LCx comparison and no interaction tests. — Tello Ayala JR … Fahed AC, JACC Adv 2026;5:102829, [doi:10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.102829](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.102829)
- **Tortuosity as protective:** stenosis was less prevalent in tortuous than non-tortuous segments of the main coronary arteries (angiography). No per-artery breakdown in abstract. — Li Y et al., J Int Med Res 2018, [doi:10.1177/0300060518804723](https://doi.org/10.1177/0300060518804723), PMID 30304979
- Tortuosity with myocardial bridging had less atherosclerosis (CTA, n=88), in "anterior" and "posterior" tortuous segments (e.g. χ²=8.606, P=0.003); stenosis severity did not differ. — An JW et al., Zhongguo Yi Xue Ke Xue Yuan Xue Bao 2018;40:151–157, [doi:10.3881/j.issn.1000-503X.2018.02.003](https://doi.org/10.3881/j.issn.1000-503X.2018.02.003), PMID 29724303
- Tortuosity linked to risk markers (hypertension 86.7% vs 30%, abnormal carotid IMT) in 30 tortuous patients vs matched controls; no vessel breakdown. — Elamragy A et al., Egypt Heart J 2021;73:34, [doi:10.1186/s43044-021-00157-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s43044-021-00157-6), PMID 33788058
- A search summary reported a dual-source CTA study of 73 asymptomatic people in which curvature was related to plaque per segment (p<0.001) and per artery (p<0.001), whereas tortuosity was related to plaque only per segment. **Bibliographic details not verified** (search pointed at [PMC6300977](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6300977), which was blocked). Notable because it suggests the *level of analysis* changes the result.
- **Curvature, LAD only:** IVUS showed plaque concentrated on the inner arc of curved LAD segments, more with greater curvature. — Iwami T et al., Am J Cardiol 1998;82:381–384, [doi:10.1016/s0002-9149(98)00340-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0002-9149(98)00340-3), PMID 9708671
- **LM bifurcation angle:** MSCT, n=50: median LM angle 88.5°; 72% of patients with a normal ostial LAD had an angle <88.5°, 63% with any LAD disease had ≥88.5° (P=0.018). >90% of plaques lay opposite the flow divider; ostial LAD affected in 64%. Effect is localized to the carina/ostial LAD. Small, cross-sectional, single centre. — Rodriguez-Granillo GA et al., Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2007, [doi:10.1007/s10554-006-9144-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10554-006-9144-1), PMID 17028928
- LAD–LCx angle correlated with Gensini score; 47.5° cut-off 86.7% sensitivity, 82.1% specificity for severe CAD (cross-sectional). — [PMC11168269](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11168269/) (2024; details from search summary only)
- 3D-QCA registry (194 bifurcations): LM/LAD/LCx distal angle 80° ± 21°, with the LM bifurcation the most variable of all bifurcations (vs LAD/diagonal proximal angle 151° ± 13°). — Tu S et al., Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2012, [doi:10.1007/s10554-011-9996-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10554-011-9996-x), PMID 22169957
- Bifurcation lateral (low-shear) walls carry more plaque than flow-divider walls (MSCT + CFD). — [EuroIntervention, plaque and shear stress distribution in human coronary bifurcations](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf) (details from search summary)

### Inferences
- Most "artery-specific" results are artefacts of design (only one artery measured), not evidence of heterogeneity. Reporting a significant effect in one artery and not another is not an interaction test.
- The inconsistent sign of tortuosity across studies is likely driven by design (angiographic referral cohorts, obstructive stenosis outcomes, myocardial bridging, confounding by hypertension/age/sex) rather than by artery differences; it argues for caution in interpreting any per-artery coefficient that the thesis data might produce.
- With ~1,077 people and a few hundred cases, segment-specific slopes for 3 arteries × 3 positions would multiply the geometry parameters by up to 9; formal interaction tests would be underpowered and the unverified literature cannot guide which interactions to prespecify.

### Gaps
- No study found with a prespecified geometry × artery or geometry × position interaction test for incident plaque.
- No longitudinal CCTA study in plaque-free people linking baseline geometry to segment-level incident plaque was found.
- No taper→plaque clinical study with per-artery results was found.

## 3. Known proximal predominance of plaque: explained by geometry or independent of it?

### Takeaway
Proximal clustering is well documented and its gradient differs by artery (steepest in LAD, flattest
in RCA). Studies describe it but do not test whether geometry explains it; plausible non-geometric
contributors (higher flow, larger diameter and wall area, branch density, longer exposure) are
unseparated. In the thesis model this is a baseline-hazard pattern, handled by segment dummies and
log length, not evidence for different geometry slopes.

### Cited Findings
- 208 STEMI patients: occlusions clustered in the proximal third of RCA, LAD and LCx; per 10 mm from the ostium the occlusion risk fell by 13% (RCA), 30% (LAD), 26% (LCx). Single centre, acute occlusions (not incident plaque). — Wang JC, Normand SL, Mauri L, Kuntz RE, Circulation 2004;110:278–284, [doi:10.1161/01.CIR.0000135468.67850.F4](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.CIR.0000135468.67850.F4), PMID 15249505
- SCAPIS (n=25,182, no known CHD, cross-sectional CCTA): atherosclerosis "predominantly found in the proximal left anterior descending artery"; any atherosclerosis in 42.1%. Population-based, but no geometry. — Bergström G et al., Circulation 2021, [doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.055340](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.055340), PMID 34543072
- Calcification starts earlier in the LAD than in other territories (median 4.5 years to second-territory involvement). — Wasilewski JP et al., Pol J Radiol 2026, [doi:10.5114/pjr/210491](https://doi.org/10.5114/pjr/210491), PMID 41705148
- QCA progression after TAVR (n=92) greatest in proximal/mid LAD (61%, 58%), lowest in distal LCx and distal RCA (38%, 39%). Small, special population. — Galo J et al., Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026, [doi:10.1002/ccd.70704](https://doi.org/10.1002/ccd.70704), PMID 42351249
- Risk factors may relate to territory differently: in 978 angiography patients, hypertension was associated with LCx (p=0.029) and RCA (p=0.002) involvement in women and proximal LAD (p=0.024) in men. Retrospective, multiple testing. — Keser N et al., Clinics 2026, [doi:10.1016/j.clinsp.2026.101149](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinsp.2026.101149), PMID 42743686
- Perivascular FAI around the proximal RCA and LAD, but not the LCx, predicted cardiac mortality (CRISP-CT) — an example of a non-geometric, artery-differential signal. — Oikonomou EK et al., Lancet 2018, [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31114-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31114-0), PMID 30170852

### Inferences
- Different proximal gradients by artery (Wang 2004) concern the baseline hazard per segment, which the thesis already lets differ via segment dummies and log length. They do not imply that the per-SD geometry effect differs.
- If geometry partly explains proximal predominance, then adding geometry would attenuate the segment dummies; this is testable in the thesis data (compare dummy coefficients with/without geometry), but no published study has done it.

### Gaps
- No study found that decomposes proximal predominance into geometric vs non-geometric parts.

## 4. Is there a mechanistic reason to expect a constant hazard ratio per within-segment-type SD?

### Takeaway
No strong mechanistic reason either way. Standardizing within segment type makes the scale
comparable but does not equalize the WSS consequence of 1 SD (flow, diameter, branching and motion
differ). The literature supports a common direction for curvature and bifurcation features, but
gives no magnitude per segment and no tested interactions; a shared coefficient is a defensible,
parsimonious default, with heterogeneity best explored (not tested as primary) via a few
prespecified, shrunken artery-level terms.

### Cited Findings
- The WSS–plaque relation depends on disease stage (strong in compensatory enlargement, lost with narrowing). — Wahle A et al. 2006, [doi:10.1016/j.media.2006.03.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2006.03.002)
- Bifurcation angle effects on WSS may be minor relative to curvature radius in idealized LAD/diagonal models. — Chiastra C et al. 2017, [doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2017.04.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2017.04.016)
- LM bifurcation geometry is the most variable of coronary bifurcations (distal angle 80° ± 21°). — Tu S et al. 2012, [doi:10.1007/s10554-011-9996-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10554-011-9996-x)
- Disturbed, low WSS occurs at branches and curvatures generically, driving atherogenic endothelial gene expression (no vessel distinction). — Chiu JJ, Chien S, Physiol Rev 2011, [doi:10.1152/physrev.00047.2009](https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00047.2009), PMID 21248169

### Inferences
- Arguments for heterogeneity: (a) flow and diameter decrease distally, so the WSS change per unit curvature/taper differs; (b) proximal segments are near bifurcations, where geometry effects are local and strongest; (c) the RCA's larger cardiac motion makes static curvature a weaker proxy; (d) z-scoring within segment type equalizes variance but SDs differ in absolute mm⁻¹ or mm/mm, so a constant per-SD HR implies different per-unit HRs across segments (and vice versa).
- Arguments for a shared coefficient: endothelial mechanobiology is the same in all segments; the literature gives a common direction but no segment-specific magnitudes; and the thesis power would not support 9 slopes. Partial pooling (random slope by artery, or a single artery × geometry interaction as a secondary analysis) is a compromise.
- Any artery- or position-specific coefficient found in CGPS data would be exploratory; prespecifying a single LR test of the geometry block (current plan) is consistent with the evidence.

### Gaps
- No source states, or tests, that the per-SD hazard ratio is constant across coronary segments.
- No study with incident plaque in plaque-free people to anchor expected effect sizes per segment.
