Already cited in sections 01–03: Asakura 1990, van der Giessen 2009, Chatzizisis 2007, Gijsen 2019, Kashyap 2022, Zhang 2024, Garcha 2025, Johnston 2007/2008/2009, Ramsay 2005, Grisan 2008, Li 2011, Tello Ayala 2026, Sangalli 2009, Goldsmith 2011, Glagov 1987, Han 2022, Tommasino 2024, Won 2022, Chen 2024, Lee 2024, Bergström 2021, Good 2026, Hageman 2023.

Verdict up front: no study found breaks the gap. The closest near misses remain Lee 2024 (plaque-free segments, radiomic texture, referred) and Chen 2024 (one LAD segment before a myocardial bridge, referred). New near misses worth adding are the low-ESS natural-history studies (PREDICTION, PROSPECT ESS, Sakellarios 2017) and the ICONIC geometry study, all in diseased patients. The gap sentence can stay but should be worded as "identified in this search".

## Q1. Has any longitudinal study measured coronary geometry on a plaque-free baseline CT/CCTA and related it to later plaque?

### Takeaway
No. No study found measures named geometry (curvature/tortuosity, bifurcation angle, diameter ratios, taper) in plaque-free persons and relates it to later plaque. Every geometry study with follow-up is in referred or diseased patients, on one segment or bifurcation, or uses events rather than plaque as outcome.

### Cited Findings
Candidate table (design, population, geometry, outcome, follow-up, plaque-free baseline, verdict):

- **Chen 2024, EHJ-CVI** (already cited). Retrospective, 192 referred patients with LAD myocardial bridge plus 103 external; centreline (curvature, arc length) and cross-section radiomics of the 30 mm before the bridge; outcome any plaque there; median 3.0 y; plaque-free LAD only. **Near miss** (one segment, bridge patients, referred) — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/25/10/1462/7680519)
- **Lee 2024, JCCT** (already cited). PARADIGM, 9,583 plaque-free segments from 1,162 referred patients; radiomic texture, not geometry; new plaque; ≥2 y; segments plaque-free, persons not necessarily. **Near miss** (no geometry) — [JCCT](https://www.journalofcardiovascularct.com/article/S1934-5925(24)00032-7/abstract)
- **Tommasino 2024 (CLAP score)** (already cited). 499 referred, LM bifurcation angle, proximal LAD progression defined as ≥30% stenosis increase or new high-risk features, re-imaging by CCTA or invasive angiography; ~1 y; plaque present. **Irrelevant to the plaque-free claim**, a progression study — [PMC11595042](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11595042/)
- **Han 2022, JAMA Cardiology (ICONIC)** (already cited as Han 2022). Nested case-control, baseline CCTA in patients who later had ACS; vessel tortuosity was one of three adverse geometric features; tortuosity linked to future culprit lesions HR 2.31 (95% CI 1.01–5.27); geometry added to plaque features for identifying culprit lesions. Outcome is the culprit lesion, not plaque development; plaque present. **Irrelevant** (diseased, event outcome) — [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2788006) (snippet)
- **Sakellarios 2017, EuroIntervention (PROSPECT-MSCT)**. Retrospective, 15 ACS patients, 17 bifurcations, 335 3-mm segments; CTCA reconstruction with side branches, CFD ESS; outcomes lumen area reduction, plaque burden and necrotic core increase; 3 y; plaque present. Low ESS predicted all three; models including side branches (Murray's law flow split) predicted best. **Near miss for the WSS mechanism** (very small n, diseased) — [EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/the-effect-of-coronary-bifurcation-and-haemodynamics-in-prediction-of-atherosclerotic-plaque-development-a-serial-computed-tomographic-coronary-angiographic-study)
- **Tantawy 2026, EHJ-CVI Supplement (PARADIGM, conference abstract)**. 1,343 referred patients with serial CCTA (median 3.3 y); 334 (24.9%) plaque-free at baseline; new lesions in 35% of plaque-free vs 46.7% with baseline plaque; predictors baseline plaque (OR 1.84), diabetes (OR 1.38), BMI (OR 1.04/kg/m²). Only stenosis and remodelling measured, no vessel geometry. **Irrelevant for geometry; useful as an update to Won 2022** — [OUP abstract](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)
- **CArTI, AHA 2025, Circulation suppl (conference abstract)**. 992 patients, deep-learning segmentation, tree-level tortuosity/curvature/torsion index; 5-y MACE; holdout C-index 0.648. Plaque status and cohort type not reported in what could be read. Outcome is MACE, not plaque. **Irrelevant to the gap, but relevant state of the art for automated whole-tree geometry** — [AHA](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4365988) (snippet)
- **Medrano-Gracia 2017, J Cardiovasc Transl Res**. Cross-sectional statistical shape model of 446 bifurcations from 211 patients with CAC = 0 and no stenosis; principal component shape scores vs risk factors; hypertension, smoking and diabetes showed no apparent association with shape. No follow-up. **Irrelevant to the gap, but the closest "plaque-free geometry" study; relevant for 02 (shape analysis) and for the claim that geometry is not just a proxy for risk factors** — [PubMed 28028693](https://www.pubmed.gov/28028693/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12265-016-9720-2)

### Inferences
- The gap as worded ("geometry of the three main coronary arteries on a plaque-free baseline scan to plaque development at follow-up") survives. A stricter and also defensible version: no study has related named geometric features in plaque-free persons to later plaque at any interval, in any cohort type.
- Medrano-Gracia 2017 supports a point the review currently does not make: in plaque-free people, bifurcation shape was not clearly tied to hypertension, smoking or diabetes, which is a reason geometry could add to risk factors (this is weak: cross-sectional, small, null results).
- Han 2022 is the only study found where geometry added to other CCTA information for a prospective outcome, but the outcome is culprit lesions in already diseased arteries.

### Gaps
- The full texts of Han 2022 and CArTI were not opened this session; the CArTI cohort type and plaque status are unknown.
- Chinese/Korean health-check serial CT cohorts were not searched one by one.

## Q2. Prospective WSS studies relating baseline geometry/WSS to later plaque, and their populations

### Takeaway
All prospective WSS natural-history studies are in patients with established CAD (stable CAD, ACS), use invasive imaging (IVUS) or CCTA-based CFD over 0.5–3 years, and study progression of existing plaque or events, not plaque development in plaque-free arteries. They support the mechanism in 01 but leave the plaque-free, population question open.

### Cited Findings
- **PREDICTION (Stone 2012, Circulation 126:172–181, PMID 22723305)**. Japanese patients after ACS/PCI (one report describes 219 enrolled); angiography + IVUS reconstruction, ESS and plaque per segment; ~6–10 month follow-up. Plaque area increase predicted by large baseline plaque burden; lumen area decrease predicted independently by large plaque burden and low ESS; combined predictors PPV 41%, NPV 92% for progression needing PCI. Diseased population — [QxMD/PubMed record](https://read.qxmd.com/read/22723305/prediction-of-progression-of-coronary-artery-disease-and-clinical-outcomes-using-vascular-profiling-of-endothelial-shear-stress-and-arterial-plaque-characteristics-the-prediction-study); [ClinicalTrials NCT01316159](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01316159); numbers from abstract/secondary coverage, not full text.
- **PROSPECT ESS (Stone et al. 2018, JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 11(3):462–471, doi 10.1016/j.jcmg.2017.01.031)**. Nested case-control within PROSPECT (697 ACS patients with three-vessel imaging); 97 patients, 145 non-culprit lesions; low ESS (<1.3 Pa) associated with non-culprit MACE, propensity-adjusted HR 4.34 (1.89–10.00); 3-y events 52.1% / 14.4% / 0.0% for high-anatomic-risk + low ESS / low-anatomic-risk + low ESS / physiological-high ESS; no lesion without low ESS caused an event. ACS population — [Mount Sinai scholars record](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/role-of-low-endothelial-shear-stress-and-plaque-characteristics-i-2/) (abstract; first author not confirmed from what I read)
- **EMERALD (Lee JM et al. 2019, JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 12(6):1032–1043, NCT02374775)**. Case-control, 72 ACS patients with CCTA 1 month–2 years before ACS; 66 culprit vs 150 non-culprit lesions; FFR-CT, ΔFFR-CT, WSS and axial plaque stress; hemodynamic parameters improved identification of culprit lesions. Diseased — [Fujita Health Univ. record](https://pure.fujita-hu.ac.jp/en/publications/prognostic-time-frame-of-plaque-and-hemodynamic-characteristics-a/) (abstract)
- **EMERALD II (2025, JACC Cardiovasc Imaging)**. 351 patients; stenosis, plaque burden and lesion pressure drop associated with shorter time to ACS, mainly within 2 years — [ACC journal scan](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/journal-scans/2025/04/23/13/28/emerald-ii) (secondary summary)
- **Sakellarios 2017** (see Q1): CTCA-based ESS, 15 ACS patients, 3 y — [EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/the-effect-of-coronary-bifurcation-and-haemodynamics-in-prediction-of-atherosclerotic-plaque-development-a-serial-computed-tomographic-coronary-angiographic-study)

### Inferences
- PREDICTION and PROSPECT ESS are the landmark human prospective evidence that low WSS precedes progression; 01_geometry_wss.tex currently leans on Chatzizisis 2007 and Gijsen 2019 and could cite them directly. Their populations (post-ACS/PCI) are the opposite end from a plaque-free population sample, which sharpens the gap.
- These studies measure WSS from CFD, not geometric features; the thesis measures geometry as a proxy for WSS. That link is still only simulation-based (Kashyap, Zhang, Garcha, Johnston).

### Gaps
- Full texts of PREDICTION and PROSPECT ESS were not opened (paywall); enrolment numbers (PREDICTION 506 enrolled vs 219 analysed?) should be checked against the paper.
- Samady 2011 (Circulation, ESS and plaque progression/remodelling in 20 patients) was not searched this session; it is a commonly cited earlier human prospective WSS study.

## Q3. Population-based serial CCTA cohorts, and whether any has published geometry analyses

### Takeaway
No population-based serial CCTA cohort has published geometry analyses. SCAPIS has baseline CCTA and a re-examination in progress (already cited); Miami Heart Study is baseline CCTA only; MESA is serial CAC, not CCTA. The longest serial CCTA follow-up found is a Dutch per-protocol 10-year rescan in referred patients, which reports risk factors and plaque, not geometry.

### Cited Findings
- **SCAPIS** (Sweden): random sample of 30,154 aged 50–64 from six university hospitals, 2013–2018; 25,182 without known CHD in the prevalence paper (Bergström 2021, already cited) — [SCAPIS DiVA thesis PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1610920/FULLTEXT01.pdf) (snippet). No 2026 SCAPIS repeat-CCTA progression results were found.
- **SCAPIS, deep-learning plaque volume vs SCORE2 (JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 2026, doi 10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004)**: automated total plaque volume improved prediction of first coronary events beyond SCORE2, CAC and manual CCTA measures, with better reclassification. Event outcome, plaque (not geometry) — [JACC](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004) (snippet only, 403 on fetch; exact C-index/NRI not obtained). **Should be cited**: it is the most recent population-based "CCTA beyond SCORE2" result and complements Hageman 2023.
- **Miami Heart Study** (Nasir et al. 2022, JACC Cardiovasc Imaging): 2,359 asymptomatic adults, cross-sectional CCTA; 58% CAC = 0; 49% had any plaque; about 15–16% of those with CAC = 0 had (non-calcified) plaque; male sex, overweight and obesity predicted plaque when CAC = 0. Baseline only, no geometry — [Houston Methodist record](https://scholars.houstonmethodist.org/en/publications/coronary-atherosclerosis-in-an-asymptomatic-us-population-miami-h/); [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/coronary-plaque-seen-half-asymptomatic-individuals-cta). Sources differ slightly on the CAC = 0 figure (15% vs 16%).
- **MESA "warranty period" of CAC = 0 (Dzaye et al. 2021, JACC Cardiovasc Imaging, PMID 33129734)**: models conversion from CAC = 0 to CAC > 0 on repeat CT; 12.6 ± 2.3 y follow-up for events; suggests rescanning in 3–7 years depending on demographics and risk; diabetes shortens the warranty period. Serial CAC, not CCTA; no geometry — [ACC journal scan](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/journal-scans/2021/05/05/19/31/warranty-period-of-a-calcium); [PMC8076346](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8076346). **Should be cited**: the closest population-based analogue of the thesis outcome (incident disease from a "zero" baseline, ~10 y).
- **Dutch 10-year per-protocol serial CCTA (Leiden/Amsterdam group, ESC 2023 abstract, Eur Heart J 44 Suppl 2, ehad655.152)**: 539 patients from an earlier CCTA cohort (van Diemen 2021) invited for repeat CCTA regardless of symptoms; plaque burden more than doubled; percent atheroma volume progression 0.4 ± 0.5%/year; baseline non-calcified plaque volume the only plaque predictor of progression. Referred (suspected CAD), plaque largely present; no geometry. Follow-ups: Lp(a) and progression (JAMA Cardiology 2024, 299 patients, 10-y interval), diabetes (Cardiovasc Diabetol 2025, >3-fold progression), AI-QCT staging over clinical factors and CACS (JACC CVI 2024) — [ESC365](https://esc365.escardio.org/presentation/266447); [PubMed 41194148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41194148/) (snippets/abstracts). **Should be cited**: it is the only other ~10-year serial CCTA cohort, so the thesis's "about 10 years" is not unique, but the population (referred) differs.
- **PARADIGM progression score (Eur Radiol 2023)**: non-obstructive plaque, 28% progression in derivation, median 3.3 y; plaque present — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00330-023-09880-x) (snippet).
- **CGPS**: no published CGPS paper on serial CCTA or geometry was found (consistent with the 2026-11-07 notes); DANE-HEART is a planned CCTA screening trial within CGPS — search snippet only, no URL with details obtained.

### Inferences
- The thesis can claim to be, as far as this search found, the first ~10-year serial CCTA in a random population sample with a plaque-free baseline, but should say "as far as identified", because SCAPIS re-examination and the CGPS group itself may publish risk-factor (not geometry) results soon.
- The 10-year Dutch cohort weakens any claim that a 10-year interval is novel; the novelty lies in the population and the plaque-free baseline.
- MESA warranty-period papers frame incident disease from CAC = 0 and are the natural comparison for the thesis outcome; the review currently lacks this "power of zero" literature.

### Gaps
- The CAC Consortium "power of zero" papers (Blaha/Budoff) were not fetched; no citable details obtained here.
- First authors of the Dutch 10-year papers (abstract and Lp(a) JAMA Cardiology 2024) were not confirmed from what I read; check before citing.
- SCAPIS 2026 C-index and NRI values for plaque volume over SCORE2 not obtained (paywall).

## Q4. Has any study tested whether coronary geometry adds to SCORE2 or another risk score?

### Takeaway
No study found tests coronary geometry against SCORE2, PCE or any clinical risk score. Incremental-value work beyond risk scores concerns CAC and plaque volume; geometry has been shown incremental only over plaque features, for culprit lesions (Han 2022).

### Cited Findings
- CAC beyond traditional risk assessment: pooled C-statistic gain 0.036 (0.020–0.052) in a meta-analysis; authors note no evidence of clinical benefit — [PMC9039826](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9039826/)
- Hageman 2023 (already cited): CAC improved SCORE2 only slightly.
- SCAPIS 2026: deep-learning plaque volume improved on SCORE2 for first coronary events — [JACC](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004) (snippet)
- Han 2022 (ICONIC): adverse geometry incremental over plaque features for future culprit lesions, not over risk scores — [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2788006) (snippet)
- Lee 2024: radiomics added to clinical risk factors (C-index 0.70 to 0.77) for new plaque in segments; risk factors, not a named score — [JCCT](https://www.journalofcardiovascularct.com/article/S1934-5925(24)00032-7/abstract)
- CArTI: C-index 0.648 for 5-y MACE; whether compared with a risk score unknown — [AHA](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4365988) (snippet)

### Inferences
- "Beyond SCORE2" for geometry is untested; the review can say so. The SCAPIS 2026 plaque-volume paper and Hageman 2023 together show that even direct plaque information adds modestly to SCORE2 for events, which sets a realistic (low) expectation for geometry.

### Gaps
- A 2025 vessel-volume-to-myocardial-mass (V/M) study in type 2 diabetes was mentioned in a snippet as an independent predictor of ischemic events, without a risk-score comparison; not verified.

## Recommended additions (summary for the report writer)
Must add (state of the art, directly bear on the gap): PREDICTION 2012 and PROSPECT ESS 2018 (prospective WSS, diseased), MESA warranty period of CAC = 0 (Dzaye 2021), Miami Heart Study 2022 (plaque with CAC = 0 in asymptomatic population), SCAPIS deep-learning plaque volume beyond SCORE2 (2026), the Dutch 10-year serial CCTA (abstract/papers, referred). Consider: EMERALD 2019 (CCTA hemodynamics before ACS), Sakellarios 2017 (CTCA ESS with side branches), Medrano-Gracia 2017 (shape in plaque-free bifurcations, no link to risk factors), PARADIGM 2026 new-lesion abstract (update to Won 2022; conference abstract), CArTI (conference abstract only, flag). None of these breaks the gap; Han 2022 is already cited and is a near miss only in the sense that geometry predicted a later outcome in diseased arteries.
