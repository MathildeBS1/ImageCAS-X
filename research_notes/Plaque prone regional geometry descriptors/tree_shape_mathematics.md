## Q1. Elastic shape analysis of curves (SRVF, elastic registration, Karcher means, fPCA)

### Takeaway
The SRVF framework is the most mature, derivation-friendly toolkit for comparing individual vessel centerlines: it gives a true distance that is invariant to translation, rotation, (optionally) scale and, crucially, reparameterisation, so it registers anatomically corresponding points along two vessels without landmarks. It has been applied to the internal carotid (AneuRisk65) and to whole brain arterial networks, but I found no coronary application. On our data it works, costs about 0.1 s per pair, is robust to the measured centerline jitter, and separates vessel types only marginally better than rotation-only alignment.

### Cited Findings
- Definition [PK]: for an absolutely continuous curve β: [0,1] → R³, the SRVF is q(t) = β'(t)/√|β'(t)| (q = 0 where β' = 0). The map β ↦ (β(0), q) is a bijection, and the L² metric on q equals (up to a constant) the elastic metric with bending weight 1 and stretching weight 1/4 on the original curves; reparameterisation γ acts isometrically as (q, γ) ↦ (q∘γ)√γ', so ‖q₁ − q₂‖ = ‖(q₁,γ) − (q₂,γ)‖, which is what makes the quotient distance d([q₁],[q₂]) = inf_{O∈SO(3), γ∈Γ} ‖q₁ − O (q₂∘γ)√γ'‖ well-defined. Scaling to ‖q‖ = 1 (unit length) puts shapes on a Hilbert sphere so the shape distance is arccos⟨q₁, q₂*⟩. Source: [Srivastava, Klassen, Joshi, Jermyn 2011, IEEE TPAMI 33(7)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2010.184) [PK]; [Srivastava and Klassen, Functional and Shape Data Analysis, Springer 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4939-4020-2) [PK]. The same definition is stated in [Elastic depths for detecting shape anomalies, arXiv 1907.06759](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.06759) [SNIP].
- Optimisation over Γ is done by dynamic programming on a grid (O(N²·K) for N samples, K neighbourhood slopes) or gradient methods; rotation by SVD (Procrustes) on the SRVFs, alternated with the warp. Python implementation exists in `fdasrsf` (Tucker) [ABS: docs landing page](https://fdasrsf-python.readthedocs.io/en/latest/).
- 1D functions (e.g. a curvature profile κ(s)): the square-root slope function (SRSF) q = sign(f')√|f'| gives the Fisher-Rao phase/amplitude separation. Tucker, Wu and Srivastava define amplitude distance d_a = inf_γ ‖q₁ − (q₂∘γ)√γ'‖ and phase distance as the Fisher-Rao distance of γ to identity, arccos ∫√γ', and build separate fPCA models for amplitude and phase: [Tucker, Wu, Srivastava 2013, CSDA 61:50–66, arXiv 1212.1791](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1791) [SNIP for the paper's existence and aim; formulas PK].
- Karcher (Fréchet) mean: μ = argmin Σ d([μ],[qᵢ])², computed by iterating: align all qᵢ to μ, average in the tangent space (or extrinsically), re-project; tangent PCA of the aligned shapes then gives principal modes [PK, Srivastava and Klassen 2016 book, above].
- AneuRisk65 (65 internal carotid arteries from 3D rotational angiography, six groups by aneurysm position and side): the special issue's shape-analysis entry by Cheng, Dryden, Hitchcock and Le aligned ICAs by translation, rotation and abscissa shift, estimated group mean size-and-shapes by weighted Procrustes, and compared curvature κ = |β'×β''|/|β'|³ and torsion τ of the group means. Upper-aneurysm group means stay closer to a plane (lower torsion); permutation test p = 0.079 (1000 permutations) for mean shape difference, so only weak evidence; leave-one-out classification 67.7 % correct: [Cheng et al. 2014, Electron. J. Stat. 8:1905–1913](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-8/issue-2/Analysis-of-AneuRisk65-data-Internal-carotid-artery-shape-analysis/10.1214/14-EJS938B.pdf) [FT].
- The same special issue includes an SRVF-based elastic registration of the ICAs using 3D coordinates plus radius and curvature along the curve, reporting improved alignment: [Analysis of AneuRisk65 data: Elastic shape registration of curves, EJS 2014](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.ejs/1414588180) [SNIP].
- Sangalli's group introduced the original AneuRisk functional analysis: free-knot regression spline estimates of 3D centerlines and their derivatives for curvature ([Sangalli, Secchi, Vantini, Veneziani 2009, JRSS-C 58(3):285–306](https://sangalli.faculty.polimi.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2009_Sangalli-Secchi-Vantini-Veneziani-JRSSC_compresso.pdf) [SNIP]), then iterative registration of radius and curvature profiles with a similarity index followed by fPCA ([Explorative FDA for 3D geometries of the ICA, Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-7908-2062-1_43) [SNIP]); the result that high ICA curvature associates with aneurysm presence is in [Piccinelli et al. / Sangalli group, "High curvature of the ICA is associated with intracranial aneurysms"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259320718_High_curvature_of_the_internal_carotid_artery_is_associated_with_the_presence_of_intracranial_aneurysms) [SNIP, title only]. The data set itself: [AneuRisk65 data paper, EJS 2014](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-8/issue-2/AneuRisk65-A-dataset-of-three-dimensional-cerebral-vascular-geometries/10.1214/14-EJS938.pdf) [SNIP].
- [PROTO] Invariance check: a centerline resampled with γ(t) = t^1.6 and rotated 40° gives SRVF L² distance 0.385 after rotation only and 0.055 after DP warping (unit-length shapes, M = 100 samples); the residual is discretisation.
- [PROTO] 435 pairs of unit-length SRVFs took 48 s single-core (about 110 ms per pair, M = 100, 3 rotation/warp alternations, pure numpy DP). Elastic distance was on median 0.91 of the rotation-only distance. 1-NN vessel-type accuracy (LAD/LCX/RCA) 0.93 elastic vs 0.90 rotation-only; mean within-type distance 0.43 vs between-type 0.58 (elastic). Half of the DP steps did not lower L² (mostly later alternations after convergence, plus grid discretisation), so a safeguard keeping the better of warped/unwarped was needed.
- [PROTO] Noise: iid 0.05 mm jitter on raw points moved the elastic distance by a median 0.016; 0.5 mm error correlated over 1 mm moved it by 0.163, which is 0.38 of the median same-type inter-subject distance (0.43). So SRVF shape distance inherits the same weakness the earlier report found for κ_a: correlated extractor error at the 1 mm scale is the threat, iid jitter is not.
- [PROTO] LAD extrinsic Karcher mean converged (last step 0.014) in 5 iterations; tangent PCA on 10 aligned LADs: 30 %, 23 %, 14 %, 11 % of variance in the first four modes (n = 10, illustrative only).

### Inferences
- SRVF directly answers the supervisor's correspondence concern at the single-vessel level: the optimal γ is an explicit, interpretable arc-length correspondence, and after registration every vessel can be resampled at M matched positions, i.e. a fixed-length token sequence with anatomical correspondence (up to how good elastic matching is at matching anatomy). Distance normalisation is built in by scaling to unit length; size can be kept as a separate scalar.
- Per-vessel phase-amplitude separation of κ(s) (and later radius r(s)) is the principled way to say "this LAD bends more at 30 % of its length than the population", which is closer to the thesis's regional plaque question than any scalar TI. The amplitude function aligned to the Karcher template is a per-position feature; phase γ records where along the vessel each feature sits.
- The elastic metric (a = 1, b = 1/2 in the SRVF case) fixes the relative cost of bending vs stretching. For vessels whose length differs mostly for anatomical reasons (dominance, truncation), a stretching-heavier metric or partial matching is worth considering; the SRVF assumes both ends correspond, which is violated when distal ends are truncated (the earlier report found truncation to be the dominant failure for TI).
- Unit-length scaling makes the SRVF distance blind to vessel length, so the length confound from the earlier report is removed by construction, and length should enter models as an explicit covariate.

### Gaps
- No coronary-artery SRVF or elastic FDA study was found in the searches run (one targeted search returned only patents and non-coronary papers). This is a gap in the literature or in my search, not a confirmed absence.
- Exact numbers (sample classification performance) of the SRVF elastic-registration AneuRisk paper and of Sangalli's k-mean alignment could not be verified (web session limit).
- Partial/open-end elastic matching (for truncated distal ends) was not researched.

## Q2. Functional data analysis of per-vessel profiles (curvature, torsion, radius vs normalised arc length), registration, functional regression

### Takeaway
FDA gives the statistical layer on top of Q1: register profiles (phase vs amplitude), then fPCA or functional regression (scalar-on-function or function-on-scalar) against outcomes. AneuRisk is the only vascular precedent found and its evidence for shape differences was weak on 65 curves, which is a realistic warning for effect sizes on small cohorts.

### Cited Findings
- AneuRisk FDA pipeline: estimate curve and derivatives by free-knot splines, register radius and curvature profiles iteratively to remove "ancillary" (phase) variability, then fPCA: [Explorative FDA of ICA 3D geometries](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-7908-2062-1_43) [SNIP]; [Sangalli et al. 2009 JRSS-C](https://sangalli.faculty.polimi.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2009_Sangalli-Secchi-Vantini-Veneziani-JRSSC_compresso.pdf) [SNIP].
- A later AneuRisk65 re-analysis used warped logistic discrimination (registration plus classification jointly): [Analysis of AneuRisk65 data: warped logistic discrimination, arXiv 1310.2236](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.2236) [SNIP, title only].
- Functional classifiers with domain selection (find the sub-interval of the profile that discriminates) have been applied to AneuRisk-type data: [Classification of functional fragments by regularized linear classifiers with domain selection, arXiv 1708.08257](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.08257) [SNIP, title only].
- Curvature and torsion formulas used on vessel centerlines: κ = |β'×β''|/|β'|³, τ = (β'×β'')·β'''/|β'×β''|² [FT, stated in Cheng et al. 2014 above].
- Tucker et al. give generative models of phase and amplitude separately, so random samples of realistic functions can be drawn: [arXiv 1212.1791](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1791) [SNIP]. This is a candidate route to "anatomically realistic data augmentation" requested by the supervisor.
- Coronary segment-level curvature and torsion (proximal, mid, distal) have been catalogued; LAD curvature, torsion and tortuosity are highest distally and LAD torsion exceeds RCA torsion: [Zhu et al., "Cataloguing the geometry of the human coronary arteries", PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/) [SNIP].

### Inferences
- For plaque-prone regions in the CGPS longitudinal design, a function-on-scalar model (κ(s) registered, regressed on later plaque indicator per position) or a scalar-on-function logistic model with domain selection are the natural FDA tools; both require registered profiles, which Q1 supplies.
- Torsion τ involves a third derivative and diverges where κ → 0, so a torsion profile is far noisier than κ(s); if torsion-like information is wanted, prefer a frame-based quantity (Q6) or the SRVF of the 3D curve, which carries the full geometry without third derivatives.
- Radius r(s) is not in the ImageCAS-X centerline files; it would have to come from the lumen surfaces. SRVF can take extra coordinates (the AneuRisk registration used radius and curvature as extra channels), so (x, y, z, r) curves are a documented extension.

### Gaps
- No coronary FDA study with phase-amplitude registration was found.
- Effect sizes for curvature-profile association with later plaque are unknown; AneuRisk's weak p = 0.079 on n = 65 suggests low power on small groups.

## Q3. Tree-space statistics: BHV, tree edit distance, QED, elastic tree shape spaces

### Takeaway
There is a clear lineage: combinatorial phylogenetic tree space (BHV) → Feragen's tree-like shape spaces (QED vs TED, airways) → elastic SRVF-based trees (Duncan: main branch + side branches; Wang, Laga, Srivastava: hierarchical, with branch sliding and birth/death) → elastic shape graphs (brain arterial networks, retinal vessels). All of them struggle with branch correspondence and are expensive; they give whole-tree distances and means rather than per-node features. For coronaries, where named-segment labels already give correspondence, the heavy combinatorial machinery is largely unnecessary.

### Cited Findings
- Feragen et al. build a shape space of tree-like shapes and compare QED (quotient Euclidean distance, arising from the quotient by tree re-orderings) with TED (classical tree edit distance). QED has existence and local uniqueness of geodesics and averages; TED has good algorithmic properties but not those geometric ones. The space has singularities where topology changes. Experiments: synthetic trees and airway trees from pulmonary CT: [Feragen et al., "Toward a theory of statistical tree-shape analysis", arXiv 1207.5371 / IEEE TPAMI 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5371) [ABS]; [Feragen et al., "Means in spaces of tree-like shapes", ICCV 2011](http://www2.compute.dtu.dk/~sohau/papers/iccv2011/Feragen_ICCV_2011_Means.pdf) [SNIP]. Computing QED is NP-hard in general [PK, stated in the TPAMI paper; not re-checked].
- BHV tree space (Billera, Holmes, Vogtmann 2001): trees with a fixed leaf set as points in a CAT(0) cube complex of orthants, each orthant a topology with edge lengths as coordinates; geodesics computable in polynomial time by Owen and Provan: [Owen and Provan, "A fast algorithm for computing geodesic distances in tree space"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47755732_A_Fast_Algorithm_for_Computing_Geodesic_Distances_in_Tree_Space) [SNIP]; BHV original [PK](https://doi.org/10.1006/aama.2001.0759). BHV needs labelled leaves and ignores embedding geometry.
- Duncan, Klassen, Srivastava model a neuron as a main branch (parameterised curve in R³) plus side branches attached at arbitrary points, impose an elastic metric, and compute geodesics between arbitrary trees: [Duncan et al. 2018, Ann. Appl. Stat. 12(3):1385–1421](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.aoas/1536652959) [SNIP].
- Wang, Laga, Srivastava extend SRVF to hierarchical tree-shaped 3D objects: metric quantifies bending, stretching and branch sliding, and handles branch birth/death; claimed to avoid the shrinkage of edge collapse and node split in QED/TED; applied to neurons and botanical trees for geodesics, means, modes, fitted distributions and random synthesis: [Wang, Laga, Srivastava, arXiv 2110.08693, IEEE TPAMI 2024 (PubMed 37983157)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08693) [ABS]. A 4D (spatiotemporal) extension exists: [arXiv 2509.19048](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19048) [SNIP].
- Elastic shape graphs for brain arterial networks: nodes connected by 3D curves; Riemannian metric, geodesics, means, covariances, PCA; networks split into four components; 92 subjects; age has a clear effect (increased shape variance with age), gender effects inconclusive: [Guo, Basu Bal, Needham, Srivastava, arXiv 2007.04793; Ann. Appl. Stat. 16(2):1130–1150, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04793) [ABS]. Theory: [Representations, metrics and statistics for shape analysis of elastic graphs, arXiv 2003.00287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00287) [SNIP].
- Retinal blood-vessel networks (STARE, DRIVE): multi-scale representation of shape graphs using effective resistance to cluster nodes, elastic averaging of edge curves, registration of graphs of very different complexity: [Basu Bal, Guo, Needham, Srivastava, arXiv 2211.15514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15514) [ABS].
- Plant roots: geometry and topology modelled with elastic trees: [arXiv 1905.06812](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.06812) [SNIP].

### Inferences
- The difficulty all of these papers solve (unknown correspondence between branches) is mostly solved for coronaries by anatomy: ImageCAS-X provides `segment_label` per centerline point (LM, LAD, LCX, RCA, D, OM, PDA...). A "labelled elastic tree" (each named segment an SRVF, attached at a registered position on its parent) needs only per-segment elastic registration plus a null curve for absent branches, which is the Duncan construction with correspondence given. That is much cheaper than graph matching.
- Whole-tree elastic distances give a population embedding via MDS or tangent PCA on the Karcher mean. That is an interpretable alternative (and a baseline) for the supervisor's learned latent-query embedding: if a GNN embedding does not beat tangent-PCA of a labelled elastic tree on the same downstream task, the network is not earning its keep on small data.
- BHV and TED are poor fits: BHV ignores geometry and needs fixed leaf sets; TED on geometric trees shrinks shapes (per Wang et al.) and has no mean.

### Gaps
- No application of these tree-shape spaces to coronary trees was found.
- Computational cost of the Wang et al. and BAN methods per pair was not extracted (full texts too large or not fetched).

## Q4. Topological data analysis: persistent homology, TMD, Reeb graphs, Euler characteristic curves

### Takeaway
Persistent homology of a tree under a scalar function (height, distance from root) summarises branching at all scales in a rotation-aware (height) or rotation-invariant (root distance) way; on brain arteries (n = 98) it correlated with age more strongly than total length. For a coronary tree with only 4 to 7 leaves per side in ImageCAS-X, the H0 barcode is essentially the list of branch path lengths, so it adds little beyond named-branch lengths; the richer H1 "thickening" analysis captures how branches fold back through space, which is closer to tortuosity at tree scale.

### Cited Findings
- Bendich, Marron, Miller, Pieloch, Skwerer analysed n = 98 brain artery trees (MRA, tube-tracking output, healthy volunteers, ages covering adulthood). H0: filtration of the tree graph by a height function h (sublevel sets G(r) of vertices with h ≤ r), persistence diagram Dgm0(h) of component births and deaths, stable under small perturbations of h. H1: loops formed when a point sample of the tree is thickened in 3D (Rips-type filtration), capturing how vessels bend near each other: [Bendich et al. 2016, Ann. Appl. Stat. 10(1):198–218; arXiv 1411.6652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.6652) [FT].
- Results: PC1 of the vector of the 100 longest H0 bars correlates with age ρ = 0.53 (p < 1e-7); for H1 ρ = 0.61 (p < 1e-10). Controlling for total length reduces the H1 age correlation to ρ = 0.35, still non-negligible. Sex: permutation test p = 0.1 on H0 vectors, p = 0.032 on H1 vectors. The age signal is carried by medium-scale features, not the most persistent ones: the two most persistent bars alone give ρ = 0.26; the 28th-longest bar alone is a useful predictor: same source [FT].
- Stated limitation: the trees are tube-tracking outputs, so H0 (which depends on connectivity) may pick up algorithm artefacts; H1 on thickened point samples is protected by the stability theorem even when connectivity changes: same source [FT].
- The TMD (Topological Morphology Descriptor) of Kanari et al. encodes a neuron's branching via a spherical (radial distance from soma) filtration into a persistence barcode where each bar is the start and end radial distance of a branch under an elder-rule pairing; used to separate real from random trees and to classify neuron types: [Kanari et al., arXiv 1603.08432](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08432) [ABS]; later theory on which trees share a barcode: [From trees to barcodes and back again, arXiv 2010.11620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11620) [SNIP]; [The fiber of persistent homology for trees, arXiv 2303.16176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16176) [SNIP].
- Accumulated persistence function, a functional summary with a brain artery application: [arXiv 1611.00630](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.00630) [SNIP].
- TDA in coronary disease so far targets plaque morphology and uncertainty, not tree geometry: [Quantifying the unknowns of plaque morphology: topological uncertainty in CAD, PubMed 40290585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40290585/) [SNIP]; a review "Topological data analysis in the assessment of coronary atherosclerosis" exists in [Mayo Clin Proc Digital Health 2025](https://www.mcpdigitalhealth.org/article/S2949-7612(25)00006-9/pdf) [SNIP; full text blocked, 403].
- [PROTO] TMD-style H0 barcode on the left tree (f = geodesic path distance from the file's `start_points` root, elder rule by leaf): 5 cases had 4 to 7 leaves and therefore 4 to 7 bars; the longest bar 107 to 151 mm (the main trunk, LAD or LCX), no bar shorter than 2 mm, at most one bar under 10 mm. So on the curated ImageCAS-X centerlines the barcode is a short list of branch lengths from bifurcation to tip.

### Inferences
- What PH captures that tortuosity does not: multi-scale branching (H0, a length-weighted branching pattern) and spatial folding, i.e. distinct vessel parts coming close in space (H1 on thickened samples). Neither is a local bending measure.
- For ImageCAS-X the H0 barcode with root-distance filtration is nearly redundant with named-segment lengths, since the trees are sparse and curated. Its value would rise on automatically extracted trees with many small side branches (e.g. from CAS-Net predictions), where it is also most exposed to spurious spurs (each spur adds a bar of its own length, and the barcode is stable in bottleneck distance only for perturbations of f, not for added branches).
- PH gives a whole-tree (or whole-side) vector, not per-node features; it could enter a GNN as a global token, but not as node features with correspondence.
- The Euler characteristic curve of a tree under a sublevel filtration is determined by the H0 diagram (no H1 in a tree), so for trees it adds nothing new [inference from definitions]. Reeb graphs of the height function on the lumen surface would recover roughly the centerline tree itself.

### Gaps
- No persistent homology study of coronary centerline trees was found.
- The Kanari 2018 Neuroinformatics full text (classification accuracies, stability statements) could not be fetched (Springer redirect).

## Q5. Currents, varifolds and LDDMM for curves and trees

### Takeaway
Currents and varifolds embed curves and trees in a reproducing-kernel Hilbert space, giving correspondence-free, topology-tolerant data terms; LDDMM then yields diffeomorphic registration and atlases. They suit registration of whole trees whose topology differs (e.g. CAS-Net output vs reference), and have been used on lung vessel and airway trees; I found no coronary atlas built this way. They yield a whole-tree momentum field, not interpretable per-segment scalars.

### Cited Findings
- Pan et al. registered lung vessel and airway trees with currents and varifolds via Deformetrica; varifolds are orientation-free, which helps when tangent orientation is ambiguous in disconnected vessel/airway trees; each segment is represented by its centre and tangent (a momentum): [Pan et al., "Current- and varifold-based registration of lung vessel and airway trees", CVPR Workshops 2016](https://mlanthology.org/cvprw/2016/pan2016cvprw-currentand/) [SNIP].
- Varifold metrics, quantisation and registration theory: [Hsieh and Charon, arXiv 1903.11196](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.11196) [SNIP]; partial matching in varifold space: [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-78191-0_10) [SNIP]; functional currents carrying a signal on the shape: [arXiv 1206.3564](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.3564) [SNIP].
- Mathematics [PK]: a curve C is represented as the current μ_C(ω) = ∫_C ⟨ω(x), τ(x)⟩ ds for vector fields ω in an RKHS W with kernel K; ‖μ_C‖²_{W*} = ∬ ⟨τ(x), K(x,y) τ(y)⟩ ds(x) ds(y). The varifold replaces ⟨τ, τ'⟩ by an even function of the angle such as ⟨τ, τ'⟩², removing orientation. Discretised: each segment contributes its centre and length-weighted tangent. Cost is O(n²) kernel evaluations per distance (or FFT/KeOps accelerated). Sources: [Glaunès, Qiu, Miller, Younes 2008, "Large deformation diffeomorphic metric curve mapping", IJCV](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11263-008-0141-9) [PK]; [Charon and Trouvé 2013, "The varifold representation of nonoriented shapes", SIAM J. Imaging Sci.](https://doi.org/10.1137/130918885) [PK]; [Durrleman et al. 2014, "Morphometry of anatomical shape complexes with dense deformations and sparse parameters", NeuroImage](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.06.043) [PK].

### Inferences
- A varifold distance is a principled, correspondence-free loss between predicted and reference coronary trees and a sanity metric for extractor agreement (the earlier report's open "extractor-to-extractor error spectrum" question). Kernel width sets the scale at which differences count.
- For population shape analysis, LDDMM atlases produce initial momenta per subject that live on the template's points; if the template is a labelled mean coronary tree, the momenta at template nodes are per-node features with correspondence given by the deformation. That is an elegant fit to a node-token model but the most expensive option here and the hardest to interpret.

### Gaps
- No coronary LDDMM/currents atlas was verified (search attempt returned lung and brain work only).
- Runtime of Deformetrica on trees of about 1000 to 3000 points was not established.

## Q6. Discrete differential geometry of space curves: Frenet vs parallel transport, writhe, twist, scale space, wavelets

### Takeaway
The Frenet frame is undefined at inflection points (κ = 0) and torsion is a third-derivative, noise-amplifying quantity; the Bishop (parallel-transport, rotation-minimising) frame is globally defined and turns the curve into two "normal development" curvatures (k₁, k₂) with κ² = k₁² + k₂². That 2D curvature vector along arc length is the cleanest local descriptor for a node-token model. Writhe measures global self-coiling (a whole-vessel number); multiscale curvature (scale-space, wavelets) formalises the "which scale bends" question the earlier report tackled with σ and chord ℓ.

### Cited Findings
- Bishop frame [PK]: T' = k₁N₁ + k₂N₂, N₁' = −k₁T, N₂' = −k₂T; exists for any C² regular curve (Frenet needs C³ and κ ≠ 0); κ = √(k₁² + k₂²) and the Frenet torsion is τ = dθ/ds with θ = atan2(k₂, k₁), so torsion appears only as the rate of rotation of the curvature vector in the (N₁, N₂) plane. Frame is unique up to one constant rotation, fixed e.g. at the ostium. [Bishop 1975, "There is more than one way to frame a curve", Amer. Math. Monthly 82(3):246–251](https://doi.org/10.2307/2319846) [PK]. Discrete version: propagate the normal by the minimal rotation taking t_i to t_{i+1} (double-reflection method) [PK, Wang et al. 2008 ACM TOG](https://doi.org/10.1145/1330511.1330513).
- Writhe [PK]: Wr(C) = (1/4π) ∬ ((T(s)×T(t))·(r(s)−r(t)))/|r(s)−r(t)|³ ds dt; Călugăreanu–White–Fuller: linking number of a ribbon Lk = Tw + Wr. For an open vessel, writhe is defined by the same double integral (non-integer); it is invariant to rotation and scale, O(n²). [Fuller 1971, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.68.4.815) [PK]. No vascular application of writhe was verified; a helical-flow literature links coronary curvature/torsion to helical blood flow and plaque ([De Nisco et al., Atherosclerosis 2020](https://www.atherosclerosis-journal.com/article/S0021-9150(20)30054-X/abstract) [SNIP, title only]).
- Curvature scale space [PK]: track zero crossings or extrema of curvature as a function of Gaussian scale σ ([Mokhtarian and Mackworth 1992, IEEE TPAMI 14(8)](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.149593)). Wavelet estimation of multidimensional curves and derivatives was also used for AneuRisk: [Wavelets in FDA: estimation of multidimensional curves and their derivatives](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/241756340_Wavelets_in_functional_data_analysis_Estimation_of_multidimensional_curves_and_their_derivatives) [SNIP].
- The earlier report already derived the chord-angle curvature estimator, its noise floors and the TI–tangent-variance identity, and briefly raised the Darboux frame (κ² = κ_n² + κ_g²) for the RCA on the heart surface: see `reports/Vessel specific tortuosity math and tests.md`. Not repeated here.

### Inferences
- A per-node feature vector (k₁, k₂)(s) in the Bishop frame is rotation-covariant only up to the initial frame; to make it invariant, use κ(s) plus the rotation rate of the curvature vector, or express (k₁, k₂) in a heart-anchored frame (e.g. the Darboux frame on an epicardial surface proxy, or the normal to a local fit of the heart surface), which also makes the RCA's imposed C-shape separable from local bending.
- A multiscale curvature vector per node (κ at σ = 1, 2, 4, 8 mm) is a cheap, interpretable "wavelet-like" node feature that lets a model find its own band, instead of fixing σ and ℓ; it also naturally encodes the RCA's baseline vs local curvature split the earlier report found.
- Writhe (per vessel, or on a sliding window as a local "coiling" measure) is the only descriptor here specific to 3D helicity; its noise behaviour on our centerlines is untested.

### Gaps
- No vascular study using writhe or Bishop-frame curvature as outcome predictor was verified.
- Noise and truncation robustness of writhe and of k₁, k₂ on ImageCAS-X not tested.

## Q7. Which give per-node or per-segment features for a GNN with learned latent queries, and which give a whole-tree embedding?

### Takeaway
Per-node or per-segment, with correspondence: SRVF/FDA registered profiles (aligned κ(s), r(s), and the warp γ), Bishop-frame curvature vectors, multiscale curvature, and LDDMM momenta on a template. Whole-tree only: elastic tree/graph distances (via MDS or tangent PCA), persistence diagrams and TMD, varifold distances, writhe per vessel. The strongest thesis design is a labelled elastic representation: named segments registered by SRVF to per-segment Karcher templates, resampled at a fixed number of matched positions, which is both an interpretable statistical model on its own and a correspondence-consistent tokenisation for the supervisors' latent-query network.

### Cited Findings
- Supervisors' requirements: tokenise the tree with anatomical correspondence so the same token position means the same anatomy; normalise distances so tree structure remains; node features such as xyz, lumen radius, arc length from root, depth, L/R embedding; variable-length input to fixed-length embedding via learned latent queries; caution about data hunger of transformers and positional encodings; interest in realistic augmentation: [from_superviser.tex](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/from_superviser.tex) [FT].
- Elastic registration supplies exactly a reparameterisation γ per curve that maps it onto a common template parameter, and tangent PCA on aligned SRVFs gives a finite-dimensional per-shape vector: [Srivastava et al. 2011 TPAMI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2010.184) [PK]; demonstrated on vessels in [BAN, arXiv 2007.04793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04793) [ABS] and ICA [Cheng et al. 2014](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-8/issue-2/Analysis-of-AneuRisk65-data-Internal-carotid-artery-shape-analysis/10.1214/14-EJS938B.pdf) [FT].
- Generative phase/amplitude models (Tucker et al.) and sampling from fitted distributions on tree shape space (Wang et al.) are published routes to synthesising realistic shapes: [arXiv 1212.1791](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1791) [SNIP]; [arXiv 2110.08693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08693) [ABS].
- Bendich et al.'s result that total length explains part but not all of a PH age signal (ρ 0.61 → 0.35 after residualising on length) shows global topological summaries carry covariate-relevant signal beyond size: [arXiv 1411.6652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.6652) [FT].

### Inferences
- Recommended pair of "advanced" methods for the thesis: (1) SRVF/SRSF elastic registration per named vessel, giving Karcher templates, registered κ(s) (and later r(s)) profiles, phase γ, and tangent-PCA modes, i.e. both an interpretable statistical baseline and a correspondence-consistent node tokenisation; (2) one whole-tree complement, either persistence (H1 on thickened point cloud, the Bendich variant that carried signal beyond length) or a labelled elastic tree distance for population embedding. Currents/LDDMM are better used as an evaluation metric between extracted trees than as a descriptor.
- Distance normalisation: unit-length SRVF removes size; keep log length, and perhaps tree extent, as separate explicit covariates, as the earlier report already argued for κ_a.
- For the CGPS longitudinal question (baseline shape of regions that later develop plaque), registration to a per-vessel template is what makes "the same region" meaningful across subjects and across the two scans; SRVF gives that directly, TDA does not.
- The prototype's modest gain over rotation-only alignment (1-NN 0.93 vs 0.90; median distance ratio 0.91) says elastic warping matters less for whole-vessel identity than for position-specific comparison; its value should be judged on regional tasks, not on vessel-type separation.

### Gaps
- Whether elastic registration matches true anatomical landmarks (e.g. D1 and OM1 take-off positions) was not tested; this is the key validation, and ImageCAS-X's labels make it cheap: measure the spread of registered take-off positions under γ.
- Scalability of DP to 800 scans × 3+ vessels: at 0.1 s per pair, a Karcher mean over 560 train vessels is about 560 alignments per iteration, so seconds to minutes; pairwise matrices (about 157k pairs per vessel type) are about 4 to 5 CPU hours per type, fine as a batch job.
- Several canonical references are tagged [PK] because the web tools reached a session limit; they should be verified (e.g. via the paper-review skill) before being cited in thesis prose.
