Perturbation definitions (shared by all sections). Jitter is added to the raw centerline points before resampling at 0.25 mm: "iid0.1" and "iid0.25" are independent Gaussian noise with SD 0.1 or 0.25 mm per coordinate (3D RMS 0.17 and 0.43 mm); "corr0.25" is the same 0.25 mm noise smoothed by a Gaussian over 1 mm of neighbouring points and rescaled to 0.25 mm SD. ## Region definition: can LM, LAD-D1, LCx-OM1, RCA ostium, crux/PDA and fixed proximal and peri-bifurcation windows be located reproducibly, and in what fraction of scans?

### Takeaway
Yes, deterministically from the segment labels, for 96 to 100 % of scans at LM, LAD-D1, crux and the RCA ostium, but only 80 % at LCx-OM1, because 20 % of trees have no OM1 label arising from the LCx. Fixed proximal windows (2 to 17 mm) are available wherever the anchor exists; a symmetric +-5 mm window around the LM bifurcation is often cut off by a short left main.

### Cited Findings
- Anchors reuse the repo's existing tree code: `topology.graph.build_tree` and `topology.angles.daughter_pair` / `takeoff_pair` / `_anchor`. LM = LAD vs LCX daughters of the left main; LAD-D1 and LCX-OM1 = D1 or OM1 against the trunk continuing past its origin; CRUX = R-PDA vs R-PLA, else L-PDA vs L-PLA; RCA ostium = the root of the RCA main vessel. Proximal windows run over arc 2 to 17 mm from the LM bifurcation (LAD, LCx) or from the RCA ostium; trunk windows run -5 to +5 mm of arc along the trunk through each bifurcation. — [extract.py](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/extract.py)
- Presence in the 100 scans: LM 98 (1 without a left main, 1 with LAD and LCX in disconnected parts of the tree); LAD-D1 97 (3 with no D1 arising from the LAD); **LCX-OM1 80** (20 with no OM1 arising from the LCX); CRUX 96 (2 with no R-PDA or L-PDA, 1 with no R-PLA, 1 with two R-PDA/R-PLA pairs at the same generation); RCA ostium 100. — [analysis.txt §A](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/analysis.txt)
- Window coverage: proxLAD and proxLCX 98/100, proxRCA 100/100, trunk windows follow their anchors. The LM trunk window (-5 mm reaches back towards the ostium) was incomplete in 12 of 98 scans at 7.2 to 8.8 mm of coverage. The 5 mm chord curvature there had enough chord support in only 60 of 98, because the short LM leaves no room for a 5 mm chord behind the junction. — [analysis.txt §C](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/analysis.txt)
- The normative left main length is 10.5 +- 5.3 mm (Medrano-Gracia 2016, n = 300 CCTA with zero calcium, doi:10.4244/EIJV12I7A139, as recorded in `docs_thesis/literature.md`). That is consistent with a -5 mm window often not fitting. — [docs_thesis/literature.md](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/docs_thesis/literature.md)
- Dominance in these 100 scans: R 95, co-dominant 3, L 2. — [analysis.txt §E](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/analysis.txt)

### Inferences
- Region location is not a reproducibility problem on the reference data. The labels decide the anchor, so there is no location noise beyond the centerline itself. The issue is **label availability**. On CGPS, which has binary lumen predictions, every regional descriptor depends on a labelling or correspondence step that does not exist yet; that step, not the geometry, is the first feasibility risk.
- The LCx-OM1 region loses a fifth of the cohort. That missingness is plausibly anatomical (a small LCx, left-dominant variation, labelling choice) and so not random; report it as a variable, not only as NaN rows.
- For the LM, use a one-sided window (daughter side only), or a parent window capped by the LM length, rather than a symmetric +-5 mm.

### Gaps
- Whether a missing OM1 means true absence or an annotation choice was not checked against the images.
- Location reproducibility on independently extracted centerlines (for example CAS-Net predictions) was not tested; only the delivered reference centerlines were used.

## Bifurcation angles in 3D: sensitivity to tangent definition and smoothing, jitter ICC, distributions against literature

### Takeaway
The angle is measurable at every located bifurcation, but its **value is defined by the tangent choice**: at the same smoothing, the six tangent definitions differ within a case by a median of 19 to 27 degrees, and short secants drift by 35 degrees between sigma 0 and 2 mm. A core-skipping fit (line over the 3 mm past the junction radius) or a 5 mm secant, at sigma 1 to 2 mm, gives LM 78 to 81 degrees, in line with the CCTA literature. It keeps ICC 0.81 to 0.92 under 0.25 mm iid jitter at the LM and 0.58 to 0.88 at the side-branch bifurcations.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The LM medians from fitcore3, sec5 and sec3 at sigma 1 (79 to 81 degrees) sit inside the 75 to 89 degree band of the CCTA cohorts. sec2 at sigma 0 (99 degrees) and fit3-8 (72 degrees) sit at its edges. The literature spread is itself about as large as our between-definition spread, which supports the repo's decision to fix one automatic definition in advance.
- Recommended pre-declared definition: **fitcore3 (or sec5) at sigma 1 mm, with sigma 2 mm as a sensitivity analysis**. It agrees with sec5 at ICC 0.95 or higher, barely moves with sigma, and at the LM survives even the 0.43 mm RMS iid stress (ICC 0.81). fit3-8 is more jitter-robust, but it measures the course of the daughters 3 to 8 mm downstream rather than the take-off (18.5 degrees lower at D1), so it is a different descriptor.
- Side-branch angles (D1, OM1, crux) are about one ICC tier weaker than the LM under heavy jitter. The side branches are thin, so the same jitter is a larger fraction of their shaft. Under realistic noise (iid0.1) every angle has ICC 0.97 or higher.
- Within-case SD under the jitter stress (about 8 degrees at sigma 1) is below the literature's 12 degree between-technique disagreement, but not by a large factor. The 12 degree disease gradient reported by Juan 2017 would still be hard to detect case by case.

### Gaps
- No ground-truth angle exists here. ICC measures stability, not accuracy, and the phantom accuracy study (Givehchi) was not replicated.
- Whether the thesis angle should be daughter-daughter or parent-relative (thetaA/thetaB) is a design question; thetaA/thetaB are less reliable (ICC 0.60 to 0.79 under iid0.25).
- Intermediate-artery presence (which shifts the LM angle by 14 degrees in Medrano-Gracia) was flagged by `topology/angles.py` but not stratified here.

## Local curvature and torsion in regional windows: ICC under jitter, resampling and window shifts; is torsion usable?

### Takeaway
Chord-based curvature at a 5 mm chord (kappa_l5) is usable in every regional window: ICC 0.97 or higher under realistic jitter, 0.70 to 0.94 under the 0.25 mm stress, and 0.86 to 0.98 under +-2 mm window shifts. Frenet finite-difference curvature is not usable under the stress at sigma 1 mm. **Torsion is not usable at this resolution**: its per-case ranking is uncorrelated between sigma 0.5 and 2 mm (Spearman -0.15 to 0.01), its jitter ICC is 0.02 to 0.41 even at 0.1 mm iid noise, and it reads larger than curvature.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions: kappa_diff is the mean Frenet curvature |r' x r''| / |r'|^3 from central differences on the 0.25 mm resampled, Gaussian-smoothed curve. kappa_l3 and kappa_l5 are the mean of theta/ell, with theta the angle between the chords Q(s - ell) to Q(s) and Q(s) to Q(s + ell) (exactly 1/R on a circle), requiring chord support over at least 80 % of the window. Torsion |tau| is the median absolute Frenet torsion in the window; tau_signed is its mean. — [extract.py](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/extract.py)

- The earlier report's noise law: torsion SD scales as sigma_n / (kappa ell^3), one power of ell and one of 1/kappa worse than curvature, and it recommended dropping torsion and using kappa_a at a 5 mm chord with sigma 1 mm. — [reports/Vessel specific tortuosity math and tests.md](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)

### Inferences
- The whole-vessel recommendation (kappa_a at a 5 mm chord, sigma 1 mm) carries over to 15 mm proximal and 10 mm peri-bifurcation windows. Regional windows keep the same noise robustness; the extra risk is window placement, and a +-2 mm shift costs about 0.1 of ICC.
- A 5 mm chord inside a 10 mm window leaves only a few independent chords, so the regional kappa_l5 is close to a single turning-angle measurement. In short windows (the LM trunk) it often cannot be computed at all. kappa_l3 is the fallback there but is less robust (iid0.25 ICC 0.29 to 0.67 at sigma 1).
- The proxRCA window lies in the segment where the earlier report flagged the shepherd's crook. Regional RCA curvature may therefore be the natural place to capture it, but that was not tested.
- Torsion should be excluded from the regional set. The only torsion-like quantity that looks tractable is the out-of-plane angle at a bifurcation (implemented in `topology/angles.py`), which was not tested here.

### Gaps
- The realistic correlated-error spectrum of an independent extractor is still unknown, as the previous report also said. The ICCs above bracket it; they do not measure it.
- No window-length sweep (10 vs 15 vs 20 mm) was run; only 15 mm proximal and 10 mm trunk windows.

## Radius: two methods, their agreement, and taper, diameter ratios, Murray exponent and Finet ratio at bifurcations

### Takeaway
Radius along the centerline is obtainable. Mask EDT and surface distance agree to r = 0.96 at point level, with EDT reading 0.135 mm larger on average (0.18 mm in vessels thinner than 0.75 mm). Parent and main-daughter calibers agree between methods at ICC 0.87 to 0.95, and **the Finet ratio is usable at the LM (edt 0.63, ICC between methods 0.85)**. Diameter ratios and Finet are weaker at side branches and poor at the crux (ICC 0.44). The **Murray exponent is not reliable**: it is unsolvable in 12 to 37 % of bifurcations and has method ICC 0.48 to 0.69. Proximal taper over 20 mm is too small to measure robustly.

### Inferences
- The two methods are not independent. The surfaces are derived from the segmentations (per CLAUDE.md), so their agreement tests the radius algorithm, not segmentation error. The EDT bias of about half an in-plane voxel matches its documented over-read. The bias is largest in thin vessels, so it inflates side-branch radii relatively more and pushes ratios in a systematic direction (Finet is 0.05 to 0.11 lower with EDT). Fix one method and never mix the two.
- r_edt is the more robust choice for bifurcation ratios: the surface distance fails where the centerline hugs the wall (4.6 % of points), which is what destroys the surface Murray deviation. A cross-sectional area measure (slice the mask or mesh orthogonal to the tangent) would be the natural third method and would remove the over-read, but it was not built.
- The LM Finet median of 0.63 to 0.66 is near the 0.678 Finet value (subject to verifying that citation). At the LM, Finet and the daughter ratio are the most defensible caliber descriptors. At the crux both are method-dependent.
- The Murray exponent is ill-conditioned: small radius errors near the solvability boundary move k by several units, and a fifth of cases have no solution. Use the cube-law deviation or the area ratio with a fixed method instead, and report the unsolvable fraction.
- Proximal taper is below the measurement's resolving power over 20 mm. A slope of -0.004 mm/mm is 0.08 mm over the window, less than one voxel, and a third of LAD/LCx slopes are positive. The positive RCA median probably reflects side-branch (conus, RV branch) junctions inflating the inscribed-sphere radius, but this is unverified. If taper is kept, compute it over longer segments, with branch-junction points masked out.

### Gaps
- No radius method independent of the delivered segmentation was available (for example an image-intensity FWHM fit on the CT volume), and `volumes/` is not present on this data root.
- Whether the junction-core exclusion (r_J) keeps calibers clear of the flow divider was not checked visually.
- Finet 2008 and the pooled Murray exponent from Taylor 2024 need `paper-review` before use.

## Which regional descriptors are length or heart-size confounded, and how should they be normalised?

### Takeaway
Absolute radii are strongly size-confounded (Spearman 0.56 to 0.75 with LM radius, 0.26 to 0.41 with inter-ostial distance and LAD length). Bifurcation angles are weakly so (0.25 to 0.33 with total tree or RCA length). Regional curvature, daughter ratios and taper are essentially not (|rho| of 0.25 or less). Distance normalisation matters for **where the windows sit** more than for the values. Caliber should be expressed as ratios, or adjusted for a size covariate, rather than divided through.

### Inferences
- Unlike whole-vessel tortuosity, regional descriptors are mostly not length-extensive, because the windows have fixed length and curvature and angles are intensive. The confound that remains sits in the calibers, which scale with the heart and the LM.
- The Finet/r_LM and Murray/r_LM correlations are partly built in: r_LM is r0 for the LM, and the ratio's noise shares that term. They are not evidence that ratios need size correction. Daughter ratios, which do not contain r0, are size-free.
- Supervisors' distance normalisation: proportional windows (for example the proximal third of the LAD, or windows scaled by inter-ostial distance) are possible. The inter-ostial distance varies little (IQR 31.8 to 36.5 mm, about +-7 %), so scaling by it would move a 15 mm window by only about 1 mm, within the +-1 mm shift that costs almost no ICC. Scaling by vessel length would tie window placement to truncation, which the earlier report showed is the dominant failure. The recommendation is to keep fixed-mm windows anchored at labelled landmarks and to carry size (inter-ostial distance or r_LM) and Image Quality as covariates.
- The CRUX angle, RCA-length correlation (0.33) and dominance are entangled. With 95 of 100 scans right-dominant, this subsample cannot separate them.

### Gaps
- Sex, age, BMI and body surface area are not in ImageCAS-X, so the size-proxy analysis uses anatomy-internal proxies only.
- Dominance stratification needs the full training split (only 5 non-right-dominant scans here).
- The CAS-Net-derived centerline comparison (reference vs prediction ICC) for regional descriptors was not run, and it is the test that decides CGPS feasibility.
