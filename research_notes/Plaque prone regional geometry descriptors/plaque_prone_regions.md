## Where does plaque form first, and why?

### Takeaway
Plaque (and plaque-driven events) cluster in the proximal third of LAD, LCx and RCA, on the lateral walls of bifurcations opposite the flow divider (carina largely spared), and in the segment immediately proximal to a myocardial bridge, with the bridged segment itself spared. In the only large serial CCTA cohort with plaque-free baselines found (PARADIGM), the LAD is where most first plaques appear. Segment-level (SCCT 18-segment) frequencies of *first* plaque in plaque-free vessels were not found in any source opened here.

### Cited Findings
- **Proximal clustering of culprit occlusions.** 208 consecutive STEMI patients (Brigham and Women's, angiography): occlusions clustered in the proximal third of each vessel; per 10 mm further from the ostium, occlusion risk fell by 13 % (RCA), 30 % (LAD) and 26 % (LCx), by Poisson regression. [SNIP] [Wang et al. 2004, Circulation, PubMed 15249505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15249505/). Population is symptomatic STEMI, endpoint is occlusion site not plaque onset.
- **First plaque in plaque-free patients goes to the LAD.** PARADIGM registry, 1,343 patients with suspected CAD and serial CCTA (age 60.4 ± 9.4 y, 42.8 % female), mean interscan 3.3 y (IQR 2.6 to 4.8). Of the 334 (24.9 %) with no plaque at baseline, 35 % developed new lesions; in them the new lesion was most often in the LAD (60 %). In patients who already had plaque, 46.7 % developed new lesions, most often in the RCA (47 %). New lesions were small (median stenosis 17.2 %, volume 21.8 mm³ in the plaque-free group), and obstructive new lesions were rare (0.9 %). Adjusted predictors of any new lesion: baseline plaque OR 1.84 (1.38 to 2.46), diabetes OR 1.38 (1.03 to 1.85), BMI OR 1.04 per kg/m² (1.01 to 1.08). No geometric predictors were reported. [ABS] [PARADIGM new plaque formation, EHJ CVI 2026 Suppl 1 jeaf367.317](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)
- **Proximal lesions progress faster.** Serial CCTA, 1,478 patients (PARADIGM): proximally located lesions had more lipid-density (low-attenuation) plaque and progressed more rapidly. [SNIP] summarised in [KJR 2023 review](https://kjronline.org/DOIx.php?id=10.3348%2Fkjr.2023.1311) and [PARADIGM "where, why and how fast" review, JCCT](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1934592521004640)
- **Bifurcations: lateral wall, not carina.** A multislice CT study of the left main bifurcation reported more than 90 % of plaques opposite the flow divider, and plaque presence in the bifurcation related to LM bifurcation angle. [SNIP] [PubMed 17028928](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17028928/) (population not verified). A separate CCTA series cited 51.3 % of plaques as bifurcation lesions, attributed to low and oscillatory flow at the lateral walls. [SNIP] [Eur Radiol 2008, plaque formation and hemodynamics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00330-008-1223-3)
- **Inner curvature and branch ostia.** Reviews state plaques localise to the inner (medial) aspect of curved vessels and to the lateral walls near branch orifices, i.e. "preferred locations at branches or bends". [SNIP] [Review of coronary geometry vs plaque on CCTA, PMC9497479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9497479/) (captcha on fetch; not read).
- **Myocardial bridge: proximal segment affected, tunnel spared.** JACC state-of-the-art review: 67 % to 98 % of bridges are in the LAD, mostly proximal and mid LAD; CCTA prevalence 19 % to 22 %; the segment entering the bridge frequently carries plaque while the bridged segment is spared. [SNIP] [Sternheim et al., JACC 2021 review](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2021.09.859)
- **MB on CCTA with CFD.** 36 patients with one MB each (age 56.6 ± 10.3 y, 28 % women, 256-slice CCTA): no plaque inside any tunnelled segment; 12 arteries had plaque proximal to the bridge (75 % calcified, 75 % non-stenotic). ESS was 1.2 Pa proximal, 0.95 Pa within (p = 0.17 vs proximal) and 2.98 Pa distal (p < 0.0001). Deep (> 2 mm, n = 11) and superficial (n = 25) bridges had comparable ESS patterns. [FT] [PMC10870693](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870693/). Note that the ESS data do not show a clear proximal low-ESS zone, which weakens the textbook mechanism.
- **MB on invasive angiography, contradicting "more proximal plaque".** 1,920 consecutive angiography patients 2004 to 2010, MB prevalence 3.96 % (89.5 % mid LAD, mean MB length 12.4 ± 4.1 mm); 76 MB patients vs 109 age and sex matched controls. Atherosclerotic load inside the MB was 4.5 ± 16.8 %. MB patients had *lower* load in proximal LAD (30.3 ± 39.9 vs 42.9 ± 41.1, p = 0.038) and mid LAD (8.1 ± 20.0 vs 25.3 ± 35.9, p < 0.001); distal similar. 10-year survival similar (76.7 % vs 74.3 %, p = 0.74). [FT] [PMC9026775](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9026775/)

### Inferences
- For a plaque-free cohort such as CGPS, the prior with the most direct support is: proximal LAD first, with proximal RCA and LCx next, and within each vessel the first ~30 to 40 mm from the ostium. This argues for a representation that preserves arc length from the ostium on every node (the supervisors' "arc-length from root") and marks bifurcation neighbourhoods, since those two variables alone encode most of the known "where".
- Bridge sparing is well supported, but the "proximal segment is worse" claim is contested at the patient level (the matched angiography study found less proximal plaque in MB patients). If MB is used, it should be a node flag (intramyocardial course) rather than a strong prior.
- Because CGPS predictions lack artery names, the LM/LAD/LCx identity and "proximal third" must be recovered from the graph (root, first bifurcation, largest daughter, direction relative to chambers). Anatomical labelling therefore sits upstream of any regional analysis.

### Gaps
- No source opened gave SCCT 18-segment frequencies of *first* plaque in initially plaque-free vessels. PARADIGM reports artery (60 % LAD) only in an abstract; segment-level tables likely exist in the full PARADIGM "how early can atherosclerosis be detected" paper ([JCCT 2023, 403 on fetch](https://www.journalofcardiovascularct.com/article/S1934-5925(23)00426-4/fulltext)) and should be read.
- Circumferential lateral-wall vs carina percentages from IVUS/OCT (e.g. Nakazawa pathology, Kimura IVUS) were not retrieved in this session.

## Which local geometry predicts future plaque or progression in serial studies?

### Takeaway
In serial studies, the strongest local predictors are haemodynamic (low ESS) and plaque-based (baseline plaque burden, positive remodelling), not raw geometry. Geometric predictors (bifurcation angle, LM-LAD angle, curvature) are supported almost only by cross-sectional CCTA studies in symptomatic patients. No serial study in healthy or plaque-free vessels testing geometry as a predictor was found.

### Cited Findings
- **PREDICTION (Stone et al. 2012).** 506 ACS patients after PCI, IVUS and CFD profiling of non-culprit arteries, repeat at 6 to 10 months. Large plaque burden and low local ESS independently and additively predicted increase in plaque burden and lumen narrowing. [SNIP] [Circulation 2012](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circulationaha.112.096438) (403). Exact ORs, n with serial imaging and PPV not verified here.
- **Samady et al. 2011.** 20 patients with CAD, VH-IVUS plus CFD at baseline and 6 months, 2,249 segments. Versus intermediate WSS: low-WSS segments had progression of plaque area (p = 0.027) and necrotic core (p < 0.001) with constrictive remodelling (vessel and lumen area fell, p < 0.001); high-WSS segments had necrotic core (p < 0.001) and dense calcium (p < 0.001) progression, fibrous and fibrofatty regression (p < 0.001), and expansive remodelling. [SNIP] [Circulation 2011, PubMed 21788584](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21788584)
- **PROSPECT low ESS sub-study.** Low ESS plus plaque characteristics predicted non-culprit MACE. [SNIP] [Stone et al., JACC CVI 2018](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2017.01.031)
- **LAD-LCx bifurcation angle (cross-sectional).** Left bifurcation angle was 94.3° ± 16.5 in patients with diseased left coronary artery vs 76.5° ± 15.9 in normal left coronary artery; LAD-LCx angle larger with ≥ 50 % stenosis, wider in non-calcified plaque, and an independent predictor of significant left stenosis. [SNIP] [PLOS One 2017, dual-source CT](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0174352); [PMC3888717](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3888717/); [PMC11168269, cross-sectional](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11168269/). A wider LM bifurcation angle is linked to severe proximal LAD plaque. [SNIP] [KJR 2023 review](https://kjronline.org/DOIx.php?id=10.3348%2Fkjr.2023.1311)
- **LM-LAD angle.** Proposed for obstructive CAD evaluation. [SNIP] [PLOS One 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0202249); [PubMed 41972218, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41972218/). Populations not verified.
- **Ramus intermedius (trifurcation).** Studied as a modifier of LM bifurcation plaque. [SNIP] [PMC10091592](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10091592/)
- **Local curvature vs plaque thickness.** One CT-IVUS reconstruction study reported circumferential plaque thickness correlated more strongly with local curvature than with WSS. [SNIP] [BMC Med Imaging 2016, PMC4719213](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4719213/). Population and effect size not verified.
- **Radiomics for new plaque.** Segments without plaque at baseline were used to predict future plaque from radiomic features (PARADIGM). [SNIP] [JCCT 2024](https://www.journalofcardiovascularct.com/article/S1934-5925(24)00032-7/abstract). Shows the design of "baseline plaque-free segment to later plaque" is feasible; whether any shape feature contributed is unknown.

### Inferences
- Bifurcation angle has the most geometric evidence, but it is cross-sectional, in symptomatic patients, and confounded: plaque at the bifurcation can itself change the measured angle. The CGPS design (healthy baseline, plaque at 10 y) is exactly what this literature lacks, which makes LM bifurcation angle (LAD-LCx, LM-LAD) a well motivated per-node feature for bifurcation nodes.
- Since low ESS is the best longitudinal predictor and ESS follows from geometry plus flow, geometry features that are proxies for low/oscillatory ESS (bifurcation angle, daughter-to-parent diameter ratio, local curvature, local area expansion or taper) are the defensible node descriptors.

### Gaps
- Effect sizes for side-branch diameter ratio, taper, lumen eccentricity, LM length and MB depth/length as predictors of *future* plaque were not found.
- ICONIC (culprit vs non-culprit ACS precursors) and Bourantas serial geometry studies were not retrieved.
- No study in asymptomatic or healthy cohorts linking baseline geometry to later plaque was found.

## Is the mechanism always low or oscillatory WSS, or also high WSS, gradients, helical flow, axial plaque stress? What can geometry approximate?

### Takeaway
Low and oscillatory WSS explains *where plaque starts and grows*; high WSS and axial plaque stress relate to *later transformation and rupture* of established stenotic plaque. For a healthy-baseline cohort the low-WSS mechanism is the relevant one, and its geometric drivers (bifurcation lateral walls, inner curvature, local expansion) can be approximated from centerline and radius alone.

### Cited Findings
- High-WSS segments showed necrotic core and calcium progression and fibrous regression, while low-WSS segments showed plaque growth with constrictive remodelling. [SNIP] [Samady 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21788584)
- EMERALD (ACS patients, CCTA plus CFD, culprit vs non-culprit lesions): likelihood of a culprit lesion rose with the number of adverse haemodynamic features, including WSS ≥ 154.7 dyn/cm² and axial plaque stress ≥ 1,606.6 dyn/cm². [SNIP] [Lee JM et al. 2019, PubMed 29550316](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29550316/)
- Axial plaque stress is determined by lesion geometry (stenosis upstream vs downstream). [SNIP] [Choi et al., JACC CVI 2015](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2015.04.024)
- Contemporary review of disturbed flow metrics (WSS, oscillatory shear, multidirectional WSS, gradients). [SNIP] [JAHA 2024 review](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/JAHA.124.037129)

### Inferences
- Axial plaque stress and high WSS require an existing stenosis, so they are irrelevant at a plaque-free baseline. Geometry-only surrogates for low/oscillatory WSS are the target.
- Helical flow and torsion-driven secondary flow are plausible links between local curvature/torsion and WSS, but no source here quantified them in coronaries.

### Gaps
- No verified evidence on helical flow or WSS gradient as predictors of plaque onset in humans was retrieved.

## Is local tortuosity associated with plaque location or progression? Does evidence contradict "tortuosity protects"?

### Takeaway
Whole-vessel "severe tortuosity" is associated with *non-obstructive* disease and with female sex in cross-sectional angiography studies. This does not contradict the local-curvature hypothesis: those studies score gross bends on angiograms, are confounded by sex and hypertension, and measure obstruction, not plaque onset. No serial study of local tortuosity and plaque onset was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Zebic Mihic et al. 2023.** 131 patients referred for angiography with suspected CAD (71 men, 60 women, age 61.6 ± 10.1 y). Severe tortuosity (≥ 3 consecutive bends of ≥ 45° on the main trunk, in systole and diastole) in 50.6 % of non-obstructive vs 14.8 % of obstructive CAD (p < 0.001); by vessel LCx 35.1 % vs 9.3 %, LAD 27.3 % vs 11.1 %, RCA 11.7 % vs 0 %. Tortuosity predicted non-obstructive CAD, OR 7.96 (1.79 to 35.40); female sex OR 17.5; left dominance was associated with non-obstructive CAD vs balanced (p = 0.008). Authors frame tortuosity as possibly protective and cite Groves 2009 as concordant. [FT] [PMC10534717](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10534717/)
- **Groves et al. 2009** (West Virginia Medical Journal): lower incidence of significant CAD in patients with severe tortuosity, more tortuosity in women and hypertensives. [SNIP] via [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26655292_Severe_coronary_tortuosity_and_the_relationship_to_significant_coronary_artery_disease)
- Coronary tortuosity has also been proposed as a phenotype for ischaemia without obstructive CAD. [SNIP] [PMC9814803](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9814803/); clinical implications in CAD patients [SNIP] [PLOS One 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024232)

### Inferences
- The "protective" finding is sex-confounded (61 % female in non-obstructive vs 24 % in obstructive), so any CGPS analysis of tortuosity must adjust for sex and dominance. This also aligns with the earlier report's finding that whole-vessel TI mostly encodes length and dominance.
- Local curvature near bifurcations (inner-wall low WSS) and whole-vessel tortuosity can point in opposite directions without contradiction, which supports per-node curvature rather than per-vessel tortuosity.

### Gaps
- No study found testing local (windowed) curvature against serial plaque onset.

## Sex, dominance, heart size

### Takeaway
Only indirect evidence: tortuosity is more common in women and is associated with left dominance and non-obstructive disease; bridge effects showed no sex difference. No study relating sex, dominance or heart size to local bifurcation or curvature predictors of plaque onset was found.

### Cited Findings
- Female sex OR 17.5 for non-obstructive CAD; tortuosity more frequent in women; left dominance associated with non-obstructive CAD. [FT] [PMC10534717](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10534717/)
- MB and controls comparable by sex (89.5 % vs 87.2 % male). [FT] [PMC9026775](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9026775/)
- PARADIGM new-lesion cohort was 42.8 % female; sex was not among reported predictors in the abstract. [ABS] [PARADIGM 2026 abstract](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)

### Inferences
- The supervisors' expectation that embeddings will separate on heart size and dominance is consistent with this literature; normalising distances (arc length by vessel length or heart size) and including dominance as a covariate are justified.

### Gaps
- No heart-size-normalised bifurcation angle or curvature study was found.
