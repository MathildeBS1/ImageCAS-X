# Bifurcation angle and diameter split vs oscillatory / multidirectional WSS (OSI, RRT, transWSS, TSVI) in coronary bifurcations

Scope note: about 16 tool calls. Full text was read for Garcha & Grande Gutiérrez 2025 (PMC), Gharleghi et al. 2025 (PMC; "Beier group" left main study), Zhang et al. (arXiv preprint version 2312.00257, not the published RSOS 2024 version) and Zheng et al. 2016 (Europe PMC XML). Everything else is from abstracts, repository pages or search snippets and is marked so. Chaichana 2011/2013 full texts, Beier 2016 full text, Huo 2012 full text, the 2026 Physics of Fluids systematic review, Rabbi, Dong, Kamangar, Malvè, Morlacchi, Pinho, Liu, Vorobtsova, Antoniadis 2015, Medrano-Gracia and Fan 2016 were **not** read (see Gaps).

## Q1. Does a wider bifurcation angle raise OSI or the oscillatory area at the lateral walls? How consistent?

### Takeaway
No consistent evidence. The only patient-specific study that tested angle against OSI and RRT directly (Gharleghi et al. 2025, n = 127, pulsatile, left main) found no association for LM–LAD angle, LAD–LCx angle or inflow angle (all |β| < 0.1, n.s.). The "wider angle → higher OSI" claim comes from idealized or stented models (Zheng 2016) and the early Chaichana models, which mostly reported WSS rather than OSI. Two idealized parametric studies (Beier 2016; Chiastra 2017) call the angle effect "minor".

### Cited Findings
- **Gharleghi et al., Sci Rep 2025;15:254 (Beier/UNSW group; arXiv 2401.12504 preprint version 2024). Full text read.** 127 patients with suspected CAD, no significant stenosis or calcification on CTCA; patient-specific left main bifurcations; transient (pulsatile) CFD, generic inflow waveform scaled by LM diameter, plug inlet, zero-pressure outlets, rigid walls, no cardiac motion, Carreau-Yasuda viscosity. Metrics: %lowTAESS (<0.5 Pa), %highOSI (>0.1), %highRRT (>4.17 Pa⁻¹), mean TSVI. Angle A (LM–LAD, 124 ± 20°), angle B (LAD–LCx, 79 ± 19°) and inflow angle (17 ± 15°) were **not** associated with any metric: angle A β = 0.020 (highOSI), 0.090 (highRRT); angle B β = 0.027 / −0.087; inflow angle β = −0.038 / −0.046; all adjusted p > 0.3. No regional (lateral wall / flow divider) analysis. Authors note a low OSI/RRT burden (highOSI 3.08 ± 2.36 % of wall, max 12.1 %) and ask for cautious reading of OSI/RRT results. — [Gharleghi et al. 2025, PMC11697190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11697190/); [PubMed 39747107](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39747107/)
- **Zhang et al. (arXiv 2312.00257; published as Royal Society Open Science 2024, the version the thesis cites). Preprint full text read; published version not read.** 39 ASOCA left coronary trees (20 non-stenosed, 12 moderate, 7 severe); transient CFD, rigid wall, literature waveform scaled Q = 1.43·D^2.55, flow split ∝ D^2.27, Carreau-Yasuda. Stenosed vs non-stenosed bifurcations: bifurcation angle 59.96 ± 20.66° vs 57.08 ± 28.13°, p = 0.742; inflow angle p = 0.058; Finet's ratio p = 0.851; %HighOSI@0.1 at bifurcations p = 0.116. The preprint reports **no correlation of angle, inflow angle or Finet's ratio with OSI, RRT or low TAESS**; the comparison is feature vs stenosis and metric vs stenosis, separately. TSVI does not appear in the preprint; according to search snippets, the published RSOS version adds TSVI ("TSVI was the only metric consistently differing between non-stenosed and stenosed cases"). Figure 3 (OSI/TSVI maps) in the published version shows metrics, not an angle–OSI regression, as far as can be judged; not verified. — [Zhang et al. arXiv 2312.00257 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.00257)
- **Beier et al., J Biomech 2016;49:1570–1582. Abstract only (via search).** Nine bifurcations, angles 40°, 60°, 80° (±1 SD of 101 asymptomatic CTA cases), idealized non-stented, idealized stented and one patient-specific non-stented geometry; only the angle varied. "Bifurcation angle had a minor effect on adverse-haemodynamic areas in idealized non-stented models", effects disappeared after stenting and were "not apparent for patient geometries"; authors conclude other shape characteristics matter more than angle. The exact "adverse" metrics (low TAWSS, OSI, WSS gradient?) and thresholds were not verified. — [PubMed 27062590 listing via search](https://www.pubmed.gov/27062590/); [repub.eur.nl citation](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/96776)
- **Chiastra et al., J Biomech 2017 (doi 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2017.04.016). Abstract and SB3C 2017 conference abstract only.** Population-based idealized LAD–first diagonal bifurcations, healthy and with 60 % stenosis per branch, varied angle and cardiac curvature radius. Bifurcation angle had a **minor** effect on near-wall haemodynamics; smaller curvature radius gave more low-TAWSS area and **less** high-OSI and high-RRT area; helicity did not depend on angle. Note: LAD/D1, not left main. — [repub.eur.nl](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/99510); [SB3C 2017 abstract (conference)](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/2677971/158355/SB3C2017_Abstract_IdealizedCoronary.pdf)
- **Zheng et al., J Diabetes Res 2016 (PMC5061990). Full text read.** Idealized LM bifurcation, angles 70°, 90°, 110°, pulsatile, rigid, Newtonian, COMSOL. **Stented models only; no native/unstented model.** Integrated OSI rose with angle in all techniques, e.g. precise placement 5.06 → 6.32 → 7.27 ×10⁻⁶ m² (normalized 1.07 → 1.33 → 1.53); crossover-only 4.75 → 5.43 (1.00 → 1.14). Area OSI > 0.25 rose only slightly (crossover-only 0.39 → 0.43 mm²). Integrated TAWSS fell with angle (precise placement 2.78 → 1.32 ×10⁻⁴ N). Low-WSS zones in the proximal LCx moved from lateral to medial wall as angle increased; a low-WSS/high-OSI zone appeared in the proximal LAD at 90° and 110°. This supports "wider angle → more OSI" only in a stented, idealized setting. — [Zheng et al. 2016, Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5061990/fullTextXML)
- **Chaichana, Sun & Jewkes, J Biomech 2011 ("Computation of hemodynamics in the left coronary artery with variable angulations"). Abstract/repository only.** 12 models (4 realistic, 8 simulated), angles 15°–120°. Wider angles gave disturbed flow, low WSS and lower WSS gradient at the bifurcation. In what was seen, the result is about WSS, WSS gradient and pressure; **OSI was not seen** in the abstract. A companion preliminary study (8 models) reported low WSS and high wall pressure in wide-angle models. A later review table lists rigid walls, Newtonian blood and no pathology as limitations. These are highly simplified models, few cases. — [Hope repository record](https://hira.hope.ac.uk/id/eprint/1505/); [Coventry portal, preliminary study](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/haemodynamic-effect-of-coronary-angulations-on-subsequent-develop/); [review table, PMC9457427](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9457427/table/jcm-11-05143-t002)
- **Garcha & Grande Gutiérrez, Sci Rep 2025 (PMC11770140). Full text read.** Did **not** compute OSI, RRT, transWSS or TSVI; only TAWSS, ALWSS (% area with TAWSS < 0.5 Pa) and AHWSS (> 7 Pa). Within its low-TAWSS metric, the angle effect depends on the diameter split: at a neutral ratio (~0.94), a wider angle increased ALWSS in the proximal LAD (R = 0.51, p = 0.011) and decreased it in the proximal LCx (R = −0.52, p = 0.009); with skewed ratios R = −0.73 (one dominance) and R = +0.86 (the other), both p < 0.001. 230 synthetic LCA models, pulsatile (4 cycles), rigid, laminar, Newtonian, 0D LPN outlets. — [Garcha & Grande Gutiérrez 2025, PMC11770140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11770140/)
- A 2026 Physics of Fluids systematic review of 108 LM-bifurcation CFD studies is summarized (search snippet only) as saying wider angles were associated with "low oscillatory shear at the LCx side"; the wording is ambiguous and was not verified. — [Physics of Fluids review (not read)](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article/38/1/011305/3378191/A-decade-of-computational-fluid-dynamics-to-study)
- A 2025 Frontiers in Physiology paper states (snippet) that a wider angle is associated with lower WSS and higher OSI, citing Sun and Chaichana. This is a secondary citation chain back to the simplified Chaichana models. — [Front Physiol 2025 (not read)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1518732/epub)

### Inferences
- The "wide angle → high OSI" statement in the literature is mostly carried by idealized/stented or Chaichana-type models and by secondary citations. The best patient-specific test (n = 127, pulsatile) is null for angle vs %highOSI and %highRRT, and two idealized parametric studies (Beier 2016, Chiastra 2017) call the angle effect minor. It should be written as inconsistent at best, not as an established effect.
- Garcha 2025 suggests the angle effect on low TAWSS changes sign with the diameter split and between LAD and LCx. A single pooled "angle" coefficient could therefore average out opposing effects. This is an inference from synthetic, rigid models, low TAWSS only.
- Lateral-wall vs flow-divider location for OSI is described only qualitatively in Zheng 2016 (stented). None of the patient-specific studies found report wall-region OSI.

### Gaps
- Chaichana 2011/2013 full texts not read: whether any OSI values were reported is unconfirmed.
- Beier 2016: exact metrics/thresholds of "adverse haemodynamic areas" not verified.
- Rabbi, Dong, Kamangar, Malvè, Morlacchi, Liu, Vorobtsova, Antoniadis 2015 review, Medrano-Gracia, Fan 2016: not found or not read within budget. No source on transverse WSS vs angle was found.
- No clinical CCTA study was found that links angle → OSI → plaque in one design with a plaque outcome at follow-up. Zhang (39 trees) is cross-sectional stenosis; Gharleghi (127) has no plaque outcome.

## Q2. Does an unequal diameter split (or deviation from Murray/Finet) affect OSI?

### Takeaway
Some evidence, but mostly for low TAWSS/RRT rather than OSI. In Gharleghi 2025, Finet's ratio was strongly linked to %lowTAESS (β = −0.84) and %highRRT (β = −0.49), but only weakly and not robustly to %highOSI (β = −0.19, p = 0.035 unadjusted, 0.247 adjusted). Garcha 2025 shows a large effect of an unequal LAD/LCx split on low-TAWSS area, but did not compute OSI. Idealized Murray-law work points to the expansion ratio (summed daughter area / parent area), not the daughter-to-daughter ratio, as what drives low-WSS regions.

### Cited Findings
- **Gharleghi et al. 2025 (n = 127, patient-specific LM, pulsatile, rigid). Full text.** Finet's ratio (0.57 ± 0.03) vs %lowTAESS β = −0.842, p < 0.001; vs %highRRT β = −0.488, p < 0.001; vs %highOSI β = −0.187, p = 0.035 (adjusted 0.247, n.s.). Absolute diameters were associated with %highOSI (LM 0.691, LAD 0.738, LCx 0.739; all p < 0.001), %highRRT (0.40–0.54) and, negatively, with mean TSVI (−0.51 to −0.58), but **not** with %lowTAESS. Caveat: inflow was scaled by LM diameter with a generic waveform, so diameter–OSI links may partly reflect the flow-scaling assumption (Reynolds/Womersley number grows with diameter); this is my inference, not the authors'. LAD/LCx daughter ratio and Murray deviation were not tested as such. — [PMC11697190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11697190/)
- **Garcha & Grande Gutiérrez 2025 (230 synthetic LCA, pulsatile, rigid). Full text.** ALWSS (TAWSS < 0.5 Pa) 1.7 ± 1.3 % at the neutral ratio (~0.94) vs 6.8 ± 3.6 % (ratio ~0.65) and 16.7 ± 4.7 % (ratio ~1.40), both p < 0.001; quadratic fit of ALWSS on the ratio R² = 0.91–0.94 in healthy models, largest ALWSS with the smallest LAD and largest LCx; in plaque models the quadratic fit was R² = 0.26, p = 0.053 (n.s.). **No OSI, RRT or TSVI.** The labelling of which ratio means LAD-dominant vs LCx-dominant is inconsistent between Methods and Results, so the direction should be checked in the paper's figures before citing. — [PMC11770140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11770140/)
- **Zhang et al. (39 ASOCA trees).** Finet's ratio did not differ between stenosed and non-stenosed bifurcations (0.669 vs 0.678, p = 0.851); side-branch diameter did (2.48 vs 2.12 mm, p = 0.041). No Finet–OSI correlation was reported. — [arXiv 2312.00257](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.00257)
- **Doutel, Pinto, Campos & Miranda, J Theor Biol 2016 (doi 10.1016/j.jtbi.2016.04.038), "Link between deviations from Murray's Law and occurrence of low wall shear stress regions in the left coronary artery". Repository abstract only.** Evaluated low-WSS, high-OSI and high-helicity regions. "The main factor leading to large regions of low WSS is the so called expansion ratio"; low-WSS region size "is independent of the ratio between the diameters of the outflow branches"; Murray-law bifurcations have expansion ratios 1–1.25 and similar low-WSS region sizes; Murray's angle law does not minimize low-WSS area. The abstract gives no OSI result. Geometry type and pulsatility not stated on the page (the group usually uses idealized LCA models; unverified). — [U. Porto repository](https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/handle/10216/97837?mode=full)
- **Huo et al., J Biomech 2012 ("Which diameter and angle rule provides optimal flow patterns in a coronary bifurcation?"). Search snippet only.** Murray-type idealized Y bifurcation had 10–40 % lower WSS and higher OSI in atherosclerosis-prone regions than HK-type (Huo–Kassab) bifurcations; coronary casts fitted HK better than Murray. — [IUPUI ScholarWorks record](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/470d0c8c-aec4-4946-807e-57d66b6b6eb5); [PMC4913552](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913552)

### Inferences
- For the thesis LM→LAD and LM→LCx diameter ratios, the closest evidence is Finet's ratio (Gharleghi; parent/sum of daughters) and the expansion ratio (Doutel). Both mainly predict low TAWSS/RRT area; the OSI link is weak or not reported.
- Garcha's LAD/LCx ratio effect is large but is a low-TAWSS result. Citing it for OSI would overstate it.

### Gaps
- No study was found that regresses OSI, transWSS or TSVI on the LAD/LCx daughter ratio or on a Murray deviation index in patient-specific coronaries.
- Doutel 2016 full text (OSI results, flow conditions) and Huo 2012 full text not read.

## Q3. Are OSI and low TAWSS co-located at bifurcations, so the features act on both?

### Takeaway
Not reliably. In patient-specific left coronary trees, the percentage of wall area with high OSI and the area with low TAESS were weakly **negatively** correlated (Zhang preprint), while low TAESS and high RRT were tightly positively correlated. Feature associations also differ between the two: Finet's ratio predicts low TAESS strongly but OSI weakly; diameters predict OSI but not low TAESS (Gharleghi); smaller curvature radius increased low TAWSS but decreased high OSI (Chiastra). So a geometric feature that acts on low TAWSS does not necessarily act on OSI.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang et al. (39 trees): %LowTAESS and %HighRRT positively correlated across thresholds (all p < 0.002, max r = 0.977); the strongest %LowTAESS–%HighOSI correlation was **negative** (%HighOSI@0.1 vs %LowTAESS@2.5 Pa, r = −0.413, p = 0.009). No spatial co-location analysis of its own. — [arXiv 2312.00257](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.00257)
- Gharleghi et al. 2025: Finet's ratio β = −0.84 for %lowTAESS vs −0.19 (adjusted n.s.) for %highOSI; diameters β ≈ 0.7 for %highOSI but n.s. for %lowTAESS; curvature correlated negatively with %highOSI (LM −0.367, LAD −0.484, LCx −0.519; all p < 0.001). Backward regression R²: lowTAESS 0.791, highOSI 0.633, highRRT 0.585, meanTSVI 0.360. No co-location analysis. — [PMC11697190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11697190/)
- Chiastra et al. 2017 (idealized LAD/D1, abstract): smaller curvature radius → larger low-TAWSS area but smaller high-OSI and high-RRT area. — [SB3C 2017 abstract](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/2677971/158355/SB3C2017_Abstract_IdealizedCoronary.pdf)
- Zheng et al. 2016 (idealized stented LM): qualitatively, low-WSS and high-OSI zones coincided in the proximal LAD at 90°/110° (carina-related reverse flow). — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5061990/fullTextXML)
- Zhang et al. discussion cites Buchanan et al. (aorta, not coronary) for early lesions co-localising with low TAESS and high OSI. — [arXiv 2312.00257](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.00257)

### Inferences
- RRT (which combines TAWSS and OSI) follows low TAWSS closely in these coronary data, so "RRT" adds little beyond low TAWSS at the person level. OSI behaves differently and should not be treated as the same signal.
- Area-percentage metrics over the whole bifurcation cannot show local co-location; results are whole-region summaries. The negative OSI–low-TAESS correlation is at the level of the region, not the point.

### Gaps
- No coronary study found that quantifies point-wise co-location of high OSI and low TAWSS at the lateral walls vs the flow divider in patient-specific models.
- Carotid-bifurcation literature (where co-location at the outer wall of the bulb is classic) was not searched within budget.
