# Oscillatory / multidirectional WSS versus low TAWSS as a driver of coronary plaque

Scope note: human coronary evidence first; animal and carotid evidence is labelled. Most detail below
comes from abstracts, repository summaries and three full texts read (Peiffer 2013, Hoogendoorn 2020,
Kok 2019, plus the Gijsen 2019 consensus PDF and Mohamied 2015). Where only an abstract or snippet was
seen, this is stated.

## 1. Origin of OSI and what the Peiffer, Sherwin & Weinberg 2013 systematic review concluded

### Takeaway
OSI began as a carotid autopsy correlate (Ku et al. 1985). It has TAWSS magnitude in its denominator,
so "low" and "oscillatory" are partly the same signal. The only systematic review (Peiffer 2013, 27
CFD studies) concluded that the evidence for the low/oscillatory theory is "less robust than commonly
assumed": every point-by-point WSS–wall-thickness comparison failed to find the expected relation.

### Cited Findings
- **Ku et al. 1985 (human carotid, post-mortem):** introduced OSI. Intimal thickness was related much
  more strongly to OSI than to mean shear. This is carotid, cross-sectional, autopsy material —
  [Peiffer 2013, PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- **He & Ku 1996:** cited in Peiffer 2013 only as the source that generalised OSI to 3D flow. Its
  coronary findings are not described there —
  [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- **Peiffer 2013 search:** PubMed to March 2012, 406 hits narrowed to 27 CFD studies of WSS versus
  lesion location from 15 institutions. 19 used human vessels; coronaries were the most common site
  (12 human, 4 porcine), then carotid bifurcation (5 human), aorta and ICA —
  [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- **Tally (categories overlap, so not a per-study count):** 22 "supported" vs 5 "not supported"
  conclusions. Steady-flow WSS 9 vs 2; TAWSS 3 vs 1; TAWSS + OSI together 4 vs 2. Four articles linked
  TAWSS, OSI or RRT to lesions and reported no negative result. Five studies found no significant
  overall relation between low WSS and disease —
  [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- **OSI and TAWSS are confounded by construction.** OSI has "the time-averaged magnitude of WSS in the
  denominator", so high OSI tends to go with low mean shear, and the two theories "have become fused to
  some extent" — [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- **Point-by-point comparisons fail.** Steinman, Joshi, Wentzel, Gijsen and Augst compared WSS with
  wall thickness point by point; "none of these studies found a significant correlation". Wentzel et
  al. found an inverse relation only after axial averaging. The review calls point-wise analysis the
  more rigorous method, and warns that correlation between neighbouring points can inflate
  significance — [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- **Steinman et al.:** TAWSS and OSI agreed with each other, but neither was associated with
  atherosclerosis — [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- **Recommendations:** longitudinal studies starting from the healthy state, larger groups of healthy
  vessels, and point-by-point comparisons with appropriate statistics —
  [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746);
  [Imperial repository abstract](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/641be3b8-0046-42ba-b47a-392079adf11e)
- **Weinberg 2022 review:** the evidence for the low-WSS theory "is not as strong as widely supposed"
  (search snippet only) — [PMC8948426](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8948426)

### Inferences
- Almost all of the evidence in the review is cross-sectional co-location: the lesion is already
  there when shear is computed. None of it shows that OSI predicts plaque in a plaque-free artery.
- Because OSI is normalised by TAWSS, a study finding "low and oscillatory" WSS at plaque sites cannot,
  on that finding alone, separate the two.

### Gaps
- The last ~8,600 characters of the Peiffer full text (tables and some conclusions) were not read.
- He & Ku 1996 itself was not retrieved.

## 2. Prospective human coronary studies (serial imaging) that tested OSI, RRT or multidirectional WSS

### Takeaway
Only a few human serial studies test OSI or multidirectional metrics, and they are small (n ≈ 20–50),
use established-disease cohorts (stable CAD on statins, ACS non-culprit vessels) and follow up for only
6–12 months. None studies incident plaque in plaque-free arteries. The large human prospective study
(PREDICTION, n = 506 ACS) and the consensus statement rest on low TAWSS alone. Multidirectional metrics
mainly track changes in **composition**, not plaque size, and add little predictive value over TAWSS
where that has been measured (pigs). The one human study that ran OSI against total plaque area found
no association with OSI alone.

### Cited Findings

**Human, serial IVUS: the Emory 20-patient cohort (same cohort in two papers)**
- **Kok et al. 2019, EuroIntervention.** 20 patients with stable CAD (abnormal stress test or stable
  angina) and a non-obstructive lesion, all on atorvastatin 80 mg. Proximal LAD only. VH-IVUS at
  baseline and 6 months; 316 3-mm segments, 2,488 regions. Metrics: TAWSS, OSI, RRT, transWSS, CFI —
  [EuroIntervention full text](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/the-influence-of-multidirectional-shear-stress-on-plaque-progression-and-composition-changes-in-human-coronary-arteries)
  - TAWSS: plaque regression increased from low to high TAWSS (p = 0.006; mean change about −0.002,
    −0.027 and −0.042 mm² by tertile).
  - **OSI: no difference in total plaque area.** High-OSI regions had more necrotic-core (p = 0.001)
    and calcium (p < 0.001) progression and more fibro-fatty regression (p < 0.001). transWSS and CFI
    gave the same composition-only pattern; high RRT went with less total regression.
  - Low TAWSS together with low or intermediate CFI gave the greatest progression (~0.15 and
    0.10 mm²). Some of these groups held only 11 and 15 regions. The TAWSS × CFI interaction was
    significant (p < 0.001).
  - CFI and OSI correlated at r = 0.83. No OSI–TAWSS coefficient or OSI distribution is reported.
    Tertiles were used because no pathological OSI thresholds exist in human coronaries.
  - The authors recommend pairing TAWSS with OSI or CFI, because RRT and transWSS depend on TAWSS.
    Limitations they list: exploratory; n = 20; statins may mask effects; VH-IVUS tissue accuracy
    debated; circumferential registration within 10° in only 78% of cross-sections.
- **Timmins et al. 2017, J R Soc Interface 14:20160972.** The same Emory design: 20 patients with
  non-flow-limiting CAD, VH-IVUS at baseline and 6 months, about 14,235 sectors (abstract). **Low and
  oscillatory sectors showed total plaque area regression; low, non-oscillatory sectors showed
  progression (p < 0.001).** The authors read the low-oscillatory pattern as a shift toward a more
  vulnerable composition (abstract only) —
  [PubMed 28148771](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28148771);
  [PMC5332583](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5332583)
- **Note:** Timmins 2017 and Kok 2019 appear to analyse the same 20 Emory patients (same design,
  authors and imaging). They are not independent replications. In this cohort, oscillatory shear on top
  of low shear goes with *less* plaque growth, not more.

**Human, ACS cohorts (Rotterdam / IMPACT-type NIRS-IVUS)**
- **Hartman et al. 2021, J Cardiovasc Transl Res 14:416–425.** Cross-sectional. 53 ACS patients,
  NIRS-IVUS of a non-culprit artery, 38 vessels analysed. Lipid-rich sectors co-located more often
  with **high** WSS, with a dose-response (abstract) —
  [Polito repository](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2918072)
- **Hartman/Kok/De Nisco et al. 2023, Cardiovasc Res.** About 40 ACS patients, NIRS-IVUS and OCT,
  1-year follow-up. **Low WSS** and lipid acted together, giving the greatest growth in lipid-rich
  regions exposed to low WSS (abstract) —
  [Polito repository](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2984937);
  [PubMed record 36575921](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/oncologiauy/resource/en/mdl-36575921)
- **De Nisco et al. 2024, ATVB.** In the same ACS programme, high TSVI, alone or with a lipid-rich
  plaque, went with more percent-atheroma-volume progression at 1 year (high TSVI 4.00%, low TAWSS
  3.60%, ≥5.90% where lipid-rich plaque met high TSVI or low TAWSS). The authors present TSVI as an
  *addition* to low TAWSS. Abstract and summaries only; no incremental-AUC or adjusted figures seen —
  [Polito repository](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2988982);
  [x-mol summary](https://ma.x-mol.com/paper/1767376828142161920)
- **Candreva et al. 2022, Atherosclerosis 342:28–35.** Angiography-based, retrospective, MI culprit
  versus non-culprit lesions. Culprits had higher TAWSS and higher TSVI, and TSVI was reported as a
  strong predictor of MI. The endpoint is MI, not plaque growth; sample size not verified —
  [Polito repository](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2972252)

**Human, cross-sectional plaque location (not prospective)**
- **Knight et al. 2010, Atherosclerosis 211:445–450.** Patient-specific RCAs, about 120 plaques.
  Sensitivity for plaque location was AWSS 72 ± 25%, AWSSG 68 ± 36%, OSI 60 ± 30% (p < 0.05 vs AWSS)
  and RRT 69 ± 59%. AWSS found the most plaques but gave more false positives; OSI and RRT had higher
  PPV (snippet-level) —
  [ETH research collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/075415bb-f7c2-4a63-ac1e-fe0384721176)
- **Rikhtegar et al. 2012.** Left coronaries, 30 patients, 96 plaques. AWSS had very high sensitivity
  but did not reach the PPV of RRT (snippet-level) —
  [ETH research collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/075415bb-f7c2-4a63-ac1e-fe0384721176);
  [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- **A 2024 Ann Biomed Eng study of ten human RCAs:** TAWSS, OSI, transWSS and CFI gave
  "non-significant or significant but low" spatial correlations with lesion prevalence; RRT was
  highest at 0.71 (search snippet only; authors and design not verified) —
  [Springer, ABME 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10439-024-03607-9)

**Human, large prospective studies: low TAWSS only, OSI not tested**
- **PREDICTION (Stone et al. 2012, Circulation).** 506 ACS patients at 17 Japanese sites, IVUS
  6–10 months after PCI. Plaque-area increase was predicted by large baseline plaque burden.
  Lumen-area decrease was predicted by plaque burden and low ESS —
  [QxMD abstract](https://read.qxmd.com/read/22723305/prediction-of-progression-of-coronary-artery-disease-and-clinical-outcomes-using-vascular-profiling-of-endothelial-shear-stress-and-arterial-plaque-characteristics-the-prediction-study);
  [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/endothelial-shear-stress-works-prediction-tool)
- **Gijsen et al. 2019 ESC consensus** on the PREDICTION result: 39/53 (74%) of the PCIs were in
  asymptomatic patients treated for angiographic progression seen on protocol imaging, and there were
  too few symptomatic events to relate them to WSS. The consensus's clinical evidence (IVUS, OCT,
  PROSPECT post hoc, PREDICTION) is about **low** (and high) TAWSS. OSI, RRT and transWSS appear only
  as metric definitions, with no human outcome evidence cited for them —
  [Gijsen 2019 EHJ, White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/153395/1/ehz551.pdf)
- **Kumar et al. 2018, JACC.** High proximal WSS predicted MI in stable CAD. TSVI was not studied.
  The "Kumar 2018 (TSVI)" attribution in the task appears to be wrong: TSVI is from the Morbiducci /
  De Nisco / Mazzi / Candreva group (2021 onwards) —
  [search result, EuroIntervention / PMC8455396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8455396)

**Animal (labelled)**
- **Hoogendoorn et al. 2020, Cardiovasc Res 116:1136–1146 (familial hypercholesterolaemic pigs).**
  10 pigs on a high-fat diet, no statins. CT, NIRS-IVUS and OCT at 3, 9 and 10–12 months; 30 arteries,
  3,648 sectors. Low TAWSS co-located with high OSI in 396/624 sectors (63%) and with high CFI in 57%.
  PPV for plaque (wall thickness > 0.5 mm) at the last time point: low TAWSS 48%, high OSI 45%, high RRT
  47%, high CFI 45%, high transWSS 40%; combinations at best 50%. PPV for FCA: TAWSS 61%, OSI 58%, RRT
  61%. The **authors conclude low TAWSS remains the strongest driver and predictor.** Absolute OSI,
  CFI and transWSS values were low, so the tertile splits "may be somewhat artificial" —
  [PMC7177495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7177495);
  [Erasmus repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/128496)
- **Mazzi et al. 2021, Ann Biomed Eng 49:2606–2621 (minipigs).** 9 coronary arteries, wall thickness
  at three time points. High TSVI and low WSS, each alone or together, went with wall thickening (abstract) —
  [Polito repository](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2928332);
  [Erasmus repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/136555)
- **Mohamied et al. 2015 (rabbit aorta, intercostal ostia; not coronary).** 8 immature and 9 mature
  cholesterol-fed rabbits, with spatial autocorrelation handled by bootstrapping. TAWSS correlated
  weakly *positively* with lesions (immature) or not at all (mature). OSI correlated weakly *inversely*
  (immature) or not at all (mature). transWSS correlated positively at both ages, with coefficients
  about three times larger. The authors found no support for the low, oscillatory theory, and OSI
  poorly separates uniaxial pulsatile flow from multidirectional flow —
  [PMC4286626](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4286626)

### Inferences
- No human study tests OSI or multidirectional WSS against **incident** plaque in a plaque-free
  coronary artery, and none uses CCTA with long follow-up. All serial human data come from diseased
  arteries (stable CAD on high-dose statin, or ACS) over 6–12 months, with outcomes defined as change
  in existing plaque area or composition.
- Where OSI and TAWSS were run side by side, OSI did not add size-progression information beyond
  TAWSS. In the one human study it went with regression (Timmins 2017) or no change (Kok 2019); in
  pigs its PPV was no better than low TAWSS. The positive multidirectional findings concern
  composition (necrotic core, calcium), or TSVI in ACS lipid-rich plaque. TSVI comes from one group
  and has not been replicated independently.
- The human samples are ~20–50 patients with thousands of correlated sectors. Mixed models were used,
  but the effective sample size is the patient count.

### Gaps
- Full texts of Timmins 2017, De Nisco 2024, Candreva 2022 and Hartman 2023 were not read, so their
  adjusted effect sizes and incremental-value statistics (if any) are unverified.
- Samady et al. 2011 (Circulation, Emory; low WSS and plaque progression / remodelling) was not
  retrieved. It may be the same 20-patient programme.
- No study found reports a formal likelihood-ratio or ΔAUC test of OSI over TAWSS in human coronaries.

## 3. Are coronary OSI values mostly near zero, and is OSI co-located with low TAWSS?

### Takeaway
Yes, qualitatively. OSI is normalised by TAWSS, so it co-locates with low TAWSS by construction. In
coronary pigs, 63% of low-TAWSS sectors were also high-OSI. Absolute multidirectional values in
coronaries are described as low, which makes tertile-based "high OSI" partly artificial. OSI also
depends on modelling choices (vessel motion) much more than TAWSS does. No source found gives an
explicit coronary OSI distribution.

### Cited Findings
- OSI has TAWSS magnitude in its denominator, so high OSI tends to accompany low mean shear —
  [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746);
  [Mohamied 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4286626)
- In pig coronaries, low TAWSS co-located with high OSI in 63% of sectors. Absolute OSI, CFI and
  transWSS values were low, so the tertile splits "may be somewhat artificial" —
  [Hoogendoorn 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7177495)
- In human LADs, OSI and CFI correlated at r = 0.83. RRT and transWSS are described as dependent on
  TAWSS. No pathological OSI threshold exists for human coronaries —
  [Kok 2019](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/the-influence-of-multidirectional-shear-stress-on-plaque-progression-and-composition-changes-in-human-coronary-arteries)
- In an MR + CFD study of the RCA, TAWSS agreed between dynamic and static models (r = 0.89–0.95) but
  OSI did not (r = 0.38–0.60). OSI is therefore sensitive to whether coronary motion is modelled
  (snippet) —
  [Univ. Bristol record](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-dynamic-vessel-motion-on-haemodynamic-parameters-in/)
- The consensus gives healthy human coronary TAWSS as ≈1.4 Pa. The "0.05 ± 0.5 Pa" low-oscillatory
  value it quotes was measured in the internal **carotid**, not a coronary —
  [Gijsen 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/153395/1/ehz551.pdf)
- A coronary CFD study reported higher OSI at lower TAWSS (snippet; study not identified in detail) —
  [NITK repository](https://idr.nitk.ac.in/handle/123456789/8591)

### Inferences
- Coronary flow is mostly unidirectional apart from bifurcations, the outer walls of branch
  ostia and post-stenotic regions. So OSI is probably near zero over most of the wall and carries
  little information that the low-TAWSS map does not already hold. This is consistent with the
  "low absolute values" remark in Hoogendoorn, but **no source found states the distribution
  quantitatively**: treat it as an inference, not a fact.
- For a geometry-only thesis this matters little directly, since OSI is not computed. But it means
  that claiming geometry acts "through oscillatory shear", as distinct from low shear, is not
  supported in coronaries.

### Gaps
- No quantitative coronary OSI distribution (e.g. median, or % of wall area with OSI > 0.1) was found.
- No human coronary OSI–TAWSS correlation coefficient was found.

## 4. Does the Clinical Background claim "plaque forms where WSS is low/oscillatory" (Chatzizisis 2007) hold?

### Takeaway
"Low" holds as a widely accepted, moderately supported association: human serial IVUS in diseased
arteries, consistent animal data and the 2019 ESC consensus. "Oscillatory" as a separate human
coronary claim does not hold up. It rests on carotid autopsy (Ku 1985), on co-location confounded by
the OSI construction, and on a few small composition-change studies. The only systematic review calls
the combined theory less robust than assumed. Safer wording: plaque tends to develop where WSS is
**low**, with disturbed or oscillatory flow often present at the same sites, mainly at bifurcations
and inner bends. Avoid presenting oscillation as an established independent driver in human coronaries.

### Cited Findings
- The ESC consensus states the mechanism as "low and/or oscillatory" WSS activating NF-κB and
  inflammatory endothelial pathways (mechanistic, largely in-vitro and animal), typically at inner
  bends and bifurcations. Its clinical evidence section cites low WSS (and high WSS for
  rupture/high-risk features), not OSI —
  [Gijsen 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/153395/1/ehz551.pdf)
- Peiffer 2013: the low/oscillatory theory is "less robust than commonly assumed", point-wise studies
  are negative, and longitudinal studies from the healthy state are needed —
  [Peiffer 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3695746)
- In human coronaries, low-and-oscillatory sectors regressed and low non-oscillatory sectors
  progressed —
  [Timmins 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28148771); OSI showed no total-area effect —
  [Kok 2019](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/the-influence-of-multidirectional-shear-stress-on-plaque-progression-and-composition-changes-in-human-coronary-arteries)
- In pig coronaries, low TAWSS is "the strongest driver and predictor"; OSI adds ≤2 percentage points
  of PPV — [Hoogendoorn 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7177495)
- In rabbits, transWSS, not low or oscillatory WSS, matched lesion location (aorta, not coronary) —
  [Mohamied 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4286626)
- The large human prospective low-WSS evidence (PREDICTION, n = 506) is in ACS patients after PCI,
  with progression endpoints driven by protocol imaging —
  [Gijsen 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/153395/1/ehz551.pdf);
  [QxMD abstract](https://read.qxmd.com/read/22723305/prediction-of-progression-of-coronary-artery-disease-and-clinical-outcomes-using-vascular-profiling-of-endothelial-shear-stress-and-arterial-plaque-characteristics-the-prediction-study)

### Inferences
- Chatzizisis 2007 is a review (JACC) of mainly mechanistic and animal work. Citing it for "low/
  oscillatory" is fine for the mechanism, but not as evidence that OSI predicts human coronary plaque.
- No human study tests incident plaque in plaque-free coronaries against any WSS metric over ~10
  years. The thesis question (geometry → incident plaque on CCTA) is therefore not answered by the WSS
  literature, and the WSS link should be framed as the motivating mechanism, not a proven pathway.

### Gaps
- Chatzizisis et al. 2007 itself was not re-read here, so its exact wording and the studies it cites
  for "oscillatory" were not checked.
- Wentzel 2012 (Cardiovasc Res review) was not retrieved.
