# Taper, lumen size and oscillatory wall shear stress (OSI, RRT, transWSS)

Scope note: about 20 tool calls. Full texts read: Johnston & Johnston 2009 (MODSIM PDF), Bijari et al. 2012 (PMC), Grande Gutierrez et al. 2019 (PMC), Sun et al. 2025 (PMC). The rest is abstract- or index-level and is marked as such. Bed and geometry type are labelled on every item: [CORONARY / CAROTID / OTHER] x [IDEALISED / PATIENT-SPECIFIC / EXPERIMENTAL].

## Q1. Does a lumen that tapers less, or widens, produce higher OSI? Is there direct coronary evidence, or only carotid/idealised?

### Takeaway
The clearest evidence that a widening lumen raises OSI/RRT comes from the **carotid bulb**. In patient-specific (but rigid, Newtonian) CFD, more flare means more disturbed-flow area. In the **coronary** bed I found **no study that relates normal-range taper or lumen diameter to OSI or RRT** in non-diseased arteries. The only coronary evidence that "wider lumen means more oscillation" comes from **pathological dilation** (aneurysm/ectasia), and it is mostly idealised or very small-sample work. Of the papers the thesis already cites, only Johnston & Johnston 2009 reports OSI. Its OSI result does **not** support "taper creates oscillation": the taper-induced low-WSS point had only moderate OSI.

### Cited Findings

**Already-cited thesis sources, checked for OSI/RRT**
- [CORONARY, IDEALISED, conference paper] Johnston & Johnston 2009, "Simulations of pulsatile blood flow in tapered S-shaped in-plane and out-of-plane coronary arteries", 18th World IMACS/MODSIM Congress (conference proceedings, not a journal paper). Geometry: rigid, circular S-shaped tubes, inlet radius 2 mm, outlet radius 1.56 mm (outlet = 78% of inlet), straight inlet 3 cm, outlet 2 cm, bend radius of curvature 3.5 cm. In-plane (s2d) and out-of-plane (s3d) variants. Flow: Newtonian (rho 1056 kg/m3, mu 0.00345 Pa s), **uniform (flat) inlet profile**, pulsatile LCA (Perktold 1991) or RCA (Matsuo 1988) waveform, T = 1 s, Re = 226, alpha = 2.6. **The paper does compute OSI** (He & Ku 1996 definition). Findings: WSS rises as the radius decreases down the artery. With taper, the global min(max WSS) point moves from C' (in the bend, constant-radius model) up to X in the straight proximal inlet. **OSI at X was only "moderate"**, lower than at the bend points C' and E. Table 1 OSI values range about 0.04–0.13, and the LCA was higher than the RCA. The authors' explanation: "the axial velocity profile is symmetric at X and there are no eddies there, the only contribution to the OSI value comes from the changes in flow due to the inlet velocity waveform." Away from the inlet, low WSS and high OSI coincide (on the inner bend). — [MODSIM 2009 PDF](https://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim09/B4/johnston_bm.pdf)
  - Caveat: the flat inlet profile and the 3 cm straight inlet mean that the proximal low-WSS region partly reflects developing flow. The authors say taper "in part" explains it. Two model geometries, no patient data.
- [CORONARY, PATIENT-SPECIFIC, n = 4 RCAs] Johnston et al. 2007 (Phys Med Biol 52): WSS correlates strongly with reciprocal radius. High WSS on the inside of bends, low on the outside, and large between-artery variation. Index-level only. Whether OSI was reported was **not confirmed** in this session. — [Griffith repository index via search](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/070a2050-b50a-5c8f-817e-0ddfefee734a)
- [CORONARY, PATIENT-SPECIFIC CCTA-CFD, 349 normal vessels / 168 patients] Schultz et al. 2023 (Int J Cardiovasc Imaging): ESS increases rapidly and scatters widely with decreasing lumen diameter. WSS correlates with distance from the ostium (rho = 0.22–0.62). Men have lower ESS than women after adjusting for diameter. Abstract-level. **No OSI/RRT found in the abstract.** — [BVS record](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-36255544)
- [CORONARY, QCA, no CFD] Pacheco et al. 2021 (Am Heart J Plus 6:100027): angiographic tapering and atheroma burden relate to coronary flow reserve in women with no obstructive CAD. Abstract-level. **No WSS/OSI computed.** The search did not show a "taper deviation vs atherosclerosis" framing. The thesis should re-check how it describes this paper. — [PMC PDF](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/278e419f0d4a8eba5ccb70a4a9eb482f/main.PMC10976284.pdf)

**Carotid bulb / flare (other bed, the clearest "widening raises OSI" evidence)**
- [CAROTID, EXPERIMENTAL scale model + cadaver] Ku, Giddens, Zarins & Glagov 1985, Arteriosclerosis 5:293–302 (the origin of the OSI). Pulsatile LDV in a scale model of the carotid bifurcation, compared with intimal thickness in cadaver bifurcations at 20 locations. Intimal thickness correlated with 1/max shear (r = 0.90), 1/mean shear (r = 0.82) and an oscillation index (r = 0.82). The thickest plaque sat at the **outer wall of the sinus (the widened bulb)**, where mean shear was near zero and instantaneous shear changed direction. Abstract-level. Note that the oscillation index did not correlate better than 1/mean shear. — [Scholarhub abstract](https://scholarhub.vn/publication/Pulsatile-flow-and-atherosclerosis-in-the-human-carotid-bifurcation-Positive-correlation-between-plaque-location-and-low-oscillating-shear-stress-/f34bbc20-c572-41c2-82f5-d3b861a0f760)
- [CAROTID, PATIENT-SPECIFIC MRI-CFD, 50 normal bifurcations / 25 young adults] Lee, Antiga, Spence & Steinman 2008, Stroke 39:2341–2347. Disturbed flow was defined as the surface area exposed to low and oscillatory shear beyond objectively defined thresholds. **Proximal area ratio (flare) and tortuosity** predicted it. Bifurcation angle, planarity and distal area ratio did not. Abstract-level. The exact OSI coefficients were not seen. — [scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/8116fb61ac93bd197c7cf5bde51f7d14); [UWO record](https://ir.lib.uwo.ca/medpub/266)
- [CAROTID, PATIENT-SPECIFIC, same 50 bifurcations] Bijari, Antiga, Gallo, Wasserman & Steinman 2012, J Biomech 45:1632–1637 (full text read). Rigid, Newtonian, Womersley inlet/outlet profiles scaled allometrically. Redefined flare FlareA = max proximal CCA area / CCA3 area (mean 2.18 ± 0.34). Model: disturbed-flow area ∝ FlareA − C x Tort2D. **Higher flare increases disturbed flow; higher tortuosity decreases it.** Adjusted R² (all p < 0.001): WSS80 0.542, WSS90 0.523, **RRT80 0.549, OSI80 0.388** (the original variables gave 0.31–0.38). Normalising area to CCA3 raised RRT80 to 0.607. **OSI is the least well explained metric.** ICC FlareA 0.90. — [PMC3371282](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3371282)
- [CAROTID] Bijari et al. (later, ARIC MRI) is described as finding geometry "an independent, albeit weak, predictor" of early wall thickening. Search-snippet level only. — [search summary, via PMC4321819](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321819)
- [CAROTID, ISMRM 2010 abstract, 64 normal carotids] The size of high-OSI regions related to dICA/dCCA, bifurcation angle and tortuosity. Conference abstract, not peer-reviewed in full. — [ISMRM PDF](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/10MProceedings/PDFfiles/3700_5611.PDF)

**Coronary dilation: aneurysm / ectasia (pathological widening)**
- [CORONARY, IDEALISED, 4 models] Wang et al. 2020, *Complexity* (a non-specialist Hindawi journal; flag). Fusiform aneurysm models at 1x–5x the normal diameter. In the aneurysm, WSS fell from 3.51 to 0.08 Pa, **OSI rose from 0.01 to 0.30 and RRT from 0.38 to 51.59 Pa⁻¹** from 1x to 5x. Abstract-level. Assumptions not checked. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/hin/complx/8507273.html)
- [CORONARY, PATIENT-SPECIFIC, Kawasaki, 10 patients / 32 aneurysms] Grande Gutierrez et al. 2019, Int J Cardiol 281:15–21 (full text read). Pulsatile, Newtonian, **deformable wall (FSI)**, closed-loop lumped-parameter outlets. Larger Dmax/Z-score went with lower TAWSS (coefficient −0.36, p = 0.044) and more low-WSS area (0.45, p = 0.011). The correlations were weak. **OSI and residence time were not reported against diameter.** Their correlations with geometry were described only as "low". OSI did not differ by thrombosis (0.14 vs 0.15). Residence time did (9.07 vs 2.05 cycles, p = 0.004). — [PMC6511338](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6511338)
- [CORONARY, PATIENT-SPECIFIC angiography-CFD, 34 patients / 53 ectatic arteries] Sun et al. 2025, Front Cardiovasc Med 12:1705263 (full text read). Rigid, Newtonian, unsteady flow with generic Doppler inlet profiles, zero-pressure outlets, branches < 2 mm omitted. Thrombosed vs non-thrombosed ectasia: OSImean 3.0 vs 0.8 (x10⁻²), RRTmean 286 vs 183 (x10⁻² Pa⁻¹), transWSSmean 7.7 vs 3.8 (x10⁻² Pa), all p ≤ 0.011. Max diameter 7.2 vs 5.3 mm (p = 0.015). **OSI, RRT and transWSS were not regressed on diameter.** Multivariable: RRT OR 1.010, transWSS OR 1.99. Only 11 thrombosed cases. — [PMC12669196](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12669196/)
- [CORONARY, Kawasaki, J Cardiovasc Comput Tomogr 2025] Aneurysm vs ectasia: TAWSS, OSI and RRT showed "variable and diffuse flow patterns with limited co-localization". Abstract/index-level only. — [MTU Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p2/1910)

**Coronary remodelling (no OSI)**
- [CORONARY, swine serial IVUS] Koskinas et al. 2010, Circulation: expansive remodelling arose in regions of very low ESS and went with the most plaque progression. **ESS only, no OSI.** Abstract-level. — [QxMD](https://read.qxmd.com/read/20439786/natural-history-of-experimental-coronary-atherosclerosis-and-vascular-remodeling-in-relation-to-endothelial-shear-stress-a-serial-in-vivo-intravascular-ultrasound-study)
- No serial coronary study relating expansive (positive) remodelling to OSI/RRT was found. — [search results, ESS-only studies e.g. EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/130863)

**Why OSI is awkward in coronaries**
- Timmins et al. (J R Soc Interface) did not compute OSI in coronary models. The reasons given were the lack of a predominant axial direction and the difficulty of defining positive flow with multiple outlets. They used a deviation metric instead. Search-snippet level. — [Royal Society](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsif.2016.0972)

### Inferences
- "Less taper or widening means higher OSI" is well supported only in the **carotid bulb**, where the widening is large (FlareA ≈ 2.2, a doubling of area) and sits just upstream of a bifurcation. Normal coronary taper is a gradual area *decrease*. The physical analogy, an adverse pressure gradient in an expanding section, is plausible, but the thesis should present it as an analogy, not as coronary evidence.
- In Bijari 2012, OSI was the metric that flare explained *least* (R² 0.39 vs 0.55 for RRT). Even in the best-case bed, a geometry-to-OSI link is moderate.
- The coronary aneurysm/ectasia results concern dilations of 1.5x to several times the reference diameter. They should not be extrapolated to between-person variation in normal taper in a plaque-free cohort.
- The Johnston 2009 result cuts the other way from a naive "taper raises OSI" claim. Taper relocated the *low-WSS* minimum to the straight proximal part, but that point had low-to-moderate OSI. In that idealised model, taper produced low but unidirectional shear there.

### Gaps
- No coronary study (idealised or patient-specific) that varies or measures normal-range taper and reports OSI/RRT/transWSS was found. Johnston 2009 compares only one taper ratio (78%) against constant radius.
- Lee 2008 and Ku 1985 full texts were not read. The exact OSI-specific coefficients in Lee 2008 are unverified.
- Zarins et al. 1983 (Circ Res; carotid bifurcation, intimal thickening at the low-shear outer sinus wall) was not retrieved this session. From memory it predates OSI and reports flow separation and low shear, not OSI. Verify before citing.
- Whether Johnston et al. 2007 (Phys Med Biol) or Schultz 2023 report any oscillatory metric could not be confirmed from abstracts.
- The Wang 2020 *Complexity* aneurysm paper's assumptions were not checked.

## Q2. Does normal coronary taper (accelerating flow) suppress oscillation?

### Takeaway
There is **no direct coronary CFD test** of this. The idea rests on a general fluid-mechanics principle (converging flow has a favourable pressure gradient and resists separation). Peer-reviewed support for it in arteries is thin and indirect. The one coronary idealised paper that computed OSI with taper (Johnston 2009) found WSS rising distally and moderate OSI in the straight proximal part, but it did not compare OSI between tapered and constant-radius models directly in this text.

### Cited Findings
- [CORONARY, IDEALISED, conference] With taper, WSS "is found to increase as the radius decreases down the artery". OSI peaks at bend points (C', E), not along the tapering straight segments. A direct OSI contrast between the tapered and uniform-radius models is not tabulated here: the uniform-radius results are in Johnston & Johnston 2008, which was not read. — [MODSIM 2009 PDF](https://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim09/B4/johnston_bm.pdf)
- [OTHER, analytical] Cen & Qin 1993 (Appl Math Mech): in a converging tapered vessel with small taper angle, the flow stays in the developing regime along its whole length. Abstract-level. — [AMM](https://www.amm.shu.edu.cn/CN/Y1993/V14/I4/319)
- [OTHER, canine femoral, in vivo + simulation] Banerjee, Back & Cho 1995 (Biorheology 32:6): with a parabolic inlet, taper increased both positive and negative shear rates. With a uniform inlet, wall shear rate fell along the axis. **So the effect of taper on reverse shear depends on the inflow assumption.** Abstract-level. — [IOS Press](https://content.iospress.com/articles/biorheology/bir32-6-10)
- [OTHER, abstract, varying-area elastic vessel] WSS and its gradient grow with taper angle in both converging and diverging vessels. Abstract-level, small Chinese-language study. — [search result, AMM/ntlib](https://ntlib.org.cn/asset/detail/0/20311089326)
- [OTHER, aorta, Biorheology 1998] Reducing the aortic taper below the celiac artery produced looping flow along the lateral walls. Abstract-level. — [IOS Press Biorheology](https://content.iospress.com/articles/biorheology/bir021)
- The claim that a gradual taper "limits flow separation" appears explicitly only in a **patent**, not peer-reviewed data. — [USPTO 6605053](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6605053)
- Inflow development itself changes the oscillatory/multidirectional character of WSS in curved tubes (idealised, **arXiv preprint**, no taper; flag). — [arXiv 2209.08440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08440)

### Inferences
- "Normal taper suppresses oscillation" is a reasonable mechanistic expectation but not a demonstrated coronary result. In thesis text it should be stated as a hypothesis derived from fluid mechanics and carotid analogy. A safe wording: taper keeps flow accelerating and WSS rising along the artery. Whether it lowers OSI in human coronaries has not been tested directly.
- Coronary OSI is low overall in most non-diseased, non-bifurcation regions (e.g. Johnston table ≈ 0.04–0.13 at the worst points; Sun 2025 non-thrombosed ectasia OSImean ≈ 0.008). The dynamic range for a taper effect on OSI may be small, and OSI is ill-defined in multi-outlet coronary models (Timmins). This argues for TAWSS or RRT rather than OSI if the thesis discusses mechanism.

### Gaps
- No coronary study comparing OSI or RRT across real taper values, or with flare vs taper parameterised, was found.
- Johnston & Johnston 2008 (constant-radius S-shaped coronary models) was not read, so a direct OSI comparison taper vs no taper could not be made.
- Classical divergent/convergent pipe-flow references (e.g. reviews by Ku 1997, Annu Rev Fluid Mech) were not retrieved.
