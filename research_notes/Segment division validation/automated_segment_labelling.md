# Validation of automated coronary segment labelling (CCTA)

Scope note: full texts were read for Cao 2017, Ren 2023, Hampe 2024, TTN (Zhang 2024), CPR-GCN (preprint version), TopoLab (preprint version), LWT-ARTERY-LABEL (preprint) and Akinyemi 2009. Brandt 2024 and Li 2024 are abstract-only. TreeLab-Net (Wu 2019), Gülsun 2014 and Yang 2011 were not read directly; their numbers come from abstracts or from citing papers, as marked.

## 1. Which papers label coronary segments automatically, and against what reference?

### Takeaway
Only a few CCTA papers split the LAD, LCx and RCA into proximal, mid and distal parts: Yang 2011, Cao 2017, Ren 2023 and Brandt 2024. All of them score against one to three expert readers who labelled, or corrected, the same centerlines. Most deep-learning papers (TreeLab-Net, CPR-GCN, TTN, TopoLab, Hampe 2024, LWT) label whole branches between bifurcations, with one label per main artery. They do not report proximal, mid or distal accuracy at all.

### Cited Findings
**Model- and rule-based methods (proximal/mid/distal labelled)**
- **Cao Q, Broersen A, de Graaf MA, Kitslaar PH, Yang G, Scholte AJ, Lelieveldt BPF, Reiber JHC, Dijkstra J. Automatic identification of coronary tree anatomy in coronary computed tomography angiography. Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2017;33(11):1809–1819. doi:10.1007/s10554-017-1169-0** (LUMC/Medis; two co-authors employed by Medis, a declared conflict of interest). Method: 3D right-dominant and left-dominant tree models. The right-dominant model comes from Dodge 1988 angiographic statistics, refined on 5 cases; the left-dominant model was built from 11 cases. Coherent-point-drift registration finds the main branches, a cost-minimising match labels the side branches, and logic rules then set the p/m/d boundaries at the AHA side-branch origins. Thresholds, for example RI if it arises < 0.5 cm from the bifurcation and AL if < 2 cm from the LCx ostium, come from "clinical experience of cardiologists" rather than from fitting. Labels: pRCA, mRCA, dRCA, pLAD, mLAD, dLAD, pLCx, LCx, D1, D2, OM1, OM2, R/L-PDA, R/L-PLB, RI/AL. In left-dominant trees the dRCA was excluded and the RCA was not split. Reference: two experts (≥ 4 years' cardiac CT experience) labelled independently, then cross-checked each other; differences that remained were kept as inter-observer variability. Test set: 83 cases (56 right-dominant, 27 left-dominant), 1,149 segments. Cases with severe proximal lesions or anomalies were excluded. Centerlines came from Yang 2012 extraction and were manually corrected where needed. — [Cao 2017, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- **Yang G, Broersen A, Petr R, Kitslaar P, de Graaf MA, Bax JJ, Reiber JHC, Dijkstra J. Automatic coronary artery tree labeling in coronary computed tomographic angiography datasets. Computing in Cardiology 2011;38:109–112** (conference paper; Cao cites it as "Comput Cardiol. 2011;20"). It is the precursor of Cao 2017 for right-dominant trees and the source of the p/m/d adjustment criteria. I did not read its numbers. — cited in [Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- **Akinyemi A, Murphy S, Poole I, Roberts C. Automatic labelling of coronary arteries. Proc. 17th EUSIPCO 2009, pp. 1562–1566** (conference; Toshiba). It uses a multivariate Gaussian classifier on geometric features plus topological rules, and leave-one-out on 42 datasets. Agreement with one human expert was 84.4% on manually segmented centerlines and 76.47% on automatically tracked centerlines. It did **not** identify the proximal, mid and distal parts of the main arteries (Cao 2017 makes the same point). — [Akinyemi 2009 PDF](https://eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2009/contents/papers/1569187117.pdf); [Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- **Gülsün MA, Funka-Lea G, Zheng Y, Eckert M. CTA coronary labeling through efficient geodesics between trees using anatomy priors. MICCAI 2014;17:521–528. doi:10.1007/978-3-319-10470-6_65** (Siemens). According to Cao 2017, it used a similar overlap measure and reached 87% (left tree) and 86% (right tree) on automatically detected centerlines. It was not used on left-dominant cases or cases with an RI. I did not read it directly. — [Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- **Ren P, He Y, Guo N, Luo N, Li F, Wang Z, Yang Z. A deep learning-based automated algorithm for labeling coronary arteries in computed tomography angiography images. BMC Med Inform Decis Mak 2023;23:249. doi:10.1186/s12911-023-02332-y** (one author from a commercial company, Shukun). Pipeline: 3D U-Net segmentation, skeletonisation, rule-based labelling, and an LCx chosen by distance to the left atrium and left ventricle (point counts < 25 mm from the LA and < 30 mm from the LV). It labels 16 SCCT segments, including p/m/d for the RCA, LAD and LCx. 157 patients, **all right-dominant**. Reference: two experts (≥ 4 years) **reviewed and corrected the algorithm's output**, and a third expert (> 10 years) resolved disagreements. This is a review-of-output design, not independent labelling, so it is biased toward agreement. — [Ren 2023, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10626726/)
- **Brandt V, Fischer A, Schoepf UJ, et al. (incl. Gülsün MA). Deep learning-based automated labeling of coronary segments for structured reporting of CCTA in accordance with SCCT guidelines. J Thorac Imaging 2024;39(2):93–100. doi:10.1097/RTI.0000000000000753**. A tree-LSTM (bottom-up encoding, top-down decoding) trained on 706 radiologist-annotated cases and tested on 104 patients (1,491 segments). The centerlines were "automatically extracted, labeled, and validated by 2 expert readers according to SCCT guidelines". — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1097%2Frti.0000000000000753%22)
- **Denzinger F, Wels M, Taubmann O, Gülsün MA, et al. CAD-RADS scoring using deep learning and task-specific centerline labeling. arXiv:2202.03671 (2022; "under review MIDL"), PREPRINT.** It introduces a "task- and model-specific, heuristic coronary segment labeling, which subdivides coronary trees into consistent parts across patients". The labelling is judged only by the downstream CAD-RADS AUC (rule-out 0.914 → 0.942), not against anatomical segments. — [arXiv 2202.03671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03671)

**Learning-based methods (branch-level labels; mostly no p/m/d)**
- **Wu D, Wang X, Bai J, et al. Automated anatomical labeling of coronary arteries via bidirectional tree LSTMs (TreeLab-Net). Int J CARS 2019;14:271–280. doi:10.1007/s11548-018-1884-6.** Uses centerline position features only and tenfold cross-validation. Reported AUC > 97% for LM, LAD, LCx and RCA (from the abstract or secondary descriptions; full text not read). — [search summary of abstract](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2FS11548-018-1884-6)
- **Yang H, Zhen X, Chi Y, Zhang L, Hua X-S. CPR-GCN: Conditional partial-residual graph convolutional network in automated anatomical labeling of coronary arteries. CVPR 2020** (Alibaba DAMO; arXiv:2003.08560). 511 subjects; centerlines extracted automatically. Two experts labelled each branch independently, then merged their labels by discussion. Five-fold CV: mean recall 95.8%, precision 95.4%, F1 0.955. The unit is the branch (the segment between bifurcations), not the point. — [arXiv 2003.08560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08560)
- **Zhang Y, Luo G, Wang W, Cao S, Dong S, Yu D, Wang X, Wang K. TTN: Topological Transformer Network for automated coronary artery branch labeling in cardiac CT angiography. IEEE J Transl Eng Health Med 2024;12:129–? doi:10.1109/JTEHM.2023.3329031.** 325 CCTA. Three radiologists annotated the centerlines manually (one third each) and then cross-checked all cases; anatomical names were assigned separately and inconsistencies discussed. Classes: RCA, R-PDA, LM, LAD, DA, LCX, OM, L-PDA, RI, R-PLB. There is **no p/m/d**. — [TTN, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10706468/)
- **Zhang Z, Zhao Z, Wang D, Zhao S, Liu Y, Liu J, Wang L. Topology-preserving automatic labeling of coronary arteries via anatomy-aware connection classifier (TopoLab). MICCAI 2023** (arXiv:2307.11959). Uses the public orCaScore set (72 CTA), re-annotated "with experienced radiologists", with 5-fold CV, plus an in-house set of 1,200 scans (≥ 2 experts) split 800/200/200. Reports 14 vessel categories with segment-level metrics. — [arXiv 2307.11959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11959)
- **Hampe N, van Velzen SGM, Wolterink JM, Collet C, Henriques JPS, Planken N, Išgum I. Graph neural networks for automatic extraction and labeling of the coronary artery tree in CT angiography. J Med Imaging 2024;11(3):034001. doi:10.1117/1.JMI.11.3.034001.** 104 patients from 2 hospitals. All points on the reference centerlines were labelled manually (AHA, 10 classes). **It explicitly does not subdivide p/m/d:** "subdivision of the three main arteries into proximal, medial, and distal segments can require landmarks that do not automatically follow from the tree topology. Therefore, in line with previous learning-based works, a single label was assigned to each of the three main arteries." — [Hampe 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/)
- **Zhang S, Gharleghi R, Singh S, Moses D, Adikari D, Sowmya A, Beier S. LWT-ARTERY-LABEL: a lightweight framework for automated coronary artery identification. arXiv:2508.06874 (2025), PREPRINT.** Combines learning with rule-based topology constraints, for example an RI rule with a 3 mm threshold to the LM end. Trained on the Coronary Atlas (380 cases) with 5-fold CV; tested on an internal set (GeoCAD, 70) and an external set (orCaScore, 72). Internal results: recall 93.06%, precision 93.41%, F1 93.17%. — [arXiv 2508.06874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06874)
- Other preprints or small studies, not examined in detail: Li Y et al., geometric deep learning, 141 patients, 13 classes, weighted F1 0.805, 5-fold CV ([arXiv 2212.00386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00386), preprint); Wang X et al., Point Transformer, 53 subjects ([arXiv 2305.02533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02533), preprint, very small); Li J et al., hierarchical PointNet++, EMBC 2024, doi:10.1109/EMBC53108.2024.10782317, private dataset, no numbers in the abstract ([Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1109%2Fembc53108.2024.10782317%22)).
- ARCADE and the AGMN/EAGMN/HAGMN line label segments on **invasive X-ray angiograms** (2D), not CCTA, so they do not transfer to centerline division. — [HAGMN-UQ, Med Image Anal 2025, doi:10.1016/j.media.2024.103374](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11609026/)
- De Santis 2022 compared two commercial vendors on how many coronary segments (16-segment SCCT-modified) each identified (one found 14.8% more segments) and on speed. It did not measure boundary accuracy. — [De Santis 2022, Diagnostics 12:1987, doi:10.3390/diagnostics12081987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9406865/)

### Inferences
- Methods that output proximal, mid and distal labels in CCTA are model- or rule-based (Yang/Cao, Ren) or come from industry (Brandt/Siemens, Gülsün). They are evaluated against expert labels on the *same* centerlines; none uses an external reference such as plaque reads.
- Cao 2017 is the closest precedent to the thesis. It comes from the same LUMC/Medis group, which is plausibly the origin of the Medis reader segment labels. Its rule set (side-branch origins for LAD/LCx) matches the thesis landmark rule.
- The literature offers no published method that is validated on plaque-free cohorts. Cao excluded severe proximal lesions; Ren, Brandt and the deep-learning sets are clinical-referral CCTA.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full text of Yang 2011, Gülsün 2014 or TreeLab-Net to verify their numbers.
- Brandt 2024 is abstract-only: per-segment p/m/d numbers and how the reference was produced are unknown.

## 2. What metrics are reported?

### Takeaway
There are three families of metrics: (a) **presence** of each label (counts, precision/recall/F1, % agreement); (b) **overlap** along the centerline, the fraction of the labelled stretch that matches the expert, which the Leiden group introduced and Ren and Brandt reused; (c) **branch- or point-level classification** P/R/F1 (deep-learning papers). None of the papers I read reports boundary (landmark) position error in mm along the centerline. The closest is Cao's overlap of start and end points.

### Cited Findings
- **Cao 2017 metric definitions:**
  - *Presence*: a label is counted as definitely correct if both experts agree with the algorithm, definitely wrong if both disagree, and semi-correct if the experts disagree with each other.
  - *Overlap*: for label A, along the extracted centerline, points with the same label in automatic and expert labelling are TP and all others FP; OV_A = TP_A / (TP_A + FP_A). It is computed only on "definitely correct" segments.
  - *Risk-score accuracy*: the share of the three main branches fully identified; proximal segments plus LM; segment involvement score (SIS) and Leaman score as a % of the expert values, assuming every segment had plaque. — [Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- **Cao 2017 numbers:**
  - Presence: definitely correct in 709/795 = 89.2% of right-dominant segments and 296/354 = 83.6% of left-dominant segments; definitely wrong in 67 (8.4%) right-dominant and 14 (4.0%) left-dominant segments. Most errors were on diagonal and OM branches (55.2% of errors in right-dominant, 78.6% in left-dominant trees). Proximal segments (pRCA, pLAD, pLCx) and LM were present in 100% of cases.
  - Overlap: all labels ≥ 70%, LM 100%. Mean overlap was 92.0% (±6.7%) in right-dominant and 90.7% (±9.5%) in left-dominant trees. 18 right-dominant and 7 left-dominant segments had zero overlap (D2, LCx, PLB).
  - "In general, the middle segment of a branch usually has a lower overlap score compared to the proximal segment of the same branch". A missed or unextracted D1 shifts the pLAD end point. A missing D2 or OM1 affects mLAD and pLCx. Errors propagate downstream.
  - All three main branches were fully correct in only 70.5% of right-dominant cases (96.3% of left-dominant). "The wrongly labeled segments all occurred in the distal part (dLAD or dLCx)".
  - SIS was 92.6% ±7.3% (right-dominant) and 93.3% ±8.1% (left-dominant) of the expert value, and identical to the expert value in only 37.5% and 46.3% of patients. Leaman score was 96.0% and 96.7%. — [Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- **Ren 2023 numbers:**
  - Presence over 2,148 segments: precision 98.5%, recall 96.6%, accuracy 95.7% ("overall accuracy 96.2%" in text), F1 97.6%. pRCA, mRCA, pLAD and mLAD were 100%; dRCA, dLAD 99.4%; pCx 99.4%; LCx 96.2%. R-PLB 79.0%, D2 88.5%, RI 84.7%.
  - Overlap: the mean over 16 segments was 94.0%; pRCA, mRCA, dRCA and pLAD were "> 78%"; pCx 98.7%; LCx 93.0%; LM 100%. Per-segment p/m/d overlap is shown only in a figure. — [Ren 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10626726/)
- **Brandt 2024:** average percentage overlap 94.4% against the expert readers, ranging from 87.1% (R-PDA) to 100% (pRCA). — [Brandt 2024 abstract](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1097%2Frti.0000000000000753%22)
- **Deep-learning branch-level P/R/F1 on 325 CCTA, all re-implemented in TTN's 5-fold CV:** AICT mean F1 0.841, TreeLab-Net 0.835, CPR-GCN 0.826, TTN 0.880. Under this re-implementation CPR-GCN falls from its self-reported 0.955 to 0.826, so the numbers depend heavily on dataset and implementation. Per-class LAD F1 was 0.882–0.931 and RCA 0.933–0.947. — [TTN 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10706468/); [CPR-GCN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08560)
- **TopoLab, orCaScore 5-fold:** weighted recall, precision and F1 ≈ 92.2%. It also introduces "topology violation" metrics (viola = the share of connections that violate the category tree; a case-level version too). — [TopoLab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11959)
- **Hampe 2024:** F1 per class on nodes (points), and accuracy as a micro-average over nodes. End-to-end labelling F1 was 0.74 and extraction F1 0.85; the 11-class variant gave labelling P/R/F1 0.75/0.72/0.73 and overall accuracy 0.93. — [Hampe 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/)
- **Akinyemi 2009:** a single "overall level of agreement": 84.4% on manual centerlines and 76.47% on automatically tracked centerlines. — [Akinyemi 2009](https://eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2009/contents/papers/1569187117.pdf)

### Inferences
- A mm-scale boundary error along the arc length is not a reported metric in this literature. A thesis that reports it, for example the signed and absolute distance of each cut from the Medis reader boundary per boundary type, would add to rather than copy the standard. Cao's overlap OV_A is the established metric to report alongside, for comparability.
- Overlap is a ratio. It hides absolute error, and short segments (for example the mRCA in a short RCA) are penalised more by the same mm error. Cao and Ren average overlap only over segments whose label was "present" or "correct", which inflates it.
- Results on manual or corrected centerlines (Cao, Akinyemi mode 1) are better than on automatically traced ones (Akinyemi 84.4% vs 76.5%; Hampe end-to-end 0.74). Centerline quality matters as much as the rule.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that reports a boundary distance in mm for coronary segment cuts.
- Per-segment overlap values for mLAD/dLAD and mRCA/dRCA appear only in figures (Cao Fig. 7, Ren Fig. 3); I could not extract the exact numbers.

## 3. How are rule parameters tuned versus evaluated?

### Takeaway
Rule-based methods mostly set their thresholds from clinical experience or atlas statistics, not by fitting. Their model building and testing use separate cases (Cao: 16 build cases, 83 test cases). Learning methods use k-fold CV (5 or 10) or a fixed train/val/test split. Only a few hold out an external test set (LWT: orCaScore; Hampe: 25 held-out scans).

### Cited Findings
- Cao 2017: 100 datasets in total. 5 right-dominant cases refined the Dodge-derived right-dominant model; 11 left-dominant cases built the left-dominant model by "leave-one-in" selection (each case served once as the template, length-normalised, and was validated on the other 10; the best was kept). The remaining 84 cases were for testing (83 used). The RI/AL thresholds (0.5 cm, 2 cm) came from cardiologist experience. — [Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- Akinyemi 2009: leave-one-out over 42 datasets. — [Akinyemi 2009](https://eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2009/contents/papers/1569187117.pdf)
- Ren 2023: rule-based, with fixed thresholds (25 mm to LA, 30 mm to LV). The paper does not describe a tuning/test split for those thresholds. — [Ren 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10626726/)
- Hampe 2024: 25 scans were randomly held out as a test set "not used during development"; 79 were used for training; hyperparameter robustness was reported on the test set. — [Hampe 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/)
- CPR-GCN and TTN: 5-fold CV over all subjects. CPR-GCN chose "the best model in each fold according to the overall testing precision", which selects on the test fold and is optimistic. — [CPR-GCN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08560); [TTN](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10706468/)
- TopoLab: 5-fold CV on orCaScore and an 800/200/200 split in-house. LWT: 5-fold CV on 380 cases plus internal (70) and external (72) test sets. — [TopoLab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11959); [LWT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06874)
- TreeLab-Net: tenfold CV (from a secondary description). — [search summary](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2FS11548-018-1884-6)

### Inferences
- For a landmark rule with one or two free fractions (the LAD mid–distal fraction without D2, and the RCA acute-margin fraction), the precedent supports fixing the fractions on a development subset of the Medis trees and reporting error on the rest, or using person-level k-fold. Cao's split (a small build set and a larger test set) is the closest analogue.
- A rule fitted to the Medis reader boundaries learns that reader's convention. Cao's LPDA example (section 4) shows that conventions differ between experts.

### Gaps
- None of the rule-based papers reports how sensitive accuracy is to the threshold values.

## 4. Inter-observer variability in segment boundaries and assignment (the ceiling)

### Takeaway
Experts agree on which segment labels are present in about 88–98% of segments and overlap by about 96–99% on average along the centerline. Disagreement concentrates in the LCx subtree, PDA/PLB and OM1/OM2 or D1/D2 numbering, and comes from differing *definitions* of where a segment starts, not only from noise. Published ceilings come from small expert pairs at single centres.

### Cited Findings
- Cao 2017, two experts after mutual verification:
  - Presence agreement: 776/795 (97.6%) right-dominant and 310/354 (87.6%) left-dominant segments. The experts disagreed on 19 (2.4%) right-dominant and 44 (12.4%) left-dominant segments; in left-dominant cases they disagreed on 12/16 (75%) OM2 segments.
  - Overlap difference between the experts averaged 1.0% (right-dominant) and 2.2% (left-dominant). It was larger in the LCx subtree, with a 10.0% average overlap difference on LPDA in left-dominant trees. The cause was a definitional disagreement: one expert started the LPDA where the LCx turns into the ventricular groove, the other at the LCx–LPLB bifurcation. — [Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- Brandt 2024: inter-reader overlap was 96.6%, against 94.4% for the algorithm. — [Brandt 2024](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1097%2Frti.0000000000000753%22)
- Ren 2023 reports expert-1 vs expert-2 agreement per segment only in a figure (Fig. 4); I could not extract the numbers. — [Ren 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10626726/)
- Even the guideline boundaries are disputed. The ASCI-PT consensus accepted the SCCT 18-segment model but reported only moderate agreement (64.7%) on the pLAD end-point convention when a large D1 lies near the first septal. This comes from a search summary; I did not read it in full. — [PMC10973734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10973734)
- Segment-level plaque reads (where the thesis outcome comes from) show inter-reader κ for plaque presence per segment of 0.77 (ROMICAT-II, 3 readers, 30 datasets), 0.85 (16-slice MDCT), and weighted κ 0.88 (64-slice). These reflect plaque detection *and* segment assignment together; none isolates segment assignment. — [ROMICAT-II, JACC 2014, doi:10.1016/j.jacc.2014.05.039](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2014.05.039); [JACC 2006, doi:10.1016/j.jacc.2005.10.005](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2005.10.005); [Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2008, doi:10.1007/s10554-008-9299-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10554-008-9299-z)

### Inferences
- A realistic ceiling for agreement between an automatic and a reader division is about 1–2% overlap difference for main-artery segments, and much worse for LCx distal, OM and PDA. Agreement with *one* reader convention (Medis) does not show agreement with the visual reader who graded plaque in CGPS, because definitions differ between readers.
- No study measured how often the visual CCTA reader places a plaque in a different segment than a centerline-based division would. This is the error that matters for the thesis outcome and is unquantified.

### Gaps
- I found no study that reports the inter-observer distance (mm) between human-placed mid/distal LAD or mid/distal RCA boundaries.
- I found no study of reader agreement on segment assignment of plaque specifically.

## 5. Rule-based (landmark/branch-origin) methods and accuracy at landmark-free boundaries

### Takeaway
The guideline definitions themselves use a fraction-of-length rule where no landmark exists: the RCA proximal/mid boundary at half the distance to the acute margin, and in SYNTAX the LAD mid/distal at half the distance from the first septal to the apex when the RAO angle is not visible. Rule-based methods inherit this. Accuracy is lowest exactly at the distal and landmark-free segments, but no paper isolates the error at those boundaries.

### Cited Findings
- SYNTAX/AHA (Austen 1975 lineage) segment definitions:
  - RCA proximal: "From ostium to one half the distance to the acute margin of the heart". RCA mid: to the acute margin. RCA distal: from the acute margin to the PDA origin.
  - LAD mid: from the first septal "extending to the point where LAD forms an angle (RAO view). If this angle is not identifiable this segment ends at one half the distance from the first septal to the apex".
  - Proximal circumflex: up to and including the OM1 origin. — [SYNTAX score segment definitions](https://syntaxscore.org/index.php/tutorial/definitions/14-appendix-i-segment-definitions)
- The SCCT reporting guideline is Leipsic J, Abbara S, Achenbach S, Cury R, Earls JP, Mancini GBJ, Nieman K, Pontone G, Raff GL. SCCT guidelines for the interpretation and reporting of coronary CT angiography. J Cardiovasc Comput Tomogr 2014;8(5):342–358. doi:10.1016/j.jcct.2014.07.003. CPR-GCN and LWT cite it as the labelling standard. I did not read its exact boundary wording, so its mid/distal LAD definition is unverified here. — [cited in LWT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06874); [CPR-GCN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08560)
- Original AHA classification: Austen WG, Edwards JE, Frye RL, et al. A reporting system on patients evaluated for coronary artery disease. Circulation 1975;51(4 Suppl):5–40. doi:10.1161/01.CIR.51.4.5. — [cited in Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- Cao 2017 sets p/m/d of the LAD and LCx at specific side-branch origins (AHA), after model matching. Error sources: missing D1, D2 or OM1 shifts the boundaries; the middle segment has lower overlap than the proximal one; all full-main-branch failures were in dLAD or dLCx. The RCA was not divided in left-dominant trees ("not important"). — [Cao 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5677991/)
- Hampe 2024 declined to divide p/m/d at all because the landmarks "do not automatically follow from the tree topology", and says this is in line with previous learning-based works. — [Hampe 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/)
- Ren 2023 reached 100% *presence* for mRCA, dRCA, mLAD and dLAD. Presence is trivial for main-artery segments, though, and the overlap for RCA and pLAD segments was only reported as "> 78%". — [Ren 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10626726/)

### Inferences
- A fixed-fraction cut at the RCA acute margin or the LAD mid–distal boundary (without D2) follows the guideline's own fallback, so it is defensible on definitional grounds. Its error against a reader then depends on (a) the reader's choice of landmark (RAO angle or acute margin judged visually) and (b) the traced centerline length, especially if the tracer stops short distally. A fraction of a truncated length moves the cut proximally.
- Published presence and overlap figures do not tell how accurate a landmark-free cut is. A thesis validation should report error separately for branch-anchored boundaries (pLAD|mLAD at D1, pLCx|LCx at OM1) and fraction-based boundaries (mLAD|dLAD, pRCA|mRCA, mRCA|dRCA). This literature gives no benchmark for the latter.
- Because the visual read stores no boundary positions, the Medis reader labels are the only boundary reference. Validation on Medis trees (plaque at baseline) carries over to the plaque-free tracer cohort only if tracer length and branch detection behave the same; Cao and Akinyemi show that automatic centerlines lower accuracy.

### Gaps
- No study reports accuracy specifically at the RCA acute-margin boundary or the LAD mid–distal boundary.
- The exact SCCT 2014 wording for the mid/distal LAD and RCA boundaries was not verified from the guideline itself.
