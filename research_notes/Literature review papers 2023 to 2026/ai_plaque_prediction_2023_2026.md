## Key Question 1: ORFAN and other FAI papers 2023 to 2026. Do they predict events without obstructive CAD or without plaque, and do they add to risk factors? Is one a must-cite?

### Takeaway
ORFAN (Chan 2024 Lancet) is by far the most cited 2023 to 2026 paper in this space and shows that a CCTA marker beyond plaque (FAI Score) predicts cardiac death and MACE independently of risk factors in patients WITHOUT OBSTRUCTIVE CAD, but these are referred patients and "non-obstructive" still includes plaque; no FAI paper found shows event prediction in plaque-free people. It is the natural single citation for "CCTA beyond plaque carries prognostic information", but for this thesis's gap it is nice to have, not must-cite, because the thesis outcome is plaque development, not events, and the SCAPIS 2026 paper covers "imaging beyond SCORE2 in a random population" more directly.

### Cited Findings
- ORFAN: 40,091 consecutive patients with clinically indicated CCTA in eight UK hospitals, MACE follow-up median 2.7 years; FAI Score prognostic value evaluated in 3,393 patients from two hospitals with 7.7 years follow-up — [PubMed 38823406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38823406/)
- Patients without obstructive CAD (32,533, 81.1%) accounted for 66.3% of all MACE and 63.7% of cardiac deaths — [PubMed 38823406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38823406/)
- Top vs bottom quartile FAI Score in all three arteries: HR 29.8 (13.9 to 63.9) for cardiac mortality, 12.6 (8.5 to 18.6) for MACE; "FAI Score in any coronary artery predicted cardiac mortality and MACE independently from cardiovascular risk factors and the presence or extent of CAD" — [PubMed 38823406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38823406/)
- The AI-Risk algorithm integrates FAI Score, plaque metrics and clinical risk factors; very high vs low/medium risk HR 6.75 for cardiac death; "well calibrated" — [PubMed 38823406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38823406/). The abstract does not report a C-statistic gain of FAI over risk factors alone (gap, see below).
- ORFAN: 314 citations (OpenAlex, 2026-11-08), ~128/yr — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(24)00596-8)
- Independent HTA (NIHR, for NICE) rapid review of CaRi-Heart: no external validation of the CaRi-Heart Risk model, since the validation data set had been used earlier to develop methods and thresholds for the FAI scores; improvement over standard care uncertain — [HTA 10.3310/WYGC4096 via INAHTA](https://database.inahta.org/article/31934); [PubMed 39023142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39023142/)
- CaRi-Heart reportedly received FDA De Novo authorisation in 2026; zero-calcium and no-risk-factor ORFAN sub-analyses are company/ESC 2026 conference presentations, not peer reviewed — [HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/cari-heart-receives-fda-de-novo-authorization-for-coronary-inflammation); [Medical Device Network](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/news/esc-2026-biomarker-indicates-coronary-inflammation-from-non-contrast-ct/)
- Lee SE et al. 2026 (PARADIGM, 412 patients with baseline plaque, 3.2 y interval): changes in plaque-free-segment PVAT were associated with plaque volume progression, but "none of the 4 baseline PVAT measures were significantly associated with plaque progression (all P > 0.50)"; motivated explicitly by reverse causation from plaque-adjacent fat — [PubMed 42900033](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42900033/)
- Overgaard et al. 2025: 200 asymptomatic T2DM participants without known CAD; high baseline PCAT attenuation (> -70.1 HU) was the only independent predictor of non-calcified plaque burden progression over 12 months (OR 3.5) — [PubMed 40155929](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40155929/)
- Zhang R et al. 2025 EJR: 249 referred patients with a normal first CCTA who developed plaque, plus 50 persistently normal controls; baseline FAI was highest in the vessels that later developed plaque and correlated with later plaque volume and necrotic core — [PubMed 40450797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40450797/)

### Inferences
- ORFAN is industry-linked (Antoniades is a founder of Caristo, which sells CaRi-Heart; inferred from the CaRi-Heart company reports and the HTA critique, not checked in the paper's declaration) and uses referred patients, so cite it for direction only, with the skeptical framing the thesis already uses.
- No peer-reviewed FAI paper found shows that baseline FAI predicts events or plaque in PLAQUE-FREE, unselected people. The baseline-FAI evidence for plaque progression is mixed: positive in a small T2DM cohort (Overgaard 2025), null in PARADIGM (Lee 2026). This null is useful for the thesis: a well-known non-geometric CCTA marker failed to predict progression from baseline.
- If the review adds one FAI citation, ORFAN is the one (one source per concept). `chan2024orfan` is already in refs.bib, so adding it costs no new bib entry. Suggested use: one clause after Hageman, e.g. that CCTA markers outside the plaque predicted events beyond risk factors in referred patients without obstructive disease (Chan 2024), which still leaves plaque-free unselected people untested.

### Gaps
- ORFAN full text not read: whether the paper reports C-statistic/NRI for FAI over risk factors alone, and how many of the 3,393 had no plaque at all, is unknown.
- Funding/conflicts declaration of ORFAN not read directly.

## Key Question 2: AI-quantified plaque volume studies 2023 to 2026 predicting events beyond risk scores (CLARIFY, Cleerly, SCAPIS deep learning)

### Takeaway
The most relevant new paper for this thesis is Malmqvist et al. 2026 (JACC CVI, SCAPIS): in 23,314 randomly selected 50 to 64 year olds, deep learning-quantified total plaque volume predicted first coronary events beyond SCORE2 and segment involvement score, but the gain was small (C 0.783 to 0.798). It is the direct counterpart to Hageman 2023 for CCTA rather than CAC, in a random population with SCORE2 as the base, and it is a must-add. The Cleerly/CONFIRM2 papers are in referred patients and industry-linked; they add nothing the SCAPIS paper does not hold for this thesis. CLARIFY (2023) is a diagnostic accuracy study, not prognosis.

### Cited Findings
- Malmqvist 2026: randomly selected men and women 50 to 64 years from a population-based cohort, no prior ASCVD, SCORE2 available; TPV and NCPV quantified by automated deep learning software; follow-up 7.8 years for CHD death or MI — [PubMed 42663364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42663364/)
- 287 events in 23,314; risk rose stepwise with TPV, "including among participants with coronary artery calcium score = 0 and in those without manually detected atherosclerosis"; adding TPV to SCORE2 + SIS: C 0.798 vs 0.783 (P = 0.022), NRI 0.093 (0.041 to 0.146); highest TPV category HR 6.36 (3.00 to 13.52) — [PubMed 42663364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42663364/)
- In CAC = 0, TPV > 0 to 32.1 mm3 HR 2.63 and > 32.1 mm3 HR 5.26 (secondary summary only) — [Diagnostic Imaging](https://www.diagnosticimaging.com/view/ccta-deep-learning-research-small-plaque-volume-coronary-atherosclerosis)
- Funding is public (Swedish Heart-Lung Foundation, Wallenberg, Swedish Research Council etc.); the software is not named in the abstract — [PubMed 42663364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42663364/)
- Citations: 4 (OpenAlex, 2026-11-08; published 3 months ago) — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004)
- Nurmohamed 2024 (Cleerly AI-QCT): 536 referred patients, 10.3 y follow-up, 114 MACE; AI-QCT improved a model of clinical risk factors + CACS (10-y AUC 0.82 vs 0.73; NRI 0.21); two authors are Cleerly employees with equity — [PubMed 37480907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37480907/)
- CONFIRM2 (van Rosendael 2026): 1,916 referred symptomatic patients, 87 MACE in 3 years; AI-QCT compared with CAD-RADS, CACS, modified Duke Index and clinical likelihood score; asymptomatic patients excluded — [PubMed 41324522](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41324522/)
- Kolossvary 2025 (SCOT-HEART, 1,750 patients, 82 MI over 8.6 y): plaque burden metrics did not improve a clinical model (C 0.70 vs 0.70), plaque radiomic features did (C 0.74) — [PubMed 39480364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39480364/)
- Miami Heart Study (2,301 asymptomatic community participants, AI-QCT/Cleerly-type): median TPV 54 mm3; the large majority had AI-detected plaque; no outcomes — [PubMed 40920149](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40920149/)
- A 2026 meta-analysis concluded AI-QCCTA gives prognostic value beyond stenosis, with sensitivity analysis restricted to Cleerly studies — [PubMed 42305883](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42305883/) (abstract seen only via search snippet; not verified)

### Inferences
- Malmqvist 2026 holds two things no cited source holds: (a) a CCTA-derived quantity added to SCORE2 in a random population sample improved event prediction only modestly (delta C 0.015), which sets a realistic expectation for any delta C the thesis reports; (b) AI found risk-bearing plaque in people with no manually detected atherosclerosis, which bears directly on the thesis's "plaque-free" definition (visual read, CGPS `ct_cag_sis == 0`): some visually plaque-free baselines may hold small plaque below reader detection. This supports the thesis's existing "not first ever" outcome wording.
- It partly overlaps Hageman 2023 (imaging adds little to SCORE2). Under one-source-per-concept, it could either replace Hageman for the "beyond SCORE2" point (CCTA rather than CAC, same base score, random sample) or be cited beside it for the second point (undetected plaque). Suggest keeping Hageman for CAC and adding Malmqvist for CCTA plus the undetected-plaque point; the author decides.
- Its outcome is events and its baseline is mixed, so it is not a near miss to the geometry gap; it is a "beyond risk factors, random population" anchor.
- Skepticism: the C gain is small and the P = 0.022 is a single comparison; it is SCAPIS (same cohort as Bergstrom 2021 and Good 2026), so the review already relies heavily on SCAPIS.

### Gaps
- Malmqvist full text not read: software name, whether the "no manually detected atherosclerosis" subgroup had an HR reported, and any industry role are unknown.
- I found no peer-reviewed SCAPIS deep-learning paper predicting plaque DEVELOPMENT (the re-examination results are not yet published, consistent with Good 2026 being design-only).
- CLARIFY not re-checked in this pass; from prior knowledge it is a diagnostic-accuracy study of AI stenosis vs expert readers (Griffin et al. 2023, JACC CVI), not prognostic, so not relevant (unverified here).

## Key Question 3: radiomics or deep-learning studies 2023 to 2026 predicting future plaque in plaque-free arteries

### Takeaway
One paper found is a closer near miss than Chen 2024 and Lee 2024 on design: Zhang R et al. 2025 (Int J Cardiol) predicted, at PATIENT level, who develops plaque after a normal (plaque-free) first CCTA, from pericoronary fat radiomics added to clinical risk factors, in 947 patients. This is structurally the thesis's "who" question (plaque-free person, incident plaque, added to risk factors), differing in feature (fat texture, not geometry), population (referred, repeat clinical CCTA) and interval. Its implausibly high AUCs (0.99 training, 0.93 validation) and single-group retrospective design warrant strong caveats. No 2023 to 2026 deep-learning GEOMETRY study predicting future plaque was found (excluding the author-excluded source).

### Cited Findings
- Zhang R 2025 Int J Cardiol: retrospective, 947 patients with two CCTAs and a normal initial scan, classified by follow-up as new plaque vs no new plaque; 279 PCAT radiomic features from baseline; clinical, radiomics and combined models; combined model AUC 0.99 (training) and 0.93 (validation), better than clinical and radiomics models (DeLong P < 0.05) — [PubMed 40812623](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40812623/)
- Same group (Zhang N, Wen Z; Beijing) published the descriptive companion (EJR 2025, n=249 + 50 controls) — [PubMed 40450797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40450797/)
- Citations: 4 (OpenAlex, 2026-11-08) — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.ijcard.2025.133778)
- Lee 2024 JCCT (already cited): 9,583 plaque-free segments in 1,162 PARADIGM patients, >= 2-year interval; clinical alone C 0.696 vs radiomics alone 0.691 vs combined 0.767 in the test set; 9.8% of segments developed plaque (PV >= 1 mm3) — [PubMed 38378314](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38378314/). This confirms the C-index values currently flagged in the review's CHECK comment as coming from a search snippet.
- Progression studies (Chen Q 2023 Circ CVI, n=214; Li Y 2024 Insights Imaging, n=1,233) predict rapid progression of EXISTING plaque from plaque or PCAT radiomics; Li found radiomics + clinical did not beat radiomics alone — [PubMed 37725670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37725670/); [PubMed 38900243](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38900243/)
- Search for ML on geometry from serial CCTA predicting plaque returned only patents (HeartFlow-type "predicting location, onset and/or change of coronary lesions"), not peer-reviewed validation — [USPTO 11663715](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11663715)

### Inferences
- Near-miss ranking against the thesis gap (geometry, plaque-free baseline, person-level incident plaque, beyond risk factors, random population, ~10 y):
  - Zhang R 2025: plaque-free PERSON, person-level incident plaque, beyond risk factors: yes. Geometry: no (fat radiomics, though radiomic shape features of the fat ROI are not artery geometry). Population: referred. Interval: unknown from abstract. Closer than Lee 2024 on unit (person, like the thesis "who" model) and baseline (whole patient plaque-free, like the CGPS clean baseline), equal on "beyond risk factors".
  - Lee 2024: plaque-free SEGMENTS in patients who may have plaque elsewhere; segment-level; beyond risk factors.
  - Chen 2024: plaque-free LAD segment proximal to a myocardial bridge only; includes vascular geometry/radiomics; narrow anatomy.
  - So Zhang 2025 is the closest on design; Chen 2024 remains the closest on feature type (geometry).
- Under one-source-per-concept the review's sentence "a plaque-free scan holds information about later plaque" is already carried by Chen and Lee. Zhang 2025 adds the person-level, whole-patient plaque-free version. Suggest either adding it to that sentence as the patient-level example, or swapping it for Lee if the review wants one non-geometric radiomics source; flag the AUC 0.99/0.93 as likely optimistic (overfitting of 279 features; retrospective; possibly same-centre validation; not verified).
- All plaque-free prediction studies found are referred cohorts with clinically indicated repeat CCTA and short or unreported intervals; none is a random population, none uses whole-tree geometry, none reaches ~10 years. The thesis gap sentence ("No study identified ... related the geometry of the three main coronary arteries on a plaque-free baseline scan to plaque development at follow-up") still holds after this search.

### Gaps
- Zhang R 2025 full text was blocked (ScienceDirect 403): number of centres, scan interval, reason for repeat CCTA, whether validation was internal or external, and the clinical variables are unknown. These must be checked before citing.
- No deep-learning geometry paper predicting future plaque (2023 to 2026, peer reviewed) was found; this may reflect search limits (PubMed/OpenAlex keyword search, about 15 queries), not absence.
- Citation counts are OpenAlex only; Scopus/Google Scholar not checked.
