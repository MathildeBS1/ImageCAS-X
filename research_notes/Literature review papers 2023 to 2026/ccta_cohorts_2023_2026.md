# Population and serial CCTA cohorts, plaque development from a clean baseline, and imaging beyond risk scores (2023–2026)

Scope note. Searches were targeted (PubMed E-utilities, OpenAlex, web search), run 2026-11-08. Citation counts are OpenAlex (OA) `cited_by_count` retrieved 2026-11-08. Citations per year = OA count divided by years since the publication month (rounded). Google Scholar was not queried and usually runs 1.3–2x higher. Earlier notes used Semantic Scholar (S2), so counts are not directly comparable: for `fuchs2023cgps` OA now gives 119 where the earlier report gave S2 63. Unless stated, findings come from PubMed abstracts, not full text. CAS-Net and shen2026geometry were not considered.

Already cited in `sec:lit-risk` (03_geometry_risk.tex): won2022paradigm, chen2024bridge, lee2024radiomics, bergstrom2021scapis, good2026scapis, hageman2023score2, glagov1987remodeling, plus the geometry papers. `fuchs2023cgps` (doi 10.7326/M22-3027) and `knuuti2019esc` are in refs.bib, but `knuuti2019esc` is the 2019 CCS guideline and has no 2024 successor in the bib.

## Which 2023–2026 papers report plaque development or progression on repeat CCTA in people plaque-free at baseline?

### Takeaway
Only one 2023–2026 full paper found reports CAD developing on repeat imaging in a group selected as plaque-free (normal coronaries) at baseline: the SCOT-HEART post hoc (Avigdor 2026). It is a referred chest-pain cohort, and repeat imaging was clinically indicated in a small, selected subset. The PARADIGM "new plaque formation" work exists only as an ESC abstract, and the Dutch 10-year cohort reports progression in mixed-baseline referred patients, not incidence from a clean baseline. No 2023–2026 population-based serial CCTA paper starting from a plaque-free baseline was found, which supports the thesis gap claim.

### Cited Findings
- SCOT-HEART post hoc: 524 patients with normal coronaries on baseline CCTA; after a median 9.3 y, 31 (6%) had repeat CCTA and 162 (31%) chest CT; CAD was found on 48% (15/31) of CCTA and 25% (41/162) of chest CT; median time to CAD detection 8.1 y; the authors conclude that one third of patients with a previously normal CCTA develop CAD on clinically indicated CT over 10 years — [PubMed 41781727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41781727/)
- In that study, CAD on follow-up CT did not change mortality or CAD death/MI, but raised invasive angiography (aHR 4.94) and revascularisation (aHR 19.99) — [PubMed 41781727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41781727/)
- Dutch 10-year cohort (Amsterdam UMC): 299 patients with suspected CAD had per-protocol repeat CCTA, median interval 10.2 y; 267 analysed after excluding CABG; mean age 57; each doubling of Lp(a) added 0.32% PAV over 10 years — [PubMed 39018040](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39018040/)
- The 2023 EHJ supplement abstract from the same group (n = 274, 10.2 y) reports median PAV 2.5% → 6.1%, mean progression 0.4%/y, and baseline non-calcified plaque as the only plaque predictor of progression; it does not report plaque-free patients or new plaque development — [EHJ Suppl 2023](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/Supplement_2/ehad655.152/7392901)
- The same Amsterdam cohort also yielded the polygenic-risk paper (JACC CVI 2024), the type 2 diabetes paper (n = 267, threefold higher progression in diabetes after adjustment) and the proteomics paper (n = 97, 8.5 y) — [PubMed 39152960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39152960/); [PubMed 41194148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41194148/); [PubMed 39658319](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39658319/)
- PARADIGM "plaque-free" 2026 paper: all 412 patients had baseline plaque; "plaque-free" means PVAT measured around plaque-free segments; the motivation is reverse causation from plaque-adjacent measurements — [PubMed 42900033](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42900033/)
- PARADIGM glycemic paper 2026: n = 1,296, median interval 3.2 y, diabetes but not prediabetes independently associated with rapid progression (PAV ≥ 1%/y) — [PubMed 42850638](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42850638/)
- Tantawy: an ESC 365 contributor page lists "New atherosclerotic plaque formation by coronary CTA in suspected coronary artery disease patients: Results from the PARADIGM Registry"; no full paper was found in PubMed (a "Tantawy PARADIGM" query returned no relevant record) — [ESC 365 profile](https://esc365.escardio.org/person/559810)
- A registered (recruiting) Chinese retrospective cohort, APPLE (NCT06498830), enrols only patients without plaque at baseline with ≥ 2 CCTAs, primary outcome any plaque formation; estimated n = 2,000 — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06498830](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06498830)

### Inferences
- Avigdor 2026 is the closest published analogue of the thesis outcome (plaque developing from normal coronaries over ~10 y). It holds a point no cited source holds: a plaque-free-baseline, ten-year incidence figure. Its weaknesses are exactly the ones the thesis argues against (referred patients, clinically indicated and selected repeat imaging, n = 31 with CCTA, mostly chest CT). That makes it a strong "nearest prior work" citation for `sec:lit-risk`. It is either a must add or a nice to have: must add if the review claims that no ten-year plaque-free-baseline study exists, since this paper partially contradicts an unqualified claim.
- Of the Dutch papers, the JAMA Cardiology 2024 Lp(a) paper is the citable full paper (highest cited, OA 109). Cite one paper for "a ten-year serial CCTA interval exists" and never the 2023 abstract. Under the one-source rule, pick #4, not #6–#8. Wording: "patients with suspected CAD", not "population".
- Chen 2024 and Lee 2024 (already cited) remain the only hospital plaque-free-baseline prediction studies found. Nothing in 2025–2026 superseded them.

### Gaps
- "CArTI": no study by that acronym was found in PubMed (the query returned only unrelated hits) or by web search. Its full name or a link is needed to check it. No full paper can be confirmed.
- Whether the Tantawy PARADIGM abstract has a full paper: none found as of 2026-11-08. The abstract's year and journal supplement were not confirmed.
- Whether the Amsterdam cohort has a published subgroup with zero plaque at baseline: not reported in any abstract read; full text not read.

## Which 2023–2026 papers test CCTA- or CAC-derived measures beyond SCORE2/PCE/PREVENT, and in which populations?

### Takeaway
The 2026 SCAPIS paper is now confirmed as a full article: Malmqvist et al., JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 2026, doi 10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004. In a random population sample, deep-learning total plaque volume raised the C-statistic over SCORE2 plus the segment involvement score by 0.015. It is the only 2023–2026 random-population CCTA paper found that formally tests a CCTA measure beyond SCORE2, and it is a "must add" because its small ΔC is the realistic benchmark for the thesis. No 2023–2026 paper found tests CAC or CCTA beyond PREVENT in MESA, UK Biobank or Miami Heart (a gap, see below).

### Cited Findings
- SCAPIS 2026: 23,314 randomly selected participants aged 50–64 without ASCVD, 287 coronary events (CHD death or MI) over median 7.8 y. Adding TPV to SCORE2 + SIS raised C from 0.783 to 0.798 (P = 0.022), NRI 0.093 (95% CI 0.041–0.146). HR 6.36 in the highest TPV category. Risk rose with TPV even with CAC = 0 and with no manually detected atherosclerosis. Adding stenosis ≥ 50% or noncalcified-only segments did not improve C — [PubMed 42663364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42663364/); [JACC CVI](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004)
- It was presented as an ESC Congress 2026 late-breaker with simultaneous publication — [ACC summary](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2026/08/24/12/30/sat-jacc-studies-esc-2026)
- SCAPIS Extremes 2025: of 10,628 non-diabetic and 189 diabetic eligible participants, no coronary plaque despite high SCORE2 risk in 1.2% (non-diabetic) and 14.0% (diabetic); severe-extensive plaque despite low SCORE2 risk in 0.7% and 0.3% — [PubMed 40644405](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40644405/)
- SCAPIS Lp(a) 2026 links Lp(a) to coronary atherosclerosis and events in the same cohort — [PubMed 42470409](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42470409/)
- Miami Heart 2026: 2,301 asymptomatic community participants, AI-QCT; median TPV 54 mm³, higher in men (80 vs 34 mm³); TPV ≥ 20 mm³ in 81.5% of men and 61.9% of women; no SCORE2/PCE comparison in the abstract — [PubMed 40920149](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40920149/)
- Amsterdam proteomics 2025 (referred, n = 97): AUC for accelerated PAV progression rose from 0.830 (risk factors + baseline plaque) to 0.909 with a protein panel (P = 0.023) — [PubMed 39658319](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39658319/)
- DANCAVAS II 2025: 31,268 Danish men aged 60–64 randomised 1:4 to invitation to CT-based screening (including CAC) vs none; all-cause mortality HR 0.94 (0.86–1.03) and MACE HR 0.96 over median 7.0 y; severe bleeding higher (HR 1.18) — [PubMed 40884758](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40884758/)
- Published commentary disputes how DANCAVAS results are represented (EHJ 2026 letters) — [PubMed 41563870](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41563870/); [PubMed 41563875](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41563875/)

### Inferences
- Malmqvist 2026 holds something no cited source holds: random population, CCTA plaque volume, SCORE2 as base, formal ΔC. Hageman 2023 holds "imaging/markers beyond SCORE2" generically; Bergström 2021 holds prevalence. Place Malmqvist in `sec:lit-risk` beside Bergström/Good. Caveats: it is three months old, uses commercial deep-learning software, the outcome is events not incident plaque, and the cohort includes people with plaque. The ΔC of 0.015 for a direct plaque measure is a useful expectation-setter for a geometry block on plaque-free baselines (expect smaller). The thesis estimates ΔC and does not test it, so cite the C values, not the P value, as the comparison.
- SCAPIS Extremes is a nice-to-have for one point: some high-SCORE2 people have no plaque and some low-SCORE2 people have extensive plaque, which motivates looking beyond SCORE2. It is cross-sectional and single-cohort, and Hageman may already carry the point.
- DANCAVAS II belongs in the Discussion only (screening RCT, events, non-contrast CAC). It is Danish and recent, so a Danish examiner may expect it in a sentence on imaging-based prevention, but it does not bear on geometry.
- Miami Heart (Ichikawa 2026; Nasir 2022 is pre-2023) adds normative plaque volumes in community volunteers; not needed under the one-source rule since SCAPIS and CGPS cover population prevalence.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 paper testing CAC or CCTA beyond the PREVENT equations was found in PubMed searches (MESA + PREVENT + CAC returned no direct incremental-value paper). Such papers may exist under other wording. The 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline is PREVENT-based and discusses CAC, but it was not read (see last section).
- UK Biobank has no CCTA plaque cohort in what was searched (only proteomic/GWAS imaging papers: [PubMed 42485930](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42485930/), [PubMed 40164586](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40164586/)).
- MESA CCTA papers 2023–2026 beyond CAC were not found.
- Full text of Malmqvist 2026 not read: how CAC = 0 participants were handled in the C-statistic models, and calibration results, are unknown.

## Any 2023–2026 CGPS / Copenhagen CCTA papers (Fuchs, Kühl, Kofoed, Nordestgaard) on plaque prevalence, risk factors or SCORE2?

### Takeaway
Beyond `fuchs2023cgps`, the main 2023–2026 CGPS coronary papers are Larsen et al. (EHJ 2026; sex differences in subclinical atherosclerosis and MI, n = 10,003) and the Danish–Spanish REACT study in NEJM 2026 (Bundgaard, Kofoed et al.; silent atherosclerosis prevalence across adult life). No CGPS paper relating CCTA findings to SCORE2 was found, and no CGPS serial (BL–FU) CCTA paper has been published as of 2026-11-08, so the thesis cohort's ten-year follow-up appears unpublished.

### Cited Findings
- Larsen 2026: 5,710 women and 4,293 men > 40 y from CGPS underwent CCTA; women had less subclinical atherosclerosis than men from 40 to > 80 y; median follow-up 6.0 y; adjusted HR for MI with vs without obstructive subclinical atherosclerosis 4.1 (1.5–11) in women and 12 (4.6–34) in men, interaction P = 0.24 — [PubMed 42583869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42583869/)
- Authors include Kühl, Sigvardsen, Afzal, Nordestgaard and Kofoed, with the CGPS (Herlev and Gentofte) affiliation listed — [PubMed 42583869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42583869/)
- REACT (NEJM 2026): 16,808 adults 18–70 y without known ASCVD in Denmark and Spain, enrolled in five age strata balanced by sex; 3-D carotid/femoral ultrasound plus CCTA; silent atherosclerosis in 57.1%; 8.7% of men and 6.7% of women aged 18–29; isolated coronary atherosclerosis uncommon (≤ 9.3% men, ≤ 5.0% women) — [PubMed 42670946](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42670946/)
- Other 2024–2026 CGPS CT papers from the same group are non-coronary (left atrium, aortic size and aneurysms, valve calcification, EAT and AF) — e.g. [PubMed 42309870](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42309870/), [PubMed 38662458](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38662458/), [PubMed 40742157](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40742157/)
- CGPS participants serve as population controls in a 2026 JACC liver-transplant CCTA study — [PubMed 41778959](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41778959/)
- `fuchs2023cgps`: OA 119 citations on 2026-11-08 — [OpenAlex via DOI 10.7326/M22-3027](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.7326/M22-3027)

### Inferences
- Under the one-source rule, `fuchs2023cgps` remains the CGPS citation for "CCTA findings predict MI in this population". Larsen 2026 adds only the sex-specific point (nice to have: could support a sentence on why `male` is in the base, but SCORE2 already holds sex). REACT is not CGPS proper, recruits by age stratum (not a random population sample), and is cross-sectional. It adds early-adult prevalence, which bears on the caveat that "plaque-free" at CCTA does not mean atherosclerosis-free. It is useful only if that caveat is cited in the review. Note that it is Danish (Rigshospitalet/Kofoed), so the supervisors may know it.
- The absence of a published CGPS serial-CCTA paper is itself a finding: the thesis can say that the ten-year re-scan has not yet been reported. That claim must be re-checked right before submission and confirmed with Kit/Kofoed.

### Gaps
- The search covered PubMed author combinations (Fuchs A + Kofoed KF; Fuchs A + Nordestgaard BG; Kühl JT). Conference abstracts from the CGPS group on the ten-year re-scan (e.g. ESC/SCCT 2025–2026) were not searched.
- No CGPS paper on SCORE2 versus CCTA plaque was found; if one exists under different wording it was missed.

## 2024–2026 guidelines or consensus documents relevant to CCTA in primary prevention

### Takeaway
The 2024 ESC chronic coronary syndromes guideline (Vrints et al.) supersedes `knuuti2019esc`, which the thesis currently cites for CCTA's clinical role, and should replace it: must add, swap not addition. The 2024 SCCT quantitative-CCTA standards are Methods-level at most. The 2025 ESC/EAS dyslipidaemia focused update and the 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline (PREVENT-based) exist but were not read. Whether they say anything about CCTA in primary prevention is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Vrints C et al. 2024 ESC Guidelines for the management of chronic coronary syndromes. Eur Heart J 2024;45(36):3415–3537, doi 10.1093/eurheartj/ehae177; OA 3,459 citations — [PubMed 39210710](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39210710/)
- Nieman K et al. Standards for quantitative assessments by CCTA: an SCCT expert consensus document. J Cardiovasc Comput Tomogr 2024;18(5):429–443, doi 10.1016/j.jcct.2024.05.232; OA 136 — [PubMed 38849237](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38849237/)
- QCI Study Group consensus statement on AI-based CCTA evaluation for individualized treatment of atherosclerosis, Nat Rev Cardiol 2026;23(2):100–115 — [PubMed 40751112](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40751112/)
- Mach F et al. 2025 Focused update of the 2019 ESC/EAS dyslipidaemia guidelines (erratum in Atherosclerosis 2026;415:120703 refers to the original in Atherosclerosis 409:120479) — [PubMed 41932222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41932222/)
- The 2026 ACC/AHA/Multisociety dyslipidemia guideline is described as PREVENT-based, with comparisons to the ESC/EAS update — [PubMed 42579320](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42579320/); [PubMed 42746420](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42746420/); CAC in ACC/AHA guidelines 2007–2026 editorial — [PubMed 42684218](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42684218/)

### Inferences
- The swap from `knuuti2019esc` to `vrints2024esc` is the single most defensible guideline change. A 2019 guideline cited in a 2026 thesis would read as stale. Its scope is symptomatic chronic coronary syndromes, so it should be cited for CCTA's clinical role, not for primary prevention.
- No 2024–2026 ESC or SCCT document recommending CCTA for asymptomatic primary prevention was found. That supports the framing that CCTA in asymptomatic people remains a research setting (SCAPIS, CGPS), but confirm it from the guideline text before writing it.

### Gaps
- The full text of Vrints 2024, the ESC/EAS 2025 update and the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline was not read. The exact recommendation class for CAC or CCTA in asymptomatic risk modification is not confirmed here. The primary DOI of the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline was not retrieved.
- No SCCT 2024–2026 document specifically on CCTA in primary prevention was found.
