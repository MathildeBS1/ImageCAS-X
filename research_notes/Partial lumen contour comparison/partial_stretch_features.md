# Comparing lumen features and lumen agreement over partial reference stretches (tracer vs Medis, CCTA)

Scope note: this search was budget-limited (about 20 tool calls). Several key full texts (Kirisli 2013, Voros 2011, Carstensen 2008) were paywalled or blocked, so their method details are listed under Gaps rather than reconstructed from memory. Statements under "Inferences" are reasoning, not cited facts.

## 1. How do lumen-segmentation and QCT validation studies compare lumen over a stretch?

### Takeaway
The published designs fall into three levels: (a) matched cross-section pairs after landmark co-registration, (b) fixed-length sub-segments (for example 5 mm) or anatomical segments that are averaged, and (c) lesion summaries (minimal lumen area, MLA). The CT segmentation benchmark adds overlap and surface metrics (Dice, mean and maximum surface distance), reported separately for healthy and diseased parts. Agreement is clearly worse in diseased parts and at the MLA, which matters because Medis contours exist only on diseased segments.

### Cited Findings
- The Rotterdam benchmark (Kirişli et al., Med Image Anal 2013;17(8):859–876) has 48 multicentre, multivendor CTA datasets with reference standards, compares algorithms from 11 groups, and concludes that "automatic lumen segmentation is possible with a precision similar to that obtained by experts". The lumen reference is expert manual annotation, the stenosis reference is QCA with one observer, and the CTA consensus reading used three observers. — [EUR repository abstract](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/41673); [search summary of the framework](https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/standardized-evaluation-framework-for-evaluating-coronary-artery-/)
- Papers using this framework report Dice, mean surface distance (MSD) and maximum surface distance (MAXSD) **separately for healthy and diseased vessel parts**. — [Automated segmentation based on statistical region growing, PMC5107877](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5107877) (seen only as a search snippet)
- One participating-method abstract reports Dice of 68% on healthy and 65% on diseased segments in one setting, and 67% vs 59% in another (42 datasets). Its distance metrics were also better on healthy segments. Flag: this is an individual method's abstract on ResearchGate, not the benchmark paper, and the snippet mixes two sets of numbers. — [ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255706811_Automatic_segmentation_detection_and_quantification_of_coronary_artery_stenoses_on_CTA)
- **Matched cross-sections plus fixed-length segments (CT vs IVUS):** Papadopoulou et al. (Atherosclerosis 2011;219:163–170) co-registered 64-slice CT and IVUS in 44 arteries from 32 patients with a fusion technique. They analysed **1,364 co-registered 1-mm cross-sections and 255 segments of 5 mm length**. Cross-section-level plaque detection was poorer than segment-level detection (sensitivity 86% vs 96%). Flag: the numbers are for plaque, not lumen area. — [EUR repository / search summary](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/detection-and-quantification-of-coronary-atherosclerotic-plaque-b/)
- **Point-pair comparison:** Luo et al. (PLoS ONE 2014;9:e86949) compared CT and IVUS lumen cross-sectional area (CSA) point pair by point pair: 1,314 pairs from 11 vessels in 5 patients. They used angiographic landmarks to co-register and to match IVUS start and end points to CT. Diameter was taken from a circle fitted to the CSA. Error was expressed as the percentage (A_IVUS − A_CT)/A_IVUS. Pooled results: diameter fit y = 0.97x + 0.057, normalized RMSE 9.5%; CSA fit y = 0.95x + 0.23, normalized RMSE 16.2%. Per vessel, mean CSA error was 7.9–11.2%. No Bland–Altman analysis was done, the effect of misregistration was not quantified, and the authors note length misalignment at the end points. — [PMC3906085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906085/)
- The same paper cites Voros et al. as reporting about 21% error in CT-reconstructed lumen area and a 39% overestimation of diameter stenosis versus IVUS. — [PMC3906085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906085/)
- **Patient, vessel and segment levels:** a 64-slice MDCT vs IVUS study compared average lumen area, minimal lumen area and minimal lumen diameter and found high correlation at all three levels. — [search result, Leiden repository](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3748464/view) (snippet only)
- **MLA-only comparisons have wide limits:** Caussin et al. 2006 (64-slice CT vs IVUS) reported MLA r = 0.88, but Bland–Altman 95% limits of −42% to +44%. Veselka et al. 2011 (dual-source CT, in-stent left main) reported r = 0.63, with CT underestimating MLA by 2.14 ± 2.24 mm². — [em-consulte record](https://em-consulte.com/article/411023/figures/comparison-of-coronary-minimal-lumen-area-quantifi); [Charles University listing](https://lf2.cuni.cz/en/faculty/major-publications/dual-source-ct-angiography-for-detection-and-quantification-of-in-stent)

### Inferences
- In both direct CT-vs-IVUS designs above, the comparison runs only over the stretch the reference covers (the IVUS pullback), with landmark co-registration and end-point matching. Restricting the comparison to the Medis-covered stretch is therefore the standard design, not a compromise.
- A defensible hierarchy for tracer vs Medis:
  1. Per matched cross-section, compare equivalent radius r = √(A/π). Match by arc length after aligning the stretch ends, or by nearest centerline point.
  2. Per stretch, compare the mean r, and the minimum r as an MLA analogue.
  3. Per stretch, compare taper (the slope of r against arc length).
  Optionally add surface metrics (mean and maximum contour distance) in the cross-sectional plane. MLA-type summaries should be expected to show the widest limits.
- All Medis stretches are diseased. Published Dice and distance values are poorer in diseased parts, so the tracer-vs-Medis agreement is a near worst-case estimate for the plaque-free cohort the thesis targets. State this directly; do not let the reader generalise it to healthy lumen.

### Gaps
- The full Kirisli 2013 text could not be retrieved, so its exact rules are unconfirmed: the region evaluated (start and end of the annotated segment, cropping perpendicular to the centerline), the vessel-diameter cut-off, and the exact numbers. I found no source confirming these, so I do not state them.
- The full Voros 2011 (JACC Cardiovasc Imaging) and Boogers 2012 QCT-vs-IVUS methods (segment matching by side branches, per-cross-section vs per-segment Bland–Altman) were not retrieved.

## 2. Should the test method's feature be recomputed on exactly the same stretch? Taper bias and variance over short segments; stenosis distorting taper

### Takeaway
I found no paper that tests this directly. The validation studies above all compare over the reference-covered stretch, so a like-for-like feature comparison requires recomputing the tracer feature on exactly the overlapping arc-length window. Taper is defined as a per-length rate (from IVUS, about 0.2–0.3 mm of diameter per 10 mm). It is small compared with CT lumen error, so a short stretch makes the slope noisy. Plaque inside the stretch also distorts any linear taper fit.

### Cited Findings
- TAG, the closest CT analogue of a slope-along-arc-length feature, is a linear regression of luminal HU on distance along the vessel. It is reported per 10 mm. Sampling ranges from 0.5 mm (Steigner) through 1 mm (automated) to 5 mm intervals, the last stopping distally where the lumen CSA drops below 2.0 mm². One 320-row study recomputed TAG with calcified lesions removed from the regression. — [PMC4104747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4104747); [PMC6002790](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6002790) (search snippets)
- Normal taper size: one source (secondhand) reports that about 89% of arteries taper significantly, by about **0.22 mm of diameter per 10 mm**. Another defines marked tapering as a fall of 10% or more in (proximal − distal)/proximal diameter over a **20 mm** segment. An IVUS study expressed LAD taper as a lumen diameter change of about 0.33 mm per 10 mm. — [e-century IJCEM](https://e-century.us/files/ijcem/9/3/ijcem0016899.pdf); [IVUS taper paper, Am J Cardiol](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0002-9149(00)80072-7) (snippets only, not verified in full text)
- Normal CT taper was measured at 1 cm intervals on curved planar reformats from the origin to the point where the lumen is 2 mm, in 526 adults with normal 64-row CTA (Chinese-language paper; regression details not seen). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221834308_Coronary_artery_lumen_diameter_and_bifurcation_angle_derived_from_CT_coronary_angiographic_image_in_healthy_people)
- Stenosis distorts taper-based reference estimates. Patent disclosures (not peer-reviewed) note two things. Ignoring taper biases percent stenosis and the remodeling index. A long plaque proximal to the stenosis can bias the proximal reference diameter, which is why the reference is interpolated from the diameters just before and just after the plaque. — [US patent 11276170](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11276170)
- At equal positions, CT lumen error versus IVUS is about 8–11% of CSA per vessel (normalized RMSE 16% for CSA, 9.5% for diameter). — [PMC3906085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906085/)

### Inferences
- **Recompute on the same window.** Taper depends on where it is measured: proximal segments taper more, and the stretch includes or excludes branch points and plaque. Comparing tracer taper over the full trunk with Medis taper over a sub-stretch would mix a measurement difference with a change in what is being measured. So compute both features on the intersection of the two arc-length ranges, after mapping the Medis stretch onto the tracer centerline.
- **Variance scales steeply with length.** For evenly spaced, independent cross-sections with residual SD σ along a stretch of length L at density n per mm, OLS slope variance ≈ 12σ²/(n·L³). This is a standard regression result, not taken from a coronary paper. Halving L multiplies the slope SD by about 2.8. Neighbouring tracer contours are autocorrelated, so the effective n is smaller than the number of steps, and naive standard errors are too small.
- **Signal-to-noise example (illustrative).** Taking taper ≈ 0.11 mm radius per 10 mm (half of 0.22 mm diameter) against a CT radius error of about 5% (about 0.08 mm at r = 1.5 mm): over a 10 mm stretch the expected radius change is about the size of one cross-section's error, so short-stretch taper is dominated by noise. Disagreement in taper over short Medis stretches is therefore expected even if both methods are unbiased. Report it as such, not as a tracer failure.
- **Stenosis inside the stretch** gives local dips in r that pull the fitted slope. Its effect on the slope depends on where the plaque sits: proximal plaque flattens or reverses taper, and distal plaque steepens it. Medis stretches are diseased by construction, so taper agreement on them tests segmentation agreement in diseased lumen. It does not show that the feature is valid for the plaque-free cohort. A robust slope (Theil–Sen) or a fit that excludes cross-sections near the MLA are reasonable sensitivity analyses, by analogy with TAG excluding calcified lesions.
- For diameter ratios (LM→LAD, LM→LCx), Medis must cover both the LM window and the proximal daughter window. Otherwise the ratio cannot be compared. Restrict to cases where both windows are covered, and report how many such cases there are.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that quantifies the bias or variance of taper as a function of segment length in CT. I also found no study comparing a taper feature between two segmentation methods.
- The IVUS taper values come from snippets or secondary citations. Check them against the primary paper before quoting numbers in the thesis.

## 3. Several disjoint reference stretches per artery, and repeated measures in Bland–Altman

### Takeaway
Treating every cross-section, or every stretch, as an independent pair makes the limits of agreement too narrow. Bland & Altman (2007) give a one-way ANOVA method for unequal numbers of pairs per subject when the true value varies within the subject (true for cross-sections along a vessel): the LoA variance is the within-subject plus the between-subject variance component. Mixed models (Carstensen et al. 2008) generalise this to nested levels such as person, stretch and cross-section.

### Cited Findings
- Bland & Altman, J Biopharm Stat 2007;17(4):571–582. The LoA method assumes independent observations. Ignoring replication and treating repeated pairs as independent "could be too narrow". Averaging per subject instead gives limits for an average of several measurements, which are too narrow for a single measurement. — [PubMed 17613642](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17613642/); [author PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/3411/1/Bland_2006_Agreement.pdf)
- **"True value varies" method (from the author PDF):** the difference is modelled as bias + random between-subject effect (heterogeneity) + within-subject error. Run a one-way ANOVA of the paired differences on subject. Within-subject variance = residual MS. Between-subject component = (MS_subjects − MS_residual)/D, where D = [(Σmᵢ)² − Σmᵢ²]/[(n−1)Σmᵢ], which reduces to m when every subject has the same count. LoA SD = √(within + between). The bias is the plain mean of all differences, which "automatically weights the observations correctly". The 1999 paper wrongly said to multiply by this factor instead of dividing. The method assumes the repeated differences within a subject are independent; the authors call this "rather strong" and suggest plotting differences against order to check for autocorrelation. — [author PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/3411/1/Bland_2006_Agreement.pdf)
- **"True value constant" case:** a separate method, in which any pairing of measurements is arbitrary. — [author PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/3411/1/Bland_2006_Agreement.pdf)
- Carstensen, Simpson & Gurrin, Int J Biostat 2008;4(1): state the variance-component model behind the classical LoA, extend it to replicates, give code, and distinguish exchangeable from linked replicates (Carstensen 2010 book). The methods are implemented in the R package MethComp. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/ijbist/v4y2008i1n16.html); [University of Melbourne record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/300386-statistical-models-for-assessing-agreement-in-method-comparison-studies-with-replicate-measurements)
- A later paper derives confidence intervals for LoA with multiple observations per individual and states that a fixed number of replicates per individual is not required. — [MetaTOC](https://metatoc.com/papers/14310-confidence-interval-estimation-for-the-bland-altman-limits-of-agreement-with-multiple-observations-per-individual)

### Inferences
- **Per-cross-section r agreement:** use the Bland–Altman 2007 "true value varies" method with person as the subject. Better, fit a mixed model of the difference on random intercepts for person and for stretch nested in person, and take the LoA SD as the square root of the summed variance components. Adjacent tracer steps are strongly autocorrelated, which breaks the within-subject independence assumption. Thin the cross-sections to about one per 1 mm, roughly the spacing of the 1-mm co-registered cross-sections in Papadopoulou 2011, or add an AR(1) residual structure, and report the spacing used.
- **Stretch-level features (mean r, min r, taper)** with several disjoint stretches per artery: the clearest unit is the stretch, with a person random effect, so the LoA reflects one stretch-level measurement. Pooling disjoint stretches into one artery-level taper fit is not advisable, because it fits a slope across uncovered gaps (and often across branch points). For an artery-level summary, use a length-weighted mean of per-stretch values. It weights in proportion to the precision of mean r; for slopes, weights ∝ L³ under the variance formula in section 2 would follow from inverse-variance weighting.
- Report the number of persons, stretches and cross-sections, and give the 2007 or mixed-model LoA next to the naive LoA, so readers can see how much clustering widens it.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the mixed-model LoA formulas from Carstensen 2008 or Parker et al. 2016 in full text. Only the abstract-level description is cited.
- I found no coronary imaging paper that applies the clustered (2007 or mixed-model) LoA to lumen cross-sections. Most cited CT-vs-IVUS studies pool point pairs or segments as if they were independent (for example, Luo 2014 pooled 1,314 pairs from 5 patients), so their agreement figures are probably too optimistic.

## 4. Minimum stretch length or number of cross-sections in published work

### Takeaway
I found no consensus minimum. The published units are 1-mm cross-sections and 5-mm segments (Papadopoulou 2011), 1 cm sampling for normal CT taper, a 20 mm window for defining marked taper, and a distal stop where the lumen is below 2.0 mm² CSA or 2 mm diameter for TAG and normal-taper measurement.

### Cited Findings
- 1-mm co-registered cross-sections and 5-mm segments in CT vs IVUS. — [EUR repository](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/detection-and-quantification-of-coronary-atherosclerotic-plaque-b/)
- TAG sampling of 0.5, 1 or 5 mm, with measurement stopping where CSA < 2.0 mm². — [PMC4104747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4104747); [PMC6002790](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6002790)
- Taper magnitude defined over 20 mm, with ≥10% counted as marked. — [e-century IJCEM](https://e-century.us/files/ijcem/9/3/ijcem0016899.pdf)
- Normal CT diameters measured every 1 cm down to a 2 mm lumen. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221834308_Coronary_artery_lumen_diameter_and_bifurcation_angle_derived_from_CT_coronary_angiographic_image_in_healthy_people)

### Inferences
- A prespecified minimum overlap for taper comparison is justifiable: for example, ≥ 10 mm and ≥ 10 cross-sections at about 1 mm spacing, with a sensitivity analysis at ≥ 20 mm (the published taper window). Point-wise r agreement needs no minimum. Report how many stretches each threshold excludes. Fix the threshold before looking at the agreement results.
- Excluding cross-sections with very small lumen (for example r below about 0.8 mm, i.e. CSA below 2 mm²) follows the TAG and normal-taper practice, and avoids the partial-volume region where both methods are unreliable.

### Gaps
- I found no published minimum segment length for estimating taper or slope reliably in CT, and no reliability study (ICC versus stretch length) for coronary taper.
