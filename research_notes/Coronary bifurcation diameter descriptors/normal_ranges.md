# Normal coronary diameters around the major bifurcations, and their determinants

Scope note: all numbers below are copied from the cited source pages (abstract, full text or the full-text PDF where it could be retrieved). Several primary full texts (Dodge 1992 PDF, the JCCT 2017 MDCT volume paper, Dickerson 2010) were paywalled or blocked, so only their abstract-level numbers are reported. Coefficients of variation (CV) marked "computed" are my arithmetic on the published mean and SD, not a published value.

## 1. Normal segment diameters (LM, pLAD, pLCx, D1, OM1, RCA, PDA) by modality and sex

### Takeaway
Across modalities, healthy-artery lumen diameters cluster at roughly LM 3.5 to 4.5 mm, proximal LAD 3.0 to 3.8 mm, proximal LCx 2.9 to 3.5 mm, proximal RCA 3.2 to 3.9 mm, and diagonal about 1.7 to 1.8 mm. CT lumen values from zero-calcium cohorts (LM 3.3 to 4.0 mm) sit below the classic angiographic Dodge values (LM 4.5 mm), and IVUS vessel (EEM) diameters sit well above both (LM about 5.0 mm), so modality and lumen-vs-vessel must be matched before any sanity check.

### Cited Findings

**Angiography (QCA), lumen**
- Dodge 1992: 83 arteriograms selected from 9,160 consecutive studies for smooth lumen borders (no visible atherosclerosis). LM 4.5 ± 0.5 mm, proximal LAD 3.7 ± 0.4 mm, distal LAD 1.9 ± 0.4 mm. Women had smaller epicardial diameters than men (-9%, p < 0.001), still significant after BSA normalisation (p < 0.01). — [Dodge et al., Circulation 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232); [PubMed 1535570](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1535570/)
- Dodge 1992, dominance: LAD diameter was not affected by dominance, but proximal RCA varied between 3.9 ± 0.6 and 2.8 ± 0.5 mm, and LCx between 3.4 ± 0.5 and 4.2 ± 0.6 mm, depending on dominance (p < 0.01) (the larger RCA / smaller LCx in right dominance and vice versa). — [Dodge et al., Circulation 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- Normal Indians (QCA), n = 229 (121 M, 108 F), age 51.7 ± 9.4 y, normal angiograms, excluding diabetes, hypertension, renal disease, cardiomyopathy: LM 4.08 ± 0.44, pLAD 3.27 ± 0.23, pLCx 2.97 ± 0.37, pRCA 3.20 ± 0.37 mm. BSA-indexed (mm/m²): LM 2.34 ± 0.28, pLAD 1.87 ± 0.21, pLCx 1.70 ± 0.23, pRCA 1.83 ± 0.22. Dominance: right 59.8%, co-dominant 23.1%, left 17.0%. — [Coronary artery dimensions in normal Indians, Indian Heart J 2017 (PMC5560873)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5560873)
- South/West Indian QCA, n = 454 (253 M, 201 F), normal to non-flow-limiting coronaries, age 53.4 ± 14.2 y. Males vs females: LM 4.29 ± 0.76 vs 3.96 ± 0.73 (p = 0.004); pLAD 3.24 ± 0.52 vs 3.10 ± 0.58; diagonal 1.83 ± 0.43 vs 1.65 ± 0.41 (p = 0.001); LCx ostium 3.25 ± 0.63 vs 2.96 ± 0.65; pLCx 3.11 ± 0.68 vs 2.81 ± 0.61 (p < 0.001); RCA ostium 3.32 ± 0.73 vs 3.02 ± 0.73 mm. No BSA indexing. — [Gender-associated dimensional differences, Indian Heart J 2018 (PMC6309284)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6309284/)
- South Indian QCA, n = 453 (257 M, 196 F), age 54.7 ± 10.7 y, clinically normal angiograms: overall LM 3.59 ± 0.58, LAD 3.50 ± 0.62, LCx 3.31 ± 0.57, RCA 3.18 ± 0.57 mm; males LM 3.70 ± 0.60, LAD 3.54 ± 0.48, LCx 3.36 ± 0.58, RCA 3.25 ± 0.62; females LM 3.45 ± 0.53, LAD 3.46 ± 0.55, LCx 3.24 ± 0.54, RCA 3.08 ± 0.59 mm. Not BSA-adjusted. — [South Indian caliber study (PMC10811496)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10811496/)

**CT angiography, lumen**
- Computational atlas of normal coronary anatomy (Medrano-Gracia et al., EuroIntervention 2016): 300 adults, zero calcium score and no stenoses, 64% female, GE LightSpeed 64. Effective diameter from lumen cross-sectional area perpendicular to centreline. LM ostium 3.7 ± 0.8 mm (another table: 3.5 ± 0.8 mm), IQR 3.2 to 4.1 mm; LCx 3.1 ± 0.7 mm; LAD tapers roughly linearly by about 0.25 mm per 10 mm; RCA first widens then tapers monotonically. LM length 10.5 ± 5.3 mm. — [Medrano-Gracia et al., EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- LM bifurcation CT study (Auckland/UNSW; patients referred with suspected CAD, excluded if any plaque or non-zero calcium), n = 127 (42 M, 85 F, age 38 to 81): median equivalent-area diameter over the first 10 mm of each branch. Males vs females: LM 4.02 ± 0.52 vs 3.33 ± 0.61; LAD 3.62 ± 0.50 vs 3.01 ± 0.50; LCx 3.51 ± 0.53 vs 2.88 ± 0.57 mm (all p < 0.001; difference 0.61 to 0.69 mm). — [Sex-specific variances in LM bifurcation, arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)
- Endurance athletes vs controls, CCTA, all Caucasian; controls n = 47, age 57.7 ± 13, 22% female. Controls: LM 4.5 ± 0.81 mm, pLAD 3.54 ± 0.67 mm, pLCx 3.07 ± 0.77 mm, mid LCx 2.17 ± 0.73 mm, pRCA 3.43 ± 0.56 mm, mid RCA 3.19 ± 0.67 mm (right-dominant/intermediate only). — [Coronary dimensions in endurance athletes by CTA (PMC8620029)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8620029/)
- Normal Indians by 128-slice CTA (end-diastole): unadjusted proximal diameters smaller than Caucasians; no significant difference once indexed to BSA. — [Coronary dimensions in normal adult Indians by CTCA, J Indian Coll Cardiol 2022](https://journals.lww.com/jicc/fulltext/2022/12030/coronary_artery_dimensions_in_normal_adult_indian.5.aspx)
- MDCT volumetric analysis, 50 women and 44 men without CAD: unadjusted CSA smaller in women in proximal LAD (p = 0.01) and proximal RCA (p = 0.002), no significant difference in other segments; the sex effect on proximal LAD and RCA persisted after adjustment for BSA and LV mass (abstract-level). — [Dickerson et al., Clin Cardiol 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/clc.20509); [PubMed 20043337](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20043337/)

**IVUS, vessel (EEM) diameters**
- Indonesian IVUS, 153 chronic coronary syndrome patients, 633 reference segments with < 50% plaque area, 75.8% male, BSA 1.71 ± 0.18 m². EEM diameter: LM 5.02 ± 0.43, pLAD 4.25 ± 0.42, mid LAD 3.86 ± 0.39, distal LAD 3.32 (IQR 2.83 to 4.30), pLCx 3.91 ± 0.42, distal LCx 3.51 ± 0.47, pRCA 4.50 ± 0.48, mid RCA 4.16 ± 0.42, distal RCA 3.81 ± 0.41 mm. LM EEM CSA 19.93 ± 3.48 mm², compared with 24.24 ± 6.29 mm² in a White cohort (Sheifer) and 25.11 ± 6.43 mm² in an Indian cohort (Reddy). — [Coronary artery size by IVUS in Southeast Asia (PMC11341788)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11341788/)

### Inferences
- A working sanity band for ImageCAS-X / CGPS CT lumen diameters (equivalent-area diameter, 10 mm around the carina): LM about 3.0 to 4.6 mm, pLAD about 2.5 to 4.2 mm, pLCx about 2.3 to 4.0 mm, mean ± 2 SD of the CT zero-calcium cohorts above. Values outside roughly 2 to 6 mm for LM are almost certainly extraction errors.
- CT effective-lumen diameters are systematically smaller than Dodge's QCA values (LM 3.3 to 4.0 vs 4.5 mm). Plausible reasons: Dodge selected across sexes with nitroglycerin-era QCA and measured at the widest smooth segment, while CT partial-volume and segmentation thresholds shrink small lumens. The thesis should compare against CT references first and treat Dodge as an upper anchor.
- IVUS EEM is a vessel (outer elastic) measure and must not be compared with CT lumen output.

### Gaps
- No CT paper found reporting D1, OM1, distal RCA or PDA diameters separately in a healthy, sex-stratified cohort; diagonal only from Indian QCA (1.65 to 1.83 mm). Dodge's full table (D1, OM, PDA, dominance strata) is in the paywalled PDF, not retrieved.
- No MESA or CONFIRM paper reporting normal coronary diameters was found; CONFIRM output located concerned plaque and outcomes, not dimensions.
- No Chinese/East Asian healthy-volunteer CTA diameter reference was located.

## 2. Bifurcation laws in normal subjects: Finet ratio, Murray exponent, LCx/LAD daughter ratio

### Takeaway
The Finet ratio in the original QCA/IVUS work is 0.678; a normal CT atlas finds 0.658 ± 0.083, and a CT study using 10 mm median diameters at the LM finds 0.57 in both sexes. The pooled diameter exponent is about 2.4 (closer to Huo-Kassab's 7/3 than Murray's 3), with no difference between healthy and diseased trees. Measured values depend strongly on where diameters are sampled.

### Cited Findings
- Finet 2008: 59 patients (35 F, 24 M, age 46 ± 8.5 y), 173 bifurcations by QCA and IVUS. Mother 3.33 ± 0.94, major daughter 2.70 ± 0.77, minor daughter 2.23 ± 0.68 mm. Dm = 0.678 (Dd1 + Dd2), held at all levels regardless of mother size. — [Finet et al., EuroIntervention 2008](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/fractal-geometry-of-arterial-coronary-bifurcations-a-quantitative-coronary-angiography-and-intravascular-ultrasound-analysis)
- Normal CT atlas (300 zero-calcium adults): Finet ratio 0.6576 ± 0.083; best-fitting Huo-Kassab exponent 2.4. — [Medrano-Gracia et al., EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- LM-only CT study (127 zero-calcium, plaque-free): Finet ratio 0.57 ± 0.02 (M) vs 0.57 ± 0.04 (F), p = 0.984. Daughter means imply LCx/LAD ≈ 0.97 (M: 3.51/3.62) and 0.96 (F: 2.88/3.01). — [arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)
- Huo-Kassab: coronary casts obey the HK 7/3 diameter law and its angle rule much more than Murray's cube law. — [Huo et al., 2012, PubMed 22365499](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22365499/)
- Meta-analysis (18 studies, 1,070 trees, 372 human): pooled exponent 2.39 (95% CI 2.24 to 2.54); humans 2.42; epicardial 2.43; healthy 2.38 (2.19 to 2.56) vs CVD 2.29 (2.10 to 2.49), not different; I² = 99%. Using 3.0 underestimates MLA targets by up to 20%. — [Systematic review of Murray's law in coronaries (PMC11380967)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11380967/)
- Myocardial mass subtended scales as M ∝ D^(8/3); side-branch territory fraction = (D_SB/D_MA)^(8/3). — [Kassab/Finet review, EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomy-and-function-relation-in-the-coronary-tree-from-bifurcations-to-myocardial-flow-and-mass)

### Inferences
- With the atlas SD of 0.083, a healthy Finet ratio of about 0.50 to 0.82 (±2 SD) is a reasonable acceptance band; the 0.57 vs 0.66 vs 0.68 spread across studies is larger than the sex effect and likely reflects measurement protocol (distance from carina, median over 10 mm vs single cross-section), so the thesis should fix and report its sampling distance.
- Dodge's dominance numbers imply LCx/LAD at the LM is higher in left dominance (LCx up to 4.2 mm vs LAD 3.7 mm) than in right dominance, so LCx/LAD must be modelled with dominance as a covariate.

### Gaps
- No study found reporting the LCx/LAD daughter ratio explicitly as a normal-range statistic stratified by dominance; only indirect values above.
- No healthy-cohort Finet or exponent values found specifically for LAD-D1, LCx-OM1 or the RCA crux.

## 3. Determinants: sex, age, BSA, LV mass, dominance, ethnicity, training, hypertension

### Takeaway
Sex (men 9% to 20% larger), body size, LV mass/hypertrophy, dominance (for LCx and RCA) and endurance training are the established determinants. Age has little effect in most cohorts. Whether sex survives BSA indexing is contested: yes in Dodge, the LM CT study and Dickerson; no in the Indian QCA cohort. Ethnic differences largely disappear after BSA indexing.

### Cited Findings
- Sex: -9% in women (p < 0.001), persists after BSA normalisation (p < 0.01). — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- Sex (CT, LM bifurcation): men 0.61 to 0.69 mm larger (about 17% to 21%), remained significant after adjusting for BMI (p < 0.001) and BSA (p = 0.001) in multivariable models. — [arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)
- Sex does not survive BSA indexing in normal Indians (p > 0.05). — [PMC5560873](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5560873)
- Indonesian IVUS: BSA independently predicted most artery sizes except LCx; after BSA adjustment female sex still predicted smaller distal LCx (β -0.453, p = 0.022). — [PMC11341788](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11341788/)
- Sex differences in coronary size are not explained by body habitus or LV mass (Hiteshi et al. 2014, per search summary); coronary size per 100 g LV mass differs by sex in a population without cardiac disease. — [PMC6309284 reference list/summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6309284/); [PubMed 11820072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11820072)
- BMI not significantly related to arterial diameters in the normal CT atlas; LM diameters differed by sex (p < 0.001). — [Medrano-Gracia et al.](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- Age: no effect on lumen diameter (Dodge). In South Indian QCA, female LM increased with age (20 to 45 y: 3.22 ± 0.41; 46 to 70 y: 3.46 ± 0.54; > 70 y: 3.87 ± 0.69 mm; p = 0.004), no age effect in men. — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232); [PMC10811496](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10811496/)
- LV hypertrophy / dilation: +17% (LVH) and +12% (dilated cardiomyopathy) lumen diameter in men (p < 0.001). — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- Dominance: RCA 3.9 ± 0.6 vs 2.8 ± 0.5 mm, LCx 3.4 ± 0.5 vs 4.2 ± 0.6 mm across dominance patterns; LAD unaffected. — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232). CT prevalence: right 84.9%, left 11.3% in 1,000 patients. — [Coronary dominance on CCTA (PMC13363422)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13363422/)
- Ethnicity: Indians smaller than Caucasians unadjusted, no difference after BSA indexing (QCA and CTA). South Asians vs Caucasians QCA comparison also exists. — [PMC5560873](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5560873); [JICC 2022](https://journals.lww.com/jicc/fulltext/2022/12030/coronary_artery_dimensions_in_normal_adult_indian.5.aspx); [PMC3253512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3253512/)
- Endurance training (CT): athletes vs matched controls, pLAD 3.84 ± 0.67 vs 3.54 ± 0.67 mm (p = 0.019), pLCx 3.46 vs 3.07 (p = 0.018), mid LCx 2.59 vs 2.17 (p = 0.008), mid RCA 3.56 vs 3.19 (p = 0.028); LM 4.6 vs 4.5 mm (p = 0.81, n.s.); myocardial mass 134 vs 123 g (p = 0.047); BSA equal (1.89 vs 1.94 m²). — [PMC8620029](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8620029/)
- Hypertension: search summaries report LM lumen area not increased in hypertensive LVH; evidence is described as complex and mixed. — [search summary, cf. PMC3305011](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3305011/) (not verified in full text)

### Inferences
- Minimum covariate set for CGPS: sex, BSA (height and weight), dominance (for LCx, OM1, RCA and LCx/LAD), plus LV mass where available. Age can be included but expected effect is small. Self-reported physical activity is a secondary candidate given about 10% larger mid-vessel diameters in athletes.
- Ratios (Finet, LCx/LAD, daughter/mother) are expected to be much less sex- and size-dependent than absolute diameters (Finet identical by sex in the CT LM study), which makes them attractive as between-person descriptors.

### Gaps
- No effect size for hypertension on coronary lumen diameter in a healthy CT population was verified.
- MESA-based coronary diameter determinants not found.
- Few studies quantify age effects in CT with multivariable adjustment.

## 4. Variability within healthy populations (CV)

### Takeaway
Absolute diameters vary by roughly 10% to 25% (CV) between healthy people, depending on modality and whether sexes are pooled; ratio descriptors vary less (Finet CV about 4% to 13%).

### Cited Findings
Computed from published mean and SD (source in each line):
- Dodge QCA: LM 0.5/4.5 = 11%, pLAD 0.4/3.7 = 11%, distal LAD 0.4/1.9 = 21%. — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- CT atlas: LM 0.8/3.7 = 22%, LCx 0.7/3.1 = 23%; Finet 0.083/0.658 = 13%; LM length 5.3/10.5 = 50%. — [Medrano-Gracia et al.](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- CT LM study, within sex: LM 13% (M) / 18% (F), LAD 14% / 17%, LCx 15% / 20%; Finet 3.5% / 7%. — [arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)
- CT controls: pLAD 19%, pLCx 25%, pRCA 16%. — [PMC8620029](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8620029/)
- IVUS EEM: LM 9%, pLAD 10%, pRCA 11%. — [PMC11341788](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11341788/)
- Indian QCA BSA-indexed: LM 12%, pLAD 11%, pLCx 14%, pRCA 12%. — [PMC5560873](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5560873)
- Measured CT diameter varies little along a 10 mm segment (SD about 0.1 mm), justifying a median. — [arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)

### Inferences
- A between-person CV of 10% to 20% against a CT measurement error likely around 0.1 to 0.3 mm (about 3% to 9% at 3.5 mm) means absolute LM/pLAD diameters can discriminate between people, but distal and side-branch diameters (D1 about 1.7 mm) will be dominated by measurement noise at 0.5 mm voxel spacing.
- The very small Finet SD in one study (0.02) vs 0.083 in another suggests protocol-driven variance; reproducibility testing on ImageCAS-X is needed before claiming a ratio discriminates.

### Gaps
- No published CT test-retest or inter-observer CV for bifurcation diameters in healthy subjects was retrieved.

## 5. Bifurcation core behaviour: flaring, carina, LM length

### Takeaway
The lumen is locally widened inside the bifurcation (polygon of confluence), so diameters must be sampled outside the core. LM length is highly variable (10.5 ± 5.3 mm) and LM bifurcation angle is about 80° (wider with an intermediate branch and in men).

### Cited Findings
- At a bifurcation the effective radius briefly increases within the polygon of confluence (where the three centrelines meet); an ellipse fitted there yields two diameters. Standard CT bifurcation analysis samples the main branch 5 mm proximal to the carina, at the carina and 5 mm distal. — [MSCT vs IVUS in true bifurcations (PMC10657987)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10657987/); [EBC QCA consensus, EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/quantitative-angiography-methods-for-bifurcation-lesions-a-consensus-statement-update-from-the-european-bifurcation-club)
- CT atlas: LM length 10.5 ± 5.3 mm; LM bifurcation angle 89° ± 21° with intermediate artery vs 75° ± 23° without (p < 0.001); other major bifurcations about 50°; males 85° vs females 74° (p < 0.001). — [Medrano-Gracia et al.](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- LM CT study: no sex difference in bifurcation angles; inflow angle 12.9° ± 11.2° (M) vs 18.5° ± 15.9° (F), p = 0.025. — [arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)
- The original Finet data showed the 0.678 ratio held regardless of mother diameter. — [Finet 2008](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/fractal-geometry-of-arterial-coronary-bifurcations-a-quantitative-coronary-angiography-and-intravascular-ultrasound-analysis)

### Inferences
- With LM length as short as about 5 mm at -1 SD, a "before bifurcation" LM sample at a fixed 5 mm proximal to the carina will sometimes fall in the ostium or flare zone; sampling should be relative to LM length (e.g., mid-LM) and presence of a ramus should be recorded.
- The 0.57 LM Finet ratio (median over 10 mm daughters) vs 0.66 to 0.68 elsewhere is consistent with mother/daughter sampling distance mattering.

### Gaps
- No quantitative normal value for the magnitude of flaring (percent increase of area in the polygon of confluence) in healthy CT was found.
- No healthy-cohort study found on the influence of LM length on LM diameter or Finet ratio.
