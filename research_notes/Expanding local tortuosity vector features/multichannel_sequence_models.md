# Multi-channel vs single-channel sequence/profile inputs for vascular geometry deep learning

## Are there published studies feeding a per-point sequence of MULTIPLE geometric descriptors (curvature + torsion + radius + angle, or Frenet-frame quantities) into a sequence model to predict a clinical/hemodynamic outcome, and what single- vs multi-channel deltas were reported?

### Takeaway
The one directly relevant coronary study (the thesis's own anchor, Tello Ayala et al., PMC13308244) uses a **single-channel** local-tortuosity profile, not a multi-channel one, so it cannot itself answer the multi- vs single-channel question; its 0.67-vs-0.60 delta is single-channel-sequence vs single-scalar-summary, a different axis. The closest genuine multi-channel-per-point examples found are (a) a non-sequence random-forest study on general vessel tortuosity that combines curvature+torsion+length-normalized variants and shows combining descriptors beats any one alone but with sharply diminishing and even reversing returns past ~3-6 features, and (b) a described-but-underdocumented recurrent model (DeepVessel-FFR) that feeds per-centerline-node hand-crafted vessel-geometry features through a bidirectional RNN to regress FFR. Direct sequence-model (1D-CNN/RNN/transformer) coronary or cerebrovascular studies that isolate a single-channel-curvature-only condition against a curvature+torsion+diameter+angle multi-channel condition, with a reported AUC delta, were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Tello Ayala et al. (PMC13308244 / ScienceDirect S2772963X26002504): in a cohort of 38,691 coronary angiograms (38,681 given in one summary; 22,334 patients, mean age 64.7±12.5, 37% female), a "local-attention neural network transformer" operating on "the ordered vector of local tortuosity along the artery" plus age and sex achieved AUROC 0.67 (95% CI 0.65-0.69) for RCA CAD prediction, versus AUROC 0.60 (95% CI 0.58-0.63) for a logistic regression on mean tortuosity + age + sex — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772963X26002504). This is single-channel (curvature/tortuosity only) on both sides of the comparison; the delta demonstrated is "full profile vs scalar summary," not "single-channel vs multi-channel."
- Full text of PMC13308244 could not be retrieved (bot-check wall on PMC, 403 on ScienceDirect and on the EuropePMC mirror), so the architecture's sequence length, exact tortuosity definition, and whether the authors discuss adding torsion/diameter/angle as future work could not be confirmed beyond the abstract-level description above — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/).
- A hybrid vessel-morphology classification study (arXiv 2607.14195, 379 valid internal-carotid ICA-C1 vessel segments from 193 subjects, Beijing Chaoyang Hospital) computed 13 curvature/torsion-derived features (Tortuosity Index, Total/Average Curvature, Total/Average Absolute Torsion, and length-normalized and ratio variants) and fed them to a random forest (not a sequence model). Using Tortuosity Index alone gave Macro-F1 of 0.815 (binary) / 0.819 (ternary); combining 6 selected features (after pruning 7 redundant ones via |Spearman ρ|>0.95 correlation-based elimination) raised Macro-F1 to 0.921 (binary) / 0.863 (ternary) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14195).
- The same study found a **non-monotonic** relationship between feature count and accuracy: a backward-ablation search found the binary-task optimum at 3 features (Macro-F1 0.926, better than the 6-feature baseline's 0.921) and the ternary-task optimum at 5 features (0.870 vs 0.863 at 6) — i.e. past a small number of complementary channels, additional geometric channels stopped helping and occasionally hurt slightly — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14195).
- The same authors argue mathematically (via phantom curves with identical Tortuosity Index but different shapes) that curvature and torsion carry genuinely complementary, non-redundant information relative to simple arc-length/chord-length tortuosity indices — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14195).
- DeepVessel-FFR (Wang et al., as summarized in a 2024 review, arXiv 2405.12224) encodes "hand-crafted features describing vessel geometry" (lesion characteristics plus proximal/distal markers, i.e. multiple geometric descriptors per centerline node) with a multilevel neural-network (MLNN) encoder, then runs a bi-directional recurrent neural network over the resulting per-node embeddings to regress FFR upstream/downstream along the vessel, reporting a correlation of 0.686 with invasively measured FFR. The review explicitly flags that the exact hand-crafted feature set is not fully documented, limiting reproducibility — [arXiv review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12224).
- General retinal-vessel cardiovascular-risk literature repeatedly lists caliber/diameter, tortuosity, branching angle, and fractal dimension together as the standard multi-descriptor geometric feature set associated with cardiovascular risk, but the specific architecture and single- vs multi-channel ablation for a "hierarchical attention" retinal model (PMC13330239) could not be verified because its full text was also behind a bot-check wall; only the search-engine summary claiming it "attends to vessel morphological descriptors such as diameter, tortuosity, and bifurcation angle" for "multi-level interpretability" was available and is unverified beyond that snippet — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13330239/), [narrative review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10011267/).
- Cerebral-aneurysm rupture-risk work (GN-Net, 2026, Interdisciplinary Sciences: Computational Life Sciences, 423 patients) fuses a "geometric branch" (geometric deep learning over local vessel/aneurysm geometric structure) with a separate "neighborhood-aware branch" (3D CNN + transformer over surrounding anatomical context), reporting 91.46% accuracy and outperforming prior single-branch state-of-the-art, and improving clinician accuracy by ~7% when used as decision support — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12539-025-00785-5), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41331222/). This is evidence that fusing a geometric descriptor stream with a second, different information stream helps, but it is not a per-point curvature+torsion+diameter+angle profile fed into one sequence model — it is two architecturally separate branches over different data types (local shape vs. surrounding context), fused later.

### Inferences
- Across the vascular-geometry literature found, "multi-channel beats single-channel" evidence exists most cleanly for non-sequence classifiers (random forest on hand-engineered curvature+torsion summary statistics) rather than for the specific 1D-CNN/RNN/transformer-per-point-profile architecture the thesis is considering; the sequence-model evidence base for this exact comparison is thin.
- Where a clean ablation exists (arXiv 2607.14195), the pattern is: complementary channels (curvature and torsion, which the authors show are not mathematically redundant despite sometimes correlating) each contribute early, but the contribution saturates and can reverse past a small handful of channels, and redundant/correlated channels should be pruned rather than concatenated wholesale.
- The Tello Ayala result the thesis cites as its baseline justification (transformer beats logistic-regression-on-mean) says nothing about channel count; it validates "read the whole profile, not just its mean," which is a completely separate design axis from "add more channels per point." Citing it as support for multi-channel expansion would be a category error unless the full text (unavailable here) shows they also tested multi-channel inputs.

### Gaps
- No coronary- or cerebrovascular-specific sequence model (1D-CNN, RNN, transformer, or GNN) was found that ran a controlled single-channel-curvature-only vs multi-channel (curvature+torsion+diameter+angle) ablation and reported an AUC/accuracy delta. This is the single most direct question asked and it remains open in the literature searched.
- Full text of the anchor paper (PMC13308244) was unreachable (bot-check/403 on all three access routes tried: PMC, ScienceDirect, EuropePMC), so it is unconfirmed whether the authors discuss extending beyond curvature/tortuosity as future work, and what exact sequence length / positional encoding / age-sex fusion mechanism the transformer uses.
- The retinal "hierarchical attention over diameter, tortuosity, bifurcation angle" claim (PMC13330239) is sourced only from a search-engine summary, not verified full text; treat as low-confidence until independently confirmed.
- DeepVessel-FFR's exact channel list is explicitly stated (by the reviewing paper) to be underdocumented in the original source, so it cannot be used to reason about which specific channels drove its 0.686 correlation.

## What does time-series/sequence deep learning more broadly say about when adding channels/features per timestep helps vs hurts small models, especially with limited samples?

### Takeaway
The general multivariate-time-series literature is dominated by large industrial/sensor benchmarks (UEA archive) where the empirical finding is that many real multivariate datasets contain redundant or irrelevant channels: dedicated channel-selection methods can drop 60-70% of channels with no loss (sometimes a gain) in accuracy, and channel-selection research explicitly treats "channel count" as a resource/noise trade-off rather than something monotonically good. The clearest single-domain analogy with matched single- vs multi-channel accuracy numbers is 12-lead vs single-lead ECG, where multi-lead consistently outperforms single-lead on classification accuracy, because the extra leads are different anatomical projections of the same electrical event, i.e. genuinely complementary rather than redundant.

### Cited Findings
- On the UEA/UCR archive (128 univariate + 30 multivariate datasets), multivariate time series classification (MTSC) has received comparatively less methodological attention than univariate, and reported findings note dynamic time warping remains competitive on multivariate tasks in a way it is not on univariate ones, indicating multivariate structure changes what strategies work rather than uniformly making bigger-input approaches win — [arXiv 2007.13156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13156).
- TSelect (channel selection for MTSC) eliminates on average 62% of channels in benchmark datasets while maintaining classification performance, and channel-selection preprocessing broadly is reported to save ~70% of computation time and data storage "with preserved accuracy" — i.e. most channels in typical multivariate benchmarks are not contributing net signal — [Springer/TSelect](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-025-01132-4), [Fast Channel Selection](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-91445-5_3).
- The rationale given for channel selection: "not all channels may be necessary as some could be irrelevant or redundant, and including these can increase run time while yielding no benefit in terms of predictive performance" — [arXiv 2206.09274 / Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-022-00909-1).
- In ECG deep learning, models trained on single-lead ECG show lower classification performance than models using all 12 leads simultaneously, described as a "clear advantage for multi-lead approaches in terms of classification accuracy" — [PMC12431083](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12431083/), [PMC erratum CNN 12-lead vs single-lead](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10973542/).
- Conversely, single-lead approaches remain attractive for portability/cost/scalability despite the accuracy gap (e.g., a VGG16 single-lead model on lead V4 reported F1 0.9811 with 4.2 ms inference), i.e. the accuracy delta from more channels is real but is weighed against deployment cost in practice, not treated as free — [PMC11817768](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11817768/).
- MLBF-Net (arXiv 2008.07263) is a multi-lead-branch fusion architecture for 12-lead ECG arrhythmia classification: each lead gets its own branch before fusion, rather than naive early concatenation of all 12 channels into one encoder — an implicit acknowledgment that treating leads/channels identically and fusing them naively is not assumed to be optimal even when more channels genuinely help — [arXiv 2008.07263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.07263).
- General clinical ML literature on small-sample, high-feature-count regimes: "the curse of dimensionality occurs when the performance of machine learning models deteriorates due to the presence of noisy, redundant, or irrelevant features," and in biological/clinical data specifically, "most features are highly correlated and some features are not always directly connected with disease explanation," producing "a high-dimensional space composed of many redundant and non-informative features that can mislead the algorithm training" — [Frontiers review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioinformatics/articles/10.3389/fbinf.2022.927312/full).
- The same review notes univariate feature-relevance methods "cannot detect feature redundancy, or interactions," implying that whether an added channel helps depends on joint (not marginal) information content relative to channels already present — [Frontiers review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioinformatics/articles/10.3389/fbinf.2022.927312/full).

### Inferences
- The clearest transferable pattern across both the ECG and the general MTSC literature is: additional channels help reliably when they are different physical/informational sensors of the same underlying process (12 ECG leads = 12 different spatial projections of the same cardiac electrical event; genuinely low mutual redundancy), and additional channels help unreliably, or must be pruned, when they are derived quantities that partially encode the same shape information (as in the UEA benchmarks that yield to 60-70% channel removal, and as curvature/torsion/tortuosity-index redundancy showed in the vessel-morphology RF study above).
- Applied to a curvature+torsion+branch-angle+diameter local feature vector: torsion and branch angle are differential/topological quantities distinct in kind from curvature (more ECG-lead-like, likely to add real signal); diameter is a caliber measurement, arguably the most independent information domain of the four (least likely to be redundant with the three curvature-family quantities); but all four are still derived from the same underlying 3D curve/vessel wall, so the analogy to fully independent sensors is imperfect and redundancy pruning (as done via Spearman-correlation elimination in arXiv 2607.14195) is a reasonable safeguard rather than a nice-to-have.
- Small-sample clinical regimes amplify the downside of adding weakly-informative or redundant channels (curse-of-dimensionality effect), which argues for treating each new channel as something to be justified/ablated rather than added by default, particularly relevant given the general finding (not vascular-specific) that most benchmark multivariate datasets tolerate large channel reductions without accuracy loss.

### Gaps
- No study found gives a quantitative small-sample-specific (e.g. n<1000 clinical cohort) multivariate-vs-univariate time-series classification comparison; the UEA/UCR and channel-selection literature surveyed is drawn from general benchmark datasets whose sample sizes and domains were not characterized in the sources retrieved (this would need per-dataset lookup not completed here).
- No source quantified how many "genuinely independent information domain" channels a small (dozens-to-low-hundreds sample) model can absorb before overfitting outweighs the added signal; this is inferred qualitatively above, not measured directly by any cited source.

## Are there known architectural techniques for multi-channel sequence inputs where channels are correlated or noisy in only some channels (channel attention, channel dropout, learned channel weighting, concatenation vs. separate branches)?

### Takeaway
Yes: the multivariate-time-series-forecasting literature has an active line of "channel strategy" architectures built specifically to stop noisy or irrelevant channels from degrading a shared representation — mask/gated attention, adaptive recalibration ("information-bottleneck" style feature cleaning), channel-time parallel attention, and attention-map sparsity regularization are all documented mechanisms; separately, the medical-imaging multimodal-fusion literature documents concatenation vs. separate-encoder-branch designs (including three-branch schemes that explicitly separate "complementary" from "redundant" feature content) as an architectural axis independent of the attention-based methods.

### Cited Findings
- DUET generates mask matrices for its attention mechanism, "allowing each channel to focus on those beneficial for downstream prediction tasks, while mitigating the impact of noisy or irrelevant channels" — [arXiv 2502.10721 survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10721v1).
- An alternative to channel-independent projection is "active information bottleneck" style adaptive recalibration that performs "proactive feature-cleaning" to actively suppress noise rather than merely processing channels independently — [arXiv 2502.10721 survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10721v1).
- CT-ParaNet (Channel-Time Parallel Attention Network) processes channel and temporal correlations in parallel (rather than serially) specifically to address "information degradation in serial structures" in noisy environments (demonstrated on bearing fault diagnosis, an industrial sensor domain) — [PMC12504641](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12504641/).
- Another documented approach regularizes attention maps with an L1 sparsity constraint to "suppress redundant dependencies," combined with the task loss during training, to improve robustness when channels are correlated/redundant — [arXiv survey summary, source per WebSearch result on channel attention multivariate time series].
- On the fusion-architecture side (medical imaging, image + non-image data generally): "separate encoder branches are employed to extract features from each modality, which are subsequently concatenated at the feature level or fused in a common representation space," with fusion possible via concatenation, element-wise addition/multiplication, weighted sum, or max pooling, and at early, middle, or late stages of the network — [PMC10288577 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10288577/).
- Early fusion (stacking channels before a single shared encoder) is explicitly noted to be "the simplest operation" but to "require high image registration/alignment quality" between the fused streams, a caveat that generalizes to stacking geometric channels that are not perfectly co-registered along the centerline — [PMC10288577 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10288577/).
- A documented three-branch autoencoder design explicitly separates "exclusive modality features" from "common structure features" and applies different fusion strategies to each, rather than fusing all channels identically — a concrete precedent for treating some channels (e.g. one clearly distinct information domain like diameter) differently from others (curvature/torsion, which may be more correlated with each other) — [PMC10288577 review, three-branch complementary/redundant example](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10288577/).
- MLBF-Net's per-lead branch design for 12-lead ECG (cited above) is a working, deployed instance of "separate branch per channel, fuse later" applied to a genuinely multi-channel sequence classification problem structurally similar (one 1D signal per channel, shared downstream label) to a multi-channel centerline profile — [arXiv 2008.07263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.07263).

### Inferences
- The literature offers at least four concrete, low-complexity techniques transferable to a small curvature+torsion+angle+diameter model: (1) simple concatenation into one shared 1D-CNN/RNN/transformer input (early fusion — cheapest, but assumes the four channels are well co-registered per point, which they should be here since all are sampled at the same centerline arclength positions); (2) per-channel branches fused late (as in MLBF-Net / the three-branch autoencoder), useful if one channel (e.g. diameter) is suspected to carry qualitatively different information than the curvature-family channels; (3) a channel-attention/masking layer (DUET-style) that lets the model down-weight a channel if it turns out to be mostly noise for a given case, at some added parameter/complexity cost; (4) explicit redundancy pruning before training (correlation-based elimination, as demonstrated directly in the vascular RF study in Q1) as the cheapest possible technique, requiring no architecture change at all.
- Given the "how much code to write" framing of this thesis's engineering conventions (elsewhere in project conventions, not part of this literature question), the redundancy-pruning-before-training and simple-concatenation approaches are the ones with direct literature precedent at low implementation cost; channel-attention layers and separate-branch encoders are precedented but add real model complexity that the small-sample literature above (Q2) suggests is a real cost, not just an engineering nuisance.

### Gaps
- None of the channel-attention/masking techniques found (DUET, CT-ParaNet, attention-map sparsity regularization) were demonstrated on a small-sample (hundreds of subjects) clinical dataset; all examples found are from larger sensor/forecasting benchmarks or industrial fault diagnosis, so their small-sample behavior is unverified.
- No source directly compared "concatenate 4 correlated geometric channels" vs "separate small encoder branch per channel then fuse" on a matched vascular dataset; the branch-vs-concatenation trade-off above is inferred from the general multimodal-fusion review and the ECG per-lead-branch precedent, not from a vascular-geometry-specific ablation.

## Is there evidence that letting a network learn interactions between geometric channels (curvature x torsion x angle) captures something a simple multivariable regression could not?

### Takeaway
The one paper with the exact comparison the thesis leans on (Tello Ayala et al.) demonstrates that a learned sequence model beats a linear model on the *same single channel's full profile vs its scalar mean* (0.67 vs 0.60 AUROC) — that is evidence for "learning over the sequence structure of one channel beats hand-summarizing it," not "learning interactions between multiple channels beats a multivariable regression on those same channels." No study surfaced by this search directly tests a transformer/RNN reading multiple raw geometric channels against a multivariable (multi-feature) logistic/linear regression on the same channels' summary statistics; the closest indirect evidence (the vessel-morphology RF study) shows that a *non-deep* model already captures substantial multi-feature interaction benefit via engineered feature combination (Macro-F1 rising from ~0.82 with one feature to ~0.92 with a pruned multi-feature set), which weakens (without refuting) the case that a deep sequence model's specific value-add is channel interaction learning per se, as opposed to profile-level (within-channel) learning.

### Cited Findings
- Tello Ayala et al.: transformer on the full local-tortuosity profile (single channel, positionally structured) + age/sex: AUROC 0.67 (0.65-0.69). Logistic regression on mean tortuosity (a single scalar collapse of that same single channel) + age/sex: AUROC 0.60 (0.58-0.63) — [PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/). This isolates the value of preserving positional/sequence structure within one channel; it does not isolate the value of cross-channel interaction learning, because only one geometric channel is used on both sides of the comparison.
- The vessel-morphology RF study shows that a substantial part of "multi-feature beats single-feature" improvement (Macro-F1 0.815→0.921 binary) is achievable with a non-deep, non-interaction-focused model (random forests do capture some feature interactions via tree splits, but this is not sequence-position-aware deep learning), using engineered summary features rather than raw per-point profiles — [arXiv 2607.14195](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14195). This suggests at least part of any multi-channel benefit is available from classical ML on hand-engineered multi-feature summaries, independent of whether a deep sequence model is used at all.
- No source found reports a controlled experiment of {sequence model on multi-channel raw profile} vs {multivariable regression on the same multiple channels' summary statistics}, which is the precise comparison needed to answer this question directly.

### Inferences
- The evidence the thesis currently has (Tello Ayala) licenses the claim "a learned model over an ordered single-channel profile beats a linear model over that channel's mean" but does not license "a learned model over multiple channels beats a multivariable regression over those channels' means/summaries." Extending the Tello Ayala framing to justify multi-channel expansion requires either finding/running that second, currently-missing comparison, or being explicit in the thesis that the two questions (profile-vs-scalar, and single-channel-vs-multi-channel) are logically separate and only the first has direct precedent here.
- Given that a non-deep RF model already captured a large fraction of the multi-feature benefit in the one vascular study with a clean ablation, the marginal value of a deep sequence model specifically for learning curvature x torsion x angle interactions (beyond what a multivariable regression with interaction terms, or a random forest, could capture) is not evidenced one way or the other by anything found in this search.

### Gaps
- No coronary, retinal, cerebral, or aortic study was found that runs the specific three-way comparison implied by the thesis's question: (a) multivariable regression on multiple channel summary statistics, (b) sequence model on a single channel's full profile, (c) sequence model on multiple channels' full profiles — needed to cleanly attribute any observed gain to "sequence structure," "multiple channels," or their interaction.
- Whether curvature, torsion, and branch angle exhibit the kind of nonlinear, multiplicative interaction that a network could learn but a linear/multivariable regression could not (as opposed to being individually informative but roughly additive) was not addressed by any source found; this is a domain (differential-geometry-of-vessels) question that the general ML-interactions literature (e.g. on tree-based methods capturing interactions, or on why deep nets outperform GLMs when true interactions exist) was not searched for in this pass and remains open.
