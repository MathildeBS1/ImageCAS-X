# Low WSS at bends and bifurcations: citation verification (chatzizisis2007ess, asakura1990flow, vandergiessen2009bifurcation)

- **van der Giessen 2009 (EuroIntervention 4:654-61): full text read.** Publisher PDF ([PDF](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)).
- **Asakura & Karino 1990 (Circ Res 66:1045-66): only the PubMed abstract of the article was read** ([PubMed 2317887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2317887)). The ahajournals full text returned a bot wall. I also read the OCR text of **Asakura's 1988 University of Tsukuba PhD thesis**, which has the same title and is the work behind the article ([Tsukuba repository](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21125/files/1.pdf)). The thesis is **not** the Circ Res article. Its wording and page numbers will differ from the article, so its quotes are marked [THESIS].

## Q1. Does Chatzizisis 2007 cover both the bend and the bifurcation mechanism?

### Takeaway
Only in part. Chatzizisis places low ESS at the inner wall of curvatures and oscillatory ESS at the lateral (outer) walls of bifurcations, each in one sentence. It never describes a mechanism for either site: no fast core thrown to the outer wall, no high-velocity flow beside the flow divider, and no use of the phrase "flow divider" at all. The word "recirculation" appears only in a generic definition of disturbed laminar flow, not tied to the inner wall of a bend or the lateral wall of a bifurcation. The CHECK note should be answered: it covers *where* (both sites), not *how* (neither mechanism).

### Cited Findings
- Introduction, p. 2379, verbatim: "atherosclerotic lesions form at specific regions of the arterial tree, such as in the vicinity of branch points, the outer wall of bifurcations, and the inner wall of curvatures, where disturbed flow occurs (3)." The cited ref 3 is VanderLaan 2004 — [Chatzizisis 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059)
- Section "Definitions of ESS and Blood Flow Patterns", p. 2382, verbatim: "Low ESS typically occurs at the inner areas of curvatures as well as upstream of stenoses (34). Oscillatory ESS is characterized by significant changes in both direction (bidirectional) and magnitude between systole and diastole, resulting in a very low time-average, usually close to 0 (4–6) (Fig. 3). Oscillatory ESS occurs primarily downstream of stenoses, at the lateral walls of bifurcations, and in the vicinity of branch points (6,10,34,35)." Ref 34 is Ku 1997, Annu Rev Fluid Mech, and ref 10 is Ku 1985 (carotid) — [Chatzizisis 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059)
- "Recirculation" appears in three places, all generic, never located at a bend or bifurcation wall:
  - Table 1 (pp. 2380-81), "Disturbed laminar blood flow": "characterized by reversed flow (i.e., flow separation, recirculation, and reattachment to forward flow). Disturbed laminar blood flow occurs in arterial segments with geometric irregularities (e.g., curvatures, branches, bifurcations), or upstream and downstream of stenoses."
  - Main text, p. 2381: disturbed laminar flow is "characterized by areas with reversed flow (i.e., flow separation, recirculation, and reattachment to forward flow) or circumferential swirling (26,29) (Fig. 2B)."
  - Fig. 2 legend, the same definition, "Adapted from Munson et al. (28)." — [Chatzizisis 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059)
- Figures 1 to 3 are generic: Fig. 1 defines ESS = μ·dv/dy, Fig. 2 is a schematic of flow types in a tube, and Fig. 3 is ESS-versus-time traces for pulsatile, low and oscillatory ESS "Adapted from Ku et al. (10)". **No figure shows a curved vessel or a bifurcation with a velocity profile or an ESS map** — [Chatzizisis 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059)
- A text search of the full paper found no "flow divider", "carina", "apex", "skew" or "helical". "Outer wall" occurs only in "the outer wall of bifurcations". Velocity is discussed only for straight undisturbed flow (Table 1: "The highest velocity is found at the centre of the lumen") — [Chatzizisis 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059)
- p. 2381: "in geometrically irregular regions, where disturbed laminar flow occurs, pulsatile flow generates low and/or oscillatory ESS." — [Chatzizisis 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059)
- Chatzizisis cites Asakura & Karino 1990 as its ref 9, in the context of CFD/autopsy studies of coronary arteries — [Chatzizisis 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059)

### Inferences
- Chatzizisis assigns different ESS types to the two sites: *low* ESS at the inner curvature and *oscillatory* ESS at the lateral walls of bifurcations. The thesis paragraph says "low WSS" for both. That is defensible under the oscillatory definition (time-average "very low, usually close to 0"), but strictly the source separates the two terms.
- The mechanistic clauses in the thesis are not supported by Chatzizisis. These are "the fast core of the flow is carried to the outer wall", "flow along the inner wall slows and can recirculate", and "the fastest flow enters each daughter branch beside the ... flow divider". Asakura's work supports them better (see Q2).

### Gaps
- Chatzizisis's own primary sources for the site claims (Ku 1997; VanderLaan 2004) were not read.

## Q2. Asakura & Karino 1990: population, method, findings at bends and bifurcations

### Takeaway
Five post-mortem human coronary trees, excised and made transparent, perfused, with flow visualised by tracer microspheres and high-speed cinemicrography. Lesions sat almost exclusively on the "outer wall (hip)" of daughter vessels at bifurcations and T-junctions, never at the flow divider, and along the inner wall of curved segments. These were the sites of slow flow, separation and recirculation, and low WSS. The study supports both the plaque-location sentence and much of the mechanism sentences. Caveats: n = 5, steady flow in fixed vessels, and the mechanism detail below is from the thesis, not the article.

### Cited Findings
- Article abstract, verbatim [ABSTRACT ONLY]: lesion locations and flow patterns "were studied in detail by flow visualization and high-speed cinemicrographic techniques using five isolated, transparent human coronary arterial trees prepared postmortem. It was found that atherosclerotic plaques and wall thickenings in left and right coronary arteries were localized almost exclusively on the outer wall of one or both daughter vessels at major bifurcations and T-junctions, which left the flow-divider free of lesions, and along the inner wall of curved segments. When flow patterns in such vessels were studied in detail, it was discovered that these sites were where flow was either slow or disturbed with the formation of slow recirculation and secondary flows and where wall shear stress was low." — [PubMed 2317887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2317887)
- Abstract conclusion: "the major hemodynamic factors directly related to the localization of atherosclerotic plaques and wall thickenings in the human arterial system are the low fluid velocity and the resultant low shear stress that acts on the vessel wall." — [PubMed 2317887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2317887)
- [THESIS, Methods §2.1, p. 44] "Five fresh and intact human hearts ... were obtained at autopsy from four male subjects aged 18, 56, 61 and 75 years, and a 51-year old female subject in whom the primary cause of death was not cardiovascular disease." — [Asakura 1988 thesis](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21125/files/1.pdf)
- [THESIS, Results, p. 41] "Flow studies were carried out on 4 transparent arterial trees." The fifth (75-year-old) was unusable because of non-transparent calcified plaque. Flow was **steady**: Figs. 6, 7 and 12 are all "in steady flow" — [Asakura 1988 thesis](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21125/files/1.pdf)
- [THESIS, LMCA bifurcation, pp. 39-40] "In all the 5 transparent arterial trees prepared and studied, atherosclerotic wall thickenings were located almost exclusively along the outer wall (hip) of the bifurcation or trifurcation ... In no case, were the flow divider and upper (pericardial side) walls affected by atherosclerotic lesions." — [Asakura 1988 thesis](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21125/files/1.pdf)
- [THESIS, bifurcation flow, p. 41, Fig. 6] "flow separation occurred at the outer walls (hips) of the bifurcation, creating wide regions of separated flow. The regions of separated flow were then filled with the spiral and recirculation flows formed as a result of a strong deflection of the flow from the LMCA at the obtuse-angled flow divider." Also, around Fig. 7: "particles traveling on streamlines very close to the lateral walls of the LMCA were separated from the vessel walls at the hips of the trifurcation and were pushed towards the inner walls downstream of the flow divider, resulting in the formation of a long, wide region of separated flow in each major daughter vessel." — [Asakura 1988 thesis](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21125/files/1.pdf)
- [THESIS, RCA bends, pp. 68-69, Fig. 12] "separation occurred at the inner wall of the first bend. At the outer wall of the bend, the fluid elements in the mainstream, having high momentum, were strongly deflected sideways resulting in the formation of thin layered spiral secondary flows ... a very thin-layered region of slow reverse flows (recirculation zone) was formed along the inner wall of the curved segment at the very place where atherosclerotic wall thickening was found." Also, p. 68: "Atherosclerotic wall thickenings were found at the inner wall of the curved segments in an alternating fashion, and at the outer walls (hips) of the major bifurcations and T-junctions of the RCA." — [Asakura 1988 thesis](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21125/files/1.pdf)
- [THESIS, Conclusion, p. 101] lesions "were localized not at flow dividers (high shear region) ... but almost exclusively on the outer wall (hip) of one or both daughter vessels at major bifurcation and T-junctions and along the inner wall of curved segments where flow was either slow (low shear region) or disturbed with formation of secondary and recirculation flows." — [Asakura 1988 thesis](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21125/files/1.pdf)
- [THESIS, LAD] Lesions of the proximal LAD sat on its right-lateral and lower walls, the "inner wall of the curved segment" — [Asakura 1988 thesis](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21125/files/1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Asakura's term is "outer wall (hip)" of the daughter vessel, the wall opposite the flow divider. That is the same wall the thesis calls the "lateral wall" and Chatzizisis calls "lateral walls"/"outer wall of bifurcations". The thesis should define the term once, e.g. "the lateral (outer) wall, opposite the flow divider".
- Asakura & Karino is the better citation for the mechanism sentences: separation and recirculation at the inner wall of bends, high-momentum flow at the outer wall, and separation and recirculation at the hips of bifurcations with no lesion at the divider. Two caveats. First, the high-momentum detail was verified only in the thesis, not the article. Second, Asakura does not phrase it as "the fastest flow enters each daughter branch beside the flow divider". The thesis says flow is deflected at the divider, with the approaching velocity profile skewed.

### Gaps
- The Circ Res 1990 full text was not obtained. Page and figure numbers in the article are unknown, and the thesis quotes may be worded differently there.
- With n = 5 hearts and 4 flow-studied trees, this is a qualitative study. No lesion-frequency statistics were found.

## Q3. van der Giessen 2009: population, plaque fractions, how WSS was obtained

### Takeaway
65 cross-sections, 1 mm distal to the flow divider of the LM (LAD/LCX) and LAD/D1 bifurcations, from 28 symptomatic patients scanned with 64-slice CT. **WSS was not computed.** Each cross-section was split into four quadrants ranked by *expected* WSS from geometry. Plaque was in the low-WSS outer (lateral) wall in 72% of cross-sections versus 31% at the flow divider, and never in the flow divider alone. The paper also finds an effect of curvature: the myocardial-side, inner-bend quadrant was second most affected.

### Cited Findings
- Population, p. 655: "We retrospectively studied 28 (18 male, mean age 59.9 ± 6.6 years) consecutive symptomatic patients, suspected of coronary artery disease who underwent MSCT coronary angiography." Scanner: Siemens Sensation64, about 0.3 mm in-plane, 0.4 mm slice — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- Sampling, p. 656: "one cross-section of each of the side-branches was obtained 1 mm distal to the flow divider". Trifurcations were excluded (n=21), along with diameter <1 mm (n=2) and heavy calcification (n=3). "In total we analysed 65 cross-sections" (Fig. 2: LAD 15, LCX 15, LADdist 17, D1 18) — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- How WSS was obtained, p. 656 (assumed, not computed): "High WSS is assumed in part IV due to the flow-division at the flow divider, while in part I, the outer, non-flow divider wall, low WSS is expected. Besides the effect of the bifurcation, the curvature of the artery over the myocardium also influences WSS. In comparison to part III, the WSS is assumed to be lower in part II, because the inner wall of a curved vessel is subjected to lower WSS than the outer wall." Limitations, p. 660: "we defined the WSS patterns on the basis of general geometrical features of coronary arteries, being the bifurcation and curvature." — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- Background, p. 655 (the paper's own literature claims): "In bifurcating arteries, the outer wall is exposed to low WSS compared to the flow divider wall [refs 7,8: Asakura & Karino 1990; Tadjfar 2004]. In curved arteries, low and high WSS regions are present at the inner and outer bend, respectively [ref 9: Kirpalani 1999]." — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- Results, p. 657 and Fig. 3: "Of the 65 cross-sections 88% contained plaque." "Plaques were found in low WSS parts I and II in 72% and 62% of the cross-sections. The high WSS parts III, and IV were less often affected than parts I and II, only in 38% and 31% of the cross-sections." The quadrants are I = outer wall, II = myocardial side ("inner bend"), III = pericardial side ("outer bend") and IV = flow divider — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- p. 657: "In 10 of the 11 minimally inflicted cross-sections plaque was present in the low WSS parts I or II. The high WSS part IV was not affected once." "Plaque at the flow divider was found in 20 out of the 65 analysed cross-sections ... In 19 out of the 20 cross-sections plaque in the flow divider was accompanied by plaque in low WSS regions I or II." The abstract says: "Plaque was never found exclusively in the flow divider part IV." — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- Mean shear stress index 2.02 ± 0.62, below the random reference of 2.5 (p<0.05) — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- Densest calcium spot, p. 658: in part II in 10 cross-sections, part I in 5, part III in 4, and "never found in high WSS part IV" — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- Discussion and conclusion, pp. 659-660: "not only the bifurcation contributes to the low WSS region and initiates plaque formation, but also the curvature of the arteries over the myocardium". Plaque "grows from the outer wall (low WSS) of the bifurcation towards the flow divider (high WSS)" is stated as "plausible". The authors note: "we did not follow patients over time" — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)
- Scope limitation, p. 660: only two bifurcation types, left coronary tree only, and no RCA — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)

### Inferences
- The paper supports "plaque is concentrated on the lateral walls of bifurcations" directly, with numbers. It does **not** measure WSS, so it cannot be cited as showing that low WSS coincides with plaque. It shows plaque at sites where low WSS is expected.
- Its data on the inner wall of bends is indirect: quadrant II is the myocardial side, curvature over the heart, sampled just past a bifurcation. It is not a study of bends.

### Gaps
- No per-patient breakdown of cross-sections was reported beyond "96% of patients had plaque".

## Q4. Overstatements in the thesis paragraph and corrected wording

### Takeaway
Three problems. (1) The mechanism sentences are attributed to Chatzizisis, which states only the sites. (2) Chatzizisis has no "flow divider" or "fastest flow beside the divider". (3) van der Giessen supports bifurcations only, with no computed WSS, so it adds nothing for bends on its own. Asakura & Karino supports the full sentence about plaque location. Best fix: cite Chatzizisis for the sites, Asakura & Karino for the flow mechanism and lesion location, and van der Giessen for the in-vivo CT bifurcation data.

### Cited Findings
- Chatzizisis states the sites (inner curvature: low ESS; lateral walls of bifurcations: oscillatory ESS; p. 2382) but no velocity mechanism — [Chatzizisis 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059)
- The abstract of Asakura & Karino states lesion sites at bifurcations (outer wall, divider spared) and bends (inner wall), each with slow flow, recirculation and low WSS — [PubMed 2317887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2317887)
- van der Giessen's WSS levels are "assumed"/"expected" from geometry — [van der Giessen 2009](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study/pdf)

### Inferences
Sentence-by-sentence verdict:
1. "Low WSS occurs at two types of site, both determined by the shape of the vessel." OK with [chatzizisis2007ess]: inner curvatures and lateral walls of bifurcations, "geometric irregularities". Chatzizisis calls the bifurcation pattern "oscillatory".
2. "In a bend, the fast core ... carried to the outer wall, while flow along the inner wall slows and can recirculate [chatzizisis2007ess]." **Not supported by Chatzizisis.** Asakura supports separation and recirculation at the inner wall. The high-momentum mainstream deflected at the outer wall is verified in the thesis only. Cite [asakura1990flow] here (and verify in the article).
3. "At a bifurcation, the fastest flow enters each daughter branch beside the ... flow divider, while flow along the lateral walls separates and recirculates [chatzizisis2007ess]." **Not supported by Chatzizisis**, which never mentions the flow divider. Asakura supports separation and recirculation at the hips and no lesions at the divider, and van der Giessen *assumes* high WSS at the divider. "Fastest flow beside the divider" has no verbatim support in what was read; Asakura's wording is that flow is deflected at the flow divider.
4. "Plaque is accordingly concentrated on the inner wall of bends and the lateral walls of bifurcations [asakura1990flow, vandergiessen2009bifurcation]." Asakura supports both halves (n = 5 post-mortem trees). van der Giessen supports the bifurcation half (72% versus 31% at the divider, 65 cross-sections, 28 patients). "Accordingly" implies a causal link that neither source proves; it is a correlation.

Suggested wording (faithful to the sources):
> "Low WSS occurs at two types of site, both set by vessel geometry: the inner wall of bends and the lateral walls of bifurcations, opposite the flow divider [chatzizisis2007ess]. In flow visualisation of five transparent post-mortem coronary trees, flow separated from the inner wall of bends and formed a slow recirculation zone. At bifurcations, flow was deflected at the flow divider and separated from the lateral walls, where slow recirculating flow formed [asakura1990flow]. Plaque in those trees lay almost exclusively at these slow-flow sites, never at the flow divider [asakura1990flow]. In vivo, CT of 65 cross-sections just distal to left-coronary bifurcations in 28 patients found plaque in the lateral wall in 72% and at the flow divider in 31%, and never at the flow divider alone [vandergiessen2009bifurcation]."

If the thesis keeps "the fast core is carried to the outer wall", cite [asakura1990flow] and confirm the sentence in the Circ Res article first. It was verified only in Asakura's 1988 thesis, pp. 68-69.

### Gaps
- The Circ Res article full text is needed to give the exact page and figure for the bend and bifurcation mechanisms. The OCR'd thesis has the same content but is not the cited item.
