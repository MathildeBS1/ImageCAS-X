## Mechanism: where the fast flow goes, separation, low/oscillatory WSS on the lateral walls; is "separates and recirculates" accurate for coronaries, and does it depend on angle and diameter ratio?

### Takeaway
The draft sentence is correct in substance and well supported for coronaries: high WSS at the flow divider (carina), low and oscillatory WSS on the lateral walls opposite it, with slow recirculation and secondary flow observed directly in human coronary trees by Asakura and Karino. The velocity-skew-toward-the-divider statement is documented most explicitly in the carotid (Zarins 1983); for coronaries it is implied by the high carina WSS. "Separates and recirculates" should be softened to "is slow, may separate, and swirls" or similar, because the extent of separation depends on flow split, angle and pulsatile phase.

### Cited Findings
- Human coronary trees (5 postmortem, transparent, flow visualisation with high-speed cine): plaques and wall thickenings were "localized almost exclusively on the outer wall of one or both daughter vessels at major bifurcations and T-junctions, which left the flow-divider free of lesions, and along the inner wall of curved segments"; these were sites where "flow was either slow or disturbed with the formation of slow recirculation and secondary flows and where wall shear stress was low." Authors conclude low velocity and resulting low WSS are the main localising factors. — [Asakura & Karino 1990, Circ Res 66:1045 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.res.66.4.1045)
- Carotid (12 autopsy bifurcations + scale models, laser-Doppler, steady flow Re 400-1200): intima thinnest within 15 deg of the flow divider, thickest at 161 +/- 16 deg "on the outer wall opposite the flow divider". Along the inner (divider) wall streamlines stay axial and unidirectional "with velocity maxima shifted toward the flow divider apex"; along the outer wall flow showed "a region of separation and reversal of axial flow as well as the development of counter-rotating helical trajectories", WSS 0 to -6 dyn/cm2 vs 31-600 dyn/cm2 at the divider wall. — [Zarins et al. 1983, Circ Res 53:502 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.res.53.4.502)
- Carotid, pulsatile flow: at the flow divider WSS was highest (mean 17 dyn/cm2) and unidirectional; at the outer sinus wall mean WSS was -0.5 dyn/cm2 but oscillated between -7 and +4 dyn/cm2 during systole; side walls showed circumferential oscillation. Intimal thickness correlated with 1/max WSS (r = 0.90) and with the oscillatory shear index (r = 0.82; 0.93 inner/outer walls only). Introduces the OSI and the "low and oscillating" formulation. — [Ku et al. 1985, Arteriosclerosis 5:293 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.atv.5.3.293)
- Coronary cast (one minimally diseased human LM bifurcation, pulsatile LDA at 14 sites on outer walls of LM, LAD, LCx and along the flow divider): intimal thickness inversely correlated with time-average and maximum wall shear rate (P < 0.005). — [Friedman et al. 1987, Atherosclerosis 68:27 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9150(87)90090-6)
- CFD of an averaged normal left coronary tree: "Low WSS regions occur at bifurcations in regions opposite the flow dividers"; at the LM bifurcation 0.75-2.25 N/m2 opposite the divider; "High WSS values are encountered at all flow dividers." — [Soulis et al. 2006, J Biomech 39:742 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2004.12.026)
- Review (full text): "Arterial bifurcations are known for their distinct flow velocity patterns resulting in flow separation, recirculation and secondary flow patterns" giving "local low and oscillatory ESS" along the lateral walls of main vessel and side branch, while "high ESS develops in the flow divider (carina)". "Flow pulsatility further aggravates the haemodynamic patterns", with low oscillatory ESS in systole. — [Antoniadis et al. 2015, EuroIntervention 11(Suppl V):V102](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/impact-of-local-flow-haemodynamics-on-atherosclerosis-in-coronary-artery-bifurcations)
- Same review on geometry: "The proportion of flow directed towards the side branch is the primary factor determining the ESS patterns within the bifurcation", and "the higher the angle and diameter the lower the ESS" (its ref. 9, not checked). — [Antoniadis et al. 2015, EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/impact-of-local-flow-haemodynamics-on-atherosclerosis-in-coronary-artery-bifurcations)
- Review framing: co-localisation of disturbed flow and lesions at bifurcations underlies the "haemodynamic hypothesis"; geometry and intravascular flow features (helical flow) are surrogates for disturbed WSS; focuses on carotid and coronary bifurcations. — [Morbiducci et al. 2016, Thromb Haemost 115:484 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1160/th15-07-0597)
- Plaques form predominantly in low-ESS regions; moderate/high ESS regions generally protected. — [Wentzel et al. 2012, Cardiovasc Res 96:234 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1093/cvr/cvs217)

### Inferences
- The draft's first clause ("the fastest flow enters each daughter branch beside the flow divider") is the coronary analogue of Zarins' "velocity maxima shifted toward the flow divider apex". It follows from inertia: the core of the parent flow continues straight and impinges on the divider. The strongest explicit citation is Zarins 1983 (carotid); for coronaries cite Asakura & Karino 1990 plus Soulis 2006 / Antoniadis 2015 (high WSS at the divider). Chatzizisis 2007 is fine as a review citation but was not checked to contain this exact wording.
- "Separates and recirculates": Asakura & Karino observed "slow recirculation" in human coronaries, so it is not wrong. But much of the classic separation evidence (Zarins, Ku) is from the carotid bulb, a dilated sinus that strongly promotes separation and which coronaries lack. Antoniadis' dependence on flow split and angle implies separation in coronaries is variable and often confined to part of the cycle. Safer clinical phrasing: "flow along the lateral walls is slow and disturbed, with transient separation and swirling secondary flow".
- "Oscillatory" should be included alongside "low": that is the Ku 1985 contribution and the standard formulation in coronary reviews (Antoniadis 2015).

### Gaps
- No coronary-specific primary source was read in full that quantifies the size or duration of the recirculation zone at physiological coronary Reynolds numbers (roughly 100-300 per a search snippet, not verified). Asakura & Karino full text (with flow-visualisation details) was paywalled.
- Chatzizisis 2007 full text not accessed; whether it describes the velocity skew toward the divider in those words is unverified.
- The source of Antoniadis' "the higher the angle and diameter the lower the ESS" (ref. 9) was not identified.

## Classic references: what each actually showed

### Takeaway
For the coronary claim, the strongest set is Asakura & Karino 1990 (flow visualisation + lesion mapping), Fox & Seed 1981 (early atheroma, divider sparing), Kimura 1996 (IVUS, proximal LAD), Nakazawa 2010 (pathology) and van der Giessen 2009 (CT). Zarins 1983 and Ku 1985 are carotid and should be cited for the mechanism (separation, low and oscillatory WSS), not for coronary plaque location.

### Cited Findings
- Fox & Seed 1981: early fatty lesions in coronaries of subjects < 40 y dying of non-cardiovascular causes; disease concentrated near vessel entrances; in the RCA "lesions were concentrated on the inner wall of the major curvature"; "Immediately downstream of the entrances of both branches of the left coronary artery, the flow-dividing walls were spared"; LAD plaques spiral further downstream. — [Fox & Seed 1981, J Biomech Eng 103:208 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3138280)
- Asakura & Karino 1990: lesions on the outer wall of daughter vessels at bifurcations/T-junctions and the inner wall of curves; flow divider free of lesions (see above). This is the paper that supports both halves of the draft's second sentence (bends and bifurcations). — [Asakura & Karino 1990](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.res.66.4.1045)
- Kimura 1996 (in vivo IVUS, n = 42 proximal LAD just distal to LM bifurcation): maximal wall thickness 1.42 +/- 0.50 mm vs flow-divider thickness 0.26 +/- 0.16 mm; "Maximal plaque thickness spared the region of the flow divider in 100% of cases and was positioned at a mean angle of 193 +/- 49 degrees from the center of the flow divider", i.e. "opposite the circumflex takeoff"; eccentricity maintained across 24-80% area stenosis. — [Kimura et al. 1996, JACC 27:825 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0735-1097(95)00551-x)
- van der Giessen 2009 (64-slice CT, 65 cross-sections near bifurcations, each split into four quadrants ranked by expected WSS: I outer wall lowest, II inner bend, III outer bend, IV flow divider): plaque in 72%, 62%, 38%, 31% of parts I-IV. "Plaque was never found exclusively in the flow divider part IV"; in 93% of cross-sections with divider plaque, parts I and/or II also had plaque. Conclusion: plaque plausibly grows from the outer (low-WSS) wall toward the divider. Note: the WSS ranking was expected, not computed per patient. — [van der Giessen et al. 2009, EuroIntervention 4:654 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.4244/eijv4i5a109)
- Nakazawa 2010 (CVPath registry, 26 non-stented coronary bifurcation lesions from patients dying of severe CAD): "the lateral wall showed significantly greater intima as well as necrotic core thickness than the flow divider." Also: after DES, neointima less and uncovered struts more at the flow divider. — [Nakazawa et al. 2010, JACC 55:1679 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2010.01.021)
- Zarins 1983 / Ku 1985: carotid; mechanism and OSI (details above). — [Zarins 1983](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.res.53.4.502); [Ku 1985](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.atv.5.3.293)

### Inferences
- "Carina spared" is accurate as a tendency, not absolute. Van der Giessen found divider plaque in 31% of quadrants (always with adjacent lateral-wall plaque); Antoniadis' review (search snippet of the EuroIntervention page) notes carina atheroma in about 30% of bifurcations needing intervention, though of small volume. In advanced disease plaque wraps around toward the carina.
- For the thesis sentence, `vandergiessen2009bifurcation` is a good CCTA-specific citation; pair with `nakazawa2010pathological` (pathology) or `kimura1996atheroma` (IVUS) so the bifurcation claim does not rest on one modality.

### Gaps
- Nakazawa 2010 full text not read: the exact definition of "lateral wall" and the numeric intima thickness values are not recorded here.

## Terminology: lateral vs outer wall; carina vs flow divider

### Takeaway
In the coronary literature "flow divider" and "carina" are used interchangeably (Antoniadis: "flow divider (carina)"), and the wall opposite it is most often called the "lateral wall" in interventional/pathology papers (Nakazawa, Antoniadis), "outer wall" in older flow papers (Asakura, Zarins, van der Giessen), or "the wall opposite the flow divider" (Soulis, Kimura). For a clinical reader, "lateral wall (opposite the carina)" is the clearest, because "outer wall" collides with the outer wall of a bend, which has high, not low, WSS.

### Cited Findings
- "outer wall of one or both daughter vessels ... flow-divider" — [Asakura & Karino 1990](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.res.66.4.1045)
- "outer wall opposite the flow divider" / "inner wall" for the divider side — [Zarins 1983](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.res.53.4.502)
- "lateral wall ... flow divider" — [Nakazawa 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2010.01.021)
- "lateral walls ... flow divider (carina)" — [Antoniadis 2015 EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/impact-of-local-flow-haemodynamics-on-atherosclerosis-in-coronary-artery-bifurcations)
- "regions opposite the flow dividers" — [Soulis 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2004.12.026)
- Van der Giessen uses "outer wall" (bifurcation, lowest WSS) alongside "outer bend" (curvature, higher WSS) in the same quadrant scheme, illustrating the ambiguity. — [van der Giessen 2009](https://doi.org/10.4244/eijv4i5a109)

### Inferences
- Note the inversion between carotid and coronary usage: Zarins calls the divider side the "inner wall" of the sinus, whereas in bends "inner wall" is the low-WSS side. Defining the wall by reference to the carina avoids this.

### Gaps
- No formal consensus document (e.g. European Bifurcation Club) was checked for a prescribed term.

## Effect of bifurcation angle on low WSS

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed. Chaichana 2011 (and the Sun & Chaichana follow-ups) and Rabbi 2020 report more disturbed flow with wider angles; Beier 2016 and Chiastra 2017, which isolated angle from other shape variables, found angle has only a minor effect, with inflow angle, diameter, curvature and tortuosity mattering more. Antoniadis 2015 names the side-branch flow fraction as the primary determinant. The thesis should not claim a strong angle effect.

### Cited Findings
- Chaichana 2011: 8 idealised LM models (15-120 deg) + 4 patient models (58-120 deg); "a disturbed flow pattern was observed in models with wider angulations"; "A low wall shear stress gradient was demonstrated at left bifurcations with wide angles." — [Chaichana et al. 2011, J Biomech 44:1869 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2011.04.033)
- Beier 2016: idealised and patient bifurcations at 40/60/80 deg (+/-1 SD of 101 CTA cases), plus patient cases at 33/42/117 deg; angle "had a minor impact on the areas of adverse hemodynamics in the idealized non-stented geometries ... and was not apparent for patient geometries"; high WSS near the carina at peak flow; "Incoming flow angle, diameter and tortuosity appear to have stronger effects." — [Beier et al. 2016, J Biomech 49:1570 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2016.03.038)
- Chiastra 2017 (idealised LAD-diagonal, stenosed and unstenosed): "bifurcation angle impacts lowly hemodynamics"; cardiac curvature radius affects helical flow and, in stenosed cases, exposure to low shear. — [Chiastra et al. 2017, J Biomech 58:79 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2017.04.016)
- Rabbi 2020: idealised bi/trifurcations and patient models (70, 95, 135 deg); branch angle "has a significant impact on the locations of adverse hemodynamics" and influences low-velocity regions. (Read only via search snippet; ScienceDirect returned 403.) — [Rabbi et al. 2020, Inform Med Unlocked 19:100314](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352914819303971)
- "The proportion of flow directed towards the side branch is the primary factor"; "the higher the angle and diameter the lower the ESS." — [Antoniadis 2015 EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/impact-of-local-flow-haemodynamics-on-atherosclerosis-in-coronary-artery-bifurcations)

### Inferences
- The disagreement follows study design: studies that vary angle alone in controlled geometries (Beier, Chiastra) find small effects, while those whose models co-vary other features or use descriptive metrics (Chaichana, Rabbi) report larger ones. Angle probably matters mainly through how it changes the flow split and inflow direction.
- Daughter diameter ratio matters through the flow split (a larger side branch takes more flow). This is supported by the Antoniadis statement but no primary diameter-ratio study was checked.

### Gaps
- No clinical imaging study linking LM bifurcation angle to lateral-wall plaque was read (search surfaced Sun & Chaichana 2017 QIMS 7:537, doi 10.21037/qims.2017.10.03, and a 2025 Sci Rep paper on reverse side-branch flow, neither checked).

## Suggested phrasing for the thesis sentence (clinical reader)

"At a bifurcation the fastest flow is directed onto the ridge where the parent vessel divides, the flow divider or carina, which therefore sees high wall shear stress, whereas flow along the opposite, lateral walls is slow and disturbed, with transient separation and secondary flow, so wall shear stress there is low and changes direction during the cardiac cycle [asakura1990flow, antoniadis2015eurointervention]. Plaque accordingly concentrates on the inner wall of bends and on the lateral walls of bifurcations, while the carina is relatively spared [asakura1990flow, kimura1996atheroma, vandergiessen2009bifurcation, nakazawa2010pathological]."

(Per thesis rules, cite Zarins/Ku only if the sentence is made explicitly about the general mechanism or the oscillatory shear index, since they are carotid.)
