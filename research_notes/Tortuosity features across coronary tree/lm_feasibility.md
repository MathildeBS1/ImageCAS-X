# Left Main (LM) Coronary Artery: Feasibility of Centerline-Derived Descriptors for CAD Prediction

## How short is the LM, and does a per-point profile even make sense for it?

### Takeaway
The LM is genuinely very short (population mean ~10-11 mm, commonly categorized as short/medium/long trunks with cutoffs at 5 mm and 15 mm), so at typical CT centerline arclength sampling a per-point curvature/torsion/diameter profile would realistically yield only a handful to a few dozen points, and for the shortest LMs (≤5 mm, which is not a rare tail) it collapses to essentially a scalar-feature vessel. The evidence supports treating LM primarily as a small set of scalar/bifurcation features rather than a dense per-point profile like RCA/LAD/LCx, though a coarse profile is not physically meaningless if the vessel is long enough (>15 mm cases).

### Cited Findings
- A computational atlas built from CT angiography in 300 patients with normal coronary anatomy (zero calcium score, no stenosis) reports mean LM length of 10.5 ± 5.3 mm — [EuroIntervention computational atlas](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- Classic pathological/anatomic literature on LM length describes three clinical categories: short main trunk (≤5 mm), medium main trunk (>5 mm and ≤15 mm), and long main trunk (>15 mm); LM length has also been reported to range from 2 mm up to 30 mm in some series, with a longest documented case around 23 mm and an average of 10.8 ± 5.5 mm — [PubMed: length of LM coronary artery, pathological features](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/920579/); [MSCT analysis of LM length](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-2671996/v1)
- The MSCT study explicitly frames its aim as relating LM length to gender, age, diameter, branching pattern, and coronary dominance, but full quantitative results (e.g., exact mean/SD stratified by sex or dominance) were not retrievable from the accessible preprint content — [MSCT analysis of LM length](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-2671996/v1) (content access limited to title; see Gaps)
- Rare anatomic case reports document extreme outliers (e.g., anomalous/ectopic LM origins with markedly longer courses), underscoring that the length distribution has a long right tail even though the bulk of cases are short — [Longest documented LM coronary artery](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167527307004007); [Rare case of longest ectopic LM](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5274676/)

### Inferences
- Given a population mean of ~10.5 mm and a "short trunk" category defined at ≤5 mm, if centerline points are sampled at a fixed arclength step comparable to this thesis's 0.5 mm isotropic resampling resolution, a typical LM would yield on the order of ~15-25 centerline samples end-to-end (10.5 mm / 0.5 mm ≈ 21, ignoring curvature-driven arclength inflation), and a short-trunk case (≤5 mm) would yield on the order of ~10 or fewer samples. This is a rough estimate derived from the cited length statistics and the pipeline's known resampling resolution, not a value reported in any source.
- With that few points, a "profile" in the RCA/LAD/LCx sense (dozens to 100+ points capturing spatial trends in curvature/torsion/diameter along a long vessel) is not really available for the LM; at best it is a short segment profile of a handful of points, and for the ≤5 mm short-trunk subgroup it is effectively 2-3 samples plus the bifurcation point itself, i.e., not meaningfully different from scalar features (start diameter, end diameter, mean curvature, bifurcation angle).
- Because LM length itself varies severalfold across patients (short vs. long trunk categories), any fixed-length input (a per-point profile with padding/truncation) would need heavy padding for most cases, which is a practical argument for scalar/aggregate features (mean, min/max curvature, length, ostial and distal diameter) over a padded per-point sequence.

### Gaps
- The MSCT paper's full stratified results (LM length vs. sex, age, diameter, dominance, branching pattern) could not be retrieved beyond the title/abstract; only the general length-distribution literature above was accessible.
- No source gave a full population histogram or explicit percentile breakdown (e.g., 5th/95th percentile) of LM length; the "short/medium/long" categorical scheme and single mean±SD values are the best available granularity.
- No literature was found reporting how many discrete points a segmentation/centerline pipeline typically extracts for the LM specifically, since this is a modeling/pipeline choice rather than a clinical measurement; the estimate above is this note's own inference, not a cited fact.

## Is LM bifurcation angle (LAD-LCx) an established CAD/plaque biomarker?

### Takeaway
Yes — the LM/LAD-LCx bifurcation angle is one of the most studied coronary geometric biomarkers, with multiple CT-based studies reporting quantitative associations with stenosis severity and plaque location, but the association is inconsistent in direction and weakens or becomes non-significant after multivariate adjustment for risk factors in at least one study, so it should be treated as a real but modest, not a definitive, independent predictor.
### Cited Findings
- In a dual-source CT study of 106 patients (126 vessels, 318 measured bifurcation angles), the LAD-LCx angle differed by stenosis category (diastole): <50% stenosis 68.3° ± 18.0°, 50-69% stenosis 91.3° ± 29.8°, 70-100% stenosis 80.0° ± 19.2°, correlating with stenosis severity (r = 0.217, P = 0.026); an optimal cutoff of 78° classified significant left coronary stenosis with sensitivity 66.7%, specificity 78.4%, AUC 0.719 (0.805 restricted to non-calcified plaque), and bifurcation angle was an independent predictor on multivariate analysis (OR 1.423, P = 0.002, 95% CI 1.140-1.777) — [PLOS ONE: Quantification of left coronary bifurcation angles and plaques](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0174352)
- The same study found non-calcified-plaque vessels had wider LAD-LCx angles than calcified-plaque vessels at systole (79.5° ± 26.6° vs. 69.8° ± 19.2°, P = 0.034), and that >90% of plaques localized opposite the flow divider, with the ostial LAD the single most common plaque site (64% of patients) — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0174352)
- A separate CTA study of 313 patients (three groups: ≥50% stenosis n=211, atherosclerosis <50% stenosis n=62, normal n=40) found mean LM bifurcation angle rising with disease severity (normal 75.53° ± 14.71°, atherosclerosis 81.16° ± 20.06°, ≥50% stenosis 87.34° ± 18.84°; overall 84.7° ± 19.0°, range 35.8-141.8°), significant on univariate comparison (P < 0.001) between the stenosis and normal groups, but the association became non-significant on multivariate analysis (P = 0.064) after adjusting for confounders such as male sex and obesity — [PMC: Comparison of LM bifurcating angle among normal, non-significantly, and significantly stenosed groups](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5431433/)
- A computational-atlas normal-anatomy cohort (n=300, zero calcium score) reports a normal-population baseline LAD-LCx angle of 89° ± 21° when an intermediate/ramus branch is present versus 75° ± 23° without one (P < 0.001), and LM ostial diameter 3.7 mm (IQR 3.2-4.1 mm) — giving a disease-free reference range against which the disease-cohort numbers above can be compared — [EuroIntervention computational atlas](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- The presence of a ramus intermedius (trifurcation rather than bifurcation) at the LM is itself studied as a modifier of bifurcation-region atherosclerosis — [CCTA study on ramus intermedius and atherosclerosis at the LM bifurcation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10091592/) (title/topic only; full quantitative content not fetched)
- Dedicated angle-outcome work exists at the level of LM disease itself: a study on 3D angle assessment and plaque-distribution classification in left main disease reports that LM bifurcation geometry (angle) and plaque distribution pattern impact clinical/angiographic outcome after LM PCI — [Three-Dimensional Angle Assessment and Plaque Distribution Classification in Left Main Disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26198560/) (abstract-level; full quantitative values not retrieved, see Gaps)
- Formal bifurcation-lesion classification frameworks (Medina classification: binary 1/0 scoring of proximal main / distal main / side-branch involvement; SYNTAX score's bifurcation-lesion scoring, which adds 1 point for a bifurcation without a diseased side branch ≥50% and 2 points when the side branch is involved) are the standard clinical morphology systems for bifurcation lesions including the LM, but in dedicated bifurcation-classification research, side-branch angle specifically was found to not be predictive of the choice of treatment strategy, whereas lesion complexity and side-branch size were — [Medina/SYNTAX bifurcation lesion definitions](https://sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/coronary-bifurcation-lesion); [Bifurcation classification schemes: impact of lesion morphology on treatment strategy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20495521/); [SYNTAX score tutorial definitions](https://syntaxscore.org/index.php/tutorial/definitions)

### Inferences
- Bifurcation angle is unambiguously a well-studied, quantitatively characterized geometric biomarker for the LM specifically (unlike torsion — see below); it is a strong candidate scalar feature for this thesis regardless of whether LM is otherwise modeled with a per-point profile.
- The two disease-association studies point in the same qualitative direction (wider angle associated with more disease/stenosis) but disagree on strength after adjustment — one keeps it significant and independent (OR 1.423) on multivariate analysis, the other loses significance (P = 0.064) after adjustment for risk factors. This is a genuine inconsistency, not something to silently resolve; the honest framing is "angle correlates with LM/LAD-LCx disease in unadjusted comparisons across at least two independent cohorts, but its status as an independent predictor once other risk factors are controlled for is unsettled."
- Medina/SYNTAX are lesion-severity classification schemes, not geometric-angle biomarkers per se — they encode where along the bifurcation a stenosis sits, not the angle value itself. They are relevant as a labeling scheme for validating any learned angle-based feature against clinically recognized bifurcation involvement patterns, but they don't supply an angle threshold or continuous biomarker on their own.

### Gaps
- Full quantitative values (angle ranges, effect sizes, outcome hazard ratios) from the dedicated "3D Angle Assessment and Plaque Distribution Classification in Left Main Disease" outcome study (PubMed 26198560) were not retrieved beyond its abstract-level summary; this would be a high-value paper to read in full given it directly studies LM-disease outcome vs. geometry.
- The ramus-intermedius / trifurcation-vs-bifurcation modifier paper (PMC10091592) was located but not fetched for quantitative content.
- No source gave a single, universally agreed "normal range" for LAD-LCx angle; reported normal-cohort means range from ~75° (no ramus) to ~89° (with ramus) in one cohort and a median of 88.5° (IQR 68.8-101.4°) in another cited search snippet, so any threshold this thesis adopts should be sourced explicitly rather than treated as textbook-fixed.

## LM diameter/caliber: independent predictor, reference/normalizing value, or both?

### Takeaway
LM diameter is used in the literature both as a direct measurement of interest (ostial/proximal caliber, with a normal-population reference around 3.7 mm) and, more distinctively for this vessel, as the anchor value in bifurcation fractal "branching laws" (Finet's law, Murray's law, Huo-Kassab's law) that predict or normalize the expected combined diameter of the daughter vessels (LAD + LCx) from the parent LM diameter — a role essentially unique to a bifurcation-parent vessel and not applicable to non-bifurcating segments of LAD/LCx/RCA in the same way.

### Cited Findings
- Normal-population LM ostial diameter: 3.7 mm mean, IQR 3.2-4.1 mm (n=300, zero-calcium-score cohort) — [EuroIntervention computational atlas](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- Finet's law: proximal main vessel reference diameter = 0.678 × (sum of the two daughter-vessel reference diameters), i.e., RefD_PMV = 0.678 × (RefD_DMV + RefD_SB) — [Fractal Laws for Bifurcation QCA to Assess LM Bifurcation Lesions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12145935)
- Murray's law (cubic form): RefD_PMV³ = RefD_DMV³ + RefD_SB³ — [Fractal Laws for Bifurcation QCA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12145935); Finet's law has also been shown to be a special case of a generalized Murray's law — [Finet's Law as a Special Case of the Generalised Murray's Law](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1007/s42979-019-0040-x)
- Huo-Kassab's law uses a 7/3 exponent rather than cubic or linear: RefD_PMV^(7/3) = RefD_DMV^(7/3) + RefD_SB^(7/3) — [Fractal Laws for Bifurcation QCA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12145935)
- Applying Finet's law to bifurcation-QCA for LM lesion assessment showed only "weak" correlation with invasive iFR functional measurement (Pearson r = -0.326) versus "moderate" correlation for standard bifurcation-QCA alone (r = -0.402), and applying Finet's law reclassified (downgraded) 8 lesions from Medina 1,0,0 to 0,0,0 — i.e., the fractal-law-derived reference diameter can be "oversensitive" and change lesion classification versus direct QCA — [Fractal Laws for Bifurcation QCA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12145935)
- Adding Finet's law to standard angiographic sizing of the ostial LM (for stent sizing) improved long-term outcomes after LM stenting — [Usefulness of the Finet law to guide stent size selection in ostial LM stenting](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1553838918301349) (abstract-level; not fully fetched)
- In a hemodynamic-surrogate study of 127 patients with suspected CAD but no significant stenosis, Finet's Ratio (the daughter-vessel-to-parent diameter ratio) significantly correlated with low Time-Averaged Endothelial Shear Stress (p < 0.001), and vessel diameters plus branch curvature independently correlated with high Oscillatory Shear Index and high Relative Residence Time (both adverse hemodynamic/atherogenic markers, p < 0.01-0.001) — [Assessing LM Bifurcation Anatomy and Haemodynamics as a Potential Surrogate for Disease Risk](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-73490-w) / [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39747107/)
- A separate note found in search snippets states Finet's ratio "showed the lowest variation within study cohorts, suggesting small changes in Finet's ratio have a substantial impact on haemodynamics" — [arxiv: Assessing LM Bifurcation Anatomy and Haemodynamics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12504) (PDF binary; content not directly extractable, corroborated via the Nature/PubMed version above)

### Inferences
- LM diameter functions in a genuinely dual role that is architecturally distinctive versus LAD/LCx/RCA trunk segments: (1) a plain per-point/scalar caliber measurement like any other vessel, and (2) the numerator/reference term in fractal branching laws that make the LM diameter a *normalizing context* for whatever diameter features are computed on the daughter-vessel (LAD, LCx) profiles. If this thesis's other agents are building LAD/LCx diameter features, an LM-anchored Finet/Murray ratio is a natural, literature-grounded cross-vessel feature (e.g., observed daughter diameter sum vs. Finet-predicted value) that could not exist without also modeling the LM.
- The fact that Finet's-law-derived reference diameters can reclassify lesion severity (Medina category changes) versus raw QCA is a caution: a model feature built from a fractal-law-normalized diameter is not a neutral measurement, it encodes an assumption (self-similar branching) that measurably disagrees with directly observed diameter in a meaningful fraction of real bifurcations.

### Gaps
- No source gave a large-cohort, disease-vs-control comparison of raw LM diameter itself (as opposed to angle or Finet ratio) analogous to the angle comparisons in Question 2; the only diameter statistic located is a normal/no-stenosis reference cohort (3.7 mm, IQR 3.2-4.1 mm), not a stenotic-cohort comparator.
- The Finet-law stent-sizing outcome paper (ScienceDirect, S1553838918301349) was identified but its full outcome statistics were not fetched.

## Is LM tortuosity/torsion a meaningful, studied concept given the vessel's short length?

### Takeaway
Evidence is sparse and, where it exists, tends to treat "LM tortuosity" as part of a combined LM-LAD or LM-LCx segment rather than isolating the LM trunk itself; no source was found that computes or reports curvature/torsion specifically confined to the LM's own short length, and one directly relevant CFD study explicitly analyzed LM-LAD/LM-LCx tortuosity as combined segments rather than the isolated LM. This is consistent with the underlying anatomical argument in the task: a vessel this short offers too little arclength to define a stable curvature/torsion profile on its own.

### Cited Findings
- General coronary tortuosity is clinically defined as ≥3 consecutive curvatures of 90-180° (severe: ≥2 consecutive curvatures ≥180°) in a major epicardial artery ≥2 mm diameter at end-diastole — a definition built for long, winding vessels (classically RCA/LAD), not short trunk segments — [Coronary Artery Tortuosity in Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circinterventions.114.001676)
- A CFD study based on in vivo dynamic CT explicitly measured tortuosity for three combined segment-pairs — LM-LAD, LM-LCx, and LAD-LCx — using the formula tortuosity(%) = 100·(d/L - 1), where d is the actual path length and L is the straight-line distance between endpoints; it did not isolate or separately analyze curvature/torsion of the LM trunk alone — [Tortuosity of Coronary Bifurcation as a Potential Local Risk Factor for Atherosclerosis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7574594/)
- That same study found LM-LAD tortuosity strongly correlated with low wall shear stress at the proximal LAD (R² = 0.719, p = 0.008), modeled as low-WSS_LAD = A·Tortuosity_LM-LAD + B, and concluded LM-LAD tortuosity could be "a surrogate marker for the onset of atherosclerosis" — but again, this is a combined LM+LAD path-length measure, not an LM-only geometric descriptor — [PMC7574594](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7574594/)
- A separate hemodynamic-surrogate study reports that "vessel diameters and curvature" (plural branches, evaluated per daughter vessel of the LM bifurcation) correlated with high Oscillatory Shear Index (p < 0.001) and that "daughter branch curvature" (i.e., LAD/LCx curvature just past the bifurcation, not LM curvature) independently correlated with high Relative Residence Time (p < 0.01) — again placing the curvature measurement on the daughter branches rather than on the LM trunk itself — [Assessing LM Bifurcation Anatomy and Haemodynamics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-73490-w)
- Optimized tortuosity-measurement methodology work (not LM-specific) found that curvature-based tortuosity measures (e.g., average-absolute-curvature) correlate more strongly with low wall shear stress than simpler tortuosity indices, "with average-absolute-curvature showing the highest coefficient of determination across all left main branches" — implying at least one methods paper computed curvature separately per branch at the LM bifurcation, though the exact isolated-LM-trunk result was not retrievable from the snippet — [Accuracy of vascular tortuosity measures using computational modelling](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-04796-w) / [PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/) (full text not fetched; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- No source treats the LM trunk as a standalone tortuosity/torsion object. Every tortuosity/curvature measurement found in the literature that touches the LM either (a) spans a combined LM+daughter-vessel path (LM-LAD, LM-LCx) or (b) is computed on the daughter branch just distal to the bifurcation, not on the LM itself. This is strong indirect evidence that isolated-LM torsion is not an established or even attempted biomarker in the literature reviewed, most plausibly because ~10 mm of vessel gives too little arclength for a torsion/curvature estimate to be numerically stable or clinically distinguishable from noise/measurement error, matching the task's working hypothesis.
- Where LM geometry does interact with tortuosity-style analysis productively, it is via the *bifurcation angle* and the combined-segment path-length tortuosity measure above, not via a per-point torsion profile. This reinforces treating LM as scalar/bifurcation-level features (angle, Finet ratio, ostial and distal diameter, LM-LAD and LM-LCx path tortuosity as two scalars) rather than attempting a dense curvature/torsion sampling series confined to the LM segment.

### Gaps
- No study was found that reports a torsion (3D out-of-plane twist, as distinct from planar curvature) value for the LM at all, isolated or combined; all located "tortuosity" measures are either path-length-ratio based (d/L) or curvature-based (2D/3D bending), not true torsion in the Frenet-frame sense. If this thesis's curvature-profile pipeline computes torsion specifically (as its RCA work reportedly does), there is no LM-specific precedent to benchmark against — this should be flagged explicitly as an evidence gap rather than assumed away.
- The vascular-tortuosity-measures methodology paper (PMC8764056) was located but not fetched in full; it may contain an isolated-LM curvature result that would refine the above.

## Beyond bifurcation angle: what other geometric/morphological predictors of LM disease exist?

### Takeaway
Beyond bifurcation angle, the literature converges on a small cluster of related morphology parameters, mean daughter-vessel diameters/curvature, Finet's ratio, and (in one recent exploratory index) a combined multi-parameter "LM anatomy risk index", as candidate structural predictors of adverse hemodynamics and, by extension, disease risk at the LM; ramus-intermedius presence and 3D plaque-distribution classification are additional recognized morphological factors, but none of these have the depth of validation that bifurcation angle itself has.

### Cited Findings
- A 2024 study modeling suspected-CAD patients without significant stenosis (n=127) used a defined panel of anatomical parameters: bifurcation angle(s), inflow angle, mean curvatures of the branches, mean diameters of the branches, and Finet's ratio, and linked several of these individually to adverse endothelial shear-stress metrics (low TAESS, high OSI, high RRT) as described above — [Assessing LM Bifurcation Anatomy and Haemodynamics as a Potential Surrogate for Disease Risk](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-73490-w)
- A newly identified (2026) "Left main coronary anatomy risk index (LMARI)" is described as an exploratory composite index for summarizing LMCA morphology to identify anatomical features that could pose risk, derived from a donor-body anatomical study; the paper's full methodology and constituent variables could not be retrieved (paywalled), so its specific formula/variables are a named-but-unverified lead rather than a confirmed finding — [Left main coronary anatomy risk index: A donor body study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949916X26000289) (access blocked, HTTP 403; title/topic only)
- Presence of a ramus intermedius (LM trifurcation rather than simple bifurcation) is studied as a distinct anatomical variant associated with atherosclerosis patterns at the LM bifurcation, and is also linked in the computational-atlas data to a wider baseline LAD-LCx angle (89° ± 21° with ramus vs. 75° ± 23° without, P < 0.001) — [CCTA study on Ramus Intermedius and Atherosclerosis at LM bifurcation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10091592/); [EuroIntervention computational atlas](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- Dedicated LM-disease outcome literature exists that classifies LM lesions by 3D bifurcation angle and by plaque-distribution pattern and links these to interventional/angiographic outcome, indicating morphology-outcome linkage is an active clinical research area for LM specifically (beyond generic bifurcation-angle-vs-stenosis correlation studies) — [Three-Dimensional Angle Assessment and Plaque Distribution Classification in Left Main Disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26198560/)
- Standard clinical narrative reviews of LM disease (pathophysiology/diagnosis/treatment) exist and would be a reasonable secondary source for framing LM disease's clinical stakes, though this note did not extract quantitative geometric content from them — [Left main coronary artery disease: pathophysiology, diagnosis, and treatment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41569-018-0001-4)

### Inferences
- The strongest, best-replicated non-angle morphological signal specific to the LM bifurcation is Finet's ratio (daughter-diameter-to-parent-diameter), which appears in both the fractal-law/QCA literature (Question 3) and the hemodynamic-surrogate literature (this question) as a significant correlate of adverse shear-stress metrics. This is a stronger evidence base than exists for LM torsion/curvature in isolation, and supports including Finet's ratio as a scalar LM feature if the thesis wants a geometry-derived, literature-grounded LM predictor beyond angle.
- The LMARI index is a promising but currently unverifiable lead: it is recent (2026), from a donor-body (cadaveric) rather than in-vivo CT study, and this note could not access its content. It should be flagged to the user as "worth chasing down directly" rather than cited as settled evidence.
- Taken together with Questions 1 and 4, the overall body of LM-specific morphology literature (angle, Finet ratio, diameter, ramus-intermedius status, combined-segment tortuosity) is built almost entirely from scalar or low-dimensional features computed at or near the bifurcation point, not from dense arclength profiles. This is an independent line of evidence, on top of the pure length argument in Question 1, for why LM is a poor fit for the same per-point profile architecture used for RCA/LAD/LCx: the clinical/biomedical literature studying this exact vessel has itself converged on scalar bifurcation-level descriptors rather than profile-based ones.

### Gaps
- LMARI (donor-body study) could not be read past the title due to a paywall (HTTP 403); its exact constituent variables and validation cohort size are unknown.
- The 3D-angle/plaque-distribution LM-disease outcome paper (PubMed 26198560) was not read beyond abstract-level summary; its specific angle thresholds and outcome statistics (e.g., hazard ratios, event rates by plaque-distribution class) remain a gap.
- No dedicated machine-learning or biomarker-panel paper was found that already combines LM morphology features (angle, Finet ratio, diameter) into a single predictive score validated against hard CAD/MACE outcomes in a large in-vivo cohort; existing "risk index" work is either hemodynamic-surrogate (CFD-based, n=127) or the not-yet-readable LMARI donor-body study.
