# Recent (2010–2026) human coronary studies relating lumen taper to plaque

Scope and method. These notes build on `Why lumen taper as a feature/taper_clinical.md` and `taper_other_vascular_beds.md` and do not repeat them (Javier 1995, Zubaid 2002, Dodge 1992, Seiler 1992, the V/M studies, Huo 2013 Radiology and Huo 2013 pigs, Wu 2024 tapering lesions, TAG are all covered there). The search used Europe PMC (title/abstract queries combining coronary with tapering, taper ratio/index, diameter gradient/decrement, reverse/abnormal/loss of tapering, lumen/vessel tapering, diffuse disease, vessel/lumen volume with plaque progression, geometric features with plaque development) and web search. Access levels are stated per source. No preprints were used as evidence. The Shen 2026 review and arXiv 2507.17109 were not used.

**Headline.** The 2010–2026 literature has almost nothing on lumen taper as an exposure for plaque. Europe PMC queries combining coronary taper terms with plaque or atherosclerosis returned mostly case reports, stent papers and drug "tapering" hits. The few relevant studies are listed below, ranked by relevance to the thesis. None is longitudinal with taper as the exposure, and none is in a population cohort.

## Q1. Has any 2010–2026 study related coronary tapering to plaque or atherosclerosis?

### Takeaway
Only one human study was found that measured a taper metric next to an atherosclerosis measure. In 51 symptomatic women without obstructive disease (WISE), angiographic tapering z-scores correlated weakly with an angiographic atheroma severity score. They were not related to coronary flow reserve, although the abstract wording suggests that they were. Every other "taper" paper of this period uses taper as a fixed normal reference, either to detect lesions or to correct pressure-drop and FFR models. None tests it as a risk factor.

### Cited Findings
- **Pacheco C et al. 2021, Am Heart J Plus 1:100027** (rank 1 of what exists). DOI 10.1016/j.ahjo.2021.100027. Open access; I read the full text via Europe PMC (PMC10976284).
  - Design: a cross-sectional substudy of WISE-CVD Continuation in **51 symptomatic women referred for invasive coronary function testing**. All had suspected INOCA, with no ≥50% stenosis.
  - Taper definition: QCA per CASS segment of the left main and the proximal, mid and distal LAD. Antegrade and retrograde tapering are compared with population-referenced values, giving an "Antegrade Tapering Z Score (ATZ = [AT − AT pb] / SD AT pb)" that "evaluates distal tapering with regards to the more proximal artery diameter in a given coronary artery segment", and a Retrograde Tapering Z Score that "assesses proximal tapering with respect to a more distal artery diameter".
  - Finding on atherosclerosis: "Some AMCA measurements directly correlated with CSS (Maximal absolute antegrade tapering z-score r = 0.29, p = 0.04; maximal absolute retrograde tapering z-score r = 0.33, p = 0.02)". CSS is an angiographic atherosclerosis extent and severity score. Also: "maximal retrograde tapering z-score was higher (more adverse) than expected population-referenced values" (absolute maximal RTZ 6.87 ± 4.65 SD).
  - Finding on CFR: "average and absolute maximal antegrade tapering z-score nor retrograde tapering z-score did not [correlate with CFR]". In the adjusted model, average ATZ gave p = 0.559 and average RTZ p = 0.886.
  - **Caveat:** the abstract calls RDZ a "tapering reference diameter Z score" and concludes that "atheroma burden, size and tapering are related to CFR". The full text shows that RDZ is a reference-diameter (size) score and that the tapering z-scores were not related to CFR. Cite the full text, not the abstract.
  - Limits: small sample, referred, women only, cross-sectional and angiographic. Taper is scored as an absolute deviation from a reference, so the direction is lost. Taper here can easily be a consequence of plaque. — [Europe PMC full text](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10976284); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ahjo.2021.100027)
- **Kang D, Slomka PJ, Nakazato R, Arsanjani R, Cheng VY, Min JK et al. 2013, Med Phys 40:041912** (rank 3; methodological). DOI 10.1118/1.4794480. I read the abstract only.
  - Design: 42 CCTA datasets from a referred clinical population, with expert consensus reads as the reference.
  - Taper use: lesions are detected with "a multi-pass algorithm which considers expected or 'normal' vessel tapering and luminal stenosis". "Expected luminal diameter is derived from the scan by automated piecewise least squares line fitting over proximal and mid segments (67%) of the coronary artery considering the locations of the small branches."
  - Finding: the algorithm "identified 42 lesions (93%) confirmed by the expert readers".
  - Relevance: this treats a linear taper along the artery as the healthy baseline on CCTA, and treats deviation from it as plaque. It supports the idea that plaque changes the measured taper (reverse causation). It is not evidence that taper predicts plaque. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1118/1.4794480)
- **Schrauwen JT, Wentzel JJ, van der Steen AF, Gijsen FJ 2014, J Biomech 47:1810** (rank 5; physiology, not plaque). DOI 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2014.03.028. I read the abstract only.
  - Design: 22 mildly diseased human arteries imaged with CT and IVUS, and CFD.
  - Finding: "Tapering and curvature added significantly to the total △p, accounting for 31.4±19.0% and 18.0±10.9% respectively at Re=250."
  - Relevance: this shows that measured taper in human arteries is large enough to matter hemodynamically. That is indirect support for the WSS rationale, with no plaque outcome. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2014.03.028)
- Tu S et al. 2021, Catheter Cardiovasc Interv (μQFR, n = 306 patients, 330 vessels; abstract only). The study replaces the "linear tapering of the reference vessel size" with "a step-down reference diameter function … based on the Murray bifurcation fractal law". Taper here is a physiology modelling assumption, not a plaque predictor. It is relevant only because it says real taper is stepwise at side branches rather than linear. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ccd.29592)
- An extended web search for CCTA "luminal tapering" with "plaque burden" found no study linking the two. — search, 2026-11-07 (no source to cite; recorded as absence).

### Inferences
- Pacheco 2021 is the only post-2010 human study I found in which a coronary taper metric goes with atherosclerosis. The association is weak, cross-sectional, in a referred population, and plausibly plaque causing abnormal taper rather than the reverse.
- The CCTA lesion-detection literature (Kang 2013) assumes that healthy arteries follow a smooth expected taper and that plaque appears as a deviation from it. This reinforces the point already made in the earlier notes: taper measured in diseased arteries is partly an effect of disease.

### Gaps
- No CCTA study of "taper ratio", "taper index", "diameter decrement" or "reverse tapering" with plaque presence or location was found.
- No PARADIGM, CONFIRM, ICONIC, SCOT-HEART, SCAPIS or CGPS secondary analysis reporting taper or a lumen-size gradient was found.
- The Mancini AMCA population reference values (refs 6, 7 and 10 in Pacheco 2021) predate 2010 and were not opened.

## Q2. Any longitudinal study in which baseline taper or a diameter gradient predicted later plaque or progression?

### Takeaway
None was found. The longitudinal CCTA studies with geometric exposures use location, bifurcation and tortuosity (ICONIC), or radiomics around myocardial bridges. Neither includes an explicit taper feature. PARADIGM progression analyses use lumen or vessel volume and plaque burden, not taper.

### Cited Findings
- **Han D, Lin A, Kuronuma K, … Dey D et al. 2022, JAMA Cardiol 7:309** (rank 2; the closest longitudinal geometry design). DOI 10.1001/jamacardio.2021.5705. I read the abstract; a PMC version exists (PMC8792800).
  - Design: an ICONIC nested case-control study of **referred patients** (116 ACS cases and 116 matched controls, 548 lesions), with geometry measured at lesions that already had plaque at baseline.
  - Exposures: "(1) distance from the coronary ostium to lesion; (2) location at vessel bifurcations; and (3) vessel tortuosity". **Taper is not among the adverse geometric characteristics.**
  - Finding: "an increasing number of AGCs was associated with a greater risk of future culprit lesions (hazard ratio [HR] for 1 AGC, 2.90 … HR for ≥2 AGCs, 6.84)", and AGCs added discrimination (AUC 0.766 vs 0.733).
  - Relevance: this is a precedent that CCTA geometry adds to lesion-level prediction. It concerns events at existing plaque, though, not incident plaque, and it has no taper. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2021.5705)
- **Chen YC, Zheng J, Zhou F, Tao XW et al. 2024, Eur Heart J Cardiovasc Imaging 25:1462** (rank 4). DOI 10.1093/ehjci/jeae135. Paywalled; the main text was read through the publisher page, but not the supplement.
  - Design: a retrospective serial CCTA study of **hospital patients with LAD myocardial bridging and no proximal plaque at index**. There were 192 in the development set and 103 in external validation from four tertiary hospitals.
  - Endpoint: "proximal plaque development of LAD MB in follow-up CCTA".
  - Finding: "The pMB CS vascular radiomics model exhibited higher AUCs in training, internal test, and external sets (AUC = 0.78, 0.75, 0.75) than the clinical and anatomical model."
  - Features: the top-ranked features named in the main text are "DistanceCorrect_Kurtosis … of pMB CL" and "MinorAxis1_Kurtosis … of pMB CS", with "MajorAxis2_Energy" the most negative feature. These are distributions of cross-sectional axis lengths and of systolic–diastolic centreline displacement. **No taper or diameter-gradient feature is named in the main text**; the full lists are in the supplement, which I did not see.
  - Clinical and anatomical model: "none of the anatomical variables reached statistical significance", so the model reduced to diabetes alone.
  - A Bayer employee is a co-author.
  - Relevance: this is the only study found with a plaque-free-at-baseline segment and incident plaque on serial CCTA that uses lumen-shape features. It is restricted to bridged LADs and a phase-dependent mechanism, so it does not support taper specifically. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjci/jeae135)
- **Tanaka S, Okada K, Kitahara H et al. 2023, Int J Cardiol** (abstract only). This is serial IVUS of the first 50 mm of the LAD in 103 heart-transplant recipients, split into proximal, middle and distal thirds. "MB patients demonstrated significantly augmented intimal formation in the proximal LAD". This is segment-wise intimal growth, not taper, in a transplant population. — [DOI 10.1016/j.ijcard.2023.03.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcard.2023.03.014)
- **PARADIGM progression papers** (for example van Rosendael SE et al. 2023, JACC CVI, 649 statin-treated patients): "lumen volume" at baseline differed between patients with and without atherosclerotic progression, but was not independently associated with it. Lumen volume is whole-tree calibre, not taper. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jcmg.2022.10.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcmg.2022.10.017)

### Inferences
- No study, old or recent, has tested baseline lumen taper as a predictor of incident plaque. The thesis can state this as a gap, worded as "no study has tested" and not as "taper is known to predict".
- The two closest designs (Han 2022, Chen 2024) both come from referred or hospital cohorts and both lack an explicit taper term. They support the general claim that CCTA lumen geometry carries prognostic information, but not the taper hypothesis itself.

### Gaps
- The Chen 2024 supplementary feature lists were not seen. A taper-like centreline feature cannot be fully excluded, but none appears in the main text.
- No serial IVUS or OCT study of native arteries with baseline taper as the exposure was found.

## Q3. Studies in plaque-free or minimally diseased arteries, and normal CCTA taper reference values

### Takeaway
No post-2010 CCTA source giving normal taper reference values (slope or ratio per segment) was found. Normal-calibre CCTA work (Medrano-Gracia atlas, Welch 2022, Langer 2021; see earlier notes) reports diameters and angles, not taper. The "population-referenced" taper norms used in Pacheco 2021 are angiographic and older.

### Cited Findings
- The Pacheco 2021 taper z-scores are referenced to pooled angiographic normal populations ("Derived from pooled population-based studies examining coronary artery dimensions [6, 8, 9]"). Those references predate 2010. — [Europe PMC full text](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10976284)
- Kang 2013 derives "expected" taper per scan by line fitting over the proximal 67% of each artery, rather than from external normal values. This implies that no CCTA taper norm was available to its authors. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1118/1.4794480)
- A web search for normal CCTA taper reference values returned only patent boilerplate. The patents state that taper is assumed linear "though other forms (log, quadratic) are possible", which is not peer reviewed. — [USPTO patent 11367190](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11367190)

### Inferences
- Taper slopes measured by the tracer in the CGPS plaque-free cohort would themselves be new descriptive reference data for a population sample. That is a minor secondary contribution, not a claim for the review.

### Gaps
- No follow-up of the Medrano-Gracia atlas (2016/2017) with taper or outcomes was found.

## Q4. AI, deep-learning or radiomics papers where taper or diameter-gradient features ranked as important for plaque

### Takeaway
None found. Chen 2024 (above) is the closest, and its top features are cross-sectional axis distributions and phase displacement, not taper. CCTA machine-learning work on ischemia ranks stenosis and low-density plaque volume, not centreline geometry.

### Cited Findings
- Chen 2024: the top features "DistanceCorrect_Kurtosis" and "MinorAxis1_Kurtosis" show no taper in the main text. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjci/jeae135)
- Han 2022: the geometry features are distance from the ostium, bifurcation and tortuosity, with no taper. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2021.5705)
- A web search for centreline-based ML plaque prediction ranking diameter taper turned up only centreline-cube radiomics for revascularisation decisions (Denzinger et al., arXiv 1912.06075). That is a preprint, has no per-feature taper ranking, and **must not be cited as evidence**. — [arXiv 1912.06075 (preprint, flagged)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.06075)

### Inferences
- The feature-importance literature does not support the claim that taper is a known strong predictor. The review should not imply that it is.

### Gaps
- A SHAP or feature-importance analysis that includes an explicit lumen-slope feature for plaque was not found.

## Ranking by relevance to the thesis (most to least)

Rank 1 is Pacheco 2021: the only taper-plus-atherosclerosis association, which is cross-sectional, referred, n = 51 and weak. Rank 2 is Han 2022 ICONIC: a longitudinal geometry precedent with no taper, in a referred cohort. Rank 3 is Kang 2013: expected taper as the healthy CCTA baseline, which supports reverse causation. Rank 4 is Chen 2024: incident plaque on serial CCTA from a plaque-free segment using lumen-shape radiomics, but without taper and only in bridged LADs. Rank 5 is Schrauwen 2014: taper accounts for a sizeable share of pressure drop in human arteries, which is physiology only. All of them are in referred, symptomatic or transplant populations. None uses a general population, and none tests taper on plaque-free arteries against later plaque.

## Candidate review sentences (inference; wording for the thesis)

- "Clinical evidence on taper itself remains thin: in symptomatic women without obstructive disease, Pacheco et al. (2021) \cite{pacheco2021} found that larger deviations from the expected angiographic taper went with a higher angiographic atherosclerosis score, but not with coronary flow reserve, and the cross-sectional design leaves open whether the plaque altered the taper."
- "Automated CCTA lesion detection treats a smooth taper along the artery as the healthy reference and plaque as a departure from it (Kang et al., 2013 \cite{kang2013}), so taper measured in diseased arteries partly reflects the disease, which a plaque-free baseline avoids."
- "Longitudinal CCTA studies have related lesion location, bifurcations and tortuosity to later culprit lesions (Han et al., 2022 \cite{han2022}), but no study has tested whether lumen taper on a plaque-free baseline predicts who develops plaque."
