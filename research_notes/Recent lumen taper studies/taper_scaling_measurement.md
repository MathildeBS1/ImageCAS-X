# Recent work on expected coronary taper (scaling laws), deviations from it, and measuring taper on CCTA

Scope and method: Europe PMC REST abstracts plus web search and fetch, about 18 tool calls. This builds on `research_notes/Why lumen taper as a feature/taper_mechanism.md` and `taper_vs_alternatives.md`, which already hold Taylor et al. 2024 (meta-analysis, exponent 2.39), Kassab & Finet 2015, Gijsen 2019, Karau 2001, Seiler 1992, Medrano-Gracia 2016, Schultz 2023, TAG reproducibility, Lenell 2026 and the patent on sub-resolution lumen overestimation. Those are not repeated here except as cross-references.

Access levels: **OA** means open access (full text read where stated). **Abstract** means only the abstract was read. **PW** means paywalled. No preprints were used. No Shen 2026 review and no arXiv 2507.17109 were used. Shen et al. 2025 (R Soc Open Sci, helical flow, a Beier-group study) came up in a search but is not used because it is off topic. Species is given wherever the study is not in humans.

**Relevance ranking** (for the thesis claim that taper should follow flow loss and can be measured on CCTA):
1. Choi et al. 2020, Physiol Rep (human CCTA; diameter scales with downstream length and myocardial mass; Theil–Sen used).
2. Huo et al. 2013, Radiology (human CCTA; deviation from the scaling law as a marker of diffuse disease).
3. Huo et al. 2013, J R Soc Interface (pig CT; positive remodelling, i.e. a lumen too wide for its tree, is detected as a deviation).
4. Medrano-Gracia et al. 2017, J Cardiovasc Transl Res (normal CCTA; the Huo–Kassab diameter law fits best).
5. Tu et al. 2021, Catheter Cardiovasc Interv, and Weng et al. 2024, EuroIntervention (a Murray-law step-down "expected" reference diameter along the artery; closest to an expected-versus-observed taper).
6. Huo & Kassab 2016, J Biomech (review); Gong et al. 2016, Am J Physiol Heart (pig; laws break in heart failure); Chen et al. 2015 (mouse; ageing).
7. Measurement: Skowronski et al. 2018 (CCTA normal lumen values by segment), Shahzad et al. 2013 and Kirişli et al. 2013 (expected healthy lumen profile; standardised lumen evaluation framework), and Huo 2013 Radiology (CT diameter error against casts).

## Q1. Recent scaling-law work on how diameter should change along coronary arteries, and whether CT-measured deviations relate to disease or WSS

### Takeaway
The Huo–Kassab laws give an explicit expected relation between a segment's diameter and the tree it supplies: D ∝ L^(3/7) against downstream (crown) length, D ∝ M^(3/8) against myocardial mass, and Q ∝ D^(7/3). One human CCTA study (Choi 2020) confirmed significant power laws of this kind in arteries without obstructive disease, although the fitted exponents were lower than the theoretical ones. On CT, deviation from the length–volume law separated metabolic-syndrome patients from controls (Huo 2013 Radiology, humans). It also detected diffuse positive remodelling (Huo 2013 Interface, pigs), which is the "too wide for its tree" state. The laws were preserved in hypertrophy but broke in heart failure (pigs). **No study links a CT-measured deviation from expected taper to WSS or to incident plaque.**

### Cited Findings
- **Choi et al. 2020, Physiol Rep 8(14):e14514, DOI 10.14814/phy2.14514. OA, full text read.** Retrospective study of 43 patients with CCTA showing no obstructive lesion and 638 epicardial arteries. Myocardial mass came from Voronoi tessellation. Flow was estimated by QFR in 106 LAD/LCx arteries. Fits used OLS, Deming and **Theil–Sen** regression. Abstract: "A significant power‐law relationship was found among L–M, V–M, D–M, V–L, D–L, and V–D". Diameter–length exponent "0.277 ± 0.075" (OLS, adjusted), against a theoretical 3/7 (difference −36.0%). D–M "0.224 ± 0.082" against a theoretical 3/8. The Discussion says the D–M and D–L exponents "were lower than the theoretical values". Q–D was "b = 2.012, difference = −13.8%" against 7/3. Theil–Sen "uses the median of all slope lines through pairs of points and is less affected by outliers". Deming and Theil–Sen exponents were slightly higher than OLS. The authors attribute the low exponents mainly to truncation (small arteries not resolved) and the resting state. They also note that CT may miss mild diffuse atherosclerosis, "which might contribute to these vessel diameter, decreased flow rate, and larger vessel volume‐to‐diameter ratio." Diameter was the proximal diameter per segment on a workstation, from 0.5–0.6 mm slices, with an automatic centerline "confirmed by reviewing cross sections". WSS is not discussed. Data inconsistency: QFR is reported for 95 vessels in one figure footnote and for 106 in the text. — [Choi et al. 2020 (PMC7387886)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)
- **Huo et al. 2013, Radiology 268(3):694–701, DOI 10.1148/radiol.13122181, PMCID PMC3750415. Abstract plus parts of the full text (author manuscript).** CCTA in 120 subjects (89 with metabolic syndrome and 31 age- and sex-matched controls). Abstract: the length–volume law "showed coefficients of 27.0 cm(-4/3) ± 9.0 (R(2) = 0.91 ± 0.08) for patients with metabolic syndrome and 19.9 cm(-4/3) ± 4.3 (R(2) = 0.92 ± 0.07) for control subjects (P < .05)"; "more than 65% of patients with metabolic syndrome had a coefficient of 23 or more ..., whereas approximately 90% of the control subjects had a coefficient of less than 23." Conclusion: "The retrospective scaling analysis provides a quantitative rationale for diagnosis of diffuse CAD." Full text: theoretical exponents "7/9 = 0.78 and 7/6 = 1.17" for the length–volume and length–area laws. Images were "reconstructed with a section thickness of 0.7 mm in 0.4-mm increments". Segmentation was automated at subvoxel precision, and the "root mean square error between optical and CT measurements was 0.16 mm" (validation against casts). Stated limitations: small intramyocardial arteries "less accurately determined with CT angiography were not considered", truncated terminal vessels were excluded, and "future studies are necessary to validate diffuse disease with intravascular US." Note: the reference is the control group, not a theoretical value, and the deviation is *too small* a lumen for the tree length (the opposite direction to the thesis hypothesis). — [Radiology record](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.13122181); [PMC3750415](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3750415/)
- **Huo et al. 2013, J R Soc Interface, DOI 10.1098/rsif.2012.1015, PMCID PMC3627114. Abstract. PIGS (Ossabaw, high-fat diet, n = 6 vs 6).** "Glagov's positive remodelling in the early stages of coronary atherosclerosis often results in plaque rupture and acute events." K_LV "decreased significantly in the high-fat diet group when compared with the control (14.6±2.6 versus 40.9±5.6)"; "The flow-length scaling power law, however, was nearly unaffected by the positive remodelling"; "K(LV) < 18 in the length-volume scaling relation is a good index of positive remodelling". — [Europe PMC / PMC3627114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3627114/)
- **Medrano-Gracia et al. 2017, J Cardiovasc Transl Res, DOI 10.1007/s12265-016-9720-2. OA, abstract read.** CCTA in 211 patients with "a zero calcium score, no stenoses", giving 446 bifurcations: "Physiological diameter laws were compared, with the Huo-Kassab model having the best fit." "Hypertension, smoking history and diabetes did not appear to have an association with shape." — [PMC5323506](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5323506/)
- **Huo & Kassab 2016, J Biomech, DOI 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2016.01.044. PW, abstract.** Review of intraspecific scaling laws. "The significance and shortcomings of these scaling laws are discussed in relation to diffuse coronary artery disease, Glagov's positive remodeling in early stages of coronary atherosclerosis ... and for estimation of outlet resistance values". — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1016%2Fj.jbiomech.2016.01.044)
- **Gong et al. 2016, Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol, DOI 10.1152/ajpheart.00084.2016. Abstract. PIGS.** The exponents in control hearts "were consistent with the theoretical predictions (i.e., 3, 7/9, and 7/3, respectively), which remained unchanged in LVH and RVH hearts"; "CHF increased the exponents of volume-diameter and flow-diameter scaling laws to 4.25 ± 1.50 and 3.15 ± 1.49". — [PMC6347071](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6347071/)
- **Chen et al. 2015, J R Soc Interface, DOI 10.1098/rsif.2015.0830. Abstract. MICE (micro-CT).** A "constant exponent of 3/4, but age-dependent scaling coefficients in a length-volume scaling law" during growth and ageing. — [PMC4707856](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4707856/)
- Already in the earlier notes, so only cross-referenced here: Taylor et al. 2024, the meta-analysis giving exponent 2.39 ([PMC11380967](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11380967/)); Kassab & Finet 2015 (exponent 3 implies uniform WSS; the real exponent is 7/3); Seiler 1992 (original; lumen scales with downstream mass and length, and CAD lumens are smaller than expected).
- WSS context on CCTA (in the earlier notes, so cross-reference only): in normal arteries, CCTA-based WSS "increase[s] rapidly and become[s] widely scattered with decreasing lumen diameter" and correlates with distance from the ostium (ρ = 0.22–0.62). — [Schultz et al. 2023, DOI 10.1007/s10554-022-02739-0, OA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9870961/)

### Inferences
- From the published laws to a per-segment taper (derivation, not in any paper): if D ∝ L_c^(3/7) holds along an artery, then d ln r/ds = (3/7)·d ln L_c/ds. Here L_c, the downstream crown length, drops at every side branch. Under Huo–Kassab, Q ∝ L_c, so the expected log-radius slope equals (3/7) times the log-flow slope. The thesis's Theil–Sen slope of log radius on arc length therefore estimates the left-hand side of a published law. The flow side is not observed on CCTA, because side branches are truncated, so the thesis's taper mixes the flow-matched part with the mismatch.
- Direction matters. Huo 2013 Radiology flags lumens *too small* for their tree (diffuse narrowing). Huo 2013 Interface (pigs) flags lumens *too large* (positive remodelling, low K_LV). The thesis hypothesis, a lumen wider than its flow and so low WSS, corresponds to the second pattern, so only pig evidence links it to atherosclerosis, and as a consequence of disease rather than a cause. In a plaque-free baseline this distinction must be stated, so that cause and effect are not reversed.
- Choi 2020's low CT exponents (D–L 0.28 vs 3/7) mean that empirical CT taper is flatter than the theory predicts. This is consistent with the patent-level concern about distal diameter overestimation in the earlier notes. It argues for standardising taper per segment rather than comparing it with a theoretical value.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that tests a CT-measured deviation from expected taper or diameter scaling against WSS, or against *incident* plaque in a population cohort. Huo 2013 Radiology is cross-sectional and case–control.
- The Huo & Kassab 2016 review was seen at abstract level only, so it cannot be quoted on segment-level taper.
- No recent Molloi-group human CT scaling paper was found within the budget. Molloi's work (lumen volume and myocardial mass) overlaps with the V/M literature in `research_notes/Per artery territory VM ratio/`.

## Q2. CCTA diameter and area profiles along the centerline: reference values, atlases, reproducibility of a slope

### Takeaway
Normal CCTA reference lumen values exist per *segment*, but as point values (proximal and mid segments, by sex and dominance; Skowronski 2018), not as slopes. Profile-based methods do exist: the atlas at 0.5 mm (earlier notes), and kernel-regression "expected healthy lumen" profiles in the Rotterdam framework (Shahzad 2013). Their accuracy against QCA is modest. Automated CT diameter error against casts is about 0.16 mm RMS (Huo 2013). **No published reproducibility (scan–rescan, inter-observer or ICC) of a lumen-diameter slope or taper on CCTA was found, which confirms the gap in the earlier notes.**

### Cited Findings
- **Skowronski et al. 2018, Am J Cardiol, DOI 10.1016/j.amjcard.2018.01.002. PW, abstract.** 201 of 2,849 CTA subjects had arteries "free from any sign of atherosclerosis (calcium score 0, no detectable plaque)". Lumen areas and diameters were measured "For all proximal and middle coronary segments". Values were smaller in women except the proximal RCA and LM. Dominance changed LM, pLCx and pRCA values (e.g. pRCA 3.0 ± 0.4 mm with left vs 4.1 ± 0.6 mm with right dominance). Distal segments and taper are not reported. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1016%2Fj.amjcard.2018.01.002)
- **Shahzad et al. 2013, Int J Cardiovasc Imaging. Abstract via search snippet; DOI not verified.** The expected healthy lumen diameter was estimated by "robust kernel regression applied to the coronary lumen diameter profile", and stenoses were found from the difference between the estimated and expected profiles. Against QCA, detection sensitivity was 29% and PPV 24%, with a 31% absolute average stenosis-degree difference. — [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/70894)
- **Kirişli et al. 2013, Med Image Anal, DOI 10.1016/j.media.2013.05.007. PW, abstract.** 48 multicentre, multivendor CTA datasets with an expert reference and 11 algorithms: "automatic lumen segmentation is possible with a precision similar to that obtained by experts." (Per-algorithm mm errors were not read.) — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1016%2Fj.media.2013.05.007)
- **Huo et al. 2013, Radiology (above):** automated CT vessel dimensions against optical cast measurement gave an RMS error of 0.16 mm, at 0.7 mm sections with 0.4 mm increments. — [PMC3750415](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3750415/)
- **Choi et al. 2020 (above):** used Theil–Sen to fit diameter scaling on CCTA. This gives precedent for the estimator, though across segments rather than along one segment. — [PMC7387886](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)
- **Medrano-Gracia 2017 (above):** a normal-population statistical shape atlas of bifurcation size, angle and curvature on CCTA, built from zero-calcium, stenosis-free scans. — [PMC5323506](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5323506/)
- A patent (not peer reviewed) describes a "lumen narrowing score", the ratio of actual to estimated healthy radius. The healthy radius comes from robust kernel regression, or from machine learning against "a database of healthy sections from a population of individuals". — [USPTO 11207045](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11207045)
- Already in the earlier notes: the atlas samples effective diameter every 0.5 mm (Medrano-Gracia 2016); TAG reproducibility is for an *attenuation* slope; and scan–rescan ICC ≥ 0.96 holds for plaque *volume* (Lenell 2026, [PMC13451515](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13451515/)).

### Inferences
- Skowronski 2018 shows that dominance shifts proximal RCA and LCx calibre by about 1 mm. Absolute or proximal-referenced taper in the RCA and LCx will therefore partly encode dominance. This supports a scale-free log-radius slope and argues for dominance as a covariate or stratifier. `ct_cag_dominance` exists in BL; check the header file.
- The kernel-regression "expected healthy profile" of Shahzad 2013 is a within-vessel smoothing reference. It describes the expected *shape* of the observed lumen, not a flow-based expectation. It is the measurement analogue of the thesis's robust slope, and it shows that profile-fitting methods tolerate local noise.
- The 0.16 mm RMS error is about one third of a 0.4–0.5 mm voxel. Over a 30 mm segment with an expected diameter change of about 0.5–1 mm, a slope fitted to around 60 samples should be estimable, but this is not demonstrated in any published taper study.

### Gaps
- No ICC, repeatability coefficient or limits of agreement for a CCTA lumen taper or log-radius slope was found. The thesis's own baseline-vs-follow-up check in segments that stay plaque-free (from the earlier notes) remains the only route.
- No normal per-segment *taper* reference values on CCTA were found. Skowronski gives only point diameters for proximal and mid segments.
- The ASOCA dataset and a SCOT-HEART normal-anatomy diameter paper were not found within the budget.

## Q3. Papers that propose or use a flow-matched or "expected versus observed" taper or diameter index

### Takeaway
Two families exist, but neither is a plaque predictor. First, scaling-law deviation indices: K_LV in the length–volume law (Huo 2013 Radiology on human CCTA; Huo 2013 Interface on pigs). These are tree-level, not segment-level. Second, the Murray-law **step-down reference diameter** in μQFR and CT-μFR. It replaces the linear-taper assumption of earlier QFR with a reference lumen that drops at each side branch according to Murray's law, which is effectively an expected flow-matched taper. It is used to grade stenosis and compute FFR, not to predict plaque.

### Cited Findings
- **Tu et al. 2021, Catheter Cardiovasc Interv, DOI 10.1002/ccd.29592. PW, abstract.** "current QFR solutions assume a linear tapering of the reference vessel size, which might decrease the diagnostic accuracy in the presence of the physiologically significant bifurcation lesions"; "A step-down reference diameter function was reconstructed based on the Murray bifurcation fractal law and used for QFR computation." In 330 vessels it reached 93.0% accuracy against FFR. This is angiography, not CT. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1002%2Fccd.29592)
- **Weng et al. 2024, EuroIntervention, DOI 10.4244/EIJ-D-24-00336. Abstract.** "Coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA)-derived Murray law-based quantitative flow ratio (CT-μFR) is a novel non-invasive method for fast computation of fractional flow reserve (FFR) from CCTA images". This shows that the Murray step-down reference has been implemented on CCTA. — [PMC11472136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11472136/)
- **Huo et al. 2013 Radiology and Interface (above):** K_LV is a population-referenced deviation index, with cutoffs ≥ 23 cm^−4/3 for diffuse disease in humans and < 18 for positive remodelling in pigs.
- **Choi et al. 2020 (above):** the authors suggest that the framework could help define diffuse disease and LV hypertrophy, but they did not test this (as reported in the full text read).

### Inferences
- The thesis's index (the per-segment log-radius slope, standardised per segment) is not flow-matched. Side-branch diameters are not measured by the tracer, so expected flow loss cannot be computed per person. The literature offers two routes to a flow-matched version: a Murray or Huo–Kassab step-down from measured side-branch diameters (μQFR-style), or downstream myocardial mass (Choi 2020, Voronoi). Both need side-branch or myocardial segmentation that the thesis does not have. This should be named as a limitation, not claimed.
- No paper calls under-tapering relative to flow loss a WSS or plaque risk factor (consistent with the earlier notes). The thesis hypothesis remains an inference from Poiseuille plus the scaling laws.

### Gaps
- No study was found that uses an expected-vs-observed taper or diameter index to predict *incident* plaque or WSS in a population.
- The full CT-μFR methods (whether the side-branch diameters come from CCTA and how the reference is built) were not read.
