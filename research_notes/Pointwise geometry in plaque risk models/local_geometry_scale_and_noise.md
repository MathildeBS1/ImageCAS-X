# Local (sub-segment) coronary geometry: spatial scale, measurement noise and plaque location

Scope note: 17 tool calls (searches and fetches). Several primary full texts (PREDICTION Circulation 2012, Schaap 2009, Tuncay 2018) could not be opened (paywall, cookie wall or 403), so some numbers come from abstracts, news reports or citations in other papers. Each such case is flagged.

## Q1. Which studies relate local geometry or haemodynamics at a location to plaque at that same location, and at what scale and effect size?

### Takeaway
The point-level evidence is almost all about **wall shear stress (ESS/WSS) computed by CFD**, not about raw geometry such as curvature or taper. It comes from invasive imaging (IVUS/OCT plus angiography) in **patients who already have CAD** (often ACS/PCI), with small n and 6–12 month follow-up. The standard analysis unit is a **3 mm segment** (0.5 mm in some newer Emory work). Effect sizes are modest: odds ratios around 2–2.6 for low ESS predicting local lumen narrowing in PREDICTION. No study I found relates curvature or taper at a point to plaque developing at that point in a plaque-free population.

### Cited Findings
- **Stone et al. 2003 (Circulation 108:438–444):** 8 patients, 12 arteries (6 native, 6 stented), IVUS plus biplane angiography plus CFD, 6-month follow-up. Native-artery regions with low baseline ESS showed more plaque thickness and outward remodelling with the lumen unchanged. Regions with physiological ESS changed little. The authors said the method "can predict areas of minor plaque likely to exhibit progression." — [Univ. Iowa abstract](https://user.engineering.uiowa.edu/~awahle/WahLIT/Abstracts/ston03b-cfd.html); [IRO record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Effect-of-Endothelial-Shear-Stress-on/9984047643502771)
- **PREDICTION (Stone et al., Circulation 2012;126:172–81):** 506 ACS patients after PCI, with vascular profiling (angiography plus IVUS plus CFD) and each artery divided into sequential 3 mm segments. Baseline large plaque burden predicted the increase in plaque area. Lumen-area decrease was independently predicted by large plaque burden and low ESS, and the two were "independent and additive." — [QxMD abstract listing](https://read.qxmd.com/read/22723305/prediction-of-progression-of-coronary-artery-disease-and-clinical-outcomes-using-vascular-profiling-of-endothelial-shear-stress-and-arterial-plaque-characteristics-the-prediction-study). The 3 mm segment length comes from a secondary abstract listing whose match to the 2012 paper I could not verify. Van den Hoogen 2022, below, also reports PREDICTION's lowest ESS positions in 6 mm steps, which fits 3 mm segments.
- **PREDICTION effect sizes (ACC 2011 presentation, TCTMD report):** 329 of the 506 patients had follow-up profiling at 6–10 months. ORs: low ESS distal to the throat, for worsening lumen narrowing, 2.07 (1.33–3.23). Maximum plaque thickness >1 mm, 2.02 (1.33–3.07). Plaque burden >60%, for worsening obstruction needing PCI, 5.26 (2.04–13.57). Low ESS at the throat, for the same endpoint, 2.64 (1.11–6.26). Progressive obstruction needing PCI occurred in 10%. Clinical events were rare. — [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/endothelial-shear-stress-works-prediction-tool). These are conference figures, not the final paper. I could not verify the often-quoted PPV figure.
- **Samady et al. 2011 (Circulation 124:779–788):** 20 CAD patients with VH-IVUS plus CFD at baseline and 6 months. Compared with intermediate-WSS segments, low-WSS segments showed progression of plaque area and necrotic core and lost vessel and lumen area. High-WSS segments showed necrotic-core and dense-calcium progression. — [BVS/MEDLINE record](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/bvseps/resource/pt/mdl-21788584); [Houston Methodist record](https://scholars.houstonmethodist.org/en/publications/coronary-artery-wall-shear-stress-is-associated-with-progression-/)
- **Kumar et al. 2018 (JACC Cardiovasc Interv 11:2072–80):** 44 patients with non-obstructive CAD (93% LAD), 3D angiography plus pulsatile CFD, analysed in **0.5 mm** segments (4,510 segments, only 2.4% with low WSS <1 Pa). Low WSS was associated with severe endothelial dysfunction (multivariable OR 9.14, 2.60–32.11). The endpoint was cross-sectional endothelial function, not plaque development. The paper reports no reproducibility figure for the geometry. — [PMC6217963](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6217963)
- **Kumar et al. 2018 (JACC 72:1926–35):** In FAME II, **high** proximal-lesion WSS (cut-point 4.71 Pa), not low WSS, predicted MI in 441 medically treated patients with FFR ≤0.80. This was angiography-derived, not CT. — [search summary of the JACC paper; Emory listing](https://eccri.emory.edu/ecbc-pubs.html)
- **Koskinas et al. 2010 (swine):** 3 mm segments (n = 304). Local ESS together with the remodelling response determined the natural history of individual lesions. — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/456c02b2-89b9-434b-bd4f-3a2a5c142f5d)
- **Serial OCT plus CFD (20 patients, 25 arteries, 6 months):** Low-ESS (<1 Pa) segments kept thin caps and widened their lipid arcs, while higher-ESS segments thickened their caps. — found via [search results, PMC8749833](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8749833)
- **CCTA-based ESS, cross-sectional (van den Hoogen et al. 2022, EHJ-CVI):** 122 patients, 188 vessels, 237 lesions. ESS was computed per **3 mm axial segment** (upstream, start, MLA, end, downstream). The paper gives no rationale for 3 mm beyond co-registration. ESS was highest at the MLA. ESS analysis failed in 93 vessels, often because of motion or blooming, and 22 more vessels were excluded for image quality. The only reproducibility metric reported is an intraobserver ICC of 0.934 for **lesion location**, none for ESS or lumen geometry. — [PMC10017098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10017098/)
- **CCTA ESS in angiographically normal arteries (Schultz et al. 2023, Int J Cardiovasc Imaging):** 3 mm segments. Smaller vessels had higher ESS, and men had lower ESS than women after adjusting for lumen diameter. It is cross-sectional, with no outcome. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10554-022-02739-0)
- **Raw geometry from CCTA, segment level (Tuncay et al. 2018, J Cardiovasc Comput Tomogr; PMID 29486988):** 73 asymptomatic people at increased risk, semi-automatic centerlines. Curvature was associated with significant stenosis per segment (p < 0.001) and per artery (p = 0.002), and was 16.7% higher in segments with stenosis. Tortuosity was associated only per segment (p = 0.002). The study is cross-sectional and analysed at segment and artery level, not per point. A related ESR 2014 poster from the same group reported curvature linked to plaque presence, but no tortuosity association, which conflicts with the final paper. — [PubMed 29486988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29486988); [ESR poster](https://epos.myesr.org/poster/esr/escr2014/458)
- **Lesion-level CCTA geometry and future ACS (Yonsei, EMERALD-type analysis):** Distance from the ostium, bifurcation location and vessel tortuosity added discrimination for culprit-lesion precursors beyond stenosis and plaque features. These are pre-existing lesions in patients who later had ACS. — [Yonsei repository](https://ir.ymlib.yonsei.ac.kr/handle/22282913/188325?mode=full)

### Inferences
- The local literature measures **haemodynamics (ESS) at 3 mm**, not curvature or taper at 3 mm. Curvature and taper enter only as inputs to CFD. No study cited here shows that raw local curvature or taper predicts where plaque appears at the same point.
- Every serial study above was done in people **with existing CAD** (ACS/PCI or non-obstructive CAD), used invasive imaging, followed people for 6–12 months and measured **progression** (change in plaque area, lumen or phenotype), not plaque **development** in a plaque-free wall. That is a different biological question from a 10-year development outcome in plaque-free people.
- The effect sizes are modest. Plaque burden dominates low ESS (OR ~5 vs ~2), so in plaque-free people the strongest local predictor used in these studies, existing plaque, is absent by design.

### Gaps
- I found no study of a **plaque-free baseline** with point-level geometry and **plaque development** at the same point in humans by CCTA.
- I could not open the PREDICTION full text to confirm the 3 mm unit directly or to get PPV/NPV.
- I did not retrieve Wentzel/Gijsen serial work (e.g. IMPACT, Hartman/Kok), the EMERALD axial-plaque-stress studies, or Bourantas' CCTA–ESS progression work in this pass.

## Q2. How reproducible are curvature and lumen radius or taper when measured from CCTA?

### Takeaway
I found **no published inter-observer, inter-algorithm or scan–rescan reproducibility study of coronary curvature or tortuosity from CCTA**. The indirect evidence is not encouraging for very short scales:
- Lumen diameter from CCTA agrees with intravascular imaging only to about **±0.8 mm** (95% limits of agreement).
- Curvature differs systematically between end-systole and end-diastole.
- Frenet curvature is known to be highly noise-sensitive and depends on the smoothing used.

### Cited Findings
- **Cardiac phase:** In the same Utrecht group, coronary curvature was significantly higher at end-systole than end-diastole, both per artery (p = 0.002) and per segment (p < 0.001). Dynamic change over the cycle was not related to CAD. — [Springer Medizin](https://www.springermedizin.de/assessment-of-dynamic-change-of-coronary-artery-geometry-and-its/17405834)
- **Manual centerline steps:** Coronary curvature workflows initialise the vessel by hand and correct the centerline by hand where it is off-centre, which is a likely source of observer variability. No coronary study quantifying this variability was found. — [ESR poster](https://epos.myesr.org/poster/esr/escr2014/458)
- **Carotid CTA tortuosity:** This is the closest reproducibility study, comparing 4 software packages. The tortuosity index was centerline length divided by chord length, a coarse whole-vessel measure, not local curvature. Intra-observer agreement was high, inter-observer agreement acceptable, and agreement differed between packages. — [PMC6348067](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6348067/)
- **Centerline benchmark (Schaap et al. 2009, Med Image Anal 13:701–714):** 32 CTA datasets with a consensus reference built from multiple observers, used to compare 13 algorithms on overlap and accuracy. I could not retrieve the per-algorithm accuracy numbers in mm. — [Erasmus repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/24468); [RUG record](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/standardized-evaluation-methodology-and-reference-database-for-ev/)
- **Lumen diameter vs intravascular imaging (left main):** CCTA minus intravascular imaging, mean −0.35 mm with LoA −1.14 to +0.45 mm. In the IVUS-only subset, −0.48 mm with LoA −1.35 to +0.39 mm. — [Monash Health repository](https://repository.monashhealth.org/monashhealthjspui/handle/1/52691)
- **Minimal lumen diameter and area (scaffolds):** CCTA vs QCA minimal lumen diameter −0.14 mm (LoA −0.88 to +0.60). CCTA vs IVUS minimal lumen area 0.73 mm² (LoA −1.85 to +3.30). — [Erasmus repub 101279](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/101279)
- **Calcified lesions:** CCTA underestimates lumen diameter and area on average. Its low accuracy there is attributed mainly to **high variance rather than systematic bias**. — [Termedia, Adv Interv Cardiol](https://www.termedia.pl/Original-paper-Lumen-and-calcium-characteristics-within-calcified-coronary-lesions-Comparison-of-computed-tomography-coronary-angiography-intravascular-ultrasound,35,20448,0,1.html)
- **Curvature estimation and noise:**
  - Frenet-formula curvature is "highly sensitive to noise, commonly found in medical imaging data."
  - Averaging curvature to reduce that noise can over-smooth and underestimate curvature in the region of interest.
  - The same source argues that curvature variations on a scale **smaller than the vessel radius** are arguably insignificant, in its case for device design.
  — [Ann Biomed Eng, s10439-008-9590-0](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10439-008-9590-0.pdf)
- **Finite differences:** Fixed-window finite differences amplify noise in centerline derivatives. A Gaussian filter (σ = 1 sample) is applied to the centerline coordinates before computing curvature. — [arXiv 2607.14195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14195)
- **Spline estimates:** Free-knot regression splines have been used to estimate arterial centerline derivatives and curvature, compared with local polynomial smoothing. Curvature estimates depend on that smoothing choice. — [Sangalli et al. 2009, JRSS-C](https://sangalli.faculty.polimi.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2009_Sangalli-Secchi-Vantini-Veneziani-JRSSC_compresso.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Curvature:** Curvature is a second derivative of position. With in-plane voxels of about 0.3–0.5 mm and centerline jitter of the same order, its noise grows roughly as (position error)/(window length)². This scaling is my own reasoning, not taken from a coronary source. A 1–3 mm window therefore mostly measures noise unless the centerline is heavily smoothed, and heavy smoothing removes the true short-scale peaks (the over-smoothing trade-off above). Measuring curvature over a window of at least one to two vessel diameters (about 5–10 mm for proximal trunks) is a defensible floor. Below the vessel radius it is not meaningful.
- **Taper:** Taper is a first derivative of radius. Over 3 mm, the true change in radius in a normal trunk is a small fraction of a millimetre, while single-cross-section CCTA diameter error is about ±0.4–0.9 mm (LoA half-width). Local taper over a few mm is therefore not measurable from CCTA. A regression of radius on arc length over the whole segment (≥10–20 mm), or over the whole artery, averages that error down. Side branches and the partial-volume effect also bias local radius.
- **Phase:** Systole and diastole curvature differ, so the reconstruction phase adds variance between people, and possibly bias if phase choice differs between scans.
- **Practical check for the thesis:** The noise question can be tested directly on the data. Compare tracer and Medis curvature and taper as a function of smoothing window, for example ICC against window length on the 600 Medis trees. This finds the shortest window where agreement becomes acceptable. It fits the planned Medis comparison, which already works on features rather than points.

### Gaps
- I found no coronary CCTA scan–rescan or inter-algorithm reproducibility of curvature, tortuosity or local taper, and no paper quantifying curvature error against smoothing window for coronary CCTA. This should be stated as a gap in the thesis rather than filled with numbers from other vessels.
- I could not retrieve the Schaap 2009 per-algorithm accuracy in mm. Typical tracking accuracy versus the consensus is often quoted at a fraction of a mm, but that is from memory and unverified.

## Q3. What spatial scale do the haemodynamics suggest?

### Takeaway
Low-ESS regions sit on the **inner curvature** and on the **lateral (outer) walls just downstream of bifurcations**, opposite the high-shear flow divider. Around lesions, the lowest ESS lies **3–9 mm** from the minimal lumen area, and in PREDICTION most often about **6 mm** distal to the throat. So the biologically relevant scale is a few mm, about 1–3 vessel diameters, which is at or below what CCTA geometry can resolve reliably.

### Cited Findings
- In PROSPECT, the lowest ESS was most often 3–9 mm proximal (31%) or distal (24%) to the MLA. In PREDICTION, it was most often 6 mm distal to the lesion throat (60%). — as cited in [van den Hoogen 2022, PMC10017098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10017098/)
- Low ESS was most often found in the one or two 3 mm segments just past an obstruction in PREDICTION. — [PREDICTION abstract listing](https://read.qxmd.com/read/22723305/prediction-of-progression-of-coronary-artery-disease-and-clinical-outcomes-using-vascular-profiling-of-endothelial-shear-stress-and-arterial-plaque-characteristics-the-prediction-study)
- **Bifurcations:**
  - Low and oscillatory shear sits on the lateral walls of the main vessel and side branch, with high shear at the carina (flow divider).
  - Pathology shows more plaque, and more necrotic core, on the lateral wall than at the flow divider.
  - Larger bifurcation angles are linked to lower lateral-wall shear.
  — [Chatzizisis/Giannoglou-group review, UNMC 2010-3](https://www.unmc.edu/intmed/_documents/cardiology/cbbl/2010-3.pdf); [EuroIntervention, atheroma and coronary bifurcations](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/atheroma-and-coronary-bifurcations-before-and-after-stenting/pdf); [Korean Circ J](https://synapse.koreamed.org/articles/1016810)
- Plaques are more likely at the inner curvature and at bifurcations. — [ESR poster (Tuncay group)](https://epos.myesr.org/poster/esr/escr2014/458)

### Inferences
- The low-ESS zones are a few mm long and **circumferentially eccentric**: inner versus outer wall, lateral wall versus carina. A centerline curvature value cannot tell which side of the wall is exposed. Per-segment visual plaque reads do not record which wall the plaque is on either. So even a perfect point-level curvature only reaches the outcome after aggregation to the segment.
- The haemodynamic reasoning favours segment-level summaries that capture **whether a high-curvature bend or a branch point is present in the segment**, such as the peak or upper-percentile curvature over a ≥5–10 mm smoothed window, or distance to the nearest bifurcation. These are better than the mean curvature at sub-mm sampling.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative source for how far downstream of a bifurcation low ESS extends in normal (non-stented, plaque-free) coronaries, in mm or diameters. The bifurcation CFD results were mostly idealised or post-stenting models with varying thresholds (<0.4 Pa, <1.5 Pa).

## Q4. Has anyone shown that per-location geometry predicts future plaque location better than segment-average geometry?

### Takeaway
I found **no study** that directly compares point-level against segment-averaged geometry, or ESS, for predicting **where** plaque develops. The literature uses 3 mm or 0.5 mm units without testing that choice against coarser aggregation. The one CCTA raw-geometry study (Tuncay 2018) worked at segment and artery level and found curvature associations at both.

### Cited Findings
- In the main CCTA ESS paper, 3 mm units were chosen for co-registration, with no comparison of scales. — [PMC10017098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10017098/)
- Curvature was associated with stenosis at both segment (p < 0.001) and artery (p = 0.002) level in 73 asymptomatic people. — [PubMed 29486988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29486988)

### Inferences (implications for this thesis)
- **The outcome sets the resolution.** The outcome is a visual read per SCCT segment, and a trunk segment spans roughly 10–40 mm (my estimate, not sourced). The model can only learn what it can observe, so any point-level feature has to be **collapsed to one value per segment** (mean, max, percentile, or "contains a bend above X") before it enters the cloglog GLMM. Point-level geometry does not create point-level prediction. It only offers a richer choice of segment summary. Which summary to use (mean or max) must be prespecified, or chosen inside cross-validation, to avoid data-driven selection across many summaries.
- **Measurement noise argues for aggregation.** Both curvature (a second derivative) and taper (a radius slope against ~±0.8 mm diameter error) become usable only when averaged or fitted over many mm. The segment scale of the outcome is therefore close to the scale at which the features are measurable, which is a defensible rationale for segment-level features. Sub-3 mm features would mainly add noise, which attenuates hazard ratios towards 1 (regression dilution).
- **Weak external evidence.** The evidence that local haemodynamics matter comes from short-term progression in diseased, invasively imaged, referral (ACS/PCI) patients, with modest ORs (~2). Its transfer to 10-year plaque development in plaque-free general-population CCTA is unproven. The thesis should present local-ESS literature as the mechanistic **motivation**, not as evidence that segment geometry will predict.
- **Weak link between geometry and outcome.** Curvature is only a proxy for ESS, and segment plaque presence is only a proxy for where low ESS acts. Two coarse proxies in series weaken the expected signal, so a null result should be interpreted with power and measurement error in mind.

### Gaps
- No direct head-to-head comparison of local versus segment-averaged geometry exists in what I found. A targeted PubMed search (e.g. "segment-averaged" AND "shear stress" AND "spatial resolution", or work from the Gijsen/Wentzel group on analysis-unit sensitivity) might find methodological papers on how the 3 mm versus 1.5 mm versus whole-segment choice changes ESS–progression associations. I did not find one in this pass.
