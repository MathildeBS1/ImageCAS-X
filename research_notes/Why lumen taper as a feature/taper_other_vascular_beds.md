# Lumen taper, widening and wall shear stress / plaque in vascular beds other than the coronary arteries

Scope note. Three geometric ideas are kept apart throughout:
- **taper**: gradual narrowing of the lumen along the vessel (what the thesis feature, slope of log radius, measures);
- **local expansion / flare**: a lumen that widens over a short stretch (carotid bulb, pre-apical flare at bifurcations, diameter mismatch at a graft anastomosis);
- **stenosis**: narrowing caused by disease, with post-stenotic expansion downstream.

Most of the evidence is about local expansion, not taper. Only two sources found treat taper itself as the exposure (Sundell & Roach 1998, human aorta; Peiffer et al. 2012, rabbit aorta). Access level is given for each source; "abstract" means only the PubMed abstract was read, so numbers come from it alone.

## 1. Human imaging / autopsy studies relating taper or local widening to plaque or wall thickness

### Takeaway
The carotid bulb (a local widening) is the classical human site where low and oscillatory WSS and plaque coincide, and imaging studies show that the degree of bifurcation flare or area ratio is a weak, independent predictor of disturbed flow and early wall thickening. One small autopsy study of the human infrarenal aorta relates taper itself to lesion distribution, with more taper going with more raised lesions; the cause and effect is unresolved.

### Cited Findings
- **Zarins et al. 1983, Circ Res** (human carotid, 12 autopsy bifurcations + steady-flow scale models; cross-sectional; DOI 10.1161/01.res.53.4.502; abstract). Intimal thickening was greatest in the carotid sinus, maximal on the outer wall opposite the flow divider, where the model showed "a region of separation and reversal of axial flow"; outer-wall WSS 0 to -6 dyn/cm², inner wall 31 to 600 dyn/cm². Conclusion: "Intimal thickening and atherosclerosis develop largely in regions of relatively low wall shear stress, flow separation, and departure from axially aligned, unidirectional flow." — [PubMed 6627609](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6627609/)
- **Ku, Giddens, Zarins & Glagov 1985, Arteriosclerosis** (human carotid, pulsatile-flow scale model compared with cadaver plaque thickness; DOI 10.1161/01.atv.5.3.293; abstract). At the outer sinus wall "where intimal plaques were thickest, mean shear stress was low (-0.5 dynes/cm2) but the instantaneous shear stress oscillated between -7 and +4 dynes/cm2"; intimal thickness correlated with the reciprocal of mean WSS (r = 0.82, p < 0.001) and with an oscillatory-shear index (r = 0.82, p < 0.001) over 20 locations. "plaques tend to form in areas of low, rather than high, shear stress, but ... marked oscillations in the direction of wall shear may enhance atherogenesis." — [PubMed 3994585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3994585/)
  - Caveat: neither study varies the bulb's widening; they show that the widened sinus is where low/oscillatory WSS and plaque co-locate, not that more widening gives more plaque.
- **Lee, Antiga, Spence & Steinman 2008, Stroke** (human carotid, CFD on MRI geometries of 25 young adults, 50 vessels; cross-sectional, no wall/plaque outcome; DOI 10.1161/STROKEAHA.107.510644; abstract). Disturbed flow (area exposed to low and oscillatory shear) was related to "proximal area ratio (beta approximately 0.5) and bifurcation tortuosity (beta approximately -0.4), but not bifurcation angle, planarity, or distal area ratio" (P < 0.002). Proximal area ratio is a measure of how much the lumen expands at the bifurcation. — [PubMed 18556585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18556585/)
- **Bijari, Wasserman & Steinman 2014, Stroke** (human carotid, ARIC Carotid MRI, ages 60–85, n = 467/346/294 in nested groups; cross-sectional; DOI 10.1161/STROKEAHA.113.003454; full text, PMC). Flare = maximum bifurcation cross-sectional area / CCA area. Rationale stated: "flaring (i.e., expansion) of the lumen promotes flow separation and consequent low and oscillatory WSS". In the group without stenosis, inward remodelling or lumen irregularity, "both Flare (β=0.173, p=0.013) and Curvature (β= −0.135, p=0.047) emerged as significant, albeit weak"; adding them raised R² "from 0.112 to only 0.135". In the full group Flare was *negatively* associated with wall thickness (β = −0.199, p < 0.001), read as inward remodelling by plaque. Conclusion: "Carotid bifurcation geometry is an independent, albeit weak, predictor of its early wall thickening". — [PMC4321819](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321819)
  - Caveat: the sign of the association flips with disease stage, which shows that lumen widening measured on a diseased vessel is partly a consequence of plaque. Directly relevant to the thesis: a plaque-free baseline avoids this reverse effect.
- **Strecker et al. 2020, J Cardiovasc Magn Reson** (human carotid, 121 high-risk patients with existing ICA plaque, 242 bifurcations, 3T CMR + 4D flow; cross-sectional; DOI 10.1186/s12968-020-00657-5; full text, open access). ICA/CCA diameter ratio was "significantly and independently but inversely associated with vessel wall thickness" (coefficient −0.302, p < 0.001); tortuosity positively (0.764, p < 0.001). WSS/OSI were not independent predictors after risk-factor adjustment. — [PMC7488078](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7488078/)
  - Caveat: patients already had plaque, so the diameter ratio is confounded by plaque itself.
- **Sundell & Roach 1998, Atherosclerosis** (human infrarenal aorta, 11 autopsy specimens aged 42–76; cross-sectional; DOI 10.1016/s0021-9150(98)00068-9; abstract). Taper was "the angle produced by the slope of the decrease in circumference with length" in three segments per aorta. Fatty-streak area "correlated strongly to the coefficients of the polynomial function, while the raised lesions showed a significant increase with an increase in taper angles"; "taper plays a role in the distribution of both fatty streaks and raised lesions, however, the nature of the cause and effect remains to be determined." — [PubMed 9699899](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9699899/)
  - This is the only human study found that uses gradual taper (not local expansion) as the exposure. Very small (n = 11), autopsy, no flow data; the direction (more taper, more raised lesions) is the opposite of what a "widening is bad" argument would predict, so it supports "taper matters" rather than a specific direction.
- **Roach / MacLean et al. 1992, 1993, 1998** (human cerebral arteries, porcine renal arteries, human aortoiliac bifurcations; autopsy geometry, no outcome; DOIs 10.1016/0021-9290(92)90039-4 [abstract, truncated], 10.1007/BF01744592 [abstract]; Front Med Biol Eng 1993 has no DOI [abstract]). Arteries are not uniform tubes: the lumen flares (expands) before the flow divider and tapers in the daughter branches; the authors argue these changes "have significant implications for the propagation of flow and pressure waves as well as for flow patterns" and that "taper, flare, and variations in both circumferential and longitudinal wall thickness need to be considered when trying to correlate physical factors in the aorta with the precise location of atherosclerotic lesions". — [PubMed 8241029](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8241029/); [PubMed 9987643](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9987643/); [PubMed 1517264](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1517264/)
- **Smedby femoral studies (1993–1998)** (human superficial femoral artery, digitised angiography in 232–301 hyperlipidaemic patients, 3-year longitudinal; abstracts). These relate curvature/tortuosity and disturbed flow, not taper, to atherosclerosis progression: e.g. "These findings are more consistent with tortuosity influencing the development of atherosclerosis than with its being a consequence of atherosclerosis" (Smedby & Bergstrand 1996, DOI 10.1007/BF02648109) and downstream plaque growth is "compatible with an atherogenic effect of fluid mechanical disturbances, such as flow separation, that may occur downstream of a stenosis" (Smedby 1997, DOI 10.1161/01.atv.17.5.912). — [PubMed 8841722](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8841722/); [PubMed 9157955](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9157955/); [PubMed 9570222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9570222/)
  - No femoral taper analysis was found in Smedby's work. Useful as a longitudinal "geometry before disease" precedent, but for curvature, not taper.

### Inferences
- The strongest human message is about local lumen expansion (carotid bulb, flare), not gradual taper. A thesis sentence must say "widening" or "expansion" when citing the carotid work, not "absence of taper".
- The carotid geometry–wall-thickness effects are weak (R² gain about 0.02) and cross-sectional; they should be cited as plausibility, not as an effect size.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal human study was found that relates baseline taper (gradual narrowing) of a non-coronary artery to incident plaque.
- Full texts of Sundell & Roach 1998, Zarins 1983, Ku 1985 and Lee 2008 were not opened; only abstracts. Their numbers above come from the abstracts.
- No aortic taper study in living humans relating taper to plaque was found.

## 2. CFD / experimental studies varying taper or divergence and reporting WSS / OSI / separation

### Takeaway
Model studies show that taper changes the WSS field: a converging (tapering) vessel raises WSS and shear-rate gradients, a diverging graft lowers WSS along its length, and in an animal aorta the degree of taper controls how far secondary flows persist downstream. None of them links these flow changes to plaque in the same study, except the rabbit aorta work, where lesions matched high rather than low shear.

### Cited Findings
- **Peiffer, Rowland, Cremers, Weinberg & Sherwin 2012, Atherosclerosis** (rabbit thoracic aorta, micro-CT casts of immature and mature animals, CFD; DOI 10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2012.04.020; abstract). Two vortices from arch curvature produced a dorsal stripe of high WSS; "their persistence into the descending aorta depended on aortic taper and was more pronounced in mature geometries"; "Blood flow characteristics in the rabbit aorta were affected by the degree of taper, which tends to increase with age in the aortic arch and strengthens secondary flows into the descending aorta. Previously-observed lesion distributions correlated better with high than low shear". — [PubMed 22658260](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22658260/)
  - The closest study to "taper as a variable that changes WSS" in a real arterial geometry. Rabbit, and lesions matched high shear, which conflicts with the human low-shear paradigm; cite only for "taper changes the flow", not for the direction of the plaque link.
- **Banerjee, Back & Cho 1995, Biorheology** (dog femoral artery, in vivo pressure/flow + numerical simulation; DOI 10.1016/0006-355X(95)00044-A; abstract). With a parabolic inlet, "the taper of the artery not only increased the magnitude of the positive and negative shear rates, but caused a steep gradient in shear rate"; with a uniform inlet the tapered flow was developing, with a "reduction in magnitude of wall shear rate along the axial direction". — [PubMed 8857355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8857355/)
  - Shows the taper effect depends on inlet conditions; no outcome.
- **Rhee & Lee 1998, Ann Biomed Eng** (in vitro rigid and elastic models of an artery joined to a divergent graft, carotid and femoral waveforms; DOI 10.1114/1.31; abstract). "The mean and peak of shear rate decrease along the divergent graft, and the decreases are more significant in the elastic model." — [PubMed 9846934](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9846934/)
  - Direct evidence that a widening (reversed-taper) conduit lowers WSS along its length; graft model, no outcome.
- **Liu et al. 2016, J Biomech** (CFD of helical arterial grafts with different smooth tapers; DOI 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2016.09.028; abstract). Taper amplified helical flow, which "would lead to highly elevated wall shear stress, remarkably reduced oscillating shear index and relative residence time at both the grafts and the anastomosis of the host vessel"; the authors were "inspired by the helical flow and tapered configuration of the arterial system". — [PubMed 27745696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27745696/)
  - Idealised device CFD; intimal hyperplasia is speculated, not measured.
- **Van Tricht, De Wachter, Tordoir & Verdonck 2004, J Surg Res** (in vitro compliant model, straight 6 mm vs tapered 4–7 mm PTFE arteriovenous graft; DOI 10.1016/S0022-4804(03)00303-2; abstract). "No significant differences are noted between the graft geometries"; the tapered graft had a pressure drop at the arterial anastomosis "three times higher". — [PubMed 15013369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15013369/)
  - Counter-evidence: taper did not change graft haemodynamics much here. Search snippets suggest later CFD from the same field found a 6-to-8 mm tapered graft gave less disturbed venous flow, but that paper was not opened, so it is not cited.
- **Lumen-expansion rationale in carotid CFD.** Bijari et al. 2014 state that "bifurcations with high flare and low curvature are at highest risk of disturbed WSS", citing Lee et al.'s finding that disturbed WSS concentrates at the bulb. — [PMC4321819](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321819)

### Inferences
- The physical mechanism carried across beds is that an expanding lumen decelerates flow near the wall, which favours separation and low, oscillatory WSS, whereas a converging lumen accelerates flow and keeps WSS higher. The evidence for the converging-side is model-only (dog femoral simulation, graft CFD).
- Directionally, taper studies do not all agree (rabbit lesions with high shear; AV graft taper with no effect), so the literature supports "taper changes WSS" more firmly than "less taper means more plaque".

### Gaps
- No systematic parametric CFD of physiological taper angles in a carotid, femoral or cerebral artery reporting OSI/low-WSS area was found in the searches (snippets of small engineering-journal stenosis models with conflicting directions were not opened and are not cited).
- Sudden-expansion endothelial-cell studies (e.g. McKinney et al. 2006, J Biomech, DOI 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2005.02.002) are in vitro flow chambers, not arteries; left out of the argument.

## 3. Animal or graft studies where taper or calibre mismatch altered intimal hyperplasia or plaque

### Takeaway
The clearest in vivo causal evidence comes from a dog vein-graft experiment: a sudden widening at a diameter-mismatched anastomosis produced slow recirculating flow and low WSS, and intimal thickening formed exactly there. This is local expansion at a surgical junction, not natural taper.

### Cited Findings
- **Sunamura, Ishibashi & Karino 2007, Surgery** (dog, 26 saphenous and 10 jugular vein interpositions in femoral arteries of 22 mongrel dogs, harvested at 3 months, flow visualisation in the transparent harvested vessels; experimental; DOI 10.1016/j.surg.2006.12.019; abstract). Intimal thickening was found distal to the anastomosis where the lumen widened; "In both cases, flow was disturbed with the formation of a slow recirculation flow due to a sudden enlargement of vessel diameter caused by a mismatch of vessel diameters at the anastomotic junction and at the location where the wall shear stress was low." Conclusion: "the key hemodynamic factors involved in the localization of intimal thickening are the low velocity of flowing blood and the resultant low shear stress acting on the vessel wall." — [PubMed 17560253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17560253/)
  - Intimal hyperplasia in a graft is not atherosclerosis, and the expansion is abrupt and surgical. Still the only study found where a lumen widening, flow and wall response were measured in the same vessel.
- **Peiffer et al. 2012** (rabbit aorta, see section 2): taper altered flow, but lesion distributions matched high shear, so it does not support a low-shear, low-taper mechanism in that species. — [PubMed 22658260](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22658260/)

### Inferences
- Animal and graft data support the mechanism (widening → recirculation → low WSS → wall thickening) but not its size in natural, gradual taper.

### Gaps
- No animal study was found that changed the natural taper of an artery (rather than an abrupt mismatch) and measured plaque.
- Clinical patency trials of tapered vs straight grafts were not opened; search snippets indicate conflicting results.

## 4. Reviews or statements that a widening vessel (carotid bulb) is a classical low-WSS, plaque site

### Takeaway
The carotid bulb as the classical site of low/oscillatory WSS and plaque rests on the primary Zarins 1983 and Ku 1985 papers; the expansion-to-separation link is stated explicitly by Bijari et al. 2014 and Lee et al. 2008.

### Cited Findings
- Zarins 1983 and Ku 1985 (section 1) are the primary sources and are better cited than reviews. — [PubMed 6627609](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6627609/); [PubMed 3994585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3994585/)
- Bijari et al. 2014: "flaring (i.e., expansion) of the lumen promotes flow separation and consequent low and oscillatory WSS". — [PMC4321819](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321819)
- Bharadvaj, Mabon & Giddens 1982 (J Biomech, steady-flow carotid model) is reported in search snippets as finding disease-prone zones with low or oscillatory shear; not opened, cite only after checking. — [jci.org reference listing](https://jci.org/references/scholar/23605/B18)

### Inferences
- No dedicated review was opened; primary sources suffice for a 1–3 sentence statement.

### Gaps
- The Giddens/Glagov reviews were not opened.

B. C. "In dog femoral arteries with interposed vein grafts of mismatched diameter, Sunamura et al. (2007)~\cite{sunamura2007mismatch} found intimal thickening where the lumen suddenly widened, at sites of slow recirculating flow and low wall shear stress, which supports the idea that the absence of narrowing along a vessel, and not only curvature, can set up the flow conditions linked to wall thickening."

Wording cautions for these sentences: call the carotid and graft findings "widening" or "expansion", not "lack of taper"; do not claim a direction for taper from Sundell & Roach or Peiffer (Sundell & Roach found more raised lesions with more taper; Peiffer's rabbit lesions matched high shear); state that all of this is cross-sectional or experimental and from other vascular beds.
