# Clinical and imaging evidence on coronary lumen calibre, taper and plaque (humans)

Scope and method. These notes build on `Taper and curvature interaction for WSS/clinical_evidence.md` and `Fourth centerline feature for plaque/radius_topology_features.md`. They do not repeat those notes, except where a claim there needs correcting. Abstracts were read verbatim through the Europe PMC REST API. **No full text was opened for any source below.** "Abstract" means the access level is abstract only; "OA" means a free full text exists but was not read. Every number below comes from an abstract that was read. No preprints were used. The Shen 2026 review and arXiv 2507.17109 were not used.

**Correction to the earlier notes.** `clinical_evidence.md` attributes "0.22 mm per 10 mm, reversed tapering in about 10%" to the ResearchGate page "Normal angiographic tapering of the coronary arteries" (Zubaid 2002). Those figures are in fact from the abstract of **Javier et al. 1995**, an IVUS study, and that abstract says lumen tapering is "almost always seen **in coronary artery disease**". The figures therefore describe diseased arteries, not normal ones. The per-artery values in the same notes (LAD 0.33, RCA 0.16, LCx 0.15 mm/10 mm, p = 0.0061) could not be checked against any abstract I could reach. Treat them as unverified until the full text is read.

## Q1. What is known about normal coronary tapering, and does plaque change taper (remodelling, Glagov)?

### Takeaway
Normal arteries taper, and the amount of taper depends on the artery and the segment. In angiographically smooth arteries the LAD tapers more than the RCA. Normal calibre is set mainly by sex, dominance, the downstream branch length and myocardial mass, and is not set by age. Plaque changes the lumen in two ways: by outward (Glagov) remodelling, which preserves the lumen until about 40% stenosis, and by uneven plaque build-up along the artery. Lumen taper measured in diseased arteries is therefore partly an effect of plaque.

### Cited Findings
- **Javier SP, Mintz GS, et al. 1995, Am J Cardiol 75:177–180** (IVUS, diseased arteries, cross-sectional; abstract only, read via an index page because Europe PMC has no abstract). The abstract says:
  - "Lumen tapering is almost always seen in coronary artery disease, and averages 0.22 mm for every 10 mm of arterial length."
  - "reversed lumen tapering is uncommon (approximately 10% of arteries)."
  - "The mechanism of lumen tapering (EEM tapering vs differential plaque accumulation) varies with lesion location."
  - Patient and artery counts were not in the text available. — [DOI 10.1016/s0002-9149(00)80072-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0002-9149(00)80072-7); [abstract index page](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/10/1880407.htm)
- **Zubaid M, Buller C, Mancini GB 2002, Can J Cardiol 18:973–980** (QCA in 13 men and 13 women with normal, right-dominant arteries; abstract only, no DOI listed; PMID 12368932):
  - Definition: T1 = (proximal − distal segment diameter)/proximal diameter, in %.
  - In women, T1 was 18% and 29% in the proximal and distal LAD, and 9% and 7% in the proximal and distal RCA. The SDs are garbled in the record and should not be quoted.
  - Men gave "similar tapering results".
  - The authors give the rationale that atheroma building up "distally and at branch points induces greater than expected tapering", so expected taper "may identify diseased segments that are not angiographically stenotic". — [Europe PMC 12368932](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12368932)
- **Dodge JT et al. 1992, Circulation 86:232–246** (QCA; 83 arteriograms chosen from 9,160 for smooth borders; abstract only):
  - Repeat measurement error was 0.12 mm.
  - Normal diameters: LM 4.5±0.5 mm, proximal LAD 3.7±0.4 mm, distal LAD 1.9±0.4 mm. This is about a halving of the LAD diameter from proximal to distal.
  - Women had 9% smaller diameters than men (p<0.001), even after normalising for BSA (p<0.01).
  - Calibre increased with branch length (p<0.001).
  - Calibre was not affected by age or tortuosity.
  - Calibre was larger in LV hypertrophy (+17%) and dilated cardiomyopathy (+12%).
  - Dominance changed the proximal RCA (3.9 vs 2.8 mm) and the LCx (3.4 vs 4.2 mm). — [DOI 10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- **Seiler C, Kirkeeide RL, Gould KL 1992, Circulation 85:1987–2003** (QCA; 12 patients without and 17 with CAD; abstract only):
  - Lumen area correlated closely with the summed distal branch length and regional myocardial mass, both with and without CAD.
  - The relations followed a 2/3 power law, consistent with minimum energy loss and limited WSS, and not with constant flow velocity.
  - In CAD the lumen was "diffusely 30–50% too small for distal myocardial bed size". — [DOI 10.1161/01.cir.85.6.1987](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.85.6.1987)
- **Medrano-Gracia P et al. 2016, EuroIntervention** (CCTA atlas of 300 adults with CAC = 0 and no stenoses; abstract only):
  - LM diameter 3.5±0.8 mm, LM length 10.5±5.3 mm.
  - LM bifurcation angle 89±21° with an intermediate artery and 75±23° without (p<0.001).
  - The abstract reports no taper values and no plaque outcome. — [DOI 10.4244/EIJV12I7A139](https://doi.org/10.4244/EIJV12I7A139)
- **Medrano-Gracia P et al. 2017, J Cardiovasc Transl Res** (211 CAC-zero, stenosis-free CCTAs, 446 bifurcations; abstract read, OA):
  - Size and bifurcation angle differed by sex.
  - "Hypertension, smoking history and diabetes did not appear to have an association with shape."
  - Huo–Kassab gave the best diameter-law fit. — [DOI 10.1007/s12265-016-9720-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12265-016-9720-2)
- **Zhou Y, Kassab GS, Molloi S 2002, Phys Med Biol** (5 swine, in vivo angiography; abstract only; animal): the model linking area, length, volume and flow was validated. A simulated diffuse disease with about 40% area reduction suggests these relations "can be utilized to quantify moderate levels of diffuse coronary artery disease". — [PMID 11936182](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11936182)
- **Glagov S et al. 1987, NEJM 316:1371** (autopsy, LM in 136 hearts; abstract only):
  - Internal elastic lamina area correlated with lesion area (r = 0.44, p<0.001).
  - Lumen area did not fall for stenosis of 0–40%, but fell steeply above 40% (r = −0.73). — [DOI 10.1056/nejm198705283162204](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejm198705283162204)
- **Tsutsui H et al. 2001, Circulation** (serial IVUS over 5 years, 38 heart-transplant recipients; abstract only; not native atherosclerosis):
  - Intimal area rose from 1.8 to 3.0 mm² in year 1.
  - EEM area expanded between years 1 and 3 (15.4 to 17.2 mm²) and then shrank by year 5 (15.1 mm²).
  - Lumen loss was caused early by intimal thickening and late by constriction. — [DOI 10.1161/hc3101.093867](https://doi.org/10.1161/hc3101.093867)

### Inferences
- Normal taper differs a great deal by artery and segment (distal LAD ≫ RCA in Zubaid). In a pooled model, segment intercepts (or segment-specific centring) are needed before taper can carry any person-level signal.
- Glagov remodelling implies that early plaque can leave the lumen intact. Lumen taper on a plaque-free CCTA is therefore close to the "native" taper. Wall thickening below CT detection, which is outwardly remodelled, would barely change it.

### Gaps
- No full text of Javier 1995 was available: the sample size, the per-artery taper values and how the two mechanisms split by location are unverified.
- No CCTA-based normal reference values for taper (mm/mm or log-slope) were found. The Medrano-Gracia atlas abstracts give diameters and angles, not taper.

## Q2. Do taper or calibre relative to flow or myocardial mass relate to plaque, diffuse disease or FFR?

### Takeaway
There is consistent evidence that a lumen too small for the myocardium it supplies goes with diffuse disease, more plaque, lower FFR and impaired perfusion. This comes from Seiler 1992, the V/M ratio studies (Taylor 2017, Grover 2017, Benetos 2021) and the Huo 2013 scaling study. All of this work is in **referred, symptomatic or diseased cohorts**, and almost all of it is cross-sectional. TAG measures the gradient of contrast attenuation, not of geometry. It adds little to CCTA and depends on lumen area and vessel length. Ectasia (diffuse dilatation) is generally regarded as a variant of atherosclerosis. Calibre therefore deviates in both directions in disease.

### Cited Findings
- **Taylor CA et al. 2017, JCCT** (NXT trial, 238 patients with suspected CAD, 438 vessels; abstract only):
  - Patients with V/M below the median (18.57 mm³/g) had more plaque, greater QCA stenosis and lower FFR (0.80±0.12 vs 0.87±0.08, P<0.0001).
  - The same held in vessels with ≤50% stenosis (FFR 0.81 vs 0.88).
  - V/M was an independent predictor of FFR ≤0.80, "independent of plaque measures". — [DOI 10.1016/j.jcct.2017.08.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcct.2017.08.001)
- **Grover R et al. 2017, JCCT** (30 microvascular-angina patients vs 32 controls matched for plaque burden; abstract only):
  - V/M was 25.6 vs 30.0 mm³/g (p = 0.007, c = 0.69).
  - Lumen volume was 2302 vs 2978 mm³.
  - Nadir FFR-CT did not differ. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jcct.2017.09.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcct.2017.09.015)
- **Benetos G et al. 2021, Eur Radiol** (60 patients, hybrid CCTA and ¹³N-ammonia PET; abstract read, OA):
  - CAVi is "proposed as an indicator of diffuse atherosclerosis".
  - CAVi correlated with stress MBF (R = 0.50) and MFR (R = 0.39).
  - CAVi was independently associated with abnormal stress MBF (OR 0.90 per mm³/g). — [DOI 10.1007/s00330-020-07586-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-020-07586-y)
- **Holmes KR et al. 2024, Radiol Cardiothorac Imaging** (ADVANCE registry, 2,874 patients with ≥30% stenosis; abstract only):
  - V/M was 24.1 in current smokers, 24.9 in former smokers and 25.8 mm³/g in never-smokers.
  - Current smoking (OR 0.74), former smoking (OR 0.81) and ≥50% stenosis (OR 0.62) were associated with higher V/M; the outcome was coded as high V/M.
  - V/M therefore tracks risk factors and disease. — [DOI 10.1148/ryct.220197](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryct.220197)
- **Kageyama S et al. 2024, JCCT** (FAST-TRACK CABG, 106 patients with severe CAD; abstract only): median V/M was 16.1 vs 24.8 mm³/g in the unselected ADVANCE cohort. V correlated weakly with SYNTAX (r = −0.26 to −0.34). — [DOI 10.1016/j.jcct.2024.06.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcct.2024.06.010)
- **Huo Y, Kassab GS et al. 2013, Radiology** (CCTA, 89 metabolic-syndrome patients vs 31 matched controls; abstract read, PMC):
  - Mean lumen CSA was 0.039 vs 0.054 cm² and total volume 2.71 vs 3.29 cm³ (P<.05).
  - The length–volume scaling coefficient was 27.0 vs 19.9.
  - More than 65% of patients had a coefficient ≥23, against about 10% of controls.
  - This is proof of concept for "diagnosis of diffuse CAD". No plaque endpoint was used: metabolic syndrome stood in for disease. — [DOI 10.1148/radiol.13122181](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.13122181)
- **Huo Y et al. 2013, J R Soc Interface** (CCTA in 6 Ossabaw pigs on a high-fat diet vs 6 controls; abstract read; **animal**): diet-induced diffuse positive remodelling lowered the length–volume coefficient (14.6 vs 40.9). Disease can therefore *enlarge* the lumen relative to the branch length it serves. — [DOI 10.1098/rsif.2012.1015](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2012.1015)
- **Huo Y, Kassab GS 2016, J Biomech** (review; abstract only): discusses scaling-law deviations in relation to diffuse CAD and to Glagov positive remodelling in early atherosclerosis. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2016.01.044](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2016.01.044)
- **TAG (transluminal attenuation gradient)**:
  - Definition: "the linear regression coefficient between luminal attenuation and axial distance".
  - **Wong DT et al. 2013, JACC** (54 patients, 78 vessels): TAG was −21 HU/10 mm in FFR-significant vessels and −11 in non-significant ones. A cut-off of −15.1 HU/10 mm gave sensitivity 77% and specificity 74%. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2012.12.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2012.12.029)
  - **Ko BS et al. 2016, Radiology** (NXT substudy): AUC was 0.72 for TAG vs 0.93 for FFR-CT. — [DOI 10.1148/radiol.2015150383](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.2015150383)
  - Several abstracts report no added value of TAG and dependence on vessel length and lumen area. This was read only as a search-result summary of these abstracts: [Xu 2019, Eur Radiol, DOI 10.1007/s00330-019-06311-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-019-06311-8); [von Felten 2022, J Nucl Cardiol, DOI 10.1007/s12350-020-02242-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12350-020-02242-w); [Henriksson 2021, Acta Radiol, DOI 10.1177/0284185120943042](https://doi.org/10.1177/0284185120943042).
  - All of these abstracts concern stenosis detection.
- **Ectasia**:
  - **Swaye PS et al. 1983, Circulation** (CASS registry; abstract only): aneurysmal disease was found in 978 patients, 4.9% of the registry. Risk factors and 5-year survival did not differ from non-aneurysmal CAD. The authors conclude it is "a variant of coronary atherosclerosis". — [DOI 10.1161/01.cir.67.1.134](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.67.1.134)
  - **Markis JE et al. 1976, Am J Cardiol** (2,457 catheterised patients, 30 with ectasia; abstract only): annual mortality was 15%, similar to medically treated 3-vessel disease. — [DOI 10.1016/0002-9149(76)90315-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0002-9149(76)90315-5)
  - **Yetkin E, Waltenberger J 2007, Clin Res Cardiol** (review; abstract read, OA): whether ectasia is atherosclerosis is unresolved. The review notes clear differences in risk factors, such as diabetes, and in pathogenesis. — [DOI 10.1007/s00392-007-0521-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00392-007-0521-0)
  - **Yang JJ et al. 2013, J Geriatr Cardiol** (1,400 older adults, CCTA): ectasia was found in 131 and was associated with hypertension and hyperlipidaemia in multivariable analysis. — [DOI 10.3969/j.issn.1671-5411.2013.01.003](https://doi.org/10.3969/j.issn.1671-5411.2013.01.003)

### Inferences
- Calibre deviates in **both directions** in disease: too small (Seiler, low V/M) and too large (positive remodelling, ectasia). A lumen that tapers too little or too much is therefore consistent with a diseased artery. This motivates a signed taper feature with a possibly non-linear effect. The direction expected at a plaque-free baseline is not set by these data.
- V/M and scaling studies use whole-tree volume. Taper per segment is a local, normalised analogue, since log-radius slope is scale-free. No study links that local form to plaque.

### Gaps
- No V/M, CAVi or scaling-law study was found in a population-based or plaque-free cohort with plaque as a *later* outcome. All were in referred, symptomatic or severe-disease cohorts, and cross-sectional (Benetos 2020 prognosis is in the earlier notes).
- Ectasia–plaque evidence is from catheterisation registries (referral) and gives no taper metric.

## Q3. Has any study related lumen taper or axial diameter change to plaque location, progression or events?

### Takeaway
No human study was found that uses lumen taper (an axial diameter slope) as an exposure for later plaque location, progression or events. The closest work is of three kinds:
- Serial IVUS of remodelling, which shows plaque grows behind a preserved lumen (Glagov; Stone 2003 in the earlier notes).
- "Expected taper" proposed as a marker of hidden diffuse disease (Zubaid 2002), without outcome testing.
- Taper as a nuisance in TAG and FFR-CT.

In interventional cardiology, "tapering lesions" are a stent-sizing problem, not a risk factor.

### Cited Findings
- Zubaid 2002 proposes expected taper to detect non-stenotic diseased segments, but tests no outcome. — [Europe PMC 12368932](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12368932)
- Javier 1995 found that lumen taper in diseased arteries partly reflects differential plaque accumulation (cross-sectional). — [DOI 10.1016/s0002-9149(00)80072-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0002-9149(00)80072-7)
- **Wu X et al. 2024, Rev Cardiovasc Med** (251 IVUS-guided "tapering lesions"; abstract read, OA): only a Huo–Kassab-based volumetric expansion index predicted 2-year device-oriented events (HR 1.91). Taper is used here only for stent sizing. — [DOI 10.31083/j.rcm2502057](https://doi.org/10.31083/j.rcm2502057)
- Tsutsui 2001 is the only multi-year serial IVUS found here: in transplant vasculopathy, EEM expanded and then constricted. — [DOI 10.1161/hc3101.093867](https://doi.org/10.1161/hc3101.093867)

### Inferences
- Testing taper as a predictor of incident plaque on a plaque-free baseline would be new. The thesis should say "no study has tested", not "taper is known to predict".

### Gaps
- No serial IVUS or OCT study of *native* arteries was opened that relates baseline local lumen expansion or axial diameter change to later plaque (beyond the WSS studies already in the earlier notes).
- No CCTA follow-up study (PARADIGM, ICONIC etc.) reporting taper was found.

## Q4. How reproducible are lumen diameter and taper on CCTA, and how do they scale with body size and sex?

### Takeaway
CCTA lumen size has moderate absolute accuracy against IVUS: an RMSE of about 10% for diameter and 16% for area in a small series, and slight underestimation in calcified lesions. Scan–rescan studies report plaque volumes, not lumen diameter or taper. A non-coronary airway CT study suggests taper is much less reproducible than lumen area. Calibre scales with sex, which persists after BSA normalisation, with dominance and with LV mass and hypertrophy, but not with age. Ethnicity and endurance training also change it.

### Cited Findings
- **Luo T et al. 2014, PLoS One** (5 patients, 11 arteries, CT vs IVUS; abstract read, OA): RMSE was 16.2% for CSA and 9.5% for diameter. — [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0086949](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0086949)
- **Noll D et al. 2013, Postepy Kardiol Interwencyjnej** (252 calcified lesions, 60 patients; abstract read, OA):
  - CCTA underestimated mean lumen diameter (2.8 vs 2.9 mm) and area (6.4 vs 7.0 mm², p<0.001).
  - There was no difference in the smallest-lumen tertile.
  - The authors attribute low accuracy to "high variance and not to systematic error". — [DOI 10.5114/pwki.2013.34022](https://doi.org/10.5114/pwki.2013.34022)
- **Iraqi N et al. 2024, JCCT** (101 patients scanned twice 1 h apart; abstract only):
  - Interscan correlation for plaque volumes was 0.93–0.99.
  - Agreement was better per patient than per vessel or lesion.
  - Agreement depended on image quality and reconstruction, and was better in the LAD.
  - Overall reproducibility was "modest". — [DOI 10.1016/j.jcct.2024.09.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcct.2024.09.009)
- **Lenell J et al. 2026, Eur Radiol** (30 participants, serial CCTA at a median of 6 days, AI software; abstract read, OA): plaque-metric ICC was ≥0.96. Lumen and taper were not reported. — [DOI 10.1007/s00330-026-12598-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12598-1)
- **Nadeem SA et al. 2020, IEEE ISBI** (**airway CT, not coronary**; repeat scans; abstract read): ICC was >0.9 for lumen area but >0.47 for **tapering**. This is the only repeat-scan taper reliability figure found, and an analogy only. — [DOI 10.1109/isbi45749.2020.9098558](https://doi.org/10.1109/isbi45749.2020.9098558)
- Dodge 1992 found women's arteries 9% smaller even after BSA normalisation, no effect of age, and +17% in LVH. — [DOI 10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- Medrano-Gracia 2017 found that size and bifurcation angle differed by sex in CAC-zero CCTA. — [DOI 10.1007/s12265-016-9720-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12265-016-9720-2)
- **Welch T et al. 2022, Open Heart** (170 CCTAs, Trinidad): there was no association between gender and coronary dimensions, but there were ethnic differences, which persisted after BSA adjustment. This partly contradicts Dodge on sex. — [DOI 10.1136/openhrt-2021-001922](https://doi.org/10.1136/openhrt-2021-001922)
- **Langer C et al. 2021, J Cardiovasc Dev Dis** (98 participants, endurance athletes vs matched controls, CCTA): athletes had larger proximal LAD, proximal and mid LCx and RCA, while the LM and distal segments were similar. Training therefore changes *proximal* calibre and so the proximal-to-distal taper. — [DOI 10.3390/jcdd8110141](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcdd8110141)
- **Skowroński J et al. 2025, Cardiol J** (CCTA, dilated cardiomyopathy without atherosclerosis vs controls matched for sex, dominance and BSA): total coronary volume was larger in DCM (2879 vs 2521 mm³). — [DOI 10.5603/cj.104850](https://doi.org/10.5603/cj.104850)

### Inferences
- Taper as a log-radius slope is dimensionless, so most body-size and sex scaling of absolute calibre cancels out. Sex, LV mass and training could still change the proximal-to-distal ratio (Langer). Keeping sex in the base model (it is in SCORE2) covers the main known modifier.
- The Medis comparison in this thesis is the only available tracer-specific agreement estimate for taper, and the literature gives no reference ICC to compare it with.

### Gaps
- No coronary CCTA study reporting inter-observer or scan–rescan reliability of **lumen diameter slope or taper** was found.
- No CCTA study of taper versus BSA, sex or age in normal arteries was found.

## Reverse causation (cross-cutting)

### Takeaway
In diseased arteries the lumen taper is partly caused by plaque: it reflects differential plaque accumulation (Javier), diffuse narrowing (Seiler) and positive remodelling or ectasia (Glagov, Huo 2013 pigs, Swaye). Any cross-sectional taper–plaque association is therefore uninterpretable as a cause. A plaque-free baseline (SIS = SSS = 0) with incident plaque about 10 years later removes visible plaque as a cause of the baseline taper. Subclinical, outwardly remodelled wall thickening below CT resolution remains possible. By Glagov, though, it should leave the lumen almost unchanged until about 40% stenosis.

### Cited Findings
- Differential plaque accumulation as a mechanism of lumen taper. — [Javier 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0002-9149(00)80072-7)
- Diffuse 30–50% lumen deficit in CAD. — [Seiler 1992](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.85.6.1987)
- Lumen preserved up to about 40% stenosis. — [Glagov 1987](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejm198705283162204)
- Low V/M tracks plaque and smoking. — [Taylor 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcct.2017.08.001); [Holmes 2024](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryct.220197)

### Inferences
- Candidate sentence material for the review (inference, phrased cautiously): "Normal coronary arteries taper in proportion to the myocardium they supply (Seiler 1992; Dodge 1992). Arteries with coronary disease show both lumens too small for their bed (Seiler 1992; Taylor 2017) and lumen tapering that partly reflects uneven plaque build-up (Javier 1995). In cross-sectional studies, therefore, taper may be a consequence of plaque rather than a cause. No study has measured taper on plaque-free arteries and followed them for incident plaque."
