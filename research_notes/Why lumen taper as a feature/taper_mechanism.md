# Why lumen taper should affect wall shear stress (WSS) and plaque location: the haemodynamic mechanism

Scope and method: web search and fetch, about 17 tool calls, building on the earlier notes in `research_notes/Taper and curvature interaction for WSS/` (cfd_mechanics.md, wss_surrogates.md) and `reports/Taper and curvature interaction for WSS.md`. Those notes already hold Johnston et al. 2009 (MODSIM), Kandangwa et al. 2022, van der Giessen et al. 2011, the Kassab 7/3 law, the Taylor et al. 2024 meta-analysis (exponent 2.39) and the Poiseuille scaling arithmetic. They are only referred to here, not repeated. Access level is given per source. Where a DOI was not shown on a page I fetched, it is marked "DOI not verified". No Shen 2026 review or arXiv 2507.17109 is used. No arXiv preprint is relied on.

## Q1. Which CFD or experimental studies varied taper (convergent / uniform / divergent) and reported the effect on WSS, low-WSS area, OSI or separation?

### Takeaway
Only one study I found directly compares tapered with uniform coronary-like geometry and reports WSS: Johnston et al. 2009, already in hand. Every other piece of evidence is indirect. Removing side branches, which removes flow loss, changes WSS substantially. Locally widened coronary segments (Kawasaki aneurysms) have very low WSS. A non-coronary parametric stenosis study shows that taper redistributes WSS. I found no coronary CFD study that compares divergent with convergent segments, and I could not confirm any taper result in Soulis et al. 2006.

### Cited Findings
- **Tapered vs. uniform idealised coronary models (already verified):** in tapered S-shaped models the global WSS minimum moved to the straight proximal inlet, which the authors attribute "in part, to the overall taper in the artery and not only to the local curvature variation". At that taper-driven site OSI was only moderate. Full text; see earlier notes — [Johnston et al. 2009, MODSIM](https://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim09/B4/johnston_bm.pdf)
- **Widest lumen, lowest ESS in normal vessels (already verified by caller):** CCTA-based CFD in 349 normal vessels, with fixed outflow and no side branches — Schultz et al. 2023, Int J Cardiovasc Imaging (DOI not re-checked here).
- **Taper and side branches together in a single RCA model:** with side branches below MRI resolution left out, flow is too high distally. Removing taper (constant mean velocity) moved the largest TAWSS and transWSS to the proximal vessel. Full text; see earlier notes — [Kandangwa et al. 2022, Front Bioeng Biotechnol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2022.962687/full)
- **Side branches (flow loss) change WSS a lot:** in angiography-based coronary models, "when the model geometry has fewer side branches, the ESS is overestimated". In one LAD, time-averaged ESS was 4.75 Pa with no side branches against 0.25 Pa with three. Removing the LAD side branches cut coronary volume flow by 72%. The authors conclude that "models that have only a subset of side branches are inadequate for biomechanical studies". Full text. **Caveat:** the same paper also reports that low and intermediate ESS were *overestimated* in the reduced models (ESS < 1 Pa in 15.2% vs. 10.6% of sections). The direction depends on the boundary-condition set-up, so this paper shows sensitivity, not a clean taper effect — [Vardhan et al. 2019, Sci Rep, DOI 10.1038/s41598-019-45342-5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6586809)
- **Boundary conditions for flow loss:** compared with an empirical flow–diameter fit, Murray's law gave about 8% higher main-branch WSS and about 13% smaller low-WSS regions near bifurcations. Abstract/repository; see earlier notes — [van der Giessen et al. 2011, J Biomech](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/the-influence-of-boundary-conditions-on-wall-shear-stress-distrib/)
- **Locally widened coronary segment (expansion analogue):** in the first patient-specific simulation of a Kawasaki-disease coronary aneurysm, WSS was about an order of magnitude lower than in a virtual normal-anatomy control of the same patient. Recirculation and high shear gradients appeared at the aneurysm neck. Abstract/record level — [Sengupta et al. 2012, record](https://omicsdi.org/dataset/biostudies-literature/S-EPMC6091534). In a follow-up of 5 aneurysm patients, haemodynamic metrics were argued to predict thrombotic risk better than diameter alone; the authors call for larger studies. Abstract — [Sengupta et al. 2014, Biomech Model Mechanobiol, DOI 10.1007/s10237-014-0570-z](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4990134). An APS DFD 2010 conference abstract reported "persistently low shear" in aneurysmal regions — [APS abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dfd/2010/ml/7/) (conference abstract, not peer reviewed).
- **Coronary ectasia, a contrary signal:** in 53 ectatic arteries, the 11 with thrombosis had *higher* mean TAWSS, OSI, RRT and multidirectional WSS than those without. Rigid walls, Newtonian fluid, angiography reconstructions. Abstract/full text via PMC — [Frontiers Cardiovasc Med 2025, DOI 10.3389/fcvm.2025.1705263](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12669196/). This compares ectatic arteries with each other, not with normal calibre, so it does not contradict "wider → lower WSS". It does show that the outcome (thrombus) did not follow low TAWSS there.
- **Parametric taper in a stenosis model (non-coronary-tree, MDPI 2026):** taper "influenced whether wall-shear loading was concentrated within the stenosis or distributed over a longer arterial segment". Area-weighted WSS distal to the stenosis was 0.49–1.73 Pa in a 70% taper configuration against about 0.12–0.56 Pa without taper. Search snippet only; MDPI journal, idealised — [Math Comput Appl 2026, 31(6):251](https://www.mdpi.com/2297-8747/31/6/251)
- **Non-coronary rigid idealised tube:** WSS and pressure drop rose as the convergent taper angle rose (0.5°, 1°, 1.5°). Low-impact journal; see earlier notes — [Univ. Baghdad J Eng](https://joe.uobaghdad.edu.iq/index.php/main/article/view/2462)
- **Soulis et al. 2006 (J Biomech), "Wall shear stress in normal left coronary artery tree":** finite-element model from averaged angiographic data, with low WSS opposite the flow dividers. I found **no confirmed statement on taper** in it. A companion paper on the wall pressure gradient (Giannoglou, Soulis et al. 2005) reportedly found that the gradient "increases substantially at distal segments due to increased velocity from vessel tapering". A 2003 companion reported "increased values at distal tree parts" for WSS. Search snippets only — [isc.ac record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/10/453451.htm)

### Inferences
- The direct coronary evidence that taper sets the WSS *level* along an artery rests on one idealised model (Johnston 2009), on side-branch/boundary-condition sensitivity studies, and on the cross-sectional Schultz finding. The aneurysm data are an extreme form of the same principle: a lumen too wide for its flow has low WSS.
- Divergent (expanding) segments: the physical expectation (deceleration, adverse pressure gradient, separation at an expansion) is textbook fluid mechanics and is consistent with recirculation at the Kawasaki aneurysm neck. I found no coronary CFD of mildly divergent, non-aneurysmal segments.

### Gaps
- No coronary CFD study was found that varies taper angle systematically (convergent / uniform / divergent) while holding curvature and branching fixed.
- The Soulis 2006 full text was not accessed, so any taper statement in it is unconfirmed.
- Wang/Ku and Liu taper studies were not found in this pass; I did not search them further.

## Q2. Must taper match flow loss through side branches for WSS to stay uniform? Which papers state it?

### Takeaway
Yes in principle, and stated explicitly in part. Gijsen et al. 2019 (expert consensus, EHJ) write that side-branch flow loss "is typically accompanied by tapering of the lumen". The Murray / Kassab–Fung literature ties uniform WSS to an exponent of 3 in the flow–diameter relation (Q ∝ d³). Kassab & Finet and Karau et al. show that real coronary trees deviate from this (exponent about 7/3, heterogeneous), so WSS is *not* exactly uniform and rises towards smaller vessels. The statement "an artery that narrows less than its branching demands is relatively wide and has low WSS" follows from Poiseuille plus mass conservation. I found it stated in no paper in those words, so it is an inference.

### Cited Findings
- Expert consensus: "This leads to a reduction of blood flow in the main vessel, which is typically accompanied by tapering of the lumen", and coronary flow "is distributed between the main vessel and side branch, mainly depending on their diameters". Diameter-based scaling laws are recommended for outlet boundary conditions when flow is unknown. Full text (first third read) — [Gijsen et al. 2019, Eur Heart J 40:3421–3433, DOI 10.1093/eurheartj/ehz551](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/40/41/3421/5576147)
- Murray's exponent of 3 implies "the wall shear stress is constant throughout the vasculature", which the authors call "not supported by experimental measurements". Epicardial coronary arteries follow an exponent of "7/3 (2.33)". Full text — [Kassab & Finet 2015, EuroIntervention 11 Suppl V, DOI 10.4244/EIJV11SVA3](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomy-and-function-relation-in-the-coronary-tree-from-bifurcations-to-myocardial-flow-and-mass)
- Murray's law can be derived from a uniform vessel-wall shear strain rate hypothesis. Measured coronary *arteriolar* bifurcations in pigs agreed closely with Murray's law. Abstract fragments only — [Kassab & Fung 1995, Ann Biomed Eng 23:13–20, DOI 10.1007/BF02368296](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF02368296)
- In simulated trees with heterogeneous branching exponents, as in real trees, variation in WSS is comparable to the variation in the exponent. WSS "tends to rise as vessel diameter falls, even when mean z is 3". The authors conclude that the influence of shear stress on vessel diameters "is not, per se, manifested in a mean value of z". Abstract (via Europe PMC) — [Karau, Krenz & Dawson 2001, Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol 280:H1256–H1263, DOI 10.1152/ajpheart.2001.280.3.H1256](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.2001.280.3.H1256)
- Generalisation of Murray's law to the coronary tree from cost minimisation and a flow–cumulative-length linearity assumption, with a truncated reconstructed tree (down to 500 µm) and a symmetric model. Abstract only — Zhou, Kassab & Molloi 1999, Phys Med Biol 44:2929 (DOI not verified) — [record](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/67773065)
- Structure–function: lumen area scales with the distal myocardial mass and summed branch length it supplies (a 2/3 power law). In CAD patients, lumens are about 30–50% smaller than expected for their bed. Aggregator abstract fragments only; figures to be checked against the original — Seiler, Kirkeeide & Gould 1992, Circulation 85:1987–2003 (DOI not verified) — [record](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W1967018012)
- Uniform shear and homogeneous perfusion trade off against each other in optimised tree models. Search snippet — Schreiner et al. 2003, J Theor Biol — [Univ. Vienna record](https://comp-phys.univie.ac.at/publications/publicationsdetailansicht/pure/c42e4478-6488-43db-a12b-9eb93a80a8cd/show/publ/Pure)
- Already in the earlier notes: meta-analysis of 1,070 coronary trees, pooled exponent 2.39 ([Taylor et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11380967/)); and side-branch diameter (hence flow loss) can be inferred from main-vessel taper via Murray's law ([Saxton et al.](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/33452/1/Saxton-EvaluationOfModels%28VoR%29.pdf)).

### Inferences
- The mechanism in one line (derivation, not a quote): Poiseuille WSS is τ = 4μQ/(πr³). Between side branches Q is constant, so any narrowing raises τ. At a side branch Q drops, so the artery must narrow for τ to stay level. Under Murray (Q ∝ r³) a perfectly matched taper keeps τ constant. Taper *weaker* than the flow loss leaves the lumen wide for its flow, which lowers τ. Taper stronger than the flow loss raises it.
- Since real exponents are about 2.3–2.4, WSS rises gently along a "normal" artery (τ ∝ r^−0.6 to −0.7). Raw taper therefore mixes a flow-matched part with a mismatch part (see earlier report). Citations for the thesis sentence: Gijsen 2019 (taper accompanies flow loss), Kassab & Finet 2015 (exponent 3 means uniform WSS; real exponent 7/3), Johnston 2009 (taper sets the WSS level in CFD).

### Gaps
- No paper was found that states explicitly that under-tapering relative to branch flow loss causes low WSS *and* plaque. No flow-matched taper index exists in the literature.
- Huo & Kassab 2016 (J Biomech, scaling laws in health and disease) was seen at abstract level only in earlier notes.

## Q3. Is WSS uniformity / shear stress homeostasis a documented principle, and does deviation relate to disease?

### Takeaway
Shear stress homeostasis is well documented as an *adaptive* principle. When flow changes, the lumen remodels until WSS returns to about baseline (Kamiya & Togawa 1980; Zarins et al. 1987). It is *not* true that WSS is uniform at about 1.5 Pa across the tree: WSS differs between vessels and species and varies inversely with diameter (Cheng et al. 2007). The link to disease runs through local deviations from the set point, chiefly low WSS at geometric features (Gijsen 2019; Chatzizisis 2007). I found no paper that links a person's or artery's deviation from a taper-based WSS homeostasis to incident plaque.

### Cited Findings
- Arteriovenous shunt in 12 dogs (carotid): lumen radius rose with flow load, and WSS returned to within about 15% of control when flow was less than four times control. The authors propose a local autoregulatory mechanism of wall shear stress. Abstract-level via secondary pages — [Kamiya & Togawa 1980, Am J Physiol 239:H14–H21](https://typeset.io/papers/adaptive-regulation-of-wall-shear-stress-to-flow-change-in-2ot1ojh5a4) (DOI not verified; usually given as 10.1152/ajpheart.1980.239.1.H14)
- Cholesterol-fed monkeys with an iliac AV fistula: lumen diameter doubled, so calculated WSS was the same on the high-flow side (16 ± 4 dyn/cm²) and the control side (15 ± 2 dyn/cm²). The authors conclude that "shear stress may serve to regulate artery lumen diameter". Abstract-level via secondary pages — Zarins et al. 1987, J Vasc Surg 5:413–420 (DOI not verified) — [record](https://mobile.jci.org/references/scholar/40280/B26)
- Contrary to the "uniform 15 dyn/cm²" assumption, WSS is not constant along the tree, varies between species and is inversely related to vessel diameter. Human values in the literature range 2–16 N/m². Across species the data fit an r² law (Zamir) rather than Murray's r³. Abstract-level — [Cheng et al. 2007, Atherosclerosis 195:225–235, PMID 17169362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17169362) (DOI not verified)
- Consensus: time-averaged WSS in healthy human coronaries is "approximately 1.4 Pa", and "1.2–1.5 Pa is the most frequently used value". Regulation by shear "plays a continuous role in the development, growth, remodelling, and maintenance of the vascular system". Low WSS "typically occurs at the inner bend of curved arteries, ostia of branches, lateral walls of bifurcations", and "low wall shear stress continues to contribute to early plaque growth". Full text (partial) — [Gijsen et al. 2019, DOI 10.1093/eurheartj/ehz551](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/40/41/3421/5576147)
- Low ESS induces an atherogenic endothelial phenotype and early plaque formation. Each early plaque's natural history depends on the local ESS and on the remodelling response. Abstract-level — [Chatzizisis et al. 2007, J Am Coll Cardiol 49:2379–2393, DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2007.02.059](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edselp,S073510970701220X)

### Inferences
- The homeostasis literature gives the thesis its framing: lumen calibre is normally adapted to flow so that WSS stays near a set point. Taper is the along-artery expression of that adaptation to side-branch flow loss. A segment whose taper departs from it has WSS off the set point. This is a chain of published premises plus an inference; the last step is not tested anywhere.
- The adaptive remodelling argument also predicts that a plaque-free lumen may already have adjusted calibre to flow. This would *weaken* the taper–WSS link in healthy arteries and is a reason to expect small effects.

### Gaps
- The Kamiya & Togawa and Zarins full texts were not accessed. The numbers come from secondary pages that agree with each other.
- No study links deviation from shear homeostasis *along a coronary artery* to plaque incidence.

## Q4. Do expert reviews state that lumen calibre or its variation along the vessel drives WSS?

### Takeaway
Partly. Gijsen et al. 2019 explicitly connect side-branch flow loss with lumen tapering and say that flow divides mainly by diameter, which makes diameter the main determinant of flow, and so of WSS, in CFD set-ups. I found no expert review sentence saying that taper itself is a determinant of plaque location. The reviews name curvature, bifurcations and ostia as the low-WSS sites, not taper.

### Cited Findings
- "This leads to a reduction of blood flow in the main vessel, which is typically accompanied by tapering of the lumen"; "Various scaling laws are available that relate the local diameter of the artery to the average velocity" — [Gijsen et al. 2019, DOI 10.1093/eurheartj/ehz551](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/40/41/3421/5576147) (full text, first third read; the remaining two thirds of the page were not read)
- The low-WSS sites listed in the consensus are inner bends, ostia and lateral bifurcation walls; taper is not listed — same source.
- Earlier notes: diameter is the most consistent single correlate of TAWSS in patient-specific studies, and ML surrogates rank radius first. Some of those sources are arXiv preprints and should be flagged, not relied on — see `wss_surrogates.md`.

### Inferences
- A defensible literature-review wording (claims each map to a cited source): "Along a coronary artery, blood leaves through side branches, and the lumen narrows with the falling flow \citep{gijsen2019}. If the diameter fell exactly in proportion to flow as described by Murray's law, wall shear stress would be the same throughout the tree; measured coronary trees deviate from this, so wall shear stress depends on how closely calibre follows flow \citep{kassab2015, karau2001}. In computational models, a tapered artery has its lowest wall shear stress in the wide proximal part \citep{johnston2009}, and in normal CCTA-based vessels wall shear stress is lowest where the lumen is widest \citep{schultz2023}." The step "so a segment that narrows less than its flow loss has low WSS and is prone to plaque" should be phrased as the thesis's hypothesis, not as an established finding.

### Gaps
- The later sections of Gijsen 2019 and the Wentzel/Stone reviews were not read in full for a taper-specific sentence.
- No published study has tested taper as a plaque predictor (consistent with the brief).
