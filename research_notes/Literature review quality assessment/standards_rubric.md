# Standards for an excellent (grade 12) master's thesis literature review: DTU / Danish context, biomedical engineering

Research date: 2026-10-04. Note on method: about 20 search/fetch calls. Several primary PDFs (Randolph 2009 on PARE, the USU dissertation-quality follow-up, the EPFL guide, DTU's own MSc thesis rules PDF) were blocked or returned 403/404; where that happened the claim is moved to Gaps or marked as from my background knowledge rather than a fetched source. The Boote & Beile (2005) rubric below was extracted verbatim from the full paper PDF.

## Q1. What does DTU / the Danish 12-point scale demand, and how does it translate to the literature review?

### Takeaway
Grade 12 is defined holistically ("excellent performance ... high level of command of all aspects of the relevant material, with no or only a few minor weaknesses"); DTU adds only that the thesis "must include elements of literature studies and criticism" and that academic content dominates the grade. There is no official DTU rubric for the literature review itself, so a grade-12 review has to be inferred: it must show command of all relevant aspects of the field (no missing major method family, no uncritical acceptance), with weaknesses limited to minor ones.

### Cited Findings
- Official grade descriptors (Danish Ministry, 7-point scale): 12 = "For an excellent performance displaying a high level of command of all aspects of the relevant material, with no or only a few minor weaknesses"; 10 = "very good performance displaying a high level of command of most aspects of the relevant material, with only minor weaknesses"; 7 = "good performance displaying good command of the relevant material but also some weaknesses"; 4 = "fair performance displaying some command of the relevant material but also some major weaknesses"; 02 = "performance meeting only the minimum requirements for acceptance". — [Uddannelses- og Forskningsstyrelsen, Grading system](https://ufsn.dk/english/education/the-danish-education-system/grading-system/)
- DTU master's thesis: external examination based on the report and an oral defence, 7-point scale; external examiner (censor) from the Civil Engineering Corps of External Examiners takes part in assessing the report. Assessment emphasises "academic content", while "formulation and spelling ability" receive "a small but nonetheless significant weight". — [DTU student rules: Master's thesis](https://student.dtu.dk/en/rules/afsluttende-projekter/kandidatspeciale)
- DTU rules: theses "must include elements of literature studies and criticism"; no quantity or citation format is specified; report format is agreed with the main supervisor. — [DTU student rules: Master's thesis](https://student.dtu.dk/en/rules/afsluttende-projekter/kandidatspeciale)
- DTU graduate competence profile listed on that page includes: identifying technical issues, applying current research, mastering methodologies and theories, communicating research-based knowledge in writing, and seeking out international research. — [DTU student rules: Master's thesis](https://student.dtu.dk/en/rules/afsluttende-projekter/kandidatspeciale)
- The thesis report must include a description of how the project meets the MSc thesis learning objectives in the programme specification. — [DTU Study Guidance, Master's thesis info meeting F25](https://student.dtu.dk/-/media/websites/student/hjaelp-og-vejledning/studievejledningen/arrangementer/masters-thesis-presentation-f25.pdf) (as summarised by search tool; slide text not fully extracted)
- DTU guidance asks the student to demonstrate "state of the art" understanding and situate the work within current scientific understanding (no volume requirement stated). — [DTU Study Guidance F25 slides](https://student.dtu.dk/-/media/websites/student/hjaelp-og-vejledning/studievejledningen/arrangementer/masters-thesis-presentation-f25.pdf)
- Biomedical Engineering / Medicine and Technology (joint KU/DTU programme lineage) thesis objectives, per the KU course description: a student who has fully achieved the goals can "plan, perform and document a structured and focused literature search and retrieve relevant literature", "employ theories and interpretations relevant to the field", "process a well-delimited and clear problem", use correct "practices with regard to quotation and statement of references", and "critically assess own results". — [KU SMTK12041E Master's Thesis, Medicine and Technology](https://kurser.ku.dk/course/smtk12041e/2014-2015) (2014-15 version, retrieved via search snippet; current page returned "not open year")
- Danish university library guidance (SDU) on a "state of the art": it should identify the theoretical foundation, define the relevance of the question, clarify focus/problems/hypotheses, justify the importance of the chosen problem, and ideally "highlight that there is a missing piece in the exact area where your problem formulation is proposing to investigate"; it should show awareness of "(recent) relevant research". — [SDU Library, State of the art](https://mitsdu.dk/da/service/bibliotek/vejledninger/opgaveskrivning/state-of-the-art?sc_lang=en)

### Inferences
- Mapping the descriptors to a lit review: "all aspects" vs "most aspects" is the 12/10 boundary. A review that covers the main methods well but omits a relevant method family, a key dataset/benchmark, or the evaluation-metric debate reads as 10 at best. "Some weaknesses" (7) typically = summary without critique, or a gap claim not convincingly supported.
- For BME specifically, "documented, structured and focused literature search" is an explicit learning objective, so stating search strategy and inclusion/exclusion is not optional decoration; it is directly checkable against programme goals (this matches Boote & Beile criterion A, the criterion dissertations score worst on; see Q2).
- Because DTU grading is holistic over report + defence, a short or imperfect literature review does not by itself cap the grade; but the censor assesses "command of the relevant material", which is evidenced mainly in the review and discussion chapters.

### Gaps
- No official DTU grading rubric or per-section weighting for the master's thesis was found publicly; DTU's PDF "Rules for MSc thesis projects at DTU" (hosted by NTNU) returned 404.
- The current DTU MSc Biomedical Engineering programme specification thesis objectives were not fetched directly (DTU pages not retrieved); the KU-hosted 2014-15 text may be outdated.
- No public examples of grade-12 DTU theses with their grade could be verified (DTU Findit/Orbit do not publish grades).

## Q2. Hallmarks of an excellent vs adequate review (Boote & Beile, Hart, Randolph, Webster & Watson, writing centres)

### Takeaway
The authorities converge: excellent reviews synthesise around concepts (not author-by-author), critically appraise methods and evidence, justify scope/inclusion, situate the problem historically and in the broader field, resolve terminology, and end in a clearly argued gap that motivates the thesis's research questions. Boote & Beile's 12-criterion rubric is the most operational instrument and the empirically weakest criteria are justified inclusion/exclusion, synthesis/new perspective, method critique, and scholarly significance.

### Cited Findings
- Boote & Beile (2005), "Scholars Before Researchers", Educational Researcher 34(6): 12-item rubric in 5 categories, adapted from Hart (1999, p. 27). Verbatim levels (1 / 2 / 3, criterion H also 4): — [Boote & Beile 2005 PDF](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)
  - Coverage. A. Justified criteria for inclusion and exclusion from review: did not discuss criteria / discussed the literature included and excluded / justified inclusion and exclusion of literature.
  - Synthesis. B. Distinguished what has been done in the field from what needs to be done: did not distinguish / discussed what has and has not been done / critically examined the state of the field.
  - C. Placed the topic or problem in the broader scholarly literature: not placed / some discussion / clearly situated.
  - D. Placed the research in the historical context of the field: history not discussed / some mention / critically examined history of topic.
  - E. Acquired and enhanced the subject vocabulary: key vocabulary not discussed / defined / discussed and resolved ambiguities in definitions.
  - F. Articulated important variables and phenomena: not discussed / reviewed relationships among key variables / noted ambiguities in literature and proposed new relationships.
  - G. Synthesized and gained a new perspective on the literature: accepted literature at face value / some critique / offered new perspective.
  - Methodology. H. Identified the main methodologies and research techniques used in the field, and their advantages and disadvantages: not discussed / some discussion of methods used to produce claims / critiqued research methods / (4) introduced new methods to address problems with predominant methods.
  - I. Related ideas and theories in the field to research methodologies: not discussed / some discussion of appropriateness of methods to warrant claims / critiqued appropriateness of research methods to warrant claims.
  - Significance. J. Rationalized the practical significance of the research problem: not discussed / discussed / critiqued practical significance.
  - K. Rationalized the scholarly significance of the research problem: not discussed / discussed / critiqued scholarly significance.
  - Rhetoric. L. Written with a coherent, clear structure that supported the review: poorly conceptualised, haphazard / some coherent structure / well developed, coherent.
- Boote & Beile on coverage: novices (students) "do not have the luxury of being assumed to know the literature", so the onus is on them to show they "purposefully decided what to review"; for a large literature the student may "focus on the best available evidence or on a smaller number of key conceptual pieces", but must "convince the audience that inclusion has been purposeful and thorough". — [Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)
- Boote & Beile on methodology: "At minimum, an author should recognize how previous researchers' methodological choices affected the research findings"; to score well candidates "could justify their own methodological choices and perhaps even suggest and justify new research methods". — [Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)
- Boote & Beile on rhetoric: after synthesis the author makes claims about the literature, which "should be articulated clearly, and the writing should be organized to support them"; Cooper (1985) found rhetorical structure was a key determinant of how persuasive readers judged a review. — [Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)
- Boote & Beile on integration: a thorough understanding of literature "should be ... evident in the entire dissertation", influencing choice of topic, methods and discussion; isolating it in one chapter may reflect shallow understanding. — [Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)
- Webster & Watson (2002, MIS Quarterly 26(2)): reviews should be concept-centric (concepts define the organising framework), not author-centric (summarising articles one by one fails to synthesise); the concept matrix (articles x concepts) is the recommended tool. — [Durham EBSE bibliography entry](https://ebse.webspace.durham.ac.uk/ebse-bibliography/analysing-the-past-to-prepare-for-the-future-writing-a-literature-review/); [UNR Writing Center, concept matrix](https://www.unr.edu/writing-speaking-center/writing-speaking-resources/concept-matrix)
- UNC Writing Center: summary recaps, synthesis "reorganizes" information to offer new interpretation; organisation can be chronological (only if order shows a meaningful trend), thematic, methodological, or by trend; in sciences, current sources matter because the field changes; writers should keep their own voice by starting and ending paragraphs with their own ideas; conclusion should state what has been drawn from the literature and "where might the discussion proceed". — [UNC Writing Center, Literature Reviews](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/literature-reviews/)
- University of Kent: synthesise ideas into "a summary of what is known" while evaluating them; a thematic approach groups ideas and "bounc[es] the ideas off each other"; introduction should outline selection strategy and focus; critically assess argument, evidence, methodology, gaps, strengths and limitations. — [Kent, Writing a literature review](https://student.kent.ac.uk/studies/literature-reviews/writing-a-literature-review)
- Computer-science thesis convention (Kessler, Linköping): distinguish background literature (textbooks, surveys, techniques used) from related work (studies addressing the same or similar questions, your "competitors"); relate your own evaluation to related work and "explain the similarities and differences, strengths and weaknesses". — [Kessler, Stylistic advice for writing a thesis (LiU)](https://www.ida.liu.se/~chrke55/exjobb/writing.shtml)

### Inferences
- Checkable distinction between grade bands, using Boote & Beile levels: level 2 everywhere ("discussed", "some critique") resembles an adequate review (roughly 7); level 3 on most criteria including G, H, I, K resembles excellent (12). The paper's language "critically examined", "resolved ambiguities", "offered new perspective" are the hallmarks of the top band.
- For medical image analysis, criterion H/I translates concretely to: critiquing evaluation protocols (datasets, splits, metrics such as Dice vs topology/centerline metrics, external validation), not only reporting reported numbers; and criterion E to resolving terminology (e.g., inconsistent definitions of segment labels, "topology", or metric names across papers).
- Criterion A for a master's thesis need not be a PRISMA-style search, but should state sources/databases, time window and what was deliberately excluded and why (BME learning objective explicitly asks for a documented search).

### Gaps
- Randolph (2009, PARE 14(13)) full text was blocked (Anubis access denied); its common-mistakes list and Cooper's taxonomy are therefore not quoted here. From background knowledge only (unverified in this session): Randolph reproduces the Boote & Beile rubric and lists mistakes such as not clearly relating findings to prior research, not taking a critical stance, and not considering contrary findings.
- Hart (1998/2018) "Doing a Literature Review" was not accessed directly; its influence is visible only via Boote & Beile's adaptation.

## Q3. Typical length and number of references for a ~30 ECTS engineering master's thesis review

### Takeaway
There is no Danish/DTU norm. Guidance ranges widely: UK writing centres say about 2,000-3,000 words for a master's dissertation review; a Danish university library (SDU) says "anything from half a page to ten" pages can be realistic; a CS thesis supervisor sets a floor of 3-4 pages and at least 5 peer-reviewed related-work papers. Reference counts for STEM master's theses are commonly cited at 30-100 total, but those figures come from low-quality aggregator sites.

### Cited Findings
- Master's dissertation literature review "approximately 2,000-3,000 words"; PhD chapter 8,000-10,000; undergraduate up to 2,000. — [University of Kent](https://student.kent.ac.uk/studies/literature-reviews/writing-a-literature-review)
- SDU (Danish): "Expectations for section length also vary greatly, anything from half a page to ten can be realistic"; longer sections require more systematic documentation of search method, databases and terms. — [SDU Library, State of the art](https://mitsdu.dk/da/service/bibliotek/vejledninger/opgaveskrivning/state-of-the-art?sc_lang=en)
- Related work chapter "should be at least 3-4 pages long"; cite and compare to "at least 5 (at least 10 for two-student theses) related-work scientific papers from peer-reviewed conferences or journals", in addition to background literature. — [Kessler, LiU](https://www.ida.liu.se/~chrke55/exjobb/writing.shtml)
- Whole CS master's thesis length guidelines elsewhere: about 50 pages excluding references (Mannheim); 60-100 pages (Antwerp). — [Mannheim guidelines](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/media/Einrichtungen/dws/Files_Research/Guidelines_for_Bachelor-Master_Thesis.pdf); [Antwerp guidelines](https://esp.uantwerpen.be/get-document/GuidelinesMasterThesisComputerScienceJune2021FINAL.pdf) (as summarised by search tool)
- Lower-quality sources (commercial blogs, treat with caution): 15,000-word master's thesis -> about 3,000-word review with 20-30 sources; STEM theses 30-100 references; "one source per 100-150 words of review". — [litrevu.com](https://litrevu.com/blog/how-long-should-a-literature-review-be); [referencechecker.org](https://referencechecker.org/blog/how-many-references-research-paper)

### Inferences
- A rough share heuristic: if the review is 15-25 percent of a 50-80 page thesis (common in guides, though not sourced authoritatively here), that is about 8-20 pages, i.e. roughly 3,000-7,000 words. 1,300 words (about 3 pages) sits at the low end: above SDU's half-page floor, below Kent's 2,000-3,000 and at Kessler's 3-4 page minimum.
- Density matters more than count: at 1 citation per 100-150 words (aggregator heuristic), a 1,300-word review would carry about 9-13 distinct citations; the full thesis reference list would normally be much larger because background, methods and discussion also cite.

### Gaps
- No DTU-, Danish-, or BME/medical-imaging-specific statistic on review length or reference counts was found. No bibliometric study of master's thesis reference counts in engineering was located.

## Q4. Common weaknesses examiners flag

### Takeaway
The recurring weaknesses: summary instead of synthesis (author-by-author listing), accepting findings at face value, no justification of scope/inclusion, no method critique, unclear or unsupported gap, weak link to research questions, haphazard structure, over-quotation, and sweeping generalisations.

### Cited Findings
- In Boote & Beile's sample, mean item scores ranged from 1.08 (SD .29) on criterion A (justified inclusion/exclusion) to 2.33 on historical context, vocabulary, and variables; low scores also on "Synthesized and gained a new perspective" (M = 1.42), "Identified the main methodologies ... and their advantages and disadvantages" (M = 1.92), and "Rationalized the scholarly significance" (M = 1.92). — [Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)
- "The worst literature reviews we analyzed were mere disjointed summaries of a haphazard collection of literature"; "the common failure to synthesize literature, critique methodology, or explain scholarly significance". The best were "thorough, critical examinations of the state of the field". — [Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)
- Author-centric reviews that summarise articles fail to synthesise; concept matrices guard against a "patchwork quilt" of references or cherry-picking. — [UNR Writing Center](https://www.unr.edu/writing-speaking-center/writing-speaking-resources/concept-matrix)
- Kent lists: unsupported sweeping generalisations, over-reliance on direct quotations, failing to distinguish central from secondary points, insufficient revision of structure and coherence. — [University of Kent](https://student.kent.ac.uk/studies/literature-reviews/writing-a-literature-review)
- UNC lists: excessive quotation, listing sources rather than organising by ideas, inaccurate paraphrase, unexplained jargon. — [UNC Writing Center](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/literature-reviews/)

### Inferences
- For a technical master's thesis, add field-specific flags that follow from criteria H/I: comparing reported scores across papers with different datasets/metrics as if commensurable; citing preprints/abstracts for strong claims; missing the most recent 1-2 years in a fast-moving deep-learning area (UNC's recency point applied to sciences).

### Gaps
- No published Danish censor reports or DTU-specific lists of common thesis weaknesses were found.

## Q5. Is a short (~1,300 words, continuous text, one table) review acceptable alongside a separate clinical background chapter?

### Takeaway
Yes in principle: guides explicitly allow short state-of-the-art sections and split background from related work, provided the review does its job (theoretical foundation, critical comparison, a well-supported gap that motivates the problem formulation, documented scope). Shortness raises the bar on density: every paragraph must synthesise and appraise, and scope exclusions must be stated so omissions read as deliberate, not as ignorance.

### Cited Findings
- SDU: "anything from half a page to ten can be realistic"; placement may be in the introduction leading to the problem formulation, or a separate chapter; it should not be confused with a systematic review but should be "somewhat systematic". — [SDU Library](https://mitsdu.dk/da/service/bibliotek/vejledninger/opgaveskrivning/state-of-the-art?sc_lang=en)
- Background (textbooks, surveys, techniques) is distinct from related work (competing studies on the same question); background should be cited but is not related work. — [Kessler, LiU](https://www.ida.liu.se/~chrke55/exjobb/writing.shtml)
- A background chapter serves to "acquaint the non-specialized reader with the necessary information to understand the thesis argument", distinct from the literature review's role of establishing novelty and refining research questions. — [HKU, Background chapters (Hout)](https://www.fe.hku.hk/clear/doc/Background%20chapters%20Hout.pdf) (via search summary)
- Boote & Beile: the review need not be isolated in one chapter; literature understanding should be evident throughout (introduction to conclusion). — [Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)
- Kent master's norm 2,000-3,000 words; Kessler minimum 3-4 pages. — [Kent](https://student.kent.ac.uk/studies/literature-reviews/writing-a-literature-review); [Kessler](https://www.ida.liu.se/~chrke55/exjobb/writing.shtml)

### Inferences
- Risks specific to a single continuous ~1,300-word text: (a) no visible organising structure (criterion L) unless paragraphs are clearly concept-led with signposting; (b) a single table can serve as a Webster & Watson concept matrix, which strengthens synthesis if it compares methods on the axes the thesis cares about (data, metric, topology handling, limitations), but weakens it if it only lists papers; (c) brevity tends to drop history (D) and method critique (H/I), the latter being where grade 12 is won.
- Whether it is "enough" depends on how much state-of-the-art material lives in the clinical background, methods and discussion chapters; an examiner judges command of the field across the thesis (Boote & Beile; DTU's holistic grading).

### Gaps
- No source specifically addresses minimum review length when a separate clinical background chapter exists; the conclusion above is an inference from the general guidance.

## Appendix-style scoring rubric (for scoring a specific chapter)
(Kept inside the last section per format constraints; derived from Boote & Beile 2005 Table 1, DTU/Danish descriptors, BME learning objectives, Webster & Watson, UNC/Kent/SDU/Kessler as cited above.)

Score each item 1 (weak) / 2 (adequate) / 3 (excellent). Rough mapping: mostly 3s with at most one or two 2s and no 1s -> 12; mostly 3s, a few 2s -> 10; mostly 2s -> 7; several 1s -> 4 or below. (Mapping is an inference, not an official DTU conversion.)

1. Scope and inclusion justified (B&B A; BME objective "documented literature search"; SDU "somewhat systematic"): states what is covered, sources/time window, and what is excluded and why.
2. State of field vs what remains (B&B B): explicitly separates solved from unsolved, with critical judgement.
3. Situated in broader literature (B&B C): links the specific problem to adjacent fields/approaches.
4. Historical development (B&B D): traces how methods evolved and why, critically rather than as a list.
5. Terminology (B&B E): key terms defined and inconsistencies across papers resolved.
6. Key variables/phenomena (B&B F): identifies what drives outcomes and notes ambiguities or proposes relationships.
7. Synthesis and own perspective (B&B G; Webster & Watson concept-centric; UNC synthesis): organised by concepts, not author-by-author; offers an original framing.
8. Methods critique (B&B H): main method families and their pros/cons; for medical imaging, datasets, splits, metrics, validation.
9. Methods vs claims (B&B I): whether evidence (metrics, cohorts, comparisons) actually warrants the claims made in the literature.
10. Practical significance (B&B J): clinical relevance argued and critiqued.
11. Scholarly significance and gap (B&B K; SDU "missing piece"): the gap is specific, supported by the reviewed evidence, and leads directly to the thesis's research questions.
12. Structure and rhetoric (B&B L; Kent/UNC): coherent argument, signposting, author's voice opens and closes paragraphs, no over-quotation, no sweeping generalisations.
13. Currency (UNC; SDU "(recent)"): includes recent work relevant to a fast-moving field.
14. Related work vs background separation (Kessler): competitors are compared to the thesis's own approach (similarities, differences, strengths, weaknesses), not merely described.
15. Citation practice (BME objective "correct use of practices with regard to quotation and statement of references"; DTU small weight on formulation): accurate attribution, claims traceable to sources.
