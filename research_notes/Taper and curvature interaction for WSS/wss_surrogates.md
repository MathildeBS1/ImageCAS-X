# Geometry-only WSS surrogates for coronary centerline + radius profiles (reduced-order, scaling-law, Dean-number and ML approaches)

Scope note: everything below is about coronary arteries and inputs that a CCTA centerline with a radius per step can supply (centerline coordinates, curvature, radius/area along arc length, branch diameters). Where a formula is a standard textbook derivation rather than a quoted result, it is placed under Inferences and labelled as such.

## 1. Poiseuille / Murray / Huo–Kassab WSS estimates from radius and flow (taper and side-branch flow loss)

### Takeaway
The simplest geometry-only WSS estimate is Poiseuille, tau = 4·mu·Q/(pi·r^3), with Q apportioned from diameters by a power law Q ∝ d^k. In coronaries the empirically supported exponents are k ≈ 2.33 (Huo–Kassab, 7/3) and k ≈ 2.55 (van der Giessen, Q = 1.43·d^2.55), not Murray's 3. Murray's k = 3 implies uniform WSS by construction, so any "WSS proxy" built from a scaling law is really a measure of how far the local radius departs from the radius the flow "expects". Poiseuille estimates agree only moderately with CFD in straight segments and fail in complex geometry.

### Cited Findings
- Poiseuille WSS for steady flow in a rigid cylinder: WSS = 4·mu·Q/(pi·r^3) (mu viscosity, Q flow, r radius), so at fixed flow WSS scales as 1/r^3 and small radius errors have large effects. Used across 20 systemic arteries (mice to cows) to plot log WSS vs log lumen radius — [Interspecific scaling of blood flow rates and arterial sizes in mammals, J Exp Biol 2019](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/222/7/jeb199554/20651/Interspecific-scaling-of-blood-flow-rates-and)
- Direct test of Poiseuille vs CFD (carotid, not coronary): Poiseuille estimates were higher than CFD in the common carotid with only moderate agreement, and in the bulb there was no significant correlation between CFD and Poiseuille WSS; the authors concluded Poiseuille assumptions are hard to apply in complex geometry — [Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol, carotid WSS/IMT study](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpheart.00989.2006)
- Huo–Kassab (HK) bifurcation law: Dm^(7/3) = Dl^(7/3) + Ds^(7/3), i.e., flow–diameter exponent 7/3 ≈ 2.33, derived from conservation of mass and a minimum-energy hypothesis (same basis as Murray) — [EuroIntervention review: Anatomy and function relation in the coronary tree (DOI 10.4244/EIJV11SVA3)](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomy-and-function-relation-in-the-coronary-tree-from-bifurcations-to-myocardial-flow-and-mass)
- Murray's exponent 3 implies constant WSS throughout the tree, which experiments do not support; HK's 7/3 is offered as the alternative — [same EuroIntervention review](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomy-and-function-relation-in-the-coronary-tree-from-bifurcations-to-myocardial-flow-and-mass)
- Kassab & Fung (1995) showed Murray's law follows from a uniform wall shear-strain-rate hypothesis — [Kassab 1995 PDF](https://people.iith.ac.in/suhailr/bm5160/Kassab1995.pdf); summarized in [EuroIntervention review](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/doi/10.4244/EIJV11SVA3)
- A generalized Murray law reduces to HK when its parameters are alpha = 8/3, beta = 1/3; alpha = 8/3 is linked to Womersley (pulsatile) flow, argued to be the regime in coronaries — [J Appl Fluid Mech, general scaling law under pulsatile flow](https://www.jafmonline.net/article_2490.html)
- Finite-element study (Huo, Finet, Lefevre, Louvard, Moussa, Kassab 2012): Murray-type Y-bifurcations had 10–40% lower WSS and higher OSI in atherosclerosis-prone regions than HK-type bifurcations — [Which diameter and angle rule provides optimal flow patterns in a coronary bifurcation?](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/470d0c8c-aec4-4946-807e-57d66b6b6eb5)
- Huo & Kassab, "Scaling laws of coronary circulation in health and disease", J Biomech 2016 (primary source for HK scaling in disease; abstract only seen) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021929016301270)
- Empirical coronary flow–diameter law Q = 1.43·d^2.55 (Q cycle-averaged flow in mL/s, d mean diameter in mm), van der Giessen et al., J Biomech 2011;44:1089–95, derived in mildly diseased patients; widely used to set inlet flow and outlet splits — [Frontiers Physiol 2022 citing it](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.871912/full); [High-intensity helical flow paper (PMC12324882) eq. 2.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12324882/)
- Exponent comparison in a CTA-based CFD study: 7/3 differs from 2.55 by 8% vs 18% for Murray's 3; reported coronary exponents range 2–3 — [J Biomech 2023, flow distribution strategy in CTA-based CFD](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021929023004281)
- The diameter-scaled inflow + flow-split strategy (Doriot's fit, Murray-based) gave R^2 = 0.87 for inlet flow vs diameter and R^2 = 0.70 for the daughter flow-split ratio against in vivo measurements (van der Giessen 2011) — [Shen et al. 2025 review, arXiv 2507.17109, Sec. 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109)
- Scaled (diameter-based) boundary conditions have been compared with measured-flow BCs for WSS in atherosclerotic human coronary bifurcations (Am J Physiol 2016) — [The impact of scaled boundary conditions on WSS computations](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpheart.00896.2015) (full text was not accessible; numbers not verified)

### Inferences
- (Standard derivation, not a quoted result.) For a segment with local radius r(s) and flow Q(s): tau_P(s) = 4·mu·Q(s)/(pi·r(s)^3). Between branches Q is constant, so taper alone raises WSS distally as (r_prox/r(s))^3; a 20% radius reduction along a branch-free stretch raises Poiseuille WSS by ~95%.
- (Derivation.) If Q(s) is apportioned at each side branch by Q_i ∝ d_i^k, then downstream of a branch Q_down = Q_up · d_down^k/(d_down^k + d_side^k). With k = 3, WSS is identical in parent and daughters at matched radii (Murray ⇒ uniform WSS). With k = 7/3, tau ∝ Q/d^3 ∝ d^(−2/3): WSS rises slowly toward smaller vessels.
- This yields a physically motivated "flow-matched taper deviation" proxy computable from the tracer output: define the expected radius r_exp(s) from the inlet radius and the cumulative side-branch flow loss (via d^k), then tau_ratio(s) = (r_exp(s)/r(s))^3 (or tau_P(s)/tau_P,ref). Values < 1 mean the lumen is wider than its flow demands ⇒ predicted low WSS. This combines taper and side-branch loss in one term instead of a raw taper slope. Note: it needs side-branch diameters, which a trunk-only centerline does not supply; without them, taper is confounded with physiological flow loss.
- Because the 1/r^3 dependence amplifies radius error, the proxy should be computed on smoothed radius (and in log form, log tau = log Q − 3 log r + const) to keep it additive in a regression.

### Gaps
- No coronary study was found that directly reports r or R^2 between a Poiseuille/scaling-law WSS estimate and 3D-CFD TAWSS along CCTA-derived coronary segments; the only direct comparison found was carotid.
- The original van der Giessen 2011 paper and the 2016 scaled-BC paper full texts were not accessed, so their WSS agreement statistics are not reported here.
- No published "flow-matched taper" index by that name was found; the formula above is a derivation.

## 2. Curvature corrections: Dean number, inner-wall WSS, pulsatile (Womersley) effects

### Takeaway
Curvature and flow combine into one parameter, the Dean number (Re·sqrt(r·kappa), i.e., Re·sqrt(curvature ratio)). Classical Dean theory gives a series expansion in which O(De^2) corrections push peak axial velocity to the outer wall, so WSS is lowest on the inner wall at moderate/high De — but there is no simple closed-form inner-wall WSS reduction formula, and the inner-wall solution is unresolved at high De. In vivo RCA Dean numbers are roughly 20–90. Coronary studies show contradictory net effects of curvature on segment-averaged WSS because curvature also generates helical flow that raises WSS.

### Cited Findings
- Dean equations for fully developed flow in a slightly curved pipe: the Reynolds number and curvature combine into a single parameter, the Dean number K. For K = 0 the solution is Poiseuille, v0 = (1/4)(1 − R^2). At O(K) a secondary cross-flow arises, psi1 = A·R(1 − R^2)^2(4 − R^2)·sin(theta) (two counter-rotating Dean vortices), which drives an O(K^2) correction to the axial flow, v = v0 + K^2·v2 with v2 = B·cos(theta)·R(1 − R^2)(19 − 21R^2 + 9R^4 − R^6); this correction moves the velocity maximum toward the outside of the bend. Full series: v = Σ K^(2n) v_n, psi = Σ K^(2n+1) psi_n (Van Dyke computed 24 terms; Tettamanti 60) — [Imperial College BioFluids lecture 16 (A. J. Mestel), Steady flows for various Dean numbers](https://www.ma.ic.ac.uk/~ajm8/BioFluids/lec1117.pdf)
- At moderate Dean numbers (numerical solutions; Collins & Dennis 1975, Dennis & Ng 1982, Siggers & Waters 2005; review Berger et al. 1983) "the wall shear stress is minimum on the inside of the bend" — [same lecture](https://www.ma.ic.ac.uk/~ajm8/BioFluids/lec1117.pdf)
- High-Dean-number asymptotics: inviscid core with a boundary layer of thickness delta ~ K^(−1/3), axial velocity scale V0 ~ K^(−1/3), streamfunction scale Psi0 ~ K^(−2/3); the boundary layer starts at the outer bend and grows toward the inner bend, and "no consistent picture at the inner wall has been found" — [same lecture](https://www.ma.ic.ac.uk/~ajm8/BioFluids/lec1117.pdf)
- Double expansion in Dean number and curvature (Topakoglu 1967; Siggers & Waters 2005) showed even small curvature (beyond the Dean limit) noticeably changes the flow — [same lecture](https://www.ma.ic.ac.uk/~ajm8/BioFluids/lec1117.pdf)
- Dean's (1927/28) small-De expansion predicts a pair of vortices and is invalid when the Dean number exceeds 576; in a weakly curved pipe WSS is dominated by its axial component — [Gammack & Hydon, J Fluid Mech 2001;433:357–382](https://www.kent.ac.uk/smsas/personal/ph282/papers/gamh01.pdf)
- In a low-Dean / microcirculation regime the opposite trend was found: curvature lowers tube resistance but increases shear near the inside wall (geometric O(delta) effect dominates when De is very small) — [PMC2867477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2867477) (as summarized by search; not fetched in full)
- In vivo human RCA (angiography + IVUS + CFD): curvature 25 ± 9 m^-1, Dean numbers 20 ± 8, 46 ± 16 and 93 ± 33 at three flow levels; inner-curve shear stress 17 ± 17, 38 ± 44 and 77 ± 54 dyne/cm^2, lower than at the outer curve, with an inverse relation between wall thickness and shear stress — [PMC1570452](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1570452) (attribution taken from search summary; verify paper identity before citing)
- Secondary flow in coronaries is a pair of counter-rotating vortices arising in curved segments and after bifurcations; there is no established method to quantify secondary flow in coronaries — [Shen et al. 2025 review, arXiv 2507.17109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109)
- Pulsatility: a generalized Murray law with alpha = 8/3 corresponds to Womersley flow and reduces to HK — [J Appl Fluid Mech](https://www.jafmonline.net/article_2490.html); unsteady toroidal-pipe flow characterisation — [arXiv 2312.00146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.00146)
- Curvature radius (idealized coronary bifurcations): smaller curvature radius gives higher helicity intensity; in stenosed cases smaller curvature radius gave higher exposure to low shear and lower exposure to oscillatory shear — [Chiastra, Gallo, Morbiducci et al. 2017](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/2677968/158335/SSS_IdealizedCoronary_Chiastra_0119.pdf)
- Torsion dictates helical flow (r = 0.64, p < 0.001, 30 coronary models; descriptors were vessel-averaged curvature and torsion), and helical flow is proposed as atheroprotective via WSS — [De Nisco et al., Ann Biomed Eng 2019, "The atheroprotective nature of helical flow in coronary arteries"](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/2726967/235290/The%20Atheroprotective%20Nature%20of%20Helical%20Flow%20in%20Coronary%20Arteries.pdf)

### Inferences
- (Standard definition.) De = Re·sqrt(r/R_c) = Re·sqrt(kappa·r), with Re = 2·rho·U·r/mu = 2·rho·Q/(pi·mu·r). Substituting Q from a scaling law (Q ∝ d^k) makes De a pure function of local radius and curvature along the tracer output: De(s) ∝ r(s)^(k−1) · sqrt(kappa(s)·r(s)) — a single composite that couples taper (through r and Q) and curvature.
- A defensible combined low-WSS index from the theory above is a Poiseuille baseline modulated by a curvature term: e.g., log tau_inner(s) ≈ log tau_P(s) + f(De(s), kappa·r), where f is negative for the inner wall. No sourced closed form for f was found, so in practice f should be estimated (e.g., from a small CFD calibration set or treated as a regression term with De or kappa·r as the variable, rather than kappa alone).
- Since segment-averaged WSS can go up with curvature (helical flow, see Section 3) while inner-wall WSS goes down, the choice of a segment-level summary (mean vs. low-WSS area fraction vs. inner-wall minimum) changes the sign one should expect for curvature.

### Gaps
- No explicit closed-form inner/outer WSS ratio as a function of De and kappa·r was found in accessible sources (Berger, Talbot & Yao 1983, Annu Rev Fluid Mech, is the likely source but was not accessed).
- No Womersley-corrected WSS formula for curved coronary segments was retrieved.
- The in vivo RCA Dean-number study could not be fully verified (journal/authors).

## 3. Composite geometric indices designed to approximate (low) WSS, and how well they correlate with CFD

### Takeaway
Among simple centerline indices, mean absolute curvature correlates best with low TAWSS while the classical tortuosity index (L/D) does not (p = 0.86). At the LM bifurcation, the diameter-ratio index Finet's ratio (D_parent / (D_daughter1 + D_daughter2)) alone explained R^2 = 0.71 of the variance in low-TAESS area in 127 CCTA patients; curvature and angles were not significant univariately. Diameter and curvature interact, and combined models fit better than single factors.

### Cited Findings
- Kashyap et al. (Sci Rep 2022;12:865), 127 CCTA patients without CAD, left main bifurcation/LAD/LCx: curvature-based tortuosity measures correlated more strongly with the fraction of area with TAWSS < 0.4 Pa than non-curvature measures; average absolute curvature had the highest coefficient of determination across all LM branches; the tortuosity index showed no significant correlation (p = 0.86); measures compared: tortuosity index, absolute curvature, squared curvature, square-derivative curvature — [PMC8764056](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8764056/); [Shen et al. 2025 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109)
- Gharleghi et al. 2024 (127 LM bifurcations from CCTA, no significant stenosis; transient CFD, uniform inlet velocity, 0 Pa outlets): low-TAESS (< 0.5 Pa) area vs Finet's ratio: standardised beta = −0.842, p < 0.001, R^2 = 0.709. Other univariate R^2: LM diameter 0.004, LAD diameter 0.026, LCx diameter 0.030, LM curvature 0.009, LAD curvature 0.000, LCx curvature 0.013; bifurcation angles non-significant. Backward multiple regression (AIC) gave R^2 = 0.791. Results insensitive to the 0.4/0.6 Pa thresholds (Finet beta −0.773, R^2 0.598 at 0.4 Pa; −0.839, R^2 0.704 at 0.6 Pa) — [arXiv 2401.12504](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12504v1)
- In the same study, diameters and curvature were associated with high OSI, and Finet's ratio, diameters and daughter curvature were independently associated with high RRT — [arXiv 2401.12504](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12504)
- Review statement: an interdependent effect between diameter and curvature on haemodynamics was reported (Huo et al.), and the combined influence aligns more closely with haemodynamics than each factor individually (Gharleghi 2024) — [Shen et al. 2025, arXiv 2507.17109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109)
- Zhang, Gharleghi, Shen, Beier (R Soc Open Sci 2024; 39 CCTA left coronary trees): obstructive disease correlated with average curvature and normalised area with high OSI; at whole-tree scale curvature showed more influence on haemodynamics — [Shen et al. 2025 review Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109); [arXiv 2312.00257](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.00257)
- Malvè et al. 2015 (8 patient models, steady CFD): significant correlation between LM–LAD tortuosity index and low ESS; bifurcation angle correlated with high ESS amplitude — [Shen et al. 2025 review Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109)
- Vorobtsova et al. 2016 (Ann Biomed Eng; idealized + patient-specific): more tortuous vessels had higher WSS, with strong correlation between tortuosity and helicity intensity; helical flow directly contributed to the WSS increase; perfusion pressure fell with tortuosity — [Erasmus repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/91183); [Polito post-print](https://iris.polito.it/bitstream/11583/2770474/3/2016%20Vorobtsova%20Chiastra%20-%20Effects%20of%20vessel%20tortuosity%20on%20coronary%20hemodynamics%20POST-PRINT.pdf)
- Conflicting evidence: other work found LMCA WSS decreased with tortuosity; inconsistent tortuosity metrics likely explain ambiguous literature — [Kashyap et al. 2022 Sci Rep](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-04796-w)
- Curvature definitions: curvature = |dT/ds| = 1/R of the osculating circle; tortuosity index compares path length L and chord D and carries no spatial information — [Shen et al. 2025 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109)
- Low WSS is an independent but moderate predictor of plaque development — [De Nisco et al. 2020 (Polito)](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2763292) (as summarized by search)

### Inferences
- Finet's ratio is effectively a "flow-matched" diameter index: with flow apportioned by diameter, a low ratio means the daughters are large relative to the inflow, so velocities and WSS fall. This maps directly onto the thesis's LM→LAD and LM→LCx diameter ratios — they are the published geometry-only quantity with the strongest CFD-WSS association found here (R^2 ≈ 0.7 for LM low-TAESS area), though in a CFD setup where inflow was not patient-specific.
- Mean absolute curvature (not tortuosity index) is the curvature summary with the best evidence; combining it with radius (kappa·r, or De) is theoretically motivated but has no published head-to-head correlation with CFD found here.

### Gaps
- Exact R^2 values per branch for Kashyap et al. 2022 could not be retrieved (PMC blocked).
- No study was found that tests an explicit "curvature × radius" or Dean-number index against CFD TAWSS in coronaries.
- No lumen-area-gradient index with reported CFD correlations was found.

## 4. 1D/0D reduced-order models and ML/deep-learning WSS surrogates trained on CFD

### Takeaway
0D/1D models resolve pressure and flow but not local WSS (1D assumes a fixed velocity profile and circular section, so its WSS is essentially Poiseuille/Womersley). ML surrogates trained on CFD reach ~2–10% normalised error versus 3D CFD in ~ms to seconds; when feature importance is reported, local radius and inflow dominate and curvature is secondary.

### Cited Findings
- 0D lumped models cannot resolve local haemodynamic tensors; 1D models assume a uniform profile, circular cross-section and uniform material behaviour — [Shen et al. 2025 review Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109)
- CNNs predicting WSS around coronary bifurcations from geometric features had < 5% deviation from CFD, with time reduced from ~2 h to < 2 min; accuracy depends on training data and generalisation to whole trees, stenosed or stented arteries remains a challenge — [Shen et al. 2025 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17109)
- Gharleghi et al. (ISBI 2020): deep learning estimate of TAWSS at the LM bifurcation from vessel geometry (radii, branching angles, curvature and other geometric features); NMAE 10.38% (SD 0.56%) as cited in later work — [IEEE ISBI 2020](https://ieeetv.ieee.org/ondemand/ieee-isbi-2020-virtual-conference-april-2020/2936/deep-learning-for-time-averaged-wall-shear-stress-prediction-in-left-main-coronary-bifurcations); [Sarkhosh et al. 2025, PMC11920710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11920710/)
- Chen, Gharleghi, Sowmya, Beier (ISBI 2022): variational-autoencoder latent shape vectors of LM bifurcations predicted OSI, RRT and TAWSS better on average than hand-crafted geometric features — [ISBI 2022](https://rc.signalprocessingsociety.org/conferences/isbi-2022/spsisbi22vid0168)
- Alamir et al. (Front Bioeng Biotechnol 2024): U-net on 50 patient vessels (+550 synthetic variants; patient-level split, 10 held out); inputs included lumen and centreline coordinates, polar wall coordinates (radius, angle), Menger curvature, distance along centreline, tangent and curvature vectors, inflow velocity. Test MAE 2.01 Pa, NMAE 6.03%; Bland-Altman LoA ≈ −3.59 to 3.37 Pa; ~35 ms vs 20 min–3 h for CFD. Integrated gradients: radius and inlet velocity most important; curvature's role reported inconsistently. Worst performance in a highly tortuous vessel with multiple stenoses; steady flow only — [Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2024.1360330/full)
- Su et al. 2020: MLR, MLP and CNN models generating WSS from 2,000 idealized coronary geometries; input described as a fixed number of centerline coordinates (effectively a point cloud) — [Frontiers Physiol 2025, 10.3389/fphys.2025.1518732](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1518732/full)
- Sarkhosh et al. 2025 (Front Physiol): U-net on point clouds of 1,800 idealized bifurcations for TAWSS; NMAE reported 2.53% across cases — [PMC11920710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11920710/)
- Suk et al. (SE(3)-equivariant mesh GCN, arXiv 2212.05023; Comput Biol Med 2024): on synthetic coronary data, approximation error 7.6% and NMAE 0.4%; handles transient WSS under varying inflow; much faster than CFD — [arXiv 2212.05023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.05023)
- Mesh CNNs for WSS on 3D artery models — [arXiv 2109.04797](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.04797)
- Gauge-equivariant mesh GCN on 1,078 coronary arteries from 748 patients (3D-QCA, not CCTA), CFD TAWSS reference: patient-specific WSS maps in < 5 s per vessel; correlation with CFD lesion-averaged WSS rose from R = 0.67 to R = 0.89 after normalisation; MI-predictive capacity comparable to CFD — [Comput Biol Med 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010482526001460)
- Physics-informed GNN on idealized concentric stenoses closely overlapped CFD along the centerline (peak magnitude and position) vs MLP/U-Net baselines — [Sci Rep 2026, PMC13212768](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13212768/)
- Reduced-order + ML in the FFR setting: Itu et al. trained ML on synthetic anatomies with a reduced-order CFD model; Fossan et al. 2021 combined a reduced-order model with neural-network pressure-loss corrections (FFR error SD 0.021) — [Applied Sciences/related, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/61e1844d5af34bffa5671801914d4558); [NTNU Open](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2781313)
- CFD WSS results depend heavily on the 3D lumen geometry input; consensus treats CFD as the standard route — [Gijsen et al. Eur Heart J 2019 expert recommendations](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/40/41/3421/5576147)

### Inferences
- The ML results support the physics: radius (and the flow assigned to it) dominates WSS, which is what the Poiseuille term captures; curvature adds a secondary, direction-dependent (inner vs outer wall) correction. A thesis-scale "physics-motivated" covariate is therefore log tau_P (radius + scaled flow) plus a curvature/Dean term, rather than separate taper and curvature terms.
- Learned surrogates are trained mostly on bifurcations, idealized geometries, or angiography-based models; none found was trained specifically on CCTA trunk centerlines with a per-step radius, so transferring them would need retraining.

### Gaps
- Itu et al. original paper (J Appl Physiol 2016) not retrieved; its features and accuracy are not reported here.
- No centerline-only (1D graph/sequence) WSS surrogate with reported accuracy on CCTA data was found.
- No study reported R/R^2 of a 1D/Poiseuille WSS against 3D CFD TAWSS in coronaries.
