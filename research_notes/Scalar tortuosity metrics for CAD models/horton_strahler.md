# Horton-Strahler Bifurcation Ratio as a Candidate CAD-Prediction Feature

## Is the Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio computable and meaningful for a CTA-scale coronary tree (as opposed to river/retinal/airway/full-anatomical vascular trees)?

### Takeaway
The Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio is well-defined mathematically for any bifurcating tree, but the number of orders it needs to be stable (roughly 5+ consecutive orders in the classical hydrology/vascular literature) is only present in the *full anatomical* coronary tree down to arterioles (obtained by corrosion casting), not in the shallow, few-generation tree that CTA segmentation and clinical naming conventions actually resolve. On a typical CTA-derived coronary tree the ratio would be computed from at most 2-4 orders of named branching, i.e. a ratio of very small integers with high sampling variance, which is a real limiting factor for this project.

### Cited Findings
- In the classic porcine coronary-cast morphometry (Kassab et al., diameter-defined Strahler system), bifurcation ratio, diameter ratio and length ratio were reported as constant across orders 2-8, with bifurcation ratio ≈ 3.30, diameter ratio ≈ 1.51, length ratio ≈ 1.63 — [Branching patterns in the porcine coronary arterial tree (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1394880/); [Relation between zero-stress state and branching order of porcine left coronary arterial tree (AJP-Heart)](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpheart.1998.275.6.H2283)
- In the same Kassab diameter-defined Strahler system, the largest (trunk) order for the coronary arterial tree of a 30 kg pig is order 11, with morphometric data grouped into orders roughly 4-11 for arterial segments — [Simulated annealing approach to vascular structure with application to the coronary arteries (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4785968/); [A Computer Reconstruction of the Entire Coronary Arterial Tree Based on Detailed Morphometric Data (Ann Biomed Eng)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10439-005-5758-z)
- This 11-order tree spans from the epicardial trunk down to precapillary arterioles/capillaries (order 1); it was built from resin corrosion casts with manual microscopic measurement of every segment, not from clinical CT — [Diameter-defined Strahler system and connectivity matrix of the pulmonary arterial tree (J Appl Physiol)](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/abs/10.1152/jappl.1994.76.2.882) (methodologically the same diameter-defined Strahler approach Kassab applied to the coronary tree)
- Diameter ratio between consecutive orders is ≈1.51 in the porcine coronary tree; propagating this down from a ~3-4 mm epicardial trunk (order ~11) implies vessel calibre falls below typical CTA spatial resolution (roughly 0.3-0.5 mm in-plane, matching this project's own 0.5 mm resampled volumes) within about 5-6 orders of the trunk — [Branching patterns in the porcine coronary arterial tree](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1394880/) (ratio value); resolution figure is this project's own pipeline parameter, not from a cited source
- The clinical/anatomical naming convention actually used to describe the coronary tree in CTA and angiography, the AHA 15-segment model (extended to 16/17 segments), divides the whole visible tree into only 15-17 named segments across the RCA, LM, LAD and LCx, e.g. LAD = proximal → mid → distal trunk plus first and second diagonal branches — [Segmental anatomy of the coronary arteries after a modified AHA classification (ResearchGate figure)](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Segmental-anatomy-of-the-coronary-arteries-after-a-modified-AHA-classification-1-RCA_fig1_7771780); [The coronary arterial anatomy of the 17-segment model using 3-Tesla CMR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378603X16301036)
- No source found in this search explicitly reports a Horton-Strahler order count or bifurcation ratio computed directly from a CTA-segmented (in vivo, non-cast) coronary tree; searches for CTA/deep-learning coronary segmentation papers reporting Strahler order or branch-order statistics returned only generic vessel-tracking/branch-detection methodology, not order-based topological metrics — [Automatic Coronary Artery Segmentation Using Active Search for Branches (PLOS ONE)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0156837); [Automated coronary artery tree segmentation using multiobjective clustering and toroidal model-guided tracking (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169260720317417)
- A paper proving a central limit theorem for the Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio of general branch order exists in the mathematical literature, confirming Rb has a well-defined asymptotic (large-tree) distribution, but the fetched abstract/metadata gave no concrete finite-tree convergence numbers (i.e., no statement of how many orders are needed before Rb variance becomes small) — [Central limit theorem for the Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio of general branch order (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03213)
- Zamir and Chee's classical human coronary morphometry measured branching angles and diameters at 850 arterial junctions in two human hearts and compared them to theoretical optimum (Murray's law / area-preserving) branching, finding human coronary branching close to optimal (within ~5% for most points) — [Branching characteristics of human coronary arteries (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3756615/); [Arterial branching in various parts of the cardiovascular system (Zamir, Am J Anat 1982)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aja.1001630403). Note: this Zamir line of work is about per-bifurcation diameter/angle optimality, not about counting segments across Strahler orders to form a bifurcation ratio.

### Inferences
- The bifurcation ratio Rb is only reported as a stable, roughly-constant value across a *range* of consecutive orders (orders 2-8, or 4-11) in studies built on full corrosion-cast anatomical trees that include hundreds to thousands of small vessels down to near-capillary calibre. This is the regime the classical hydrology/vascular literature (Horton, Strahler, Kassab) implicitly assumes when it calls Rb "constant" and thus meaningful.
- A CTA-derived coronary segmentation (and a fortiori the segment/branch-order structure implied by the AHA 15-17 segment naming scheme) captures only the largest few orders of that tree; a single named major vessel (e.g. one LAD) typically resolves to on the order of 2-4 generations of branching (trunk, one or two diagonal/septal generations) before falling below CT spatial resolution. That is too few orders to compute a bifurcation ratio as a ratio of segment *counts* between consecutive orders without it degenerating into a ratio of very small integers (e.g. 3 segments at order i versus 1 at order i+1), which will have high patient-to-patient and even scan-to-scan variance and limited discriminative value as a smooth scalar feature.
- This reasoning is inference from the Kassab/porcine order-depth data plus the AHA segment model, not from a study that directly measured Rb on CTA-segmented human coronary trees at ImageCAS-X's resolution; no such direct study was found.

### Gaps
- No study was found that computes the Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio (or any branch-order-count ratio) directly on CTA-segmented, non-cast, in vivo human coronary trees at the branch depth actually visible in datasets like ImageCAS-X. All quantitative Rb values found for coronary arteries come from ex vivo porcine corrosion casts (Kassab group), which resolve far more orders than any current CT segmentation pipeline can.
- No source directly states the typical number of Strahler orders present in a segmented human coronary CTA volume; the "~2-4 orders per named vessel" estimate above is this researcher's inference from combining the AHA segment model with Kassab's diameter-ratio scaling, not a cited measurement.
- The CLT paper for the Horton-Strahler ratio was only read at abstract/metadata level (WebFetch could not retrieve the full technical content); its precise finite-sample variance/convergence results as a function of tree size were not obtained.

## Is there published evidence linking the Horton-Strahler ratio (or closely related branching-topology metrics) to CAD, stenosis, plaque burden, or coronary outcomes, in coronary arteries specifically or in other vascular beds?

### Takeaway
Evidence for the Horton-Strahler ratio itself in coronary arteries and disease is essentially absent; all coronary-Strahler literature found is normal-anatomy morphometry (porcine casts) rather than disease-comparison studies. The closest and best-evidenced disease link is not Horton-Strahler at all but retinal vascular **fractal dimension** (a related but distinct branching-complexity metric) and coronary-angiographic fractal dimension, both of which are associated with coronary/cardiovascular disease and mortality.

### Cited Findings
- No paper was found reporting a Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio (or Strahler order count) compared between diseased and healthy human coronary arteries, or correlated with stenosis severity, plaque burden, or coronary outcomes. All coronary Strahler-ordering literature retrieved (Kassab group and related) is descriptive normal/porcine morphometry for hemodynamic modeling, not disease-association studies — [Branching patterns in the porcine coronary arterial tree](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1394880/); [Bifurcation asymmetry of the porcine coronary vasculature (AJP-Heart)](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpheart.00371.2004)
- Decreased retinal microvascular fractal dimension is significantly associated with cardiovascular disease risk, particularly in arterioles, and has been proposed as an adjunct CVD risk-stratification marker — [Retinal Vascular Fractal Dimensions and Their Association with Macrovascular Cardiac Disease (Ophthalmic Research, Karger)](https://karger.com/ore/article/64/4/561/820998/Retinal-Vascular-Fractal-Dimensions-and-Their)
- In a 14-year population study, participants with "suboptimal" retinal vascular fractal dimension (lowest and highest quartiles) had 50% higher coronary heart disease mortality than those with fractal dimension in the middle quartiles, after adjustment for age, blood pressure, and other risk factors — [Fractal analysis of retinal microvasculature and coronary heart disease mortality (Eur Heart J)](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/32/4/422/440005)
- Lower retinal main venular fractal dimension was associated with 7.2-8.2 times higher risk of prior CABG in the two lowest quartiles versus the top quartile — [A spectrum of retinal vasculature measures and coronary artery disease (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002191501731328X)
- Directly in coronary angiography (not retina), coronary ramification has been treated as a fractal object, and fractal-dimension variability/net-difference measures computed via box-counting on left coronary ramification angiograms could mathematically distinguish severe occlusive arterial disease from normal coronary trees — [Variability of the fractal dimension of the left coronary tree in patients with severe occlusive arterial disease (OSTI/ETDEWEB)](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/20809615)
- A related but conceptually distinct line of work applies a fractal/Murray's-law geometric relationship *at individual coronary bifurcations* (parent-to-daughter diameter exponent) via QCA/IVUS, and found this could "unmask" diffuse atherosclerotic left main disease — [Diffuse atherosclerotic left main coronary artery disease unmasked by fractal geometric law applied to QCA (EuroIntervention)](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/diffuse-atherosclerotic-left-main-coronary-artery-disease-unmasked-by-fractal-geometric-law-applied-to-quantitative-coronary-angiography-an-angiographic-and-intravascular-ultrasound-study); [Fractal geometry of arterial coronary bifurcations (EuroIntervention)](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/fractal-geometry-of-arterial-coronary-bifurcations-a-quantitative-coronary-angiography-and-intravascular-ultrasound-analysis)
- In pulmonary vasculature, Strahler order and tree depth (via topological data analysis) were used as combinatorial invariants that quantitatively distinguish control from hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension micro-CT arterial trees, i.e. a direct Strahler-order-based disease discrimination result, but in mouse pulmonary arteries, not coronary — [A Topological Data Analysis Study on Murine Pulmonary Arterial Trees with Pulmonary Hypertension (arXiv/ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0025556423000974)
- No source was found linking Horton-Strahler-type branching ratios to disease in hepatic/portal vasculature (cirrhosis, portal hypertension); search results returned only general portal vein branching-pattern anatomy, not Strahler-order disease comparisons — [Hepatic portal vein branching patterns according to different liver assessment methods (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0940960223001590)
- In cerebral vasculature, Strahler-order/branching-geometry morphometry (Cassot et al.) exists as detailed normal-anatomy characterization, and branching architecture is discussed as relevant to collateral flow redistribution after stroke-model vessel occlusion, but no direct Strahler-ratio-vs-stroke-outcome statistic was retrieved — [Quantitative Analysis of the Cerebral Vasculature on Magnetic Resonance Angiography (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7311427/)
- Coronary bifurcation *geometry* more broadly (angle, area ratio, flare, curvature, tortuosity) is described as a plausible marker of atherosclerosis risk because of the hemodynamic disturbances bifurcations create, and atherosclerosis in bifurcations preferentially localizes to outer walls rather than the flow-divider carina — [The effect of coronary bifurcation and haemodynamics in prediction of atherosclerotic plaque development (EuroIntervention)](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/the-effect-of-coronary-bifurcation-and-haemodynamics-in-prediction-of-atherosclerotic-plaque-development-a-serial-computed-tomographic-coronary-angiographic-study). This is about single-bifurcation geometry (angle, area ratio), not about the Horton-Strahler count-ratio across the whole tree.

### Inferences
- There is no direct published evidence, in coronary arteries or elsewhere, that specifically validates the Horton-Strahler *bifurcation ratio* (a segment-count ratio across tree orders) as a CAD or vascular-disease biomarker. The one directly relevant Strahler-order disease result found is in murine pulmonary hypertension via micro-CT, a different organ, different species, and a different imaging modality with far greater resolvable tree depth than clinical coronary CTA.
- The genuinely evidence-backed coronary/cardiovascular disease association in this space is retinal and coronary-angiographic **fractal dimension**, which is mathematically related to Horton's laws (the fractal/similarity dimension of a self-similar tree can be derived from the bifurcation and length ratios, D ≈ log(Rb)/log(Rl), per classical Horton-law theory) but is a different, more aggregate descriptor computed by box-counting over the whole vascular image rather than by counting discrete segments per order. Citing fractal-dimension evidence as motivation for including a Horton-Strahler-ratio feature would be citing a related-but-distinct metric, and that distinction should be made explicit rather than implied.
- Given the shallow branch depth available from CTA-derived coronary trees (see previous question), it is unlikely this literature gap is simply an oversight; it plausibly reflects a real practical barrier (too few resolvable orders in clinical human coronary imaging) rather than an unexplored opportunity.

### Gaps
- No coronary-specific Horton-Strahler-ratio-vs-CAD/stenosis/plaque study was found; this evidence gap should be reported as essentially total for coronary arteries specifically.
- No hepatic/portal vasculature Strahler-disease evidence was found.
- The cerebral-vasculature Strahler literature located here is normal-anatomy characterization plus general collateral-flow discussion, not a quantitative Strahler-ratio-vs-stroke-outcome finding; a more targeted search (e.g. specifically for Cassot et al. cerebral Strahler papers and any disease cohort using them) could not be completed within this research pass's tool budget.

## Does the Horton-Strahler ratio capture information independent of curvature-family metrics (mean absolute curvature, RMS curvature) and angle-family metrics (bifurcation angle)?

### Takeaway
By first-principles construction the Horton-Strahler ratio is categorically different from curvature and bifurcation-angle metrics: it is a discrete, whole-tree topological count (how many segments exist at each branch order, and how that count shrinks from one order to the next) with no dependence on the differential geometry (local bending, twisting) of any individual vessel centerline or on the angle at which two vessels meet. No direct evidence was found either confirming or refuting statistical redundancy between Rb and curvature/angle features in coronary data specifically, so this conclusion rests on reasoning from the metrics' definitions rather than an empirical correlation study.

### Cited Findings
- The Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio Rb is defined purely from segment counts at consecutive orders (Rb ≈ N_i / N_(i+1)), following the ordering rule that a segment's order increases only at a confluence of two equal-order segments, and otherwise takes the maximum of the two parents' orders — [Simulated annealing approach to vascular structure with application to the coronary arteries (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4785968/); general definition corroborated in [Central limit theorem for the Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio of general branch order (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03213)
- Curvature and bifurcation-angle metrics used in the coronary bifurcation/atherosclerosis literature (bifurcation angle, area ratio, flare, proximal curvature, tortuosity) are properties of the local 3D shape of a vessel segment or the local geometry at a single junction, evaluated per-bifurcation or per-point along a centerline, not counts of segments across the whole tree's hierarchy — [The effect of coronary bifurcation and haemodynamics in prediction of atherosclerotic plaque development (EuroIntervention)](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/the-effect-of-coronary-bifurcation-and-haemodynamics-in-prediction-of-atherosclerotic-plaque-development-a-serial-computed-tomographic-coronary-angiographic-study)
- Zamir's coronary branching-angle/diameter work explicitly separates two geometric optimality relationships at a bifurcation, the diameter (area) relationship and the branching-angle relationship, and finds human coronary bifurcations close to theoretically optimal on both — [Branching characteristics of human coronary arteries](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3756615/). This shows angle-family metrics are themselves already treated as a distinct axis from diameter/count-based descriptors like Rb, reinforcing that Rb (counts) and angle (local junction geometry) are different constructs even within the same classical literature.
- No study was found statistically testing correlation or redundancy between a Horton-Strahler bifurcation ratio and curvature or bifurcation-angle metrics within the same coronary (or other) vascular dataset.

### Inferences
- Rb is a graph/topology-level scalar (one number, or a small set of numbers, summarizing how the branch *count* thins out with each order across the whole visible tree), while mean absolute curvature and RMS curvature are shape descriptors computed pointwise along a single vessel's 3D centerline and then aggregated, and bifurcation angle is a purely local property of one junction's geometry. There is no mathematical dependency by which segment counts per order are determined by, or determine, local centerline bending or junction angle: a tree could have identical Rb but very different curvature profiles (straight vs. tortuous segments at every order), and conversely two trees with identical curvature statistics could have different branch-order count structures (more or less profuse side-branching). On these first-principles grounds, Rb is not redundant with the curvature-family or angle-family metrics already in the project's candidate list.
- This independence claim is a structural/definitional argument, not an empirically measured near-zero correlation in coronary data; no coronary dataset was found in this search that reports the empirical correlation, so the practical degree of redundancy (e.g. via shared confounding by overall vessel calibre, patient size, or image quality) remains untested.

### Gaps
- No empirical (measured) correlation or redundancy analysis between Horton-Strahler ratio and curvature/angle metrics in any vascular tree, coronary or otherwise, was found in this search.

## Is the Horton-Strahler ratio typically computed per-patient (whole visible tree) or per-vessel/per-subtree, and which fits a model organized around per-vessel local vectors plus per-patient scalars?

### Takeaway
In the classical/morphometric literature, Rb is computed over an entire arterial tree from a single input trunk down to the terminal (capillary or smallest-resolved) segments, i.e. it is a whole-tree (or whole-named-artery-from-its-ostium) statistic, not typically reported separately per named daughter branch. For this project's architecture, computing Rb per major named vessel (RCA, LAD, LCx, each rooted at its ostium/LM) rather than one number for the whole coronary tree would be the more natural and more standard-consistent choice, since the classical definition is already anchored to "one tree, one Rb," and the coronary tree is anatomically three (or four, with LM) largely separate trees fed from two ostia.

### Cited Findings
- Kassab's diameter-defined Strahler system numbers the entire coronary arterial tree of one animal from its single largest input trunk (order 11) down to the smallest capillary segments (order 1), producing one hierarchical order structure (and hence one Rb, or a small set of order-pair ratios) per whole tree studied — [A Computer Reconstruction of the Entire Coronary Arterial Tree Based on Detailed Morphometric Data (Ann Biomed Eng)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10439-005-5758-z); [Simulated annealing approach to vascular structure with application to the coronary arteries (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4785968/)
- The same whole-tree framing is used for the pulmonary arterial tree (one Strahler ordering and one set of Horton ratios Rb/Rl/Rd per pulmonary arterial tree) — [Diameter-defined Strahler system and connectivity matrix of the pulmonary arterial tree (J Appl Physiol)](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/abs/10.1152/jappl.1994.76.2.882)
- The retinal literature applies a customized Horton-Strahler ordering per retinal arterial (or venous) tree as a whole, from the central retinal artery/vein down to arterioles/capillaries, again one ordering scheme per whole visible tree (here, per eye/quadrant) rather than per individual named branch — [customized Horton-Strahler method applied to retinal arterial tree, described in synthesized search result](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2395300/)
- Anatomically, the coronary circulation is not a single tree from one root: the right coronary artery (RCA) arises from the right ostium and the left main (LM) trunk (which then splits into LAD and LCx) arises from the left ostium, i.e. it is standardly described and imaged as two (or, splitting LM's daughters, three: RCA / LAD / LCx) largely independent trees, matching the AHA segmentation's grouping of segments 1-4 (RCA), 5 (LM), 6-10 (LAD system), 11-15 (LCx system) — [Segmental anatomy of the coronary arteries after a modified AHA classification](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Segmental-anatomy-of-the-coronary-arteries-after-a-modified-AHA-classification-1-RCA_fig1_7771780)

### Inferences
- Because Rb's classical definition requires one connected tree rooted at a single trunk, and the coronary circulation is anatomically two-to-three such trees (RCA; LAD; LCx, both of the latter two downstream of a shared LM stub), the standards-consistent way to apply it here is per major named vessel/subtree (RCA-rooted, LAD-rooted, LCx-rooted), not as one pooled number for "the whole coronary tree" merging both ostia. Computing one whole-patient Rb by combining RCA and LM/LAD/LCx segment counts across two anatomically and developmentally distinct trees would be a non-standard construction not seen in the literature reviewed here.
- A per-vessel Rb (one value each for RCA, LAD, LCx, optionally LM if it is modeled as its own short trunk) would also better match this project's Model 1/Model 2 architecture, which is already organized per-vessel for the local curvature-vector inputs; a per-vessel scalar branching-ratio feature could sit alongside those per-vessel curvature vectors more naturally than a single collapsed whole-patient number would.
- This inference follows the same order-depth caveat raised in the first question: whichever way it is aggregated (per-vessel or per-patient), the number of orders available per named coronary subtree in CTA-derived segmentations is small, so a per-vessel Rb inherits the same small-integer-ratio instability, likely to an even greater degree than a pooled whole-tree Rb would (since pooling more branches would average out some of that noise, at the cost of mixing anatomically distinct subtrees).

### Gaps
- No paper was found computing Rb separately per coronary subtree (RCA vs. LAD vs. LCx) and reporting whether per-vessel values differ or agree with a pooled/whole-tree value; this specific methodological choice (per-vessel vs. per-patient Rb for coronary CTA) does not appear to have precedent in the literature located here, so the "which is more appropriate" conclusion above is this researcher's inference from tree anatomy and the classical definition, not a cited direct precedent.
