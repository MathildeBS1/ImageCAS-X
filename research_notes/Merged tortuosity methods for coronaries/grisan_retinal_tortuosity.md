# Grisan, Foracchia, Ruggeri (IEEE TMI 2008) turn-based tortuosity density, and its 2D-to-3D generalisation

Primary source read in full: `tortuosity/A_Novel_Method_for_the_Automatic_Grading_of_Retinal_Vessel_Tortuosity.pdf`
(IEEE TMI 27(3):310-319, 2008, doi:10.1109/TMI.2007.904657; DOI link used below as [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)).
Equations, tables and figures were checked on page images rendered from the PDF (pp. 312-317), not only extracted text.
Page numbers are the journal page numbers (310-319).

## Q1. Exact formula and every step of the pipeline (inflection detection in 2D, noise handling)

### Takeaway
τ(s) = ((n−1)/n) · (1/L_c) · Σ_{i=1..n} (L_c,si / L_χ,si − 1): split the centerline into n "turn curves" of constant curvature sign, sum each turn's arc/chord excess, divide by the whole vessel's arc length, and weight by (n−1)/n so a vessel with no sign change scores exactly 0. On real data the split points are not raw zero crossings but midpoints between crossings of a hysteresis band on signed curvature, applied after cubic smoothing-spline interpolation (γ = 0.005) of hand-placed points.

### Cited Findings
- **Curve and notation (§III-A, eqs. 5-8, p. 312).** Vessel is a planar curve s(l) = [x(l), y(l)] on curvilinear coordinate l ∈ D; chord L_χ = distance between end points (eq. 6); curve length L_c = arc length (eq. 7); signed planar curvature κ(l) (eq. 8). Notation trap: **L_c = arc, L_χ = chord.** — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Turn curve definition (eq. 17, p. 313).** A segment s_i on compact subdomain D_i is a turn curve iff [κ(l) ≥ 0 ∀ l ∈ D_i] ∨ [κ(l) ≤ 0 ∀ l ∈ D_i]. A "twist" is a change of curvature sign; a turn curve is "a segment between two consecutive twists" (§II-C, p. 312). — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Straight stretches (p. 313, Fig. 3).** Because κ = 0 segments make the decomposition non-unique, and because dropping them would make two turns joined by a straight line score the same regardless of the straight link's length/orientation (Fig. 3), each straight segment is split in half, one half assigned to the preceding turn and one to the following. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Decomposition and index (eqs. 18-19, p. 313).** s = s_1 ⊕ s_2 ⊕ … ⊕ s_n (eq. 18); τ(s) = ((n−1)/n)(1/L_c) Σ_{i=1..n} [L_c,si / L_χ,si − 1] (eq. 19). Verified on the rendered equation image. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Stated interpretation (p. 313).** τ "has a dimension of 1/length and thus may be interpreted as a tortuosity density, allowing its comparison on vessels of different length"; without length normalisation a sinusoid over few cycles would score lower than over more cycles. n = 1 gives τ = 0 ("vessels with a constant convexity have zero tortuosity"); τ > 0 whenever at least one twist exists. It sums *local* contributions per turn, "at variance with all other methods (including that described in [9])" which are global. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Claimed property compliance (p. 313).** Composition via the summation in (19); amplitude modulation via per-turn arc/chord; frequency modulation "implicitly by means of the curve splits". Curvature magnitude is scale-dependent but its sign is not, so the split is unique regardless of magnification; arc/chord is scale-invariant, so τ is magnification-invariant "as long as the total length L_c is expressed in common length units (e.g., μm)" (pp. 313-314). — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Interpolation/smoothing (§IV-B, p. 314).** Hand-placed centerline points are sparse and user-dependent; vessels are assumed C¹, so a cubic smoothing spline is fitted with "regularization parameter set to γ = 0.005" (Fig. 5). The convention of γ (which term it weights, units) is not defined. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Noise handling for inflection detection (§IV-B, p. 314; Fig. 6-7, p. 315).** Real vessels show "small oscillations (changes of convexity with very small amplitude) around the main vessel direction, due to the presence of noisy samples" which would create "a great number of artificial turn curves". "An hysteretic threshold on curvature was thus used ... the vessels are not split where their curvature crosses the zero line, but in the midpoint of two hysteresis-crossing points." Fig. 6 plots κ in px⁻¹ vs curvilinear coordinate in px with dashed hysteresis lines; read from a 400 dpi render they sit at about ±0.01 px⁻¹ (value is not given in the text). Fig. 7 shows the resulting split points and per-turn chords on an artery. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **No other parameters.** No minimum turn length, no curvature scale parameter beyond γ and the hysteresis band, and no resampling step are reported. Curvature is computed on the spline (derivative method not stated). — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)

### Inferences
- Pipeline as implementable: (1) fit smoothing spline to points; (2) evaluate signed κ(l) densely; (3) find intervals where κ > +h or κ < −h; a split is placed at the midpoint between the exit of one band and entry into the opposite band (my reading of "midpoint of two hysteresis-crossing points"); (4) κ = 0 / sub-threshold straight runs are shared half-and-half between neighbours; (5) for each piece compute arc length and end-to-end chord; (6) apply eq. 19 with L_c the whole-vessel arc.
- Because arc/chord − 1 ≈ θ²/24 for a circular arc of turning angle θ (small θ), τ is dominated by turns with large turning angle; shallow wiggles that survive the hysteresis add to n but contribute little excess. The hysteresis h therefore mainly controls n, and n enters τ only through (n−1)/n and through where cuts fall.
- h in px⁻¹ is resolution-dependent; porting to mm on CCTA requires re-choosing it (no value transfers).

### Gaps
- Exact hysteresis value, spline software, and curvature estimator are not stated; ±0.01 px⁻¹ is read from Fig. 6 only.
- How the band handles a curve that re-enters the same-sign band without crossing (e.g., +h → |κ|<h → +h) is not described; presumably no split.

## Q2. Claimed properties and validation (dataset, statistics, results)

### Takeaway
Four properties are posited (rigid invariance, composition "not less than any part", frequency and amplitude monotonicity). Validation: synthetic sinusoids/arcs, plus 30 arteries and 30 veins (60 fundus images, 34 subjects) hand-traced and ranked by one retina specialist; τ had the highest Spearman ρ (0.949 arteries, 0.853 veins) but only about 0.01 above tc/L_χ, with no test of the difference. Several internal inconsistencies between the text and Figs. 9-11 were found (flagged below).

### Cited Findings
- **Properties (§II, pp. 311-312).** Invariance to translation and rotation; to scaling only for vessels of similar calibre. Composition (eq. 2): tortuosity of a combined vessel is not less than that of any component, rejecting Hart 1999's "in-between" rule (eq. 1) with Fig. 2(a) (three non-tortuous pieces forming a tortuous curve). Frequency modulation (eq. 3): more turn curves at equal amplitude → more tortuous. Amplitude modulation (eq. 4): larger amplitude at equal number of turns → more tortuous. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Clinical premise (§I p. 310; §VI p. 317).** Because the retina is near-hemispherical, "not only straight vessels but also long vessels presenting a smooth semi-circular or parabolic shape are considered as non-tortuous"; the "non tortuous paradigm should be the circle arc". This is why constant-sign vessels score 0. Ophthalmologists are said to integrate "how many times a vessel changes convexity" and "how large is the amplitude of each ... turn curve" (§III-B, p. 313). — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Comparators (§III-A, eqs. 9-16, pp. 312-313).** 12 measures: L_c/L_χ; tc = ∫|κ| and tsc = ∫κ² and their ratios to L_c and L_χ (Hart 1999); DCI (Patasius 2005, integral of squared dκ/dl, eq. 10); MAC (mean direction-angle change, eq. 14); TN (count of angle changes ≥ π/6, eq. 15); ICM (Bullitt 2003, eq. 16); τ. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Synthetic sets (§IV-A, p. 314).** (1) s(l) = A·sin(f·l), A = 2, l ∈ [0, 6π], f ∈ {0, π/12, …, π/2} (eq. 20); (2) f = π, A ∈ {0,…,5}; (3) a semicircle of radius r = 4 divided into 1-6 arcs rearranged with alternating curvature sign (Fig. 4), stated to have constant |κ| and constant total length (§V, p. 315). The text says the lowest value should be on the semicircle. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Synthetic result claim (§V, p. 315).** "Only six of the twelve measures (τ, tc, tsc, tsc/L_c, ICM, and DCI) increase in all cases." — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Real data (§IV-B, p. 314).** 60 fundus images, different eyes of 34 subjects, normal or hypertensive retinopathy of varying severity, 50° FOV, digitised 1300×1100. 30 arteries and 30 veins "of similar length and caliber", chosen as major vessels with minimal overlap/entangling; centerlines traced by hand ("No automatic vessel identification algorithm was used"). One retina specialist ordered each set by increasing tortuosity (Acknowledgment names Dr S. Piermarocchi, Padova). Funded by Nidek Technologies. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Table I (p. 317), Spearman ρ vs specialist, arteries / veins:** τ 0.949 / 0.853; tc/L_χ 0.939 / 0.842; tsc/L_χ 0.928 / 0.804; tsc 0.925 / 0.826; tc 0.922 / 0.837; MAC 0.920 / 0.814; tc/L_c 0.919 / 0.814; tsc/L_c 0.917 / 0.773; TN 0.838 / 0.695; L_c/L_χ 0.792 / 0.656; DCI 0.787 / 0.589; ICM 0.684 / 0.575. All p < 0.0001 against H0: ρ = 0 (p. 316). — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Robustness (§IV-C p. 315; Table II p. 317).** Noise model s(k) = s(l_k) + e(k), e ~ N(0, Σ), Σ = diag(0.5, 0.5) px² (99 % within 2.12 px), 100 noisy repetitions, re-smoothed. Median (SD) ρ, arteries / veins: τ 0.945 (0.006) / 0.804 (0.044); tc/L_χ 0.935 (0.008) / 0.779 (0.044); L_c/L_χ 0.793 (0.006) / 0.659 (0.012); tsc 0.918 (0.008) / 0.752 (0.047); ICM 0.582 (0.083) / 0.259 (0.108); DCI 0.792 (0.029) / 0.500 (0.067). Text claims τ's median is always highest and its variance smaller "than all but the L_c/L_χ ratio" (p. 316). — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Later independent validation.** Ramos et al. (BMC Med Res Methodol 2018): 5 ophthalmologists, intra-rater κ 0.35-0.83, inter-rater κ 0.22-0.76; individual experts' AUC vs consensus 0.61-0.83; the Grisan metric was the best computational metric with AUC 0.80, attributed to integrating "how many times a vessel changes convexity". — [Ramos 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6245828/)

### Disagreements and errors found (paper vs itself, and vs existing note `docs_thesis/papers/grisan2008tortuosity.md`)
- **Fig. 11 contradicts the stated construction of synthetic set 3.** Read off Fig. 11 (p. 317): tc/L_c is flat at 0.50 and tsc/L_c flat at 0.25, implying |κ| = 0.5 (radius 2), not r = 4 (which would give 0.25 and 0.0625). tsc and tc grow linearly with number of arcs (tsc ≈ 1.6 → 9.4), which for constant |κ| means total length grows about 6×, contradicting "total curve length remain constant". tc/L_χ ≈ 0.79 = 0.5·π/2 and L_c/L_χ − 1 ≈ 0.57-0.585 ≈ π/2 − 1 are consistent with the plotted curves being a chain of k *full semicircles of radius 2*, not a semicircle cut into k arcs. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Fig. 11 plots τ ≈ 0.031 (not 0) at one arc**, although eq. 19 gives τ = 0 for n = 1 (read at 400 dpi: bottom of the τ axis is below the 0.04 tick by ~0.009). Values at 2 arcs (≈0.045) match the chain-of-semicircles model ((k−1)/k · (π/2−1)/(2π) = 0.045), so the k = 1 point is unexplained. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **"Six measures increase in all cases" is contradicted for tsc/L_c** by Fig. 11 (flat) and, in Fig. 10, tc/L_c is non-monotone (dips at A = 2). The existing note repeats the six-measure claim without this flag. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Eq. 16 misprint confirmed by the paper's own figure.** Eq. 16 prints ICM = (n_ic + 1)·L_χ/L_c, but Fig. 11's ICM values (≈2 → ≈9, i.e. ≈ k·π/2) only match (n_ic + 1)·L_c/L_χ, the Bullitt definition ("multiplied this number (plus one) times the total path length and then divided by the distance between endpoints"). Existing note's flag is correct. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657); [Bullitt 2008 via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2517122/)
- **Fig. 9** caption says "function of the number of twists" but the axis is "Sinusoid Frequency", plotted only from 1 to ≈1.55, whereas eq. 20 lists f from 0. Existing note flags the caption only. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Table II misprint:** tc veins "0.752 (0.380)", an SD larger than the median's distance to 1; almost certainly 0.038. Not in the existing note. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Variance claim:** In veins τ's SD (0.044) ties tc/L_χ (0.044) and exceeds L_c/L_χ (0.012); in arteries it ties L_c/L_χ (0.006). So "smaller than all but L_c/L_χ" is not strictly true. Existing note's flag is correct. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- **Ref [9]** (Bullitt 2003) is printed as *Med. Image Anal.*, no. 9, pp. 1163-1171; it is IEEE TMI 22(9):1163-1171, as other authors cite it. — [Bullitt 2008 via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2517122/)
- Everything else in the existing note (Table I/II numbers, γ, noise Σ, 34 subjects, hysteresis ≈ ±0.01 px⁻¹ by eye, straight-segment halving, C-vs-U blind spot, scale-dependency concession) checks out against the PDF.

### Inferences
- The discriminating advantage over tc/L_χ (0.010 arteries, 0.011 veins on n = 30) is well inside what a single rater's ranking noise could produce; Ramos 2018's multi-rater inter-rater κ as low as 0.22 shows how noisy the reference itself is.
- Using my own arithmetic on the stated set-3 construction (fixed length and |κ|, semicircle r = 4 cut into k equal arcs of angle π/k), eq. 19 would give τ = (k−1)/(4π) · [(π/k)/(2 sin(π/2k)) − 1], which *decreases* for k ≥ 2 (≈0.0088 at k = 2, ≈0.0046 at k = 6). So, had the stated construction been used, τ would not have been monotone in number of turns: at fixed total turning, splitting into more, flatter arcs lowers the arc/chord excess. The frequency property holds for τ only when each added turn keeps its amplitude (as in sets 1 and the plotted chain). This matters for the "one wide arc vs several tight bends" selection criterion.

### Gaps
- No inter/intra-rater data, no absolute grades, no significance test between correlated ρ values in the original paper.
- Whether the authors' later work (automatic tracing, ref [18]) re-validated τ on automatic centerlines was not found.

## Q3. Why a signed-curvature inflection point is undefined for a 3D space curve, and how later work defines "turns" in 3D

### Takeaway
In 3D the Frenet curvature κ = |r′×r″|/|r′|³ is a non-negative magnitude with no sign; "sign" only exists for a plane curve relative to the plane's fixed normal. A generic space curve never has κ = 0, so a strict inflection almost never occurs. The accepted substitute (Bullitt 2003) defines an inflection as a local minimum of curvature where the Frenet normal N (and binormal) flips by about 180°, detected as large local maxima of ΔN·ΔN; alternatives are curvature minima, plane-projection-based convexity (Gabrielides & Sapidis 2020), or CPR-view line crossings (van Zandwijk 2019).

### Cited Findings
- Grisan et al. themselves note Bullitt's ICM counts "points in which the Frenet normal axis to the vessel changes direction by π radians, that is equivalent to a change in sign of the curvature for planar curves" (§III-A-5, p. 313), i.e. the 3D bridge is Bullitt's, not theirs; Grisan's paper stays 2D throughout. — [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)
- Bullitt definition (as restated in Bullitt et al. 2008): "For a 3D space curve, an inflection point was defined as a locus that exhibits a minimum of total curvature ... the Normal and Binormal axes of the Frenet frame change orientation by close to 180° as the frame passes through an inflection point"; detection searches for "large local maxima of the dot product ΔN • ΔN". ICM "multiplied this number (plus one) times the total path length and then divided by the distance between endpoints" (dimensionless); ICM "is effective in detecting larger amplitude curves"; SOAM "summed the angles between consecutive trios of points ... normalized by path length" and "is effective in detecting high-frequency, low-amplitude coils". Original: Bullitt et al., IEEE TMI 2003;22:1163-1171. — [Bullitt et al. 2008, PMC2517122](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2517122/)
- Gabrielides & Sapidis, "Inflection points on 3D curves", Computer Aided Geometric Design 2020 (doi:10.1016/j.cagd.2020.101932): state that existing definitions of 3D inflection "lack the direct relation to local shape-characteristics that the corresponding definition for planar curves has", and propose a generalisation tied to local convexity of the 3D curve and its orthogonal projections on planes of interest. Full text not accessible (403); only abstract-level content verified. — [search record](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1309221570072514560); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167839620301199)
- Coronary CCTA practice (van Zandwijk et al., J Digit Imaging 2019, 71 patients): inflection points counted semi-automatically as "the maximum number of intersections ... that the straight connection between beginning and endpoint has with the centerline when rotating the CPR view"; i.e. a projection-based chord-crossing count, not differential geometry. Mean inflections per segment 0.63 end-systole vs 0.51 end-diastole (p < 0.001); curvature (Menger, 5 mm step) 0.085 vs 0.078 mm⁻¹; no association of dynamic changes with stenosis or plaque type. — [van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)
- Johnson & Dougherty, Med Eng Phys 2007;29(6): 3D tortuosity metrics from unit-speed parameterisations of 3D curvature of approximating polynomial splines, which "obviates the need for arbitrary filtering"; two of three metrics scale invariant and additive (abstract-level; full text 403). Companion clinical validation: Med Eng Phys 2007 (PubMed 17419088). — [search record, IOPscience](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1016/j.medengphy.2006.07.008); [clinical validation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17419088/)

### Inferences
- Standard differential geometry (not sourced here, textbook-level): for r(l) in ℝ³, κ = |r′×r″|/|r′|³ ≥ 0 and N = T′/|T′| is undefined where κ = 0. A planar inflection is where κ passes through 0 and N jumps by π. For a generic space curve, κ = 0 requires r′×r″ = 0 (two independent scalar conditions on one parameter), so it generically never happens; instead κ dips to a small positive minimum while N rotates rapidly (through roughly π) about T, and the torsion τ_F = (r′×r″)·r‴/|r′×r″|² spikes there. Hence "inflection" in 3D must be a thresholded event (how fast/how far N rotates, how low κ dips), not a sign test. This is why Bullitt's detector looks for large ΔN·ΔN rather than a zero crossing.
- Noise consequence: N and torsion depend on third derivatives, so on sampled CCTA centerlines a Bullitt-type flip detector needs smoothing plus a hysteresis analogue (e.g. require κ to drop below h_low between two regions above h_high, and N rotation > some angle). This is the natural 3D analogue of Grisan's hysteresis on signed κ, but no paper was found that validates it.
- A practical 3D port of Grisan's τ: cut at detected 3D inflections (Bullitt N-flip, or κ minima below a threshold), share low-κ runs half-and-half, then apply eq. 19 per piece using 3D arc and chord. Every other step of eq. 19 is dimension-free (arc/chord works in 3D unchanged). Only the segmentation rule needs replacement.
- Planar-projection alternatives (Gabrielides-Sapidis, CPR-based counting) are view-dependent unless the projection plane is defined intrinsically (e.g. local osculating plane or best-fit plane per window); a helix, for example, has no inflection by N-flip but shows many sign changes in a side projection.
- A helix (constant κ, constant torsion) is the 3D analogue of Grisan's C-shape blind spot: n = 1, τ = 0, although it is visually highly tortuous. Coronaries wrapping the heart surface can be helix-like, so this blind spot is more serious in 3D than in the retina.

### Gaps
- No source found that formally defines "twist" or "turn curve" for 3D vessels specifically in Grisan's sense, beyond Bullitt's N-flip.
- Bullitt 2003's exact threshold on ΔN·ΔN and smoothing were not retrievable (IEEE full text not fetched); only the 2008 restatement.

## Q4. Published 3D versions of Grisan's index, and 3D/coronary/cerebral applications

### Takeaway
I found no peer-reviewed paper that ports Grisan's eq. 19 (per-turn arc/chord excess with (n−1)/n and 1/L_c) to 3D vessels. The 3D literature uses Bullitt's DM/ICM/SOAM (cerebral MRA), curvature-integral families (coronary CCTA, Kashyap 2022), spline-curvature metrics (Johnson & Dougherty 2007), chain-code 3D tortuosity (Mateos 2024, brain structures), and in coronaries mostly L/D, curvature and CPR-based inflection counts. Grisan is cited in 3D work only as a 2D precedent.

### Cited Findings
- Bullitt's DM, ICM, SOAM were the first metrics applied to regional tortuosity of 3D intracerebral vasculature from MRA and used in tumour malignancy studies. — [Bullitt et al. 2008, PMC2517122](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2517122/)
- Kashyap et al. (Sci Rep 2022), CTCA of 127 patients without CAD, LMCA bifurcation: tested tortuosity index (L/D), average absolute curvature, RMS curvature, average squared-derivative curvature against CFD low TAWSS. Average absolute curvature correlated best (LMCA R² = 0.196, LAD R² = 0.232, bifurcation R² = 0.112, all p ≤ 0.001); L/D showed no significant correlation (p = 0.86). No inflection-based or Grisan-type metric was tested. — [Kashyap 2022, PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)
- Mateos et al. (BMC Med Imaging 2024) extend Bribiesca's Slope Chain Code to a 3D tortuosity τ3D on voxelised objects (central sulcus, lobes; AD vs controls, p < 0.05); they cite Grisan only among 2D methods ("a novel automatic grading method for retinal vessel tortuosity [15]") and note "only a few authors have attempted to measure 3D tortuosity" (local PDF `tortuosity/3D Tortuosity computation as a shape descriptor.pdf`, p. 2). — [Mateos 2024](https://bmcmedimaging.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12880-024-01312-6)
- van Zandwijk 2019 is the only CCTA coronary source found that counts inflection points, and does so by CPR-view chord crossings (see Q3). — [van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)
- A 2020 review of nerve-fibre/retinal tortuosity describes Grisan's method as combining arc-chord ratios with the number of inflection points and states that inflection-counting and angle-sum methods "have recently been extended to 3D, with varying degrees of success" (search-snippet level; full text 403, so the specific 3D works it cites were not verified). — [Applied Sciences 2020, doi:10.3390/app10144788](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10144788)
- Other 3D contexts surfaced but not verified as Grisan ports: OCT-angiography "vessel tortuosity index" on cerebral penetrating vessels; skeleton-based cerebrovascular analysis (PMC5168872, blocked by CAPTCHA); Hidden-Markov-model coronary tortuosity (Springer 2025 chapter); ML-driven coronary tortuosity (JACC Adv 2026). — [OCTA cerebral](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13155846/); [HMM coronary](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-95841-0_27); [ML coronary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772963X26002504)

### Inferences
- The absence of a validated 3D Grisan port is itself a finding: a CCTA implementation would be novel and needs its own synthetic checks (helix, planar sinusoid rotated in 3D, sinusoid on a cylinder) and threshold-sensitivity analysis (selection criteria 1-3 in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`).
- Given Kashyap 2022 (curvature integrals beat L/D on coronaries) and Grisan's own Table I (tc/L_χ within 0.01 of τ), a curvature-integral index is a strong, threshold-free comparator that has no n = 1 floor; τ adds value only if the turn segmentation is stable in 3D.
- The retina-specific premise (circular arc is "non-tortuous") has no obvious coronary counterpart; coronaries follow the epicardial surface, so a constant-sign or helical course is normal anatomy and the n = 1 → 0 floor could hit many segments.

### Gaps
- Full texts of Bullitt 2003, Johnson & Dougherty 2007, Gabrielides & Sapidis 2020, Applied Sciences 2020 review, and the HMM/ML coronary papers were not retrieved (paywall/403); claims above from them are abstract- or restatement-level.
- No source found on O'Flynn or Diedrich 3D tortuosity specifically; the "O'Flynn" search returned Johnson & Dougherty 2007 as the 3D spline-curvature paper.
- Search coverage was limited to about 15 web calls; a Google Scholar "cited by" sweep of Grisan 2008 restricted to 3D/CT/MRA was not possible with the available tools.
