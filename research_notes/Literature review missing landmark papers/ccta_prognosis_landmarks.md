## Q1. Population CCTA / CAC landmark cohorts

### Takeaway
The one population landmark a reviewer will look for and not find is the MESA serial-CAC evidence on **incident** coronary calcium in people starting at CAC = 0 (Kronmal 2007, or Dzaye 2021 for timing): it is the only large population-based evidence on who develops coronary atherosclerosis from a disease-free baseline, which is the thesis outcome. Fuchs 2023 (this cohort) is in refs.bib and should be re-cited beside Bergström 2021 in Sec. 3. Detrano 2008, Blaha 2016 and Nasir 2022 are about events or cross-sectional prevalence and are covered under the one-source rule by Hageman 2023 and Bergström 2021.

### Cited Findings
- **Kronmal RA, McClelland RL, Detrano R, et al. Risk factors for the progression of coronary artery calcification in asymptomatic subjects: results from the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA). Circulation 2007;115(21):2722-2730. DOI 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.106.674143.** Semantic Scholar 546 citations. Not in refs.bib. Community cohort without clinical CVD, follow-up CAC in 5,756 participants, mean 2.4 years between scans; incident (newly detectable) CAC about 6.6% per year; age, male sex, white ethnicity, hypertension, BMI, diabetes, glucose and family history were associated with both incident CAC and growth; LDL, HDL and creatinine only with incident CAC — [catalogue abstract record](https://ersearch2.cvtisr.sk/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c419t-cb6c3411af72e50b03713630eee18ba9e33e9b42797a70d16cc501dd1420e6f52/UserComments?lng=en) (abstract fragments via search; full text not read). **Verdict: must add (or Dzaye 2021 instead, not both).** Adds what no cited source holds: a random/community population followed from a disease-free baseline, with risk factors for incident disease. ### Inferences
- The review has no evidence on the *risk-factor determinants of developing atherosclerosis from zero in a population*; Won 2022 is the only "who develops" study and it is referred and found risk factors non-predictive. Kronmal 2007 (risk factors do predict incident CAC in a population) is the natural counterweight, and its contrast with Won 2022 sharpens why a population sample matters. This is the single highest-value addition found.
- A Danish or European incidence analogue (e.g. Heinz Nixdorf Recall CAC progression, Rotterdam) might be preferred by a Danish reviewer but was not searched.

### Gaps
- No CGPS paper on plaque prevalence by risk factors or SCORE2 (Fuchs/Kofoed/Kühl) was found beyond Fuchs 2023; a CGPS calibrated mass CAC paper (2,985 participants) exists but its details were not obtained. The CGPS group may have papers not indexed in the searches used; ask the supervisors.
- CARDIA incident CAC papers (young adults) were not searched.
- Google Scholar counts not obtained.

## Q2. Prognostic CCTA trials and registries (CONFIRM, SCOT-HEART, PROMISE, ICONIC, Motoyama)

### Takeaway
None of these belongs in Sec. 3. All are symptomatic or referred patients, their outcome is clinical events, and their predictors are stenosis or plaque features, i.e. disease already present on the scan. They answer "does visible disease predict events", the opposite end from "does geometry on a plaque-free scan predict disease". At most one could be cited in the introduction for "CCTA findings are prognostic", and Fuchs 2023 already does this for an asymptomatic Danish population.

## Q3. Serial CCTA plaque progression (PARADIGM and others)

### Takeaway
The missing piece is the **PARADIGM design paper (Lee 2016)**, which is needed as the source for the fact, written in Sec. 3, that PARADIGM patients had repeat CCTA for clinical reasons (Won 2022 does not state it; flagged in the claim check). The PARADIGM statin paper (Lee 2018) is a landmark but concerns treatment of existing plaque. The Dutch 10-year serial CCTA is the only other long-interval study and is a conference abstract. A 2024 EHJ state-of-the-art review could serve as one umbrella source for serial CCTA if needed.

### Cited Findings
- **Lee SE, Chang HJ, Rizvi A, et al. Rationale and design of the Progression of AtheRosclerotic PlAque DetermIned by Computed TomoGraphic Angiography IMaging (PARADIGM) registry: a comprehensive exploration of plaque progression and its impact on clinical outcomes from a multicenter serial CCTA observational study. Am Heart J 2016;182:72-79. DOI 10.1016/j.ahj.2016.09.003.** Semantic Scholar 101 (DOI resolved there). Not in refs.bib. **Verdict: must add (small, factual).** It is the only source for the registry's clinically indicated repeat CCTA, which carries the "scanned for a clinical reason" caveat applied to Won and Lee 2024. Read at least the abstract before citing (claim check had it at abstract level).
- **Lee SE, Chang HJ, Sung JM, et al. Effects of statins on coronary atherosclerotic plaques: the PARADIGM study. JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 2018;11(10):1475-1484. ### Inferences
- No serial CCTA landmark is missing that would change the gap: all large serial CCTA studies (PARADIGM, Dutch 10-year) are referred cohorts with plaque present at baseline in most patients.

### Gaps
- Exact PARADIGM design page numbers and the clinical-indication wording were not verified from the paper itself.
- Whether the Dutch 10-year study has since appeared as a full article (2024-2026) was not confirmed.

None of the methodology papers belongs in the literature review; they belong in the technical background or methods, and two of them should not be cited at all given the analysis plan. The review needs SCORE2 only as the named baseline (score2_2021 is in refs.bib and cited in the introduction) and one "imaging beyond the risk score" source (Hageman 2023, cited; Detrano 2008 as fallback).
