# Left main (LM) bifurcation angle: normal values, definitions and known correlates

Scope: angle B (LAD–LCx, "distal" or inter-branch angle), angle A (LM–LCx, "proximal") and angle C (LM–LAD, main-vessel angle), in human in-vivo CCTA and angiography. The notes are written to check whether tracer-derived angles on CGPS (Danish general population, ~1,200 plaque-free and ~600 plaque baselines) group people as expected.

Convention warning: in most CT and QCA papers, A and C are "obtuse" angles between the *proximal* LM direction (pointing back toward the ostium) and each branch, so they are about 120–145°. Some papers instead report the angle between the LM *flow direction* and the branch, which is 180° minus that value (about 35–60°). An example is the CKD paper, which reports LM–LAD as 35.9 ± 11.4°. Check which convention the tracer uses before comparing.

## 1. Normal distribution and definitions / measurement method

### Takeaway
On CCTA, the LM angle B in people without stenosis averages about 72–80°, with a very wide SD of about 20–23° (range about 25–180°). Angle A averages about 126–143° and angle C about 121–139°. 3D QCA in LM-disease cohorts gives larger angle B values (about 86–96°). Absolute values depend strongly on the definition: 2D vs 3D, how far along each branch the direction is fitted, and cardiac phase. Compare distributions and directions of effect, not absolute means.

### Cited findings
- **Medrano-Gracia et al. 2016 computational atlas** (EuroIntervention, DOI 10.4244/EIJV12I7A139). n = 300 adults with CACS = 0 and no stenosis; 7 excluded for poor mesh. About 64% female, younger than earlier cohorts and ethnically homogeneous. Scans were GE LightSpeed 64-slice, retrospective gating, reconstructed at 75% R-R (end-diastole), with beta-blockade to about 60 bpm and sublingual nitroglycerin. Overall LM angle B was about 80°. Age, BMI and dominance distributions are not reported in the text. — [Medrano-Gracia, EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
  - **Definition:** the bifurcation point is where the centrelines split. The bifurcation plane is a least-squares fit to centreline points from the bifurcation point out to a chosen depth, sampled every 0.5 mm. Angle B is the 3D angle between the daughter branches, **averaged over a 5–10 mm depth interval** using linear centreline approximations. The inflow angle is the angle of the proximal vessel to that plane; its absolute value rarely exceeds 30°. — [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
  - The atlas notes that earlier CT studies mostly reported larger angles. Pflederer and Godino were closest to the atlas values. Kawasaki and Dzavik reported lower values, which the atlas attributes to methodology. — [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
  - The LM bifurcation (about 80°) is wider than other major bifurcations (about 50°). LM ostial diameter is 3.5 ± 0.8 mm, LM length 10.5 ± 5.3 mm, and the Finet ratio 0.658 ± 0.083. — [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- **Values from the same atlas, as tabulated in a scoping review:** angle B 78.9 ± 23.1°, angle A 126.1 ± 21.1°, angle C 138.6 ± 18.9°. — [Scoping review, PLoS One 2022, PMC9385039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **Kawasaki et al. (64-slice CT):** n = 209, age 66 ± 12, 34% female, suspected disease without significant stenosis. Their angle B' was 72 ± 22°, and the scoping review lists A 143 ± 13° and C 121 ± 21°. — [Atlas comparison table, PDF](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy/pdf); [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **Pooled CCTA ranges across studies with more than 200 patients:** angle A 126–143°, B 72–79°, C 121–139°. The review explains the larger 3D-QCA angle B in SYNTAX by more severe LM disease in that cohort. — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **Temov & Sun 2016 (Int J Cardiovasc Imaging; conference-level report):** n = 196 CCTA, LAD–LCx angle 79.40 ± 22.97°, range 35.5–178°. — [Curtin espace record](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/4621?show=full); [search summary of review table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **Sun/Cao-type small CCTA studies:** angle B was 76.5 ± 15.9° with a normal left coronary artery vs 94.3 ± 16.5° with a diseased one (P < 0.001). An earlier study of about 30 patients reported 89.1 ± 13.1° vs 75.5 ± 19.8°. These are small, mixed-disease samples measured in 2D or volume rendering. — [Sun 2013, BioMed Res Int, PMC3888717](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3888717/); [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/21584714)
- **Handran et al. 2015 (J Invasive Cardiol):** n = 44 CCTA, chest pain, no significant LM disease. Dual-source 64-slice CT, reconstructed every 5% of R-R. Angles were measured on an MPR plane containing all three centrelines (Vitrea), using centreline segments **at least 1 cm long**. LAD–LCx median 74° in diastole (range 25–158°) and 69° in systole. LM–LCx median 130° (range 74–170°) in diastole and 133° in systole. LM–LAD range 108–178°. The source has internal inconsistencies in its ranges. — [Handran, HMP/JIC](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/left-main-bifurcation-angle-and-changes-throughout-cardiac-cycle-quantitative-implications-left-main-bifurcation-stenting-and-stents)
- **3D QCA, LM disease (SYNTAX, Girasis 2010, JACC Cardiovasc Interv 3:41–48):** 354 LM-PCI patients, of whom 266 (75.1%) could be analysed. Pre-PCI end-diastolic proximal BA was about 106° and distal BA about 96°. The two were inversely correlated (r = −0.75, p < 0.001). — [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/28713); scoping review gives distal 95.6 ± 23.6° in n = 226 — [PMC9385039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **3D QCA (CAAS), 170 LM-PCI patients at end-diastole:** median distal BA 86.3°, proximal BA 106.9°, main-vessel (LM–LAD) angle 141.4°. — [PMC4299691](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4299691)
- **Angle nomenclature:**
  - The EBC consensus (7th meeting) defines a proximal angle A (proximal main vessel to side branch) and a distal angle B (between the distal branches). — [EBC consensus](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/consensus-from-the-7th-european-bifurcation-club-meeting)
  - Bif-ARC adds angle C (proximal to distal main vessel). — [Bif-ARC definitions](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/definitions-and-standardized-endpoints-for-treatment-of-coronary-bifurcations)
  - For the LM, the side branch is conventionally the LCx: A = LM–LCx, B = LAD–LCx, C = LM–LAD. — [PMC4299691](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4299691)
  - Some studies use "BA-α" (the complement of angle A). Main-vessel curvature widens angle B but leaves BA-α unaffected. — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **2D vs 3D:** 2D QCA suffers from overlap and foreshortening. 3D QCA and CCTA are described as more accurate and reproducible. — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)

### Inferences
- For a plaque-free CGPS baseline, an expected tracer angle B of about 70–85° with an SD of about 20° is consistent with the CACS-0 atlas, which used 3D centrelines at end-diastole over 5–10 mm. A mean far outside about 65–95° would more likely reflect the definition (fitting distance, flow vs proximal convention) than the population.
- The SD is so large (about 20–23°) that per-person noise of 5–10° is small relative to the between-person spread. Even so, group differences of the order seen in the literature (about 10°) need hundreds of scans to detect.
- Angles A and B are mechanically coupled (r = −0.75 in SYNTAX). A tracer that reports A, B and C should reproduce a strong negative A–B correlation. This is a cheap internal consistency check.

### Gaps
- Pflederer et al. 2006 (Invest Radiol 41:793–798) and Rodriguez-Granillo et al. 2007 (CT, LM bifurcation): primary values were not retrieved. The atlas says only that Pflederer's values were close to its own.
- No source retrieved gives a population-based, unselected (non-referral) normal distribution. All CT cohorts are referral or chest-pain patients.
- No study found that systematically varies the branch-fitting distance (e.g. 2 vs 5 vs 10 mm) and reports how angle B changes. Methods range from "at least 1 cm" segments (Handran) to 5–10 mm averaging (atlas). In 3D QCA the distance is set by the software.

## 2. Associations with demographic, anthropometric and clinical variables

### Takeaway
The only correlates replicated across independent CT cohorts are **male sex** (about 10° wider) and the **presence of a ramus intermedius / trifurcation** (about 14° wider). **BMI** is positive in one study with weak methods. Age, hypertension, diabetes, heart rate, dominance and heart size have no adequate primary evidence, or none was found. Cardiac phase changes the angle by a mean absolute about 7° per person, with a small mean signed change.

### Cited findings
- **Sex (robust):**
  - Atlas: men 85° vs women 74° (p < 0.001). The difference remains after adjusting for height (p = 0.002). The inflow angle does not differ by sex (12° vs 6°, p = 0.07). — [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
  - Temov & Sun (n = 196): male sex is associated with angle B > 80° (risk ratio 2.07, P = 0.003). — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/); [Curtin record](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/4621?show=full)
- **Ramus intermedius (robust, large effect):** in the atlas, angle B was 89 ± 21° with an intermediate artery vs 75 ± 23° without (p < 0.001). The ramus affected angle B only, not the inflow angle or LM diameter. — [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- **BMI / body size (weak, one study):**
  - Temov & Sun: BMI > 25 kg/m² is associated with angle B > 80° (risk ratio 2.54). No multivariable analysis was done and stenosis was not accounted for. — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
  - The atlas used height as a covariate, and the sex effect survived. A separate height or BMI effect on angle B is not reported in the text retrieved. — [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- **Risk factors in a disease-stratified cohort (Juan et al., Sci Rep 2017, n = 313: 40 normal, 211 non-significant, 62 significant stenosis):**
  - Male sex, hypertension and BMI differed across the stenosis groups (BMI 25.49 ± 3.58 normal vs 26.92 non-significant, p = 0.047).
  - Age did not differ (58.1 vs 58.2 vs 54.7 years, p = 0.135).
  - These are group differences by stenosis status, not direct correlations of risk factors with the angle.
  - [Juan, Sci Rep 2017 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28473705/); [PMC5431433](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5431433/)
- **LDL-C:** untreated LDL-C is associated with angle B > 80° (OR 3.77; Murasato). — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **Age:**
  - In the 2022 RCA–aorta angle study, the LAD–LCx angle was essentially uncorrelated with age (r = 0.010, p = 0.873). — [PMC9917625](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9917625/)
  - In a Polish LM trifurcation study, wider angles were linked to earlier age at onset of calcification. This is an outcome-type association, not angle vs age. — [Czaja-Ziółkowska, Pol Heart J 2022](https://journals.viamedica.pl/polish_heart_journal/article/download/KP.a2022.0161/67640)
- **Cardiac phase:**
  - Handran (n = 44): mean signed change from diastole to systole was +1° for LM–LAD, −3° for LM–LCx and +3° for LAD–LCx. Mean absolute change was 6° (range 2–8°) for LM–LAD and 7° (3–9°) for LM–LCx and LAD–LCx. LAD–LCx increased in 67% of patients. — [Handran](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/left-main-bifurcation-angle-and-changes-throughout-cardiac-cycle-quantitative-implications-left-main-bifurcation-stenting-and-stents)
  - SYNTAX 3D QCA: both proximal and distal angles are affected by cardiac motion. — [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/28713)
  - MITO (n = 300, 3D QCA): a diastole–systole change > 7.2° predicted target-lesion failure after LM PCI (adjusted HR 5.85). — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
  - Beton et al. 2017 (Folia Morphol) measured all three LM angles at end-diastole and end-systole. Mean LM length was 10.0 ± 4.5 mm; their angle values were not retrieved. — [Folia Morphol](https://journals.viamedica.pl/folia_morphologica/article/view/48172)
- **Chronic kidney disease:** the LM–LAD angle (in the flow convention) was 35.9 ± 11.4°. A narrower angle A was linked to more calcification. — [PLoS One 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0198566); [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)

### Inferences
- Heart rate matters mainly through which phase is reconstructed and how much motion blur there is, not as a physiological correlate. CGPS scans reconstructed at mixed phases (e.g. systolic for high heart rate) could add about ±3–7° of within-person noise but little bias in the mean.
- Hypertension, diabetes and LDL "associations" in the literature come from disease-enriched groups. They may reflect confounding by plaque, so they should not be used as validation checks.

### Gaps
- **Coronary dominance:** no study was found reporting the LM angle by dominance (left vs right vs co-dominant). The atlas does not report dominance in the text retrieved.
- **Heart size / LV mass:** no study was found that correlates the LM bifurcation angle with LV mass, cardiac volume or heart weight. A targeted search returned nothing.
- **Age:** only one weak null correlation was found, from a study that was not primarily about the LM angle. The atlas does not report an age effect in the text retrieved.
- **Hypertension, diabetes, smoking, eGFR, BP, heart rate as continuous correlates:** no adequate direct evidence was found.
- **Primary Temov & Sun data:** this is a conference-level report, so effect sizes as continuous slopes are not available.

## 3. Wider angle and plaque at the bifurcation (context only)

### Takeaway
Many small, mostly cross-sectional CCTA studies report a wider angle B in people with left coronary or LM plaque or stenosis, with differences of about 10–18°. Reverse causation (plaque or remodelling changing the measured angle) and confounding by sex and BMI are not excluded. A 2024 CAC study found no association.

### Cited findings
- **Juan 2017 (n = 313):** angle B was 75.5 ± 14.7° without LM disease vs 87.3 ± 18.8° with it (p < 0.05). — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28473705/)
- **Cui:** angle B was 68.3 ± 18.0° with LM stenosis < 50% vs 80.0 ± 19.2° with stenosis > 70%. — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **Sun 2013:** angle B was 94.3 ± 16.5° with a diseased vs 76.5 ± 15.9° with a normal left coronary artery. — [PMC3888717](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3888717/)
- **Papadopoulou:** wider angle B was found with high-risk plaque (71 ± 19° vs 55 ± 19°) and with non-calcified plaque (74 ± 20° vs 50 ± 14°) in the proximal main vessel. — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **Czaja-Ziółkowska 2022:** a wider LM trifurcation angle tracked with higher left coronary calcium score. — [Pol Heart J](https://journals.viamedica.pl/polish_heart_journal/article/download/KP.a2022.0161/67640)
- **Contrary finding (Eur J Anat, 2024):** LAD–LCx angles showed no significant association with CAC presence or severity, whereas age and male sex did. — [Eur J Anat](https://eurjanat.com/articles/assessment-of-the-left-coronary-artery-bifurcation-angle-as-a-coronary-artery-disease-risk-factor-and-its-impact-on-coronary-artery-calcification/)
- **Yakushiji (IVUS):** plaque distribution was similar at LAD/diagonal (angle B 51.5°) and LM (97.7°) bifurcations despite the very different angles. — [Scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- **SYNTAX:** distal-angle tertiles did not predict 12-month MACCE after LM PCI (82.8 / 85.4 / 81.1%, p = 0.74). — [Search summary of Girasis 2010](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/28713)
- **Haemodynamic CT cohort (n = 127 without stenosis):** Finet ratio, diameters and curvature, not explicitly the angle, independently correlated with adverse WSS metrics. — [arXiv 2401.12504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12504)

### Inferences
- In the CGPS 600 plaque baselines, the angle is expected to be wider than in the 1,200 clean baselines by roughly 5–15°. Part of this difference is expected from the higher male share in the plaque group. Only a difference that persists after adjusting for sex counts as a weak sanity check. A null result would not contradict the literature (Eur J Anat 2024).

### Gaps
- No prospective study was found in which the baseline angle on plaque-free CCTA predicts new plaque. This is the CGPS question itself, so no prior benchmark exists.

## 4. Reproducibility and sensitivity to branch-fitting length

### Takeaway
Formal ICCs for the CT LM angle were not found in retrieved sources. Interobserver differences reported for semi-automatic 3D CT methods are small (mean about 2°, SD about 6°), and the atlas pipeline is automatic after segmentation. Cardiac-phase variation (about 7° per person) is likely a larger error source than the observer. No study systematically quantifies sensitivity to fitting length.

### Cited findings
- **Casciaro et al. 2009 (Cardiovasc Eng; 64-row CT, 40 atherosclerotic + 35 healthy):** 3D cylinders were fitted to the LM, LAD and LCx. Interobserver mean difference was 1.8 ± 5.8°. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10558-009-9084-1)
- **Atlas:** analysis is automatic after semi-automatic segmentation by a single analyst. No ICC is reported. — [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- **Angiographic (2D) LM angles:** interobserver kappa was 0.553 for a "wide-angle" category; PABAK was 0.76 for a dedicated projection. Agreement is poorer in 2D. — [search summary; PMC7374237](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7374237); [UFU thesis](https://repositorio.ufu.br/handle/123456789/18107)
- **Handran:** phase-related within-person change has a mean absolute value of about 6–7° (range 2–9°). — [Handran](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/left-main-bifurcation-angle-and-changes-throughout-cardiac-cycle-quantitative-implications-left-main-bifurcation-stenting-and-stents)
- **Fitting distance:** the atlas averages over 5–10 mm, and Handran uses segments of at least 10 mm. The atlas attributes lower angles in Kawasaki and Dzavik to methodology. — [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)

### Inferences
- With a short LM (about 10 ± 5 mm) and branch curvature, direction vectors fitted over very short distances (< 3 mm) are dominated by the carina geometry and centreline noise. Fits over long distances (> 15 mm) pick up branch curvature, and LAD/LCx curving away tends to change B. Reporting B at a few fitting depths (e.g. 3, 5–10 and 15 mm) and checking rank stability (Spearman between depths) is a reasonable internal robustness check. There is no external benchmark for it.
- Medis centerlines on the 600 BL trees can serve as the inter-method comparison (tracer vs Medis) in place of an inter-observer ICC. The expected agreement is a few degrees SD if both use the same fitting depth.

### Gaps
- No CT study was found reporting an ICC for LM angle B, or quantifying B as a function of fitting distance.

## 5. Which correlates are robust enough for validation checks, and CGPS availability

### Takeaway
Use **male sex → wider angle B (about +10°)** as the primary external validation check: it is replicated, survives adjustment for height, and the `male` column is available. Use the ramus intermedius (about +14°) if it can be derived from centerlines. Treat BMI as a secondary, weak check. Do not use age, hypertension, diabetes, heart rate or dominance as validation checks: there is no adequate evidence or no expected direction.

### Cited findings and mapping to CGPS variables

| Correlate | Expected direction / size | Evidence strength | CGPS availability |
|---|---|---|---|

| Ramus intermedius / trifurcation | Wider B, about +14° (89 vs 75°) | One large normal cohort (atlas) plus trifurcation studies | Not a tabular variable. Segment `im` reads exist only for plaque. Derive from tracer/Medis centerline topology |
| BMI > 25 | Wider B (RR 2.54 for > 80°) | Single study, univariable | `BMI`, height, weight, BSA (BL, FU) |
| Height | Only a confounder adjusted for in the atlas | Weak | Height (BL, FU) |
| LDL-C | Wider B (OR 3.77 for > 80°, untreated) | Single study, disease cohort | LDL (BL, FU) |
| Plaque / stenosis present | Wider B, about 10–18°; one null CAC study | Many small cross-sectional studies; possible confounding | Visual reads `ct_cag_*`, `sis`, `sss`; CACS per vessel incl. LM |
| Age | Probably no association (r ≈ 0.01 in one study) | Thin | `scan_age` |
| Hypertension | Differed across stenosis groups only | Confounded | Clinic BP, antihypertensive meds (BL, FU); derived hypertension is FU only |
| Diabetes | No direct evidence found | None | `diabetes`, HbA1c |
| Heart rate / phase | Phase shifts B by about ±3° on average (mean abs. about 7°); heart rate affects phase choice and blur | Small studies (n = 44) | Heart rate in preparation; `hr_during_scan` only 268 BL. The reconstructed phase must come from the DICOM/NIfTI header if available |
| Dominance | No evidence found | None | `ct_cag` dominance variable (BL, FU) |
| Heart size / LV mass | No evidence found | None | Not tabular. Could be derived from CT volumes (cardiac segmentation), outside scope |
| Smoking, eGFR, SCORE2 | No direct evidence on the LM angle | None | Available |

- Sex effect and ramus effect: [Medrano-Gracia](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- BMI and sex: [Temov & Sun via scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)
- Age null: [PMC9917625](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9917625/)
- Phase: [Handran](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/left-main-bifurcation-angle-and-changes-throughout-cardiac-cycle-quantitative-implications-left-main-bifurcation-stenting-and-stents)

### Inferences
Suggested validation hierarchy for the tracer angle:
1. Is the marginal distribution plausible (mean about 70–85°, SD about 20°)?
2. Do men have about 8–12° wider angle B than women in the clean cohort?
3. Is B wider when a ramus or trifurcation is detected?
4. Is there a strong negative correlation between A and B?
5. Does the tracer agree with Medis on the shared scans?

If the sex effect is absent or reversed, suspect the extraction, for example branch labelling (LCx vs ramus swap) or fitting depth.

A misassigned ramus, labelled as LCx, would inflate or deflate B. The atlas ramus effect is larger than the sex effect, so branch-labelling errors are the main threat to grouping people correctly.

### Gaps
- No published effect of dominance or heart size exists, so a null in CGPS can be neither confirmed nor refuted against the literature.
- The CGPS population (Danish, general population, older than the atlas cohort) differs from all reference cohorts, which are referral, chest-pain or LM-PCI patients. Absolute values may shift.
