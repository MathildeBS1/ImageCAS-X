# Coronary taper and luminal diameter: normal values and known correlates (CCTA / IVUS / QCA)

Scope: human in-vivo data on (a) normal lumen diameter and taper per vessel/segment and how taper
is defined, and (b) patient variables associated with diameter/taper, mapped to the variables the
CGPS cohort actually has. Several primary full texts (Dodge 1992, Zubaid 2002, Mancini 2007,
the 2015 nitroglycerin review) were paywalled/403; where numbers come from abstracts, snippets or
secondary citations this is stated.

## Q1. Normal diameters and normal taper per vessel/segment; how taper is defined

### Takeaway
Normal proximal lumen diameters are roughly 4–5.7 mm (LM), 3–3.7 mm (proximal LAD), 2.7–2.9 mm
(proximal LCx) and 2.9–3.5 mm (proximal RCA), depending on modality and nitrate use. Taper is
reported in incompatible ways: as a proximal-to-distal percentage, as mm per 10 mm, or through
diameter–length and area–length power laws. No CCTA paper I found gives normal taper per segment
in a general population, so a plaque-free CGPS reference would be new.

### Cited Findings
**Angiographic (QCA) norms: Dodge et al. 1992, Circulation 86:232–246**
- About 83 patients with normal coronary angiograms, some of whom had LV hypertrophy or dilated cardiomyopathy. The groups included 20 normal right-dominant men and 10 patients in each of six other groups. The editorial gives the total, but I did not verify it against the paper. — [Dodge 1992 PDF (AHA), snippet](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232); [Editorial "A normal coronary artery: what size is it?"](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.CIR.86.1.331)
- With sex, dominance and branch length accounted for, normal lumen diameter at each standard anatomic point could usually be specified with a population SD of ±0.6 mm or less. — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- Proximal RCA diameter depended on dominance: 3.9±0.6 vs 2.8±0.5 mm (p<0.01). Presumably the larger value is the right-dominant group. LAD diameter was unaffected by dominance. — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- An automated secondary summary says lumen diameter was not affected by age or by vessel tortuosity. — [typeset.io summary of Dodge 1992](https://typeset.io/authors/j-t-dodge-iyddw2dlzp) (not the journal abstract; verify)

**Angiographic taper**
- Zubaid, Buller & Mancini 2002, "Normal angiographic tapering of the coronary arteries," Can J Cardiol 18:973–980. A secondary description gives n=26 with normal coronaries and a tapering index of (proximal − distal diameter)/proximal diameter. That index was about 18% (proximal LAD) and 30% (distal LAD), and about 9% (proximal RCA) and 8% (distal RCA), similar in both sexes. — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11090643_Normal_angiographic_tapering_of_the_coronary_arteries) (full text not accessed; numbers via search summary, verify)
- Mancini group, "Redefining the normal angiogram using population-derived ranges for coronary size and shape" (Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2007). It gives equations for segment-specific size and taper against sex- and segment-specific normal values, validated against IVUS. In patients an experienced interventionalist judged normal, 100% of men and women had at least one size/shape abnormality by these criteria, most often in the LM. — [Springer DOI](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10554-006-9199-z); [ctisus journal club](https://ctisus.com/learning/journalclub/252672)
- A 2026 Sudanese angiographic study defined taper as % proximal→distal reduction: LCx about 15% proximal and 35% distal, LAD about 12% proximal and 42% distal. Women had higher proximal LCx taper. — [Springer 2026 (abstract via search)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44200-026-00138-7)
- One secondary source gives average lumen diameter loss of about 0.22 mm per 10 mm of arterial length, from an angiographic/IVUS CAD context rather than healthy vessels. In Banka et al., measured over 1 cm on either side of a stenosis, about 19% of arteries tapered 0.5–0.99 mm and about 8% showed reverse taper. — [JCDR case report citing Banka](https://jcdr.net/articles/PDF/9288/22241_CE[Ra1]_F(DK)_PF1(RO_PRG_DK)_PFA(DK)_PF2(SH_DK).pdf) (secondary; weak)

**IVUS**
- Ge, Erbel et al. 1994, Br Heart J 71:572–578: 16 risk-factor-free patients with plaque-free, angiographically normal arteries. End-diastolic lumen CSA was LM 17.33 mm² (SEM 7.98), proximal LAD 13.56 mm² (SEM 5.85) and mid LAD 9.75 mm² (SEM 4.67). That is about 30% less area from proximal to mid LAD. Lumen CSA changes by about 8–10% over the cardiac cycle, with the maximum in early diastole. Among 55 patients with normal angiograms, 25 had IVUS plaque at 72 of 413 sites. — [PMC1025457](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1025457/)

**CT**
- Electron-beam CT in 11 healthy young men gave apparent lumen diameters of about 4.5 mm (LM), 3.7 mm (LAD), 2.9 mm (LCx) and 3.5 mm (RCA). Visualised lengths were about 10, 65, 45 and 58 mm. No taper was computed. — [AJR 1997](https://www.ajronline.org/doi/10.2214/ajr.169.1.9207507)
- Non-contrast 64-slice MDCT (ACCURACY substudy, n=140) measured within 3 mm of the ostium: LM 5.67±1.18 mm and RCA 4.66±1.08 mm. — [Hamirani 2013, Tex Heart Inst J, PMC3709210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709210/)
- CCTA without vs with nitrate (2006): LM 4.3±1.1 vs 4.8±0.9 mm, LAD 3.0±0.6 vs 3.5±0.5, LCx 2.7±0.6 vs 3.2±0.7, and RCA 2.9±0.9 vs 3.5±0.7 mm. — [Thieme 2006](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-2006-926755.pdf)

**Scaling laws (diameter–length, area–length, diameter–mass)**
- Seiler, Kirkeeide & Gould 1992 (Circulation 85:1987–2003) related epicardial lumen area to summed distal branch length and regional myocardial mass, with a 2/3-type power law tied to adaptive wall shear stress. Lumen area in CAD patients was about 30–50% below normal for the bed it supplied. Diffuse disease therefore shrinks the whole tree, not just focal sites. — [via EuroIntervention review](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomy-and-function-relation-in-the-coronary-tree-from-bifurcations-to-myocardial-flow-and-mass); [De Bruyne slides](https://www.escardio.org/static-file/Escardio/Education/Courses/Resources%20from%20past%20courses/Documents/103-B.%20De%20Bruyne.pdf)
- Zhou, Kassab & Molloi 1999 (Phys Med Biol 44:2929) generalised Murray's law. Their 2002 paper validated the rules in vivo and applied them to diffuse disease. The diameter–length relation is D/Dmax = (L/Lmax)^exponent, where L is cumulative crown length. I have not verified the exact exponent from the primary source. — [Patent US8903472](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8903472B2/en); [Springer reference list](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10554-008-9303-7)
- Huo/Kassab and colleagues (Radiology 2013) proposed CT length–volume scaling as a signature of normal trees, with deviations indicating diffuse CAD. In the pig model a reduced length–volume coefficient flagged positive remodeling. — [CT diffuse CAD, Wright State](https://corescholar.libraries.wright.edu/cse/309); [compensatory enlargement, pigs](https://corescholar.libraries.wright.edu/cse/308)
- Human CCTA (Choi et al. 2020; 43 patients without obstructive lesions, 638 arteries) gave these individual-adjusted exponents: proximal diameter vs myocardial mass 0.224±0.082 (theory 3/8), cumulative length vs mass 0.795±0.140 (theory 3/4), volume vs mass 1.080±0.229, diameter vs cumulative length 0.277±0.075, and volume vs length 1.322±0.175. Sex, BSA, age, hypertension and diabetes were not analysed. — [PMC7387886](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)

**How taper is computed (reported conventions)**
- Endpoint ratio or % reduction: (Dprox − Ddist)/Dprox. — [Zubaid 2002 via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11090643_Normal_angiographic_tapering_of_the_coronary_arteries); [Sudanese 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44200-026-00138-7)
- Absolute mm per 10 mm. — [JCDR case report](https://jcdr.net/articles/PDF/9288/22241_CE[Ra1]_F(DK)_PF1(RO_PRG_DK)_PFA(DK)_PF2(SH_DK).pdf)
- A continuous diameter profile along the centerline with a fitted linear (sometimes log or quadratic) model. This is taken from commercial CCTA patents, which are not peer-reviewed. — [USPTO 11315247](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11315247)
- Power law in cumulative distal length (Kassab/Choi). — [PMC7387886](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)

### Inferences
- Endpoint taper depends heavily on where the segment ends and on side branches: diameter steps down at each branch, as the Murray/Finet/Huo–Kassab bifurcation laws imply. A robust regression slope of log(area-equivalent diameter) on arc length, per segment, is less sensitive to single noisy contours than Dprox/Ddist. It also turns the taper into a fractional rate (%/cm), which removes the overall body-size scaling.
- Normal taper differs by vessel. The LAD tapers more (it gives off diagonals and septals) and the RCA tapers less over its proximal and mid course (Zubaid figures, if correct). Tracer-derived taper should therefore show LAD > LCx ≥ RCA per unit length. If that ordering does not appear, the extraction is suspect.
- From the Ge IVUS numbers, a 30% CSA loss from proximal to mid LAD is about a 16% diameter loss.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed CCTA study with per-segment normal taper in a general (non-referred) population was found. MESA and SCOT-HEART do not appear to report it, and I found no source.
- I could not retrieve Dodge 1992 Table values per segment, the Zubaid 2002 primary numbers, the Mancini 2007 equations or the exact Zhou–Kassab–Molloi exponent.

## Q2. Patient variables associated with coronary diameter and taper (direction, magnitude, availability in CGPS)

### Takeaway
The consistent correlates are male sex (larger), body size (BSA, height, weight or BMI: larger),
LV mass or hypertrophy (larger), and dominance (RCA vs LCx size). Hypertension and age show weaker
positive associations. Diabetes has no consistent reported effect on diameter in normal vessels.
The literature is split on whether body size fully explains the sex difference. Dodge 1992 and
Sheifer 2000 say no; other studies say body size dominates. Effect sizes are modest: R² < 0.1 for
LM area in one IVUS study.

### Cited Findings
**Sex and body size**
- In Dodge 1992 (QCA), women's epicardial diameters were 9% smaller (p<0.001). The difference persisted after normalising for BSA (p<0.01). — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- In an IVUS study of 257 normal LMs (Clin Cardiol 2006), BSA and sex were the two independent predictors of LM lumen area, and body size had the stronger effect. — [search summary citing the 2006 IVUS study](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/factors-determining-left-main-coronary-artery-luminal-area)
- In Sheifer et al. (Am Heart J 2000; IVUS, 50 men and 25 women), sex independently predicted LM and LAD arterial area after BSA correction. — [via search, PMC6654584](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6654584)
- In an IVUS LM study (Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2018), women had smaller lumen area. In the multivariable model, height predicted LM mean lumen area and weight predicted LM length, but every R² was < 0.1. — [search result](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/factors-determining-left-main-coronary-artery-luminal-area)
- Leung et al. 1991 (IVUS) found no significant BSA effect, as cited in Hamirani 2013. — [PMC3709210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709210/)
- In an Indian angiographic study, BSA-indexed coronary size did not differ by sex. — [ScienceDirect, Indian Heart J](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0019483216302668)
- In a CCTA study (64-slice; 50 women, 44 men without CAD; Clin Cardiol 2010), women had smaller proximal LAD (p=.01) and proximal RCA (p=.002) cross-sectional area. Distal segments and the LCx did not differ. BSA and LV mass were in the regression, but adjusted results were not in the accessible text. — [PubMed 20043337](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20043337/); [PMC6653227](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6653227/)
- In a Thai CCTA abstract (n=560 normal LMs), proximal and distal LM dimensions correlated with sex, height, weight, BMI, BSA, left dominance and CAC. — [ESC365 abstract](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/1177) (conference abstract)
- In non-contrast MDCT (n=140, referred for ICA), male sex gave an adjusted OR of 5.65 (2.78–11.50) for a larger LM diameter category. It was the only significant LM factor. For the RCA, adjusted ORs were 4.35 for male sex, 1.36 per 10 y of age (1.00–1.86), 3.13 for hypertension (1.26–7.78) and 1.60 per SD of BMI (1.16–2.22). Dyslipidaemia had no effect. The abstract wording contradicts the results ("larger in women"). — [PMC3709210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709210/)

**LV mass / hypertrophy / myocardial territory**
- In Dodge 1992, lumen diameter was larger in men with LVH (+17%, p<0.001) and dilated cardiomyopathy (+12%, p<0.001). — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- In Boudoulas et al. (J Invasive Cardiol 2017), LV mass was the only independent predictor of LM lumen area in multiple regression, ahead of BSA and sex. — [HMP Global JIC](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/factors-determining-left-main-coronary-artery-luminal-area)
- Proximal diameter scales with subtended myocardial mass with exponent about 0.22 (CCTA, n=43). — [PMC7387886](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)

**Dominance**
- In Dodge 1992, proximal RCA was 3.9±0.6 vs 2.8±0.5 mm by dominance group, and the LAD was unaffected. — [Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- In the Thai CCTA abstract, left dominance was associated with LM dimensions. — [ESC365](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/1177)

**Age, hypertension, diabetes**
- Age had no effect in Dodge 1992 according to the secondary summary ([typeset.io](https://typeset.io/authors/j-t-dodge-iyddw2dlzp)). In non-contrast MDCT the RCA OR was 1.36 per 10 y, which is borderline ([PMC3709210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709210/)).
- Hypertension gave an RCA OR of 3.13 adjusted, but was non-significant in the quartile trend, in a referred population. — [PMC3709210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709210/)
- For diabetes, I found no study showing an independent association with lumen diameter of normal coronaries. In the MDCT study diabetes was 11% prevalent and had no reported effect. — [PMC3709210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709210/)
- Larger LM and RCA diameter quartiles had more local calcium: RCA CAC ≥213 in 5% of Q1 vs 32% of Q4. This is consistent with positive remodeling and calcified plaque enlarging the outer vessel. — [PMC3709210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709210/)

**Availability in CGPS (BL/FU headers)**

| Correlate | Expected direction | CGPS variable |
|---|---|---|
| Male sex | larger diameter; sex effect partly but not fully explained by BSA | `male` (BL, FU) |
| BSA / height / weight / BMI | larger | height, weight, BMI, BSA (BL, FU) |
| LV mass / LVH | larger (+17% in LVH) | **not available as a variable**; derivable from the CT volumes (LV myocardial segmentation) |
| Dominance | right: larger RCA, smaller LCx; left: the reverse; LAD unaffected | dominance (`ct_cag_*`, BL, FU) |
| Age | null or weakly larger | `scan_age` |
| Hypertension | weakly larger (one referred-population study) | clinic BP and `med_*` antihypertensive (BL, FU); derived hypertension **FU only** |
| Diabetes | no established effect | `diabetes`, HbA1c, `med_*` diabetes (BL, FU) |
| Lipids | no effect on diameter (dyslipidaemia, one study) | total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, triglycerides |
| Local CAC | larger outer/lumen diameter in larger-CAC vessels (remodeling) | `ct_cacs_*` per vessel; ~0 in the clean cohort by construction |
| Nitroglycerin | 8–30% larger (Q3) | nitro at FU only (`scandata_nitro`); BL nitro use is not in the headers |
| Heart rate / phase | lumen CSA varies 8–10% over the cycle | heart rate during preparation and scan; `hr_during_scan` only 268 BL values |

### Inferences
- A sanity-check battery for the tracer could include: diameter (not taper) larger in men and increasing with BSA; proximal RCA larger and proximal LCx smaller in right-dominant people; LAD diameter not differing by dominance; and LAD taper exceeding RCA taper. Effect sizes should be small. Dodge's 9% sex gap on a 3 mm vessel is about 0.27 mm, below the voxel size of many CCTA scans. A weak but correctly signed correlation is therefore what to expect.
- Fractional (log-slope) taper should be nearly independent of body size, if diameter scales multiplicatively with body size along the whole vessel. A strong BSA or sex effect on fractional taper would therefore be unexpected and worth checking. I am not aware of a paper testing taper vs BSA directly.

### Gaps
- No study found reports *taper* (as opposed to diameter) versus BSA, sex, age, hypertension or diabetes on CCTA in a general population. The Sudanese QCA study reported only a sex difference in proximal LCx taper.
- Athletic training: not searched successfully, and no source found in this pass.
- Ectasia prevalence and its risk-factor associations: not covered.

## Q3. Nitroglycerin, heart rate/phase, remodeling, and measurement pitfalls

### Takeaway
Sublingual nitroglycerin increases CCTA lumen diameter by about 8–30%, with a larger relative
gain distally, so it changes taper as well as size. Because nitro is recorded only at FU in CGPS,
BL–FU diameter comparisons are confounded unless nitro use is the same at both visits. Plaque with
positive or negative remodeling distorts the local diameter profile, which is why the taper
reference has to come from plaque-free vessels. CCTA lumen measurement against IVUS is only
moderately reproducible in diseased segments.

### Cited Findings
- **Nitro magnitude:** a systematic review (Eur Radiol 2015) found that all studies showed larger diameters with nitroglycerin, ranging from 8 to 30%. The relative increase was larger in distal than in proximal segments. — [Eur Radiol 2015, via search](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00330-015-3791-3)
- In 2006 data the increase was +12% for LM, +17% for LAD, +19% for LCx and +21% for RCA. — [Thieme 2006](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-2006-926755.pdf)
- Okada et al. (J Cardiol 2014, 64-slice) reported per-segment increases of 7.5–22.3%. — [search result, Burnet listing](https://burnet.edu.au/knowledge-and-media/research-articles/the-effects-of-sublingual-nitroglycerin-administration-in-coronary-computed-tomography-angiography)
- In Decramer et al. (AJR 2008, randomised, n=42), mean RCA diameter was 2.63 mm without and 3.48 mm with nitrate. — [AuntMinnie summary](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/ct/article/15585517/sublingual-nitroglycerin-improves-coronary-cta)
- Spray given 10 min before the scan dilates more than spray given 2 min before. — [search result](https://www.ctisus.com/learning/journalclub/232131)
- **Cardiac phase:** IVUS lumen CSA changes by about 8–10% over the cycle, with the maximum in early diastole. — [Ge 1994, PMC1025457](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1025457/)
- **Remodeling:** in Glagov et al. 1987 (136 autopsy LMs), vessel area grew with plaque area (r=0.44). The lumen was preserved until plaque occupied about 40% of the IEL area, then narrowed steeply (r=−0.73). — [JCI Insight reference listing](https://insight.jci.org/references/scholar/6633/B1)
- IVUS shows both compensatory enlargement and paradoxical shrinkage (negative remodeling) in vivo. — [PMC1728600](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1728600)
- In diffuse CAD, lumen area was about 30–50% below normal for the myocardial bed (Seiler 1992). — [EuroIntervention review](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomy-and-function-relation-in-the-coronary-tree-from-bifurcations-to-myocardial-flow-and-mass)
- **CCTA vs IVUS lumen:** in 100 lesions with 5 readers, all readers underestimated lumen CSA. Correlation with IVUS minimal lumen area was r≈0.15–0.28, and inter-observer ICC was 0.44–0.73. — [PMC3915947](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3915947)
- In calcified lesions, CTCA underestimated mean lumen diameter (2.8 vs 2.9 mm IVUS). It overestimated the maximum lumen diameter in the smallest tertile and underestimated it in the largest. Intra-observer ICC for minimum lumen area was up to 0.97 for dual-source CT. — [Termedia, Adv Interv Cardiol](https://termedia.pl/Original-paper-Lumen-and-calcium-characteristics-within-calcified-coronary-lesions-Comparison-of-computed-tomography-coronary-angiography-intravascular-ultrasound,35,20448,1,1.html)
- The reconstruction kernel, slice thickness and iterative-reconstruction strength change CCTA–IVUS agreement: minimum lumen area ICC was 0.64 for the best approach. — [Univ. Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/implications-of-computed-tomography-reconstruction-algorithms-on-/)
- Window/level settings affect CT–IVUS agreement for lumen area. — [ECR 2011 poster C-0881](https://epos.myesr.org/poster/esr/ecr2011/C-0881) (poster)

### Inferences
- Taper is affected because nitro dilates distal segments relatively more: it flattens fractional taper. Any BL–FU taper change, or pooled analysis across visits, needs nitro as a covariate. A BL-only analysis is safe only if BL protocol was uniform, which cannot be checked from the headers.
- A calcified spot, or blooming in a vessel with plaque, inflates the local outer contour and can shrink the lumen. Positive remodeling creates a local bulge and negative remodeling a local narrowing. Either produces spurious taper, especially with endpoint-based estimates. This argues for (i) the plaque-free cohort as the normative reference, (ii) robust (median, Theil–Sen or Huber) slopes over the whole segment rather than endpoints, and (iii) excluding steps adjacent to branch ostia.
- CCTA diameters of about 2–3 mm span only 4–8 voxels at about 0.4–0.5 mm. Partial volume makes small distal vessels look relatively larger, which biases taper toward flat in distal segments. An area-equivalent diameter (sqrt(4A/π)) from each contour is less noise-sensitive than min/max diameter.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed reproducibility (ICC) of CCTA lumen diameter in *normal, plaque-free* segments was found. Published ICCs are for diseased lesions vs IVUS.
- No study found on how nitro changes *taper* explicitly. The proximal-vs-distal difference in % dilation is the only indirect evidence.
- No blooming-specific quantification of lumen-diameter bias was retrieved.
