**Common pipeline** ([metrics.py]($S/metrics.py)): arc-length resample to 0.25 mm, Gaussian smoothing along arc with SD sigma (mm; ends padded by point reflection), then chord resampling at Euclidean chord ell. kappa_a@ell = mean(turning angle)/ell; kappa_rms = RMS angle/ell; Q = (kappa_rms/kappa_a)^2; K = sum of turning angles; bends = turning-angle peaks above ell/15 (local radius < 15 mm) at least 5 mm apart; bend_density = bends per 100 mm; kappa_a_corr = per-vertex noise-floor subtraction, sqrt(max(theta^2 - floor^2, 0)), floor from a straight-line calibration indexed by each curve's own measured residual. TIms_area = integral over log(ell), ell in {1,2,3,5,8,13,20} mm, of L/L_chord-polygon(ell) - 1 (the earlier run's multiscale metric; **not** the math note's sliding window). Added in this pass ([ext.py]($S/ext.py)): TI(Delta) = mean over all 0.25 mm-stepped windows of arc length Delta of Delta/chord - 1 (math_foundations.md section 3 definition), Delta in {2, 5, 10, 20} mm; Kd@ell = K/L.

**ICC:** ICC(2,1) two-way random, absolute agreement, single measure ([analyze_icc.py]($S/analyze_icc.py), standard mean-squares formulas). Random perturbation families: 3 seeded replicates per vessel, original excluded. Deterministic truncation families: 2 columns (original, truncated), so absolute agreement also penalises systematic bias; ICC(3,1) is given where the difference matters. Bootstrap CIs: 1000 patient resamples, percentile.

## 1. Synthetic suite with known ground truth: bias, noise floor, monotonicity

### Takeaway
With the measured jitter (about 0.05 mm) and sigma 1 mm, TI, kappa_a, kappa_rms, K and the sliding-window TI(Delta) are all monotone in amplitude, frequency, helix pitch and bend radius, and their straight-line noise floors are small against real values. Their biases are structural, not noise: chord aliasing (kappa_a@ell collapses when the wavelength is 2 ell or less), end effects on short curves with isolated bends (+13 % to +32 % for kappa_a@5), and smoothing shrinkage (TI at sigma 2 loses 28 % to 66 % on 8 to 20 mm wavelengths). Q and the bend count fail monotonicity. TI(Delta) is intensive on stationary curves, as the math note derived, but its noise floor is **not** negligible without smoothing, contradicting the note's "white noise barely touches it for Delta >= 2 mm".

### Inferences
- The chord must be shorter than about a quarter of the shortest wavelength that should count as tortuosity; ell 5 mm is blind to periodic wiggles of wavelength 10 mm or less and underestimates 15 mm wavelengths by about 20 %. If tight coronary "corkscrew" segments matter clinically, ell 3 mm is the largest defensible chord, and then sigma must be at most about 1 mm (sigma 2 already cuts kappa_a@3 by 12 % to 28 % on lambda 10 to 20 sinusoids, [analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)).
- The math note's claim that TI(Delta) is intensive is confirmed on stationary synthetic curves. Its claim that white noise barely touches it is wrong for this sampling: the numerator (arc length inside the window) inflates with the jitter of every sample inside the window, which the note's endpoint-only derivation (about -2 sigma^2/Delta^2) did not include. At sigma 0 the floor at s 0.05 (0.013 for Delta 5) is as large as the real P100 medians at sigma 0 (0.013 to 0.023, see section 4). TI(Delta) is usable only on a smoothed curve.
- Kd = K/L is exact for isolated bends but not intensive on stationary curves, while kappa_a is the reverse. Neither is universally unbiased; on real vessels they rank patients identically (section 2), so Kd adds nothing.
- The noise-floor correction (kappa_a_corr) works on a straight line but over-corrects on curved segments at high noise, and on the real data it changes kappa_a@5 by 0.2 % at sigma 1 (median ratio 0.998 in all three vessels; 0.99 at ell 3), so it has no practical effect at the measured noise level.

### Gaps
- Synthetic jitter is i.i.d.; correlated jitter was only applied to real curves (section 2), not to synthetic curves with a known truth.
- Uniform scaling (declared exponents 0 for TI and K, -1 for kappa_a) and rotation invariance were built into the sampling model (random rigid motion per replicate) but not reported as a separate test.
- The 3D Grisan index and SCC density were tested by `tortuosity/run_merged_tests.py synthetic` on its own curves; its synthetic output is not saved to disk and was not rerun here.

## 2. Real training centerlines: ICC per metric, per vessel, per perturbation

### Takeaway
At the measured jitter every metric except the bend count is essentially perfectly reproducible (ICC >= 0.99), so the measured noise does not rank the metrics. They separate under three harder perturbations: correlated 0.5 mm jitter (the report's step 4), distal truncation, and to a lesser extent proximal start shifts. On those, TI is the most jitter-proof but the most truncation-sensitive (ICC 0.60 LAD at a 20 mm distal cut); kappa_a at ell 5 to 8 and TI(Delta) at Delta 10 to 20 are the best compromise; Q, bends and bend density are poor everywhere (ICC as low as 0.49 to 0.52 under 0.25 mm correlated jitter). The ranking of vessels is consistent: the RCA is most fragile at small chord and the LCx most fragile under truncation.

## 3. Which smoothing sigma and chord ell maximise ICC per vessel, and does the optimum differ between LAD, LCx and RCA?

### Takeaway
ICC maximisation is a degenerate criterion: under every jitter family ICC rises monotonically with sigma and with ell, so "maximise ICC" always selects the most smoothing on the grid while silently discarding signal (synthetic bias and rank change grow with sigma). A pre-specified rule of "smallest sigma whose ICC lower 95 % bound is >= 0.90" does give vessel-specific answers, but only under the harsh 0.5 mm correlated jitter, and those answers are unstable under bootstrap. At realistic noise (combo) sigma 0 to 0.5 already passes in every vessel, and under truncation sigma makes no difference. The data do **not** demand different scales per vessel at the measured noise level; they would demand larger sigma for the RCA and LCx only if extractor error turns out to be about 0.5 mm correlated over 1 mm.

### Cited Findings
- **ICC versus sigma (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4 mm)** ([ext_analysis.txt]($S/ext_analysis.txt) section 3). Under jit_pc05, kappa_a@5 goes 0.81 to 0.96 (LAD), 0.68 to 0.93 (LCx), 0.61 to 0.96 (RCA); kappa_a@3 0.56 to 0.95, 0.27 to 0.93, 0.10 to 0.95; TI(10) 0.82 to 0.96, 0.63 to 0.96, 0.42 to 0.98. Under jit_p and combo the same metrics reach 0.97 to 0.999 by sigma 2. Under trunc20, sigma barely matters: kappa_a@5 0.94 to 0.95 (LAD), 0.87 to 0.90 (LCx), 0.92 to 0.93 (RCA); TI falls slightly with sigma (LAD 0.61 to 0.54).

Every optimum is at the upper sigma edge.

- **What smoothing costs** ([ext_analysis.txt]($S/ext_analysis.txt) section 4, P100 unperturbed): Spearman against sigma 0.5 at sigma 4: kappa_a@5 0.94/0.89/0.95; kappa_a@3 0.93/0.85/0.90; TI(5) 0.89/0.77/0.81; TI 0.97/0.98/1.00. On synthetic curves, sigma 2 instead of 1 changes kappa_a@5 on low-amplitude lambda 20 sinusoids from -22 % to -31 % into -34 % to -41 % bias, and TI on the same sinusoids from -8 % to +3 % into -28 % to -31 % ([analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)).
- **Pre-specified rule applied per vessel** ([ext_analysis.txt]($S/ext_analysis.txt) section 7; rule wording from `fairness_methodology.md` section 2: smallest sigma whose ICC(2,1) lower 95 % bootstrap bound >= 0.90):
  - jit_pc05: kappa_a@5 picks sigma 3 (LAD), none up to 4 (LCx; the sigma 4 lower bound rounds to 0.90 but falls just short), 3 (RCA); kappa_a@8 picks 3/none/2; TI(10) picks 2/3/3; TI(20) picks 0.5/2/1; TI passes at sigma 0 in all vessels.
  - combo: every metric passes at sigma 0 or 0.5 in every vessel.
  - trunc20: TI fails at every sigma in every vessel (lower bounds 0.44 to 0.76); kappa_a@5 passes at sigma 0.5 for LAD only; LCx and RCA fail at every sigma.
  - Stability of the choice (share of 1000 bootstrap resamples whose point ICC first reaches 0.90 at each sigma, a proxy for the rule): under jit_pc05, kappa_a@5 LCx splits s2 0.29, s3 0.47, s4 0.19, none 0.04; LAD s2 0.71, s3 0.26; RCA s3 0.98.

### Inferences
- The label-free rule proposed in `fairness_methodology.md` needs a second, opposing criterion or it reduces to "smooth as much as the grid allows". Candidates that stay label-free: cap sigma by a synthetic bias tolerance (for example |bias| <= 10 % on the lambda 20 and bend-radius phantoms), or require rank agreement >= 0.95 with the lightly smoothed curve. With that cap (sigma <= 1 mm for ell 3 to 5, from the synthetic numbers above), the rule becomes informative.
- At the measured noise the data support **one (sigma, ell) for all three vessels**; the fairness note's "same formula, vessel-specific scale" is permitted but not required. The RCA shows the only consistent vessel effect: its small-chord metrics (ell 3, TI(5)) degrade most under i.i.d. and 1 mm-correlated jitter (it has the lowest curvature, CV 0.27, so the least signal to protect), and it prefers ell 8 under jitter. That points to ell 5 to 8 rather than 3 if one chord must serve all vessels.
- With 100 vessels per name, the vessel-specific sigma chosen by the rule under strong noise is not reproducible from one sample to the next (the LCx choice spreads over three sigma values), so a vessel-specific scale would itself be a noisy parameter. A single pre-declared scale is the more defensible choice unless step 7 shows 0.5 mm-scale correlated extractor error.
- The user's suspicion that each vessel needs its own **method** is not supported: the same metrics rank the same way in all three vessels, and the per-vessel differences are in magnitude (LCx lowest under truncation, RCA lowest under small-scale jitter), not in which metric wins.

### Gaps
- The ell grid for ext.py was {3, 5, 8}; the earlier grid {2, 3, 5, 8} was run only at sigma <= 2.
- The bias-cap criterion is proposed, not calibrated; its tolerance must be fixed before real values are looked at, which is no longer possible on P100 for this author, so it should be fixed and then checked on the remaining 460 train scans.

## 4. Truncation and length: which metrics are length-independent? Fixed windows versus whole vessel versus densities

### Takeaway
Only intensive metrics survive distal truncation, and on real vessels even they drift, because coronary tortuosity is not stationary along the vessel: cutting the distal 20 mm lowers kappa_a and TI(Delta) in the LAD and LCx by 0 % to 13 % and raises them in the RCA by 6 % to 14 %. Whole-vessel TI is extensive (Spearman with length 0.71 to 0.84, -29 % to -32 % after a 20 mm cut, -48 % to -66 % after 40 mm) and should not be compared across vessels of different length. A fixed proximal 40 mm window is immune to distal truncation by construction but moves with the start point and measures a different thing (Spearman with the whole-vessel kappa_a 0.49 in the LAD).

### Cited Findings
- **Length leakage on unperturbed P100, sigma 1** (Spearman with L) ([ext_analysis.txt]($S/ext_analysis.txt) section 5; [analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)): TI +0.73/+0.84/+0.71 (LAD/LCx/RCA); K@5 +0.52/+0.84/+0.69; TI(2 to 20) -0.00 to +0.26; kappa_a@3 to @8 +0.01 to +0.19; Kd@5 +0.13 to +0.24; TIms_area +0.09 to +0.22; Q@5 +0.19 to +0.38; bend_density@5 -0.09 to -0.02. This matches `per_vessel_empirics.md` (TI 0.70 to 0.83, kappa_a 0.04 to 0.30) on its own sample.
- **Median bias after distal truncation, sigma 1** ([ext_analysis.txt]($S/ext_analysis.txt) section 2), LAD/LCx/RCA:
  - 5 mm: TI -11/-9/-6 %; kappa_a@5 -0/-1/+2 %; TI(10) -1/-1/+3 %.
  - 10 mm: TI -21/-17/-13 %; kappa_a@5 -2/-2/+4 %; K@5 -9/-16/-6 % ([analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)).
  - 20 mm: TI -32/-31/-29 %; kappa_a@5 -6/-3/+8 %; TI(10) -9/-10/+13 %; TI(20) -13/-3/+14 %; K@5 -20/-29/-13 %.
  - 40 mm: TI -48/-58/-66 %; kappa_a@5 -13/-7/+8 %; TI(10) -28/-25/+15 %.
- **ICC(2,1) after truncation, sigma 1** ([ext_analysis.txt]($S/ext_analysis.txt) section 1): at 20 mm TI 0.60/0.81/0.83 (ICC(3,1) 0.83/0.93/0.95, so most of the loss is systematic shortening); kappa_a@8 0.96/0.90/0.91; TI(20) 0.95/0.92/0.96; kappa_a@5 0.95/0.88/0.93. At 40 mm (LCx n 79): TI 0.32/0.44/0.45; kappa_a@5 0.80/0.77/0.79; kappa_a@8 0.83/0.78/0.81; TI(20) 0.79/0.69/0.85. TI(10) minus TI ICC at trunc20 is +0.336 [+0.258, +0.436] for the LAD ([ext_analysis.txt]($S/ext_analysis.txt) section 8).
- **Proximal 40 mm window** ([extra.txt]($S/extra.txt), [analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)): trunc20 leaves win40 identical (ICC 1.000) for every vessel that keeps at least 60 mm, but win40 becomes undefined for 25 of 100 LCx after a 20 mm cut and for 5 LCx before any cut. Under prox, win40 kappa_a@5 ICC is 0.968/0.972/0.972 versus whole-vessel 0.988/0.980/0.993. Spearman between win40 and whole-vessel values on unperturbed curves: TI 0.08/0.32/0.60; kappa_a@5 0.49/0.71/0.81; Q@5 0.24/0.50/0.46.
- **Synthetic contrast** ([synth_ext.txt]($S/synth_ext.txt)): on stationary curves TI(Delta) and kappa_a@5 change by 0 % to 7 % between 40 and 130 mm, which is the intensive behaviour derived in `math_foundations.md` section 5.

### Inferences
- Truncation removes real anatomy (distal LAD and LCx are more tortuous per mm than their average; the distal RCA beyond the acute margin is straighter per mm), so no length normalisation can make a distally truncated vessel equal the full one. A 20 mm distal loss costs an intensive metric about 0.05 to 0.12 in ICC; a 40 mm loss costs about 0.15 to 0.30. Whole-vessel values are comparable across patients only if the vessel end is defined the same way in both cohorts (for CGPS predictions that is not guaranteed).
- Three options, in order of preference: (1) intensive metric on the whole vessel (kappa_a@5 to 8, TI(10 to 20)) with length reported as a covariate; (2) intensive metric between anatomical landmarks (ostium to first major side branch, and so on), which fixes both ends; (3) fixed proximal window, which removes distal truncation but introduces start-point sensitivity, excludes short LCx and discards most of the LAD. Whole-vessel TI should be reported only with length adjustment or per-vessel reference distributions, as `per_vessel_empirics.md` already concluded.
- `math_foundations.md` section 5 is confirmed on synthetic curves and on the length correlation of real TI, but its "TI(Delta) is intensive" is only approximately true on real vessels because they are not stationary; truncation bias for TI(Delta) is as large as for kappa_a.

### Gaps
- Proximal truncation beyond 2 mm, and trimming at both ends, were not tested.
- A length floor (report step 5) was not set: the trunc30/trunc40 data could fit a curve of ICC versus remaining length, but that was not done.
- The compositionality check (vessel value versus length-weighted branch mean) needs branch segmentation and was not run.

## 5. Status of the report's validation ladder, and how to run extractor agreement later

### Takeaway
Steps 1 to 5 of the ladder in `reports/Choosing a coronary tortuosity descriptor.md` are now largely executed on 100 training scans. The report's two primaries hold up on reproducibility with qualifications: TI is the most noise-robust but fails truncation and is length-confounded, and kappa_a at ell 5 passes realistic noise but not 0.5 mm correlated jitter at its default sigma 1 mm. TI(Delta) at 10 to 20 mm is a credible replacement for whole-vessel TI but is 94 % to 98 % rank-correlated with kappa_a, so it does not add a second axis. Step 7 (extractor agreement) cannot run until CAS-Net predictions and a centerline extractor exist.

### Cited Findings
- Step 1 (synthetic): done (section 1). The report's Q check is reversed: Q fails monotonicity in most settings even without noise ([analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)).
- Step 2 (noise calibration): raw turning 3.6 to 4.1 times kappa_a@5 at sigma 1 ([analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)); jitter is 0.054 to 0.062 mm equivalent i.i.d.
- Step 3 (rank stability, suggested bar 0.9): TI passes (min rho 0.99); kappa_a passes when ell is fixed (>= 0.97 across sigma 0.5 to 2) but fails for the LCx (0.86) across the full ell grid; Q (0.49 to 0.82) and bends (0.44 to 0.70) fail ([analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)).
- Step 4 (0.5 mm correlated jitter, bootstrap CIs): TI lower bound 0.96 to 0.97 at sigma 0; kappa_a@5 at sigma 1 point ICC 0.87/0.76/0.71 (fails 0.90), reaching lower bound >= 0.90 only at sigma 3 (LAD, RCA) ([ext_analysis.txt]($S/ext_analysis.txt) sections 1 and 7).
- Step 5 (truncation): distal cuts of 5 to 40 mm done (section 4); length floor, compositionality and tie-break sensitivity not done.
- Step 6 (floor, ceiling, confounds): no floor for kappa_a (0 % of P100 vessels have kappa_a_corr@5 < 0.005) and bends@5 = 0 in 0 % to 2 % ([analysis.txt]($S/analysis.txt)), against 39 % to 83 % zero bend counts in `per_vessel_empirics.md`, which used a different bend definition; length leakage in section 4; Image Quality not run (permission refused).
- The report's "smoothing sigma 1 shortens arc length by 2.1 %" and "default 5 mm chord" are consistent with the synthetic bias numbers (TI within 1 % on arcs at sigma 1; kappa_a@5 within 1 % on arcs).

### Inferences
- **How to run step 7 later** (no code written, since no predictions exist): (1) run `inference.py` for CAS-Net on the 160 test scans only; (2) extract centerlines from each binary prediction with the same method used to build the reference VTKs if it is known, otherwise a VMTK-style or skeleton-plus-graph extractor, and label LAD/LCx/RCA by nearest-reference-branch matching (the prediction has no labels); (3) for each labelled vessel, compute the frozen metric panel on reference and predicted centerlines with the same (sigma, ell); (4) report per vessel ICC(2,1) with bootstrap CI, Bland-Altman bias and limits, and the fraction of vessels whose predicted length differs by more than 10 mm (truncation is the dominant failure mode found here); (5) measure the extractor-to-extractor error spectrum directly (point-to-curve distance as a function of arc position, then its autocorrelation length), which decides whether jit_pc3-like (benign) or jit_pc05-like (harmful) perturbations are the realistic model. This spectrum is the single measurement that would settle the sigma choice in section 3.
- **Recommended frozen panel from reproducibility alone** (to be confirmed on the 460 unused train scans): kappa_a at ell 5 mm (or 8 mm if step 7 shows 1 mm-correlated error of 0.3 mm or more), sigma 1 mm, whole vessel, length as covariate; TI(Delta) at Delta 20 mm as the large-scale companion if a second value is wanted, knowing it is 0.94 to 0.97 rank-correlated with kappa_a on these vessels; whole-vessel TI reported for literature comparability only, with length adjustment. Drop Q, bend counts, bend density, Kd and kappa_a_corr on these grounds.

### Gaps
- Extractor agreement, the cardiac-phase effect and scanner effects are untested; the ICCs above are perturbation sensitivities of a single reference centerline.
- None of the thresholds used here (0.90 lower bound, 15 mm minimum remaining length) was fixed in a dated commit before the numbers were computed; they were taken from `fairness_methodology.md` and `tortuosity/run_merged_tests.py` and applied after the earlier run had already produced the ICC tables.
- All scripts live in a session scratchpad under `/tmp` and will disappear; they need to be moved into `tortuosity/` with their parameters for the numbers to meet the thesis reproducibility rule.
