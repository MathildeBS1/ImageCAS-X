Notation: centerline c(s), arc length s in [0, L], unit tangent T = c', curvature kappa = |T'|, torsion tau, chord D = |c(L) - c(0)|, TI = L/D - 1, total curvature K = integral of kappa ds, kappa_a = K/L, kappa_r = (L^-1 integral kappa^2 ds)^(1/2), bending energy E = integral kappa^2 ds = L kappa_r^2. Raw points p_i = c(s_i) + e_i with e_i iid N(0, sigma^2 I_3), spacing h. Chord or smoothing scale ell (or Gaussian width w). Coronary context from the task: h about 0.45 mm (factor 2 spread), L 20 to 130 mm, bend radii 3 to 30 mm (kappa 0.03 to 0.33 /mm), proximal radius 1 to 2 mm.

## 1. Geometric foundations: arc, chord, total curvature, bending energy and how they relate

### Takeaway
L/D has an exact closed form in terms of the variance of the tangent indicatrix, which makes TI a second-moment, low-frequency quantity and kappa_r a second-moment, high-frequency quantity of the same tangent spectrum; the two are tied by sharp inequalities (TI <= sec(K/2) - 1 and a Poincare bound), but not by any identity, so they carry partly independent information. Only TI, K and the products L kappa_a, L kappa_r (or ell kappa for a declared ell) are dimensionless; kappa_a, kappa_r and E carry 1/length and change under rescaling.

### Cited Findings
- **Exact identity.** Let Tbar = L^-1 integral T ds (mean tangent). Then D = |integral T ds| = L |Tbar|, so L/D = 1/|Tbar|. Because |T| = 1, the tangent variance V = L^-1 integral |T - Tbar|^2 ds = 1 - |Tbar|^2 exactly. Hence **L/D = (1 - V)^(-1/2)** and **TI = V/2 + 3V^2/8 + ...** [verified: t1, max relative error 1.1e-5 over 300 random 3D curves, the residual being quadrature error].
- **Small-deflection expansion, spectral form.** Expand T - Tbar in any orthonormal basis on [0, L] (Fourier modes with Neumann ends). Parseval gives V = sum over nonzero modes |T_hat_j|^2 and, since |T'| = kappa, kappa_r^2 = L^-1 integral |T'|^2 ds = sum (omega_j)^2 |T_hat_j|^2. So to first order **TI = (1/2) x zeroth moment** and **kappa_r^2 = second moment** of the tangent-indicatrix power spectrum [derivation]. For a sinusoid y = A sin(2 pi x/lambda) with small A this gives **TI = kappa_r^2 lambda^2/(8 pi^2)** and kappa_a = 8 pi A/lambda^2, kappa_a/kappa_r = 2 sqrt(2)/pi = 0.900 [verified: t1d, TI 0.00882 vs predicted 0.00862 at A = 0.3, lambda = 10; ratio 0.896 vs 0.900]. Consequence: at fixed kappa_r, TI scales with wavelength squared, so TI is dominated by long, gentle bends and kappa_r by short, tight ones.
- **Upper bound on TI from total curvature (chord-arc bound).** The tangent indicatrix is a path of spherical length K on S^2, so all of it lies within geodesic distance K/2 of its midpoint e. Then D >= integral T.e ds >= L cos(K/2), i.e. **TI <= sec(K/2) - 1 for K < pi**, about K^2/8 [verified: t1b, bound held on all 300 random curves, minimum slack 0.0077]. The bound is attained in the limit of a single sharp corner (a V of opening K); a single circular arc of angle K gives the much smaller TI = (K/2)/sin(K/2) - 1, about K^2/24 [verified: t1c]. So at fixed total turning, TI varies by a factor of about 3 between a smooth C-bend and a kink: TI and K are not interchangeable.
- **Poincare (Wirtinger) link to bending energy.** For T - Tbar with zero mean on [0, L] with free ends, integral |T - Tbar|^2 <= (L/pi)^2 integral |T'|^2, so **V <= L^2 kappa_r^2/pi^2** and **TI <= (1 - L^2 kappa_r^2/pi^2)^(-1/2) - 1, about L^2 kappa_r^2/(2 pi^2)**, with equality at first order for a single half-cosine C-bend [verified: t1b, minimum slack 0.0014; t1c shows the arc case at 0.0507 bound vs 0.0429 actual for K = 1]. There is no lower bound: a tight small-amplitude coil has large kappa_r and TI near 0 [derivation].
- **Cauchy-Schwarz.** kappa_a <= kappa_r, equality iff kappa is constant; hence K^2 <= L E [verified: t1b, kr - ka >= 0 on all curves]. The ratio kappa_r/kappa_a is a scale-free "peakiness" of curvature along the vessel (the earlier reports' Q is its square) [derivation].
- **Fenchel and its open-curve analogue.** For closed curves K >= 2 pi with equality iff planar convex (Fenchel), K >= 4 pi if knotted (Fary-Milnor) [citation only: e.g. Milnor 1950, Ann. Math. 52:248, https://doi.org/10.2307/1969467]. For an open vessel the only analogue is K >= angle(T(0), T(L)), since the indicatrix path is at least the geodesic between its endpoints [derivation]; this gives no useful lower bound on K for tortuosity.
- **Polyline turning sums are inscribed-polygon lower bounds.** Milnor defines total curvature as the supremum of the turning sums of inscribed polygons, so for noise-free vertices on the curve, sum theta_i (chords of length ell) <= K and inscribed length <= L [citation only: Milnor 1950]. Numerically on a coronary-like test curve (K = 6.14 rad, L = 80 mm) the noise-free chord estimate of kappa_a was 0.0760, 0.0749, 0.0720 /mm at ell = 0.9, 1.8, 3.15 mm against a true 0.0767 [verified: t4d]. Discretisation biases K and L downward; noise (section 2) biases them upward.
- **Discretisation bias of length.** A chord across arc length a of local curvature kappa is a(1 - kappa^2 a^2/24 + O(a^4)), so the chord polyline length at scale ell is **L_ell = L (1 - kappa_r^2 ell^2/24)** to leading order [verified: t4a, -0.00120 vs predicted -0.00122 at ell = 2 mm; the approximation degrades to -0.0164 vs -0.0195 at ell = 8 mm as expected].
- **Menger curvature.** For three points with equal chords ell and exterior turning angle theta, the circumradius curvature is **c = 2 sin(theta/2)/ell**, which agrees with theta/ell to O(theta^3) [verified: t5c, exact to 5 decimals]. Menger curvature and the chord turning angle are therefore the same estimator for coronary-scale theta (under 0.5 rad), with the same noise law.
- **Dimensions and scaling.** Under c -> gamma c: TI and K are invariant; kappa_a, kappa_r, K/D and E = integral kappa^2 ds scale as 1/gamma; E/L = kappa_r^2 and E/D scale as 1/gamma^2 [derivation]. Hart et al. tabulate a "response to scaling" property for their seven measures (t1 = s/chord - 1, t2 = tc, t3 = tsc, t4 = tc/s, t5 = tsc/s, t6 = tc/chord, t7 = tsc/chord), and report that inverse scaling was empirically preferred, "contrary to our ophthalmological intuitions", with t3 (total squared curvature) marginally best and t3, t4 closest to clinical grading [full text: Hart 1999, https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf; the scaling column itself was garbled in the PDF extraction, so the exponents above are from the derivation, not copied].
- **Reference lengths.** Dimensionless pairs available without an extra parameter: TI, K = L kappa_a, L kappa_r, and D kappa_r. Every curvature estimate is additionally indexed by its chord or smoothing scale ell, so kappa_a at 2 mm and at 4 mm are different quantities; "dimensionless" does not mean "scale-free" [derivation, consistent with the scale dependence in t4d].

### Inferences
- TI and kappa_r are the zeroth and second moments of one spectrum. That is the mathematical reason the earlier report's two-metric panel (TI, kappa_a) is not redundant: they weight different wavelength bands, and the ratio TI/(L kappa_r)^2 is a dimensionless "bend-wavelength" index (large for single long bends, small for coils).
- The sec(K/2) bound shows TI saturates relative to K only for kinks. Coronary bends are smooth, so in practice TI is near the arc value K^2/24 per isolated bend and the two metrics will be strongly but not perfectly correlated.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a published derivation of the exact identity L/D = (1 - V)^(-1/2) for vessels; it is elementary but I could not attribute it.
- Hart's scaling-response table could not be read reliably from the PDF text; the per-measure exponents should be checked against the printed table before citing Hart for them.

## 2. Estimation under noise: bias, variance, smoothing shrinkage and choosing the scale

### Takeaway
With iid isotropic jitter sigma, every estimator has a closed-form noise law: turning angle at chord ell is a Rice variable with noise scale sqrt(6) sigma/ell, so the kappa_a floor is sqrt(3 pi) sigma/ell^2, the bending-energy bias is exactly 12 sigma^2/ell^4 (additive and subtractable), and raw polyline length is inflated by 2 sigma^2/h^2. Discretisation and smoothing bias go the other way (-kappa_r^2 ell^2/24 for length, -(w^2/2) kappa_r^2 for Gaussian smoothing), so a principled scale exists where the two cancel or where the noise floor is a fixed fraction of the signal; for coronary numbers both rules land between about 1 and 5 mm, and the data themselves imply sigma is nearer 0.016 mm than 0.06 mm.

### Cited Findings
- **Chord direction noise.** A chord u = p_{i+m} - p_i of length ell carries noise e_{i+m} - e_i with covariance 2 sigma^2 I; its two components perpendicular to the chord give direction-angle variance 4 sigma^2/ell^2 in total, i.e. angle noise ~ sigma/ell [derivation].
- **Turning angle between consecutive chords (straight line).** The turning vector noise is (e_{i+1} - 2 e_i + e_{i-1})_perp/ell, variance 6 sigma^2/ell^2 per perpendicular component, so theta is Rayleigh with scale s = sqrt(6) sigma/ell: **E theta = sqrt(3 pi) sigma/ell = 3.07 sigma/ell, E theta^2 = 12 sigma^2/ell^2, median = 2.88 sigma/ell** [verified: t2a, e.g. ell = 1 mm, sigma = 0.06: E theta 0.1841 vs 0.1842, E theta^2 0.04307 vs 0.04320, median 0.1730 vs 0.1730].
- **Noise floors per unit length.** kappa_a floor = E theta/ell = **sqrt(3 pi) sigma/ell^2**; total-curvature floor over a vessel **K_noise = sqrt(3 pi) sigma L/ell^2**; bending-energy floor **E_noise = 12 sigma^2 L/ell^4**, i.e. kappa_r^2 floor 12 sigma^2/ell^4 [verified: t2b mean K within 4 % of prediction, shortfall is the two missing end angles; these match the formulas quoted without derivation in the earlier reports].
- **Variance of the K floor.** Consecutive second differences are correlated (lag-1 correlation -4/6 = -2/3, lag-2 +1/6) [derivation], so the SD of K on a straight vessel is not the iid value; empirically **SD(K_noise) = 2.0 to 2.15 x sigma sqrt(L)/ell^1.5** across L = 40 to 160 mm and ell = 1 to 4 mm [verified: t2b]. The mean floor grows like L/ell^2 but its fluctuation only like sqrt(L)/ell^1.5.
- **Signal plus noise: second moment is exactly additive.** For a turning vector with true magnitude theta_0 and isotropic 2D noise, E theta^2 = theta_0^2 + 2 s^2 exactly, so **E[kappa_r^2 estimate] = kappa_r^2(ell) + 12 sigma^2/ell^4 regardless of curvature** [verified: t2d on circles R = 5 to 30 mm, measured excess 0.04287 to 0.04309 vs 0.0432]. The first moment follows the Rice mean E theta = s sqrt(pi/2) L_{1/2}(-theta_0^2/(2 s^2)), bias about s^2/(2 theta_0) = 3 sigma^2/(kappa ell^3) per angle when theta_0 >> s [verified: t2d Monte Carlo Rice matches measured mean to 4 decimals; first-order form is poor when theta_0 is comparable to s, e.g. R = 10, ell = 1: 0.208 vs 0.205].
- **Arc-length bias of the raw polyline.** Each segment |h t + n| with n ~ N(0, 2 sigma^2 I_3) has E = h + (3 - 1)(2 sigma^2)/(2h) = h + 2 sigma^2/h, so **L_hat/L - 1 = 2 sigma^2/h^2**, and for non-uniform spacing the excess is 2 sigma^2 sum(1/h_i) [verified: t2c, 0.03560 vs 0.03556 at h = 0.45, sigma = 0.06]. D is unbiased to first order (relative bias about 2 sigma^2/D^2), so TI inherits the full length bias: TI_hat - TI = (1 + TI) 2 sigma^2/h^2 [verified: t4b, +0.0496 vs +0.0488 at h = 0.45, sigma = 0.06].
- **Opposite biases cancel at a computable chord.** Combining the two length laws, the TI error at chord ell is **(1 + TI)(2 sigma^2/ell^2 - kappa_r^2 ell^2/24)**, zero at **ell* = (48 sigma^2/kappa_r^2)^(1/4)** [verified: t4b on a coronary-like curve, kappa_r = 0.085 /mm: at sigma = 0.015 errors +0.0030, +0.0005, -0.0004, -0.0010, -0.0028 at ell = 0.45 to 2.7 mm against predictions +0.0030, +0.0004, -0.0004, -0.0012, -0.0030; ell* = 1.10 mm; at sigma = 0.06, ell* = 2.21 mm]. The SD of the TI error, 0.0006 (sigma = 0.015) and 0.0024 (sigma = 0.06), was nearly independent of ell, i.e. dominated by endpoint noise on D rather than by L [verified: t4b].
- **Gaussian smoothing, noise side.** Smoothing iid noise at spacing h with a Gaussian of width w (arc length) leaves variances **sigma^2 h/(2 sqrt(pi) w)** (position), **sigma^2 h/(4 sqrt(pi) w^3)** (first derivative) and **3 sigma^2 h/(8 sqrt(pi) w^5)** (second derivative, per component) [verified: t3a, all three within 1 % at w = 0.5, 1, 2 mm]. So curvature noise SD ~ sigma sqrt(h) w^-5/2, the smoothed analogue of sigma/ell^2 with an extra sqrt(h/w) averaging gain. Residual length inflation after smoothing is **sigma^2 h/(4 sqrt(pi) w^3)** [verified: t3b, 2.22e-4 vs 2.28e-4 at w = 1 mm].
- **Gaussian smoothing, shrinkage side.** Smoothing a circle of radius R shrinks it by the exact factor exp(-w^2 kappa^2/2); the smoothed curve sits **about kappa w^2/2 inside** the original [verified: t3c, R = 5, w = 1: 0.98026 vs 0.98020 and offset 0.0987 vs 0.1000 mm]. For a Fourier mode of wavenumber k the amplitude is multiplied by exp(-k^2 w^2/2), so TI of that mode shrinks by **exp(-k^2 w^2)** [verified: t3c, 0.908 vs 0.906 at lambda = 20, w = 1]. Coordinate smoothing is the heat flow at time w^2/2, and under curve-shortening-type flow dL/dt = -integral kappa^2 ds, giving **Delta L/L ~ -(w^2/2) kappa_r^2** at first order [verified only to first order: t3c, -0.0022 vs -0.0024 at lambda = 20; the first-order formula overshoots for short wavelengths, -0.026 vs -0.039 at lambda = 10, w = 1]. For coronary kappa_r of 0.1 to 0.26 /mm and w = 1 mm this is a 0.5 % to 3.4 % length loss, the same order as TI differences between vessels [derivation from the verified law].
- **Savitzky-Golay (local quadratic) derivative noise.** For a quadratic fit on 2M + 1 equally spaced points, Var(f'') = **180 sigma^2/(h^4 M(M+1)(2M+1)(2M-1)(2M+3))**, asymptotically 45 sigma^2 h/(2 W^5) with W = M h [verified: t3d against scipy's savgol coefficients and Monte Carlo, e.g. M = 3: 4.17e-3 vs exact 4.18e-3; the continuum form is only asymptotic, 8.1e-3 at M = 3]. A local quadratic has no O(w^2) shrinkage bias on curvature (it reproduces quadratics exactly), unlike the Gaussian; the price is a larger noise constant [derivation]. A least-squares osculating-circle fit is locally a quadratic fit and follows the same sigma^2 h/W^5 law [derivation].
- **Lewiner et al. give the same scaling as a convergence theorem.** With noise normal to the curve of SD sigma, the second-derivative estimate needs sigma <= gamma delta_1^2 (delta_1 = smallest neighbour distance) and its error variance is bounded by a term proportional to (sum l_i^4)^-1 for symmetric unit-weight windows; there is no convergence proof for torsion, and experimentally the torsion error "stabilizes" rather than converging when sigma/delta^3 is fixed [full text: Lewiner, Gomes, Lopes, Craizer 2005, Computers & Graphics 29:641, http://thomas.lewiner.org/pdfs/curvature_cg.pdf]. With l_i = i h, sum l_i^4 ~ 2 W^5/(5 h), reproducing Var ~ sigma^2 h/W^5 [derivation].
- **Taubin lambda|mu smoothing.** On a uniform chain, each pass multiplies a mode with graph-Laplacian eigenvalue k = 1 - cos(omega h) by f(k) = (1 - lambda k)(1 - mu k), with mu < -lambda < 0 and pass-band k_PB = 1/lambda + 1/mu; low frequencies are passed with gain slightly above 1 (no shrinkage, slight inflation) [verified: t3e, gain 1.00005 at omega = 0.011 and 0.888 at omega = 0.61, matching f^30 exactly]. The method and its non-shrinkage claim are from Taubin 1995 [abstract-only: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Curve-and-surface-smoothing-without-shrinkage-Taubin/849489efbf2fc88dffa62afdabdfa2e7e425efa9]. Kashyap 2022 used VMTK Taubin smoothing with pass-band 0.03 and 30 iterations and resampled to 0.01 mm [full text via fetch summary: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/]; by the laws above, resampling a smoothed curve at 0.01 mm adds no information and no noise, the effective scale is set by the pass-band [derivation].
- **What the data imply about sigma.** The earlier report measured a median raw turn of 5.9 degrees at h = 0.45 mm. If that were pure jitter, sigma = median x h/2.884 = **0.016 mm**; allowing true curvature 0.08 or 0.15 /mm lowers it to 0.015 or 0.013 mm [verified: t2e]. Jitter of sigma = 0.06 mm would instead produce a median raw turn of **22 degrees** [verified: t2e]. So the task's "median 0.07 mm from a 1 mm-smoothed curve" cannot be white jitter; it is consistent with smoothing offset kappa w^2/2 = 0.04 to 0.13 mm for kappa = 0.08 to 0.26 /mm if "1 mm" means a Gaussian width w = 1 mm [verified law: t3c; the attribution is inference], or with spatially correlated noise that does not show at lag h.
- **A spacing-robust sigma estimator.** For uneven spacing, use d2 = (p_{i+1} - p_i)/h_2 - (p_i - p_{i-1})/h_1 (annihilates straight lines), keep its component perpendicular to p_{i+1} - p_{i-1}, and divide by its per-component noise weight 1/h_1^2 + (1/h_1 + 1/h_2)^2 + 1/h_2^2; then sigma_hat^2 = median(|d2_perp|^2/weight)/(2 ln 2) [verified: t4f with spacing uniform in 0.3 to 0.6 mm: estimates 0.018, 0.030, 0.056 for true 0.015, 0.030, 0.060; the small upward bias at 0.015 is true curvature leaking in].
- **Principled scale rules.** (a) Noise-floor fraction: require sqrt(3 pi) sigma/ell^2 <= epsilon kappa_a, i.e. **ell >= (3.07 sigma/(epsilon kappa_a))^(1/2)** [derivation from verified floor]. With kappa_a = 0.08 /mm and epsilon = 0.1: ell >= 2.5 mm for sigma = 0.016 and ell >= 4.8 mm for sigma = 0.06 [arithmetic]. (b) Length-bias cancellation: ell* = (48 sigma^2/kappa_r^2)^(1/4), 1.05 mm (sigma 0.016) to 2.0 mm (sigma 0.06) at kappa_r = 0.1 [verified form: t4b]. (c) MSE-optimal Gaussian width for curvature: bias ~ (w^2/2) kappa'' against variance ~ 3 sigma^2 h/(8 sqrt(pi) w^5), giving **w_opt proportional to (sigma^2 h/kappa''^2)^(1/9)** [derivation, not verified]; the 1/9 power makes the optimum insensitive to sigma (a factor 4 in sigma moves w by 1.36). (d) Resolution ceiling: a bend of radius R and angle Phi has arc length R Phi; resolving it needs ell below about R Phi/2, which is 2.4 mm for the tightest coronary bend (R = 3 mm, 90 degrees) [derivation].
- **Brummer et al.'s "sampling rate" is not this scale.** They fit P-splines (at most 20 per vessel), then oversample the fitted spline, compute curvature and torsion by fifth-order central differences, trim the first and last 10 % of each vessel against end oscillations, and find 10 to 100 points/mm sufficient for vessels of minimum radius above 1 mm, with under 5 % change between 10x and 100x sampling for all metrics except inflection counts, which moved by up to 439 % in coronaries [full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.12316]. Because the samples are of a smooth spline, oversampling converges to the spline's own curvature; the smoothing scale is the knot spacing, not the sampling rate [derivation/inference].

### Inferences
- On the delivered centerlines (sigma likely about 0.015 mm), raw-polyline TI is biased by only about 2 sigma^2/h^2 = 0.2 %, but that bias varies by a factor of 4 across the observed spacing range, so it should still be removed by computing L at ell >= 1 mm. If sigma were 0.06 mm the raw bias would be 3.6 %, comparable to between-vessel differences.
- The white-noise laws put the floor far below the signal at ell = 2 to 5 mm; the real risk is correlated error (from segmentation boundaries and skeletonisation), which behaves like signal at those scales. The diagnostic is the lag structure: for white jitter, Var of the perpendicular second difference at lag m is 6 sigma^2 per component, independent of m, plus a signal term growing like m^4 [derivation]. A plateau across m = 1 to 4 confirms white jitter; a rise before the m^4 regime indicates correlated error.
- Rules (a) to (d) intersect at ell of about 2 to 5 mm, consistent with the earlier report's "4 to 5 mm" and with Brummer's radius criterion (reconstruction error below the minimum radius, 1 to 2 mm here). A single declared ell = 2.5 to 3 mm is defensible if sigma is confirmed near 0.015 mm; otherwise 4 to 5 mm.

### Gaps
- sigma of the actual centerlines has not been estimated with the t4f estimator on real data; the 0.016 mm figure is an inference from a median turning angle reported in an earlier report, and the meaning of "1 mm-smoothed" in the task statement is unknown to me.
- No source found that derives the Rice-law bias of discrete turning-angle curvature for vessel centerlines; the result here is derived and simulated only.
- The MSE-optimal width (c) was not checked numerically.

## 3. Scale-free and multiscale descriptors, and bias correction

### Takeaway
The cleanest multiscale object is the sliding-window arc/chord function TI(Delta) = < Delta/|c(s + Delta) - c(s)| > - 1: it is independent of vessel length, reduces to kappa_r^2 Delta^2/24 at small Delta (so its small-scale slope is the bending energy) and saturates towards global tortuosity at large Delta, and white noise barely touches it for Delta >= 2 mm. The only exact bias correction is for the second moment (subtract 12 sigma^2/ell^4); first-moment corrections are approximate and naive Richardson extrapolation fails because noise and resolution biases have different, curvature-dependent forms.

### Cited Findings
- **Tortuosity spectrum (fixed arc window).** Define TI(Delta) as above with arc length along a lightly smoothed curve. By the chord expansion, **TI(Delta) = kappa_r^2 Delta^2/24 + O(Delta^4)** and it is the mean of window-level arc/chord, hence intensive [verified: t4c, 0.00120 vs 0.00122 at Delta = 2 mm; the first 40 mm and the full 80 mm of the same curve gave 0.00703 vs 0.00709 at Delta = 5 and 0.0251 vs 0.0250 at Delta = 10]. At large Delta it departs from the Delta^2 law as bends of wavelength about Delta are integrated (0.071 vs 0.122 at Delta = 20). Noise on chord endpoints lowers TI(Delta) by about 2 sigma^2/Delta^2 (1e-4 or less for Delta >= 2 mm at sigma <= 0.06) [derivation from the verified chord law, t2c].
- **Chord-scale Richardson plot.** TI computed with chord ell, TI(ell), is the vessel analogue of the coastline plot; its small-ell behaviour is -kappa_r^2 ell^2/24 (resolution) plus +2 sigma^2/ell^2 (noise) [verified: t4a, t4b]. Fitting TI(ell) = a + b ell^2 + c ell^-2 over a range of ell recovers TI (a), kappa_r^2 (-24 b) and sigma^2 (c/2) simultaneously [derivation; the component laws are verified, the joint fit is not].
- **Curvature and tangent power spectra.** From section 1, TI and kappa_r^2 are the zeroth and second moments of the tangent spectrum S_T. White position noise adds (2 pi f)^2 sigma^2 h to S_T per perpendicular component and (2 pi f)^4 sigma^2 h to the curvature spectrum [derivation]. The spectra cross at a noise frequency f_n where signal and noise curvature spectra are equal; a Wiener-type cutoff at f_n, or octave-band energies of S_T below f_n (a wavelet decomposition of the tangent indicatrix), are scale-free in the sense that the cutoff is estimated rather than chosen [derivation]. No vessel study using this construction was found.
- **Curvature scale space.** Mokhtarian and Mackworth represent a planar curve by the zero crossings of curvature under Gaussian arc-length evolution across scales (CSS image, with renormalised and resampled variants) [abstract-only: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/34.149591]; for space curves Mokhtarian used torsion zero crossings (torsion scale space, TSS) [abstract-only: https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/1993/mokhtarian1993cvpr-multi/ and CVIU 68(1) 1997 per search snippet]. For tortuosity the useful scale-space outputs are the continuous curves K(w), TI(w), E(w) rather than zero-crossing maps, which are inflection-type features and inherit the fragility documented for inflection counts (439 % sampling swing) [inference; Brummer full text above].
- **Exact second-moment correction.** kappa_r^2_corr = <theta^2>/ell^2 - 12 sigma^2/ell^4 is unbiased for the noise-free chord-scale value [verified: t4d at sigma = 0.06: at ell = 1.8 mm naive 0.0111, corrected 0.00695, noise-free-at-ell 0.00697, true 0.0073; at ell = 0.9 mm the correction is unbiased on average (0.0079 vs 0.0072) but its SD, 0.0105, exceeds the signal]. The residual gap to the continuum value is the discretisation bias of section 1.
- **First-moment corrections are only approximate.** Subtracting the straight-line floor sqrt(3 pi) sigma/ell^2 over-corrects badly on a curved vessel (0.036 vs 0.077 true at ell = 1.8) because the Rice bias shrinks when the signal dominates; the per-angle moment correction sqrt(max(theta^2 - 12 sigma^2/ell^2, 0))/ell is closer but still biased (0.064 at ell = 1.8; 0.0698 vs noise-free 0.0720 at ell = 3.15) [verified: t4d].
- **Naive Richardson extrapolation fails.** Assuming K(ell) = K + c ell^-2 and combining ell and 2 ell gave 3.42 and 4.48 rad against a true 6.14 rad [verified: t4e]. The noise bias of |theta| is ell^-2 only in the noise-dominated regime and ell^-4 x kappa^-1 in the signal-dominated one, and the resolution bias also changes with ell [derivation].

### Inferences
- For a length-independent, multiscale TI, report TI(Delta) at two or three declared windows (for example 5, 10 and 20 mm), rather than whole-vessel L/D. This also answers the correspondence concern in the earlier report, because windows can be anchored to anatomical landmarks.
- For curvature, prefer reporting kappa_r^2 with the exact 12 sigma^2/ell^4 correction as the bias-corrected quantity, and kappa_a at a chord where its floor is under 10 % (section 2 rule a) rather than attempting a first-moment correction.

### Gaps
- No vessel-tortuosity paper using TI(Delta) sliding windows, tangent-spectrum moments or wavelet bands was found in this pass; the constructions are derived here and are untested on coronary data.
- CSS and TSS papers were seen only as abstracts.

## 4. Frames and torsion: Frenet vs Bishop, torsion conditioning, writhe, Menger and osculating circles

### Takeaway
Torsion is ill-conditioned by construction: tau = (k1 k2' - k2 k1')/kappa^2 in a Bishop frame, so its noise scales as sigma/(kappa ell^3), one power of ell and one power of 1/kappa worse than curvature. Any metric that sums torsion angles on raw points (Bullitt's SOAM as written) is dominated by noise on near-straight coronary segments; a Bishop (rotation-minimising) frame avoids the singularity for description, but does not make torsion itself better conditioned.

### Cited Findings
- **Bishop frame.** A rotation-minimising frame (T, U, V) has U', V' parallel to T, so T' = k1 U + k2 V with kappa = (k1^2 + k2^2)^(1/2); it is defined where kappa = 0, unlike Frenet [abstract-only: Bishop 1975, Amer. Math. Monthly, per search snippets such as https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.08495]. The Frenet normal rotates relative to it by the angle phi = atan2(k2, k1), and **tau = phi' = (k1 k2' - k2 k1')/kappa^2**, so the Frenet frame's accumulated rotation equals integral tau ds [verified: t5d, helix kappa 0.1, tau 0.05, s = 60 mm: 2.9999 vs 3.0000 rad].
- **Torsion noise law.** The curvature vector's direction in the normal plane has angular noise about (sigma/ell^2)/kappa, and torsion differences it over ell, so **SD(tau_hat) ~ C sigma/(kappa ell^3)** [verified: t5a, C = 4.2 to 5.8 across kappa = 0.025 to 0.1 /mm and ell = 1 to 2 mm; e.g. kappa = 0.1, ell = 1 mm, sigma = 0.02: robust SD 1.04 /mm against a true tau of 0.05]. For the coronary case sigma = 0.016, kappa = 0.1, the SD is about 0.1 /mm at ell = 2 mm and 0.0125 /mm at ell = 4 mm [arithmetic from the verified law]. The median discrete torsion was also biased at ell = 1 mm (0.064 vs 0.050) [verified: t5a].
- **Bullitt's SOAM on noise.** Bullitt et al. define IP_k (angle between consecutive chords), TP_k (angle between consecutive binormals T1 x T2 and T2 x T3), CP_k = (IP_k^2 + TP_k^2)^(1/2) and SOAM = sum CP_k/sum |P_k - P_{k-1}|, in rad/cm, on skeletons sampled at one point per voxel [full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603/]. On a straight noisy line the binormals of consecutive vertices are built from second differences with correlation -2/3, so they tend to flip: **E TP = 2.30 rad per vertex, independent of sigma** (not pi/2, which would hold for independent binormals) [verified: t5b]. The SOAM floor is then 2.3/ell rad/mm, 5.1 rad/mm at 0.45 mm spacing; on a clean planar circle of kappa = 0.1 /mm with sigma = 0.02 mm, SOAM read 4.73 rad/mm while the in-plane part alone read 0.32 rad/mm [verified: t5b].
- **Brummer's view of filtering.** Bullitt's filter skipped curvature and torsion where |r_{j+1} - 2 r_j + r_{j-1}| < 1e-6 cm; Brummer et al. show that removing torsion spikes in near-straight regions produces "significantly erroneous rotation" in reconstruction, and they report that torsion values double between raw and 10x sampling on a coronary vessel (their Fig. 3) and that torsion varies far more than curvature [full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.12316]. Their metrics include AC, AT = mean |tau|, ACCT = mean (kappa^2 + tau^2)^(1/2) and totals TC, TT, TCCT, and they note the median percent shifts with sampling are consistently signed only for the arc-length-normalised metrics (TC/L, TT/L, TCCT/L, SOA) [full text].
- **Piccinelli (VMTK) framework** describes centerline geometry by Frenet curvature and torsion and defines bifurcation reference systems on centerlines [abstract-only: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19447701/].
- **Discrete RMF.** The standard discrete construction is the double-reflection method (Wang, Juttler, Zheng, Liu 2008, ACM TOG 27(1):2) [citation only]; the first-order projection transport used in t5d agreed with integral tau to 1e-4 at ds = 1e-3 mm [verified: t5d].
- **Writhe.** Wr = (1/4 pi) double integral (T(s) x T(t)) . (c(s) - c(t))/|c(s) - c(t)|^3 ds dt; for closed ribbons Lk = Tw + Wr (Calugareanu-White), and total twist of the Frenet ribbon is integral tau ds [citation only]. For an open vessel Wr depends on how the ends are closed and on the whole-curve geometry, so it is a global chirality measure, not a local tortuosity [derivation]. Not recommended as a primary descriptor.
- **Menger and osculating-circle curvature** share the turning-angle noise law (Menger = 2 sin(theta/2)/ell, section 1, verified t5c) and the local-quadratic law (circle fit ~ SG, section 2) respectively [derivation].

### Inferences
- Any torsion-based term should be computed only on a smoothed curve at ell >= 4 mm, weighted by kappa^2 (i.e. integrate k1 k2' - k2 k1', which is well conditioned, instead of tau), or reported as a whole-vessel chirality such as total signed Frenet rotation. Integral kappa^2 tau ds = integral (k1 k2' - k2 k1') ds has no 1/kappa^2 singularity [derivation].
- SOAM must not be applied to the raw 0.45 mm points; with IP only (no TP) it reduces to kappa_a with the section 2 floor.

### Gaps
- The analytic value 2.30 rad for E TP on noise was only simulated; I did not derive the closed form for the angle between correlated 2D Gaussian vectors with rho = -2/3.
- Bishop 1975 and the double-reflection paper were not opened.

## 5. Statistics for population use: distribution shape, length dependence, length-independent constructions

### Takeaway
For a stationary random-curve model, whole-vessel TI is extensive: its mean rose 14-fold from 20 to 130 mm and it is extremely right-skewed (skew 3 to 7), while log TI is nearly symmetric. kappa_a is intensive (same mean at every length, CV falling as L^-1/2) and sliding-window TI(Delta) is intensive. TI/L is not length-independent. Population analyses should use log TI with length adjustment, or better TI(Delta) at fixed windows or between fixed anatomical landmarks, and kappa_a (log or raw) as an intensive measure.

### Cited Findings
- **Simulation model.** 3000 vessels with curvature vector driven by an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (per-component SD 0.07 /mm, correlation length 5 mm, kappa_rms = 0.099 /mm) in a Bishop frame, truncated at 20, 40, 80 and 130 mm [verified: t6]. Tangent correlation fitted to exp(-s/P) gave P = 18.0 mm against the predicted 1/(2 k_c^2 xi) = 20.4 mm for tangent diffusion on S^2, valid for s >> xi [verified: t6].
- **Length dependence.** Mean TI = 0.095, 0.286, 0.785, 1.33 at L = 20, 40, 80, 130 mm; mean kappa_a = 0.0873, 0.0873, 0.0876, 0.0874 /mm; mean TI(10 mm) = 0.0294, 0.0294, 0.0297, 0.0295; TI/L = 0.0047 /mm at L = 20 and 0.0102 /mm at L = 130 [verified: t6].
- **Why TI is extensive.** With tangent correlation C(u), <D^2> = 2 integral_0^L (L - u) C(u) du; for C = exp(-u/P) this is the Kratky-Porod result <D^2> = 2 P L - 2 P^2 (1 - e^{-L/P}) [derivation; citation only for the polymer-physics name]. For xi << L << P, 1 - D^2/L^2 ~ L/(3P) so TI ~ L/(6P), linear in L; for L >> P, L/D ~ (L/2P)^(1/2) - 1 [derivation]. In t6 the model is not in the L << P regime (P about 20 mm) and the observed <D^2> exceeded the Kratky-Porod value by 25 % at L = 130, attributable to the non-exponential tangent correlation at lags below xi [verified: t6, qualitative only].
- **Distribution shape.** Skewness of TI was 2.96, 6.39, 6.85, 3.64 across the four lengths; skewness of log TI was -0.13, 0.13, 0.26, 0.28 [verified: t6]. From section 1, TI ~ V/2 is a quadratic form in the tangent deviations, i.e. a generalised chi-square dominated by the few longest-wavelength modes (weights proportional to wavelength squared), which explains the skew [derivation]. The CV of TI stayed near 1.0 to 1.26 at all lengths, i.e. TI does not become more precise for longer vessels [verified: t6].
- **kappa_a concentrates.** CV of kappa_a was 0.237, 0.173, 0.124, 0.097 at L = 20, 40, 80, 130, following L^-1/2 (ratios 0.73, 0.72, 0.78 against 0.71, 0.71, 0.79) because it is an average over about L/xi weakly dependent windows [verified: t6]. Its noise floor (section 2) is additive and length-independent, so across-vessel comparisons at a common ell are unbiased relative to each other even if the floor is not subtracted [derivation].
- **Grisan and Hart normalisations.** Grisan et al. partition a vessel into constant-sign-curvature turns and combine per-turn evaluations [abstract-only: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18334427/]; the earlier report records the formula tau = ((n - 1)/n)(1/L_c) sum (L_si/chi_si - 1) [from the earlier report, `reports/Merged tortuosity methods for coronaries.md`]. Each per-turn arc/chord term is local, so the sum is extensive and dividing by L makes it approximately intensive, unlike TI/L [derivation]. Hart's t4, t5 (per arc length) have the compositionality property and t6, t7 (per chord) do not [full text: Hart 1999].
- **Sampling-rate confounding.** Brummer et al. suggest that accounting for vessel length "may be a viable way of controlling for covariation between vessel length, sampling rates, and tortuosity metrics" [full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.12316].

### Inferences
- Whole-vessel TI for LAD, LCx and RCA should enter population models on the log scale and with log L as a covariate, or be replaced by TI over a fixed proximal length (for example ostium to 40 mm) or between fixed landmarks (ostium to first major side branch). When the distal end is set by segmentation reach, L depends on image quality and disease, so any extensive descriptor inherits that dependence.
- kappa_a and TI(Delta) are the natural intensive quantities; the persistence length P from the tangent autocorrelation is a further intensive, dimensioned (mm) alternative that summarises long-range wandering independently of L.
- The heavy right tail of TI means a few vessels will dominate linear-scale effect sizes; rank-based or log-scale tests are the defensible default.

### Gaps
- The OU random-curve model is a stand-in. Whether real coronary TI and kappa_a follow these distribution shapes and length laws must be checked on the 560 training centerlines; no coronary paper reporting the distribution shape of TI or kappa_a was found in this pass.
- No source found that treats truncation of vessel length by segmentation reach as a confounder of tortuosity; this is inference.
