# Risk-prediction precedents: imaging markers beyond established risk scores, and incident plaque in disease-free people

Scope note. Every number below comes from the PubMed abstract of the cited paper (retrieved through NCBI E-utilities on 2026-11-07). Full texts were not opened. Numbers that would need the full text (for example Detrano's exact AUCs) are listed under Gaps. "Events" means clinical endpoints (MI, CHD death, CVD). "Plaque/CAC" means imaging endpoints. These are kept apart throughout.

## 1. Incident plaque or CAC in people without baseline disease, and its predictors

### Takeaway
Among people who start with CAC = 0, new CAC is common over a decade. In MESA about half have CAC > 0 at 10 years, and incidence is about 6.6% per year over shorter intervals. It is predicted mainly by age, sex, race/ethnicity, diabetes, hypertension, BMI and lipids. The only serial CCTA data on plaque-free people at baseline come from a referred registry (PARADIGM: 35.6% developed plaque over a median of 3.6 years). No population study found models incident CCTA plaque in a plaque-free cohort over about 10 years, or adds imaging-derived anatomy as a predictor. The CGPS serial cohort and the SCAPIS re-examination would be the first.

### Cited Findings
- MESA (population, no clinical CVD). 5,756 people had a repeat CAC scan, mean 2.4 years apart. "The incidence of newly detectable CAC averaged 6.6% per year", rising from <5%/year under age 50 to >12%/year over age 80. Predictors of incident CAC: age, male sex, white race/ethnicity, hypertension, BMI, diabetes, glucose and family history of heart attack. LDL, HDL and creatinine were related only to incident CAC, not to progression. — Kronmal et al., Circulation 2007;115:2722–30, doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.106.674143 — [PubMed 17502571](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17502571/)
- MESA CAC = 0 "warranty period". 3,116 people with baseline CAC = 0 and follow-up scans over 10 years; mean age 58, 63% women, mean 10-year CVD risk 14%. At 10 years, CAC > 0 had developed in 53%, CAC > 10 in 36% and CAC > 100 in 8%. The time to CAC > 0 was modelled with a Weibull survival model, giving a warranty period of 3–7 years depending on sex and race/ethnicity (at a 25% testing yield). Diabetes shortened the warranty period significantly; family history and smoking had small effects. 19% of 10-year coronary events occurred in people with CAC = 0 before a repeat scan at 3–5 years. — Dzaye et al., JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 2021;14:990–1002, doi:10.1016/j.jcmg.2020.06.048 — [PubMed 33129734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33129734/)
- 15-year warranty, Valenti 2015. 9,715 people had CAC imaging; 4,864 with CAC = 0 were followed for a mean of 14.6 years. The endpoint is **all-cause mortality**, not new CAC. The warranty period (<1% annual mortality) was "almost 15 years" for low- and intermediate-risk people. CAC improved discrimination beyond the Framingham Risk Score (AUC 0.71 vs 0.64) and NCEP ATP III (0.72 vs 0.64). The abstract does not describe how participants were recruited; the cohort is not a random population sample (see Gaps). — Valenti et al., JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 2015;8:900–9, doi:10.1016/j.jcmg.2015.01.025 — [PubMed 26189116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26189116/)
- PARADIGM, plaque-free at baseline (**referred, clinically indicated serial CCTA registry**, retrospective case-control). 402 patients without coronary plaque at baseline CCTA; mean age 57.6, 49.3% men. Over a median inter-scan period of 3.6 years (IQR 2.7–5.0), "newly developed coronary plaques ... were observed in 35.6%" and rapid progression (≥1.0%/year PAV) in 4.2%. Traditional risk factors (age ≥60, male sex, hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidaemia, obesity, current smoking) were not significantly associated with rapid progression. HbA1c **at follow-up** was. The predictors of new plaque itself are not given in the abstract. — Won et al., Cardiovasc Diabetol 2022;21:239, doi:10.1186/s12933-022-01656-9 — [PubMed 36371222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36371222/)
- PARADIGM design: a multinational registry of patients who had serial CCTA for clinical reasons. — Lee et al., Am Heart J 2016, doi:10.1016/j.ahj.2016.09.003 — [PubMed 27914502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27914502/) (abstract not opened; this is a design paper)
- SCAPIS baseline (population, cross-sectional). 25,182 people aged 50–64 without known CHD. Any CCTA atherosclerosis in 42.1%, stenosis ≥50% in 5.2%, any noncalcified plaque in 8.3%. Onset was delayed by about 10 years in women. Plaque was found mainly in the proximal LAD. — Bergström et al., Circulation 2021;144:916–29, doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.055340 — [PubMed 34543072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34543072/)
- SCAPIS re-examination (design paper). About 15,000 participants (50% of the original cohort) re-examined a median of 8.1 years after baseline, aged 55–75, with imaging that repeats the baseline protocols. Interim data on the first 5,000 describe risk-factor change only (hypertension 22%→37%, diabetes 4%→8%, current smoking 7.5%→3.4%). **No incident-plaque results are reported yet.** — Good et al., J Intern Med 2026;299:467–80, doi:10.1111/joim.70068 — [PubMed 41558989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41558989/)
- CGPS CCTA (population, Copenhagen). 9,533 asymptomatic people aged ≥40: 54% had no subclinical coronary atherosclerosis, 36% nonobstructive disease, 10% obstructive disease. Over a median of 3.5 years there were 71 MIs. Obstructive disease carried an adjusted RR of 9.19 for MI. The endpoint is **events**, not incident plaque. — Fuchs et al., Ann Intern Med 2023;176:433–42, doi:10.7326/M22-3027 — [PubMed 36972540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36972540/)
- Miami Heart (MiHEART, population-based, asymptomatic). The PubMed hits are cross-sectional plaque analyses (plaque volume, CAC vs plaque, Lp(a), depression, sex hormones), e.g. Ichikawa et al., JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 2026, doi:10.1016/j.jcmg.2025.08.001 — [PubMed 40920149](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40920149/). No serial or incident-plaque report was found (abstracts not opened).

### Inferences
- The closest population precedent for "who develops disease from a clean start" is incident CAC in MESA, not incident CCTA plaque. CAC misses noncalcified plaque, so the CGPS rate of incident plaque of any grade will probably be higher than an incident-CAC rate over the same interval.
- The predictors found (age, sex, diabetes, BP, lipids) overlap closely with the SCORE2 factors. This motivates using a SCORE2 base. Diabetes is excluded from SCORE2, and SCORE2's own population has no diabetes, which is worth noting.
- None of these studies tested anatomy or geometry as a predictor of incident disease. This is the gap the thesis fills.
- The PARADIGM plaque-free figure (35.6% in 3.6 years) comes from referred patients with a clinical reason for repeat CCTA. It is likely to overstate population incidence and should not be presented as a population rate.

### Gaps
- No published CGPS (Herlev–Østerbro) serial CCTA result on incident plaque was found.
- SCAPIS re-examination incident-plaque results are not yet published (design paper only, 2026).
- How Valenti's cohort was recruited (it is said to be a clinical CAC-scanning cohort, but this is not confirmed in the abstract) was not verified.
- Budoff's MESA progression papers and Lee 2018 PARADIGM were not opened. No Miami Heart serial data were found.
- Kronmal's annual rate comes from a short interval (2.4 years) and cannot be extrapolated linearly to 10 years.

## 2. Precedents for adding an imaging marker to a risk score, and how added value was measured

### Takeaway
CAC is the reference case. On **event** endpoints it has given large increments over Framingham/PCE-type models in selected intermediate-risk groups: ΔAUC up to about 0.16, NRI 0.25–0.66. When added on top of SCORE2 across large pooled cohorts the increment is much smaller (ΔC about 0.02 for CAC percentile; 0.015 for deep-learning plaque volume over SCORE2 + SIS). A ΔC of 0.00–0.02 is therefore in line with what even the strongest imaging marker gives on top of SCORE2. The methods literature supports testing the new block once (LR/Wald test of its coefficients) and estimating ΔC and calibration without testing them, and it advises against relying on NRI p-values.

### Cited Findings: CAC on traditional models (events)
- MESA, 6,722 people, median follow-up 3.8 years, 162 coronary events. CAC 101–300 vs 0: adjusted risk ×7.73; >300: ×9.67. A doubling of CAC raised the risk of a major coronary event by 15–35%. "The areas under the ROC curves ... were higher when the calcium score was added to the standard risk factors". The abstract gives no AUC values. — Detrano et al., NEJM 2008;358:1336–45, doi:10.1056/NEJMoa072100 — [PubMed 18367736](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18367736/)
- MESA, 5,878 people without diabetes, median follow-up 5.8 years, 209 CHD events. Model 1 was age, sex, tobacco, SBP, antihypertensive medication, total and HDL cholesterol and race/ethnicity (close to the SCORE2 factor set); model 2 added CAC. NRI = 0.25 (95% CI 0.16–0.34). 23% of people with events moved up to high risk and 13% of people without events moved down to low risk. — Polonsky et al., JAMA 2010;303:1610–6, doi:10.1001/jama.2010.461 — [PubMed 20424251](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20424251/)
- MESA intermediate risk (FRS 5–20%, n = 1,330), median follow-up 7.6 years, 94 CHD events. AUC rose from 0.623 (FRS + race) to 0.784 with CAC; NRI was 0.659 for CAC, 0.102 for carotid IMT, 0.024 for FMD, 0.036 for ABI, 0.079 for hs-CRP and 0.160 for family history. CAC HR 2.60 (1.94–3.50). Carotid IMT and FMD were not independently associated. — Yeboah et al., JAMA 2012;308:788–95, doi:10.1001/jama.2012.9624 — [PubMed 22910756](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22910756/)
- Heinz Nixdorf Recall (Germany, population, n = 4,129, age 45–75), 5 years, 93 coronary deaths or nonfatal MIs. AUC rose from 0.681 to 0.749 (FRS) and from 0.653 to 0.755 (ATP III). NRI in intermediate-risk people was 21.7% (10–20% definition) and 30.6% (6–20% definition). IDI was 1.52%. — Erbel et al., JACC 2010;56:1397–406, doi:10.1016/j.jacc.2010.06.030 — [PubMed 20946997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20946997/)

### Cited Findings: imaging on top of SCORE2 (events)
- Pooled UK Biobank, ARIC, MESA, EPIC-NL and HNR (n = 409,757), 16,166 CVD events over 10.0 years. Risk modifiers were added to SCORE2 with Fine–Gray subdistribution HRs. The largest discrimination gain came from CAC percentile, **ΔC = 0.0198 (95% CI 0.0115–0.0281)**, followed by hs-troponin T (0.0100). In external validation in CPRD (n = 518,015) the C-index rose from 0.737 to 0.740 when several modifiers were combined, and calibration was not harmed. — Hageman et al., Eur J Prev Cardiol 2023;30:1705–14, doi:10.1093/eurjpc/zwad187 — [PubMed 37264679](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37264679/)
- SCAPIS (population, age 50–64, n = 23,314), median 7.8 years, 287 CHD deaths or MIs. Adding deep-learning total plaque volume to SCORE2 + segment involvement score raised the C-statistic from **0.783 to 0.798 (P = 0.022)**; NRI 0.093 (0.041–0.146). Stenosis ≥50% or noncalcified-only plaque added little. Risk rose with plaque volume even when CAC = 0. **Online ahead of print (Aug 2026).** — Malmqvist et al., JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 2026, doi:10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004 — [PubMed 42663364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42663364/)
- Portugal, GENEMACOR controls (n = 1,002, 73.8% men). C rose from 0.608 for SCORE2 to 0.749 with CACS and to 0.802 with a genetic risk score as well. The continuous NRI "reclassified >70%". This is a small single-centre sample drawn from case-control controls, and the large ΔC should be read with caution. — Temtem et al., Eur J Prev Cardiol 2024;31:709–15, doi:10.1093/eurjpc/zwae005 — [PubMed 38175668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38175668/)

### Cited Findings: CCTA perivascular fat / radiomics (referred patients, events)
- CRISP-CT. Post-hoc analysis of **consecutive clinically referred** CCTA patients (Erlangen n = 1,872; Cleveland n = 2,040). RCA perivascular FAI predicted cardiac mortality (HR 2.15 derivation; 2.06 validation) after adjustment for risk factors, the Duke CAD index and high-risk plaque. The cut-off was −70.1 HU. — Oikonomou et al., Lancet 2018;392:929–39, doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31114-0 — [PubMed 30170852](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30170852/)
- Fat radiomic profile (machine-learning radiomics). In SCOT-HEART (n = 1,575, patients with stable chest pain) it improved MACE prediction beyond risk factors, CAC, stenosis and high-risk plaque, **ΔC = 0.126**. Training was on 101 cases and 101 matched controls. — Oikonomou et al., Eur Heart J 2019;40:3529–43, doi:10.1093/eurheartj/ehz592 — [PubMed 31504423](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31504423/)

### Cited Findings: methods and critique
- Pencina et al. introduced NRI and IDI as measures "in addition to the AUC". — Stat Med 2008;27:157–72, doi:10.1002/sim.2929 — [PubMed 17569110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17569110/)
- Cook: the c statistic is insensitive to new markers. "A biomarker with an odds ratio of 3 may have little effect on the c statistic", calibration is as important, and established risk factors individually have only marginal impact on c. — Circulation 2007;115:928–35, doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.106.672402 — [PubMed 17309939](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17309939/)
- Pepe, Kerr et al.: a null hypothesis of no improvement in performance is equivalent to "Y is not a risk factor given X", so testing ΔAUC/NRI is redundant. Standard ΔAUC tests are "extremely conservative". Recommendation: test only the coefficient(s), and estimate performance measures without testing them. — Stat Med 2013;32:1467–82, doi:10.1002/sim.5727 — [PubMed 23296397](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23296397/)
- Pepe et al. simulated four useless biomarkers. NRI false-positive rates were 63.0% in training data and 18.8–34.4% in test data, against about 5.0% for the likelihood-ratio test. "Use of NRI P values in scientific reporting should be halted." — J Natl Cancer Inst 2014;106:dju041, doi:10.1093/jnci/dju041 — [PubMed 24681599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24681599/)
- Kerr et al.: recommend against NRI with three or more categories. The category-free NRI "can mislead investigators by overstating the incremental value ... even in independent validation data". Tests of regression coefficients are superior to NRI for testing. The preferred single-number summary is the improvement in net benefit. — Epidemiology 2014;25:114–21, doi:10.1097/EDE.0000000000000018 — [PubMed 24240655](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24240655/)
- Vickers and Elkin: decision curve analysis (net benefit). — Med Decis Making 2006, doi:10.1177/0272989X06295361 — [PubMed 17099194](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17099194/) (abstract not opened)

### Inferences
- The design (one prespecified LR test of the geometry block; ΔC and calibration estimated, not tested) follows Pepe 2013 and Kerr 2014 directly, and Pepe 2014 justifies leaving NRI p-values out.
- The large CAC increments (ΔAUC 0.07–0.16) come from Framingham-era bases, short follow-up and often intermediate-risk subsets. On a SCORE2 base in large pooled data, CAC adds about 0.02 (Hageman). Geometry is not a marker of disease that is already present, so it should add less. ΔC 0.00–0.02 is the plausible range, and Cook's argument explains why a real HR can still give a small ΔC.
- The radiomics ΔC values (0.126) come from small case-control training samples and referred chest-pain populations with event endpoints. They are not a benchmark for a population plaque-incidence model.
- Every precedent found uses **event** endpoints. No precedent was found of an imaging marker added to SCORE2 to predict **incident plaque**.

### Gaps
- No European CAC-on-SCORE2 analyses from ROBINSCA or DANCAVAS were found in this search. Matta et al. 2026 (SCORE2 vs CAC, Eur J Intern Med, doi:10.1016/j.ejim.2026.106700, [PubMed 41506959](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41506959/)) has no abstract in PubMed, so its numbers are unknown.
- Detrano 2008's AUC values need the full text.

## 3. SCORE2: what it predicts, recalibration, and use against plaque or CAC

### Takeaway
SCORE2 predicts 10-year fatal plus nonfatal CVD in people aged 40–69 without CVD or diabetes. It is recalibrated to four European risk regions. In CGPS it slightly under-predicts (predicted/observed ratio 0.8). It correlates with prevalent CCTA plaque and CAC cross-sectionally in SCAPIS, but no study was found that uses SCORE2 to predict **incident** plaque or CAC. Applying it to a plaque outcome therefore requires recalibration or refitting.

### Cited Findings
- SCORE2 was derived on 45 cohorts (677,684 people, 30,121 CVD events). The models are sex-specific and adjusted for competing risks, with age, smoking, SBP, total cholesterol and HDL. They are recalibrated to four risk regions from CVD mortality and incidence data. External validation used 25 cohorts (1,133,181 people), with C-indices from 0.67 to 0.81. Target population: age 40–69, without previous CVD or diabetes; endpoint 10-year fatal and nonfatal CVD. — SCORE2 working group, Eur Heart J 2021;42:2439–54, doi:10.1093/eurheartj/ehab309 — [PubMed 34120177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34120177/)
- CGPS (n = 66,909, age 40–69, free of ASCVD, diabetes, CKD and statins; mean follow-up 9.2 years). SCORE2 had a predicted/observed ASCVD event ratio of **0.8**, against 1.3 for PCE, 1.3 for QRISK3 and 5.8 for SCORE1. — Mortensen et al., JAMA Cardiol 2022;7:836–43, doi:10.1001/jamacardio.2022.1876 — [PubMed 35793078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35793078/)
- SCAPIS, cross-sectional (10,628 people without diabetes). Absence of plaque despite high SCORE2 risk: 1.2%. Severe-extensive plaque despite low SCORE2 risk: 0.7%. "SCORE2-risk correlated well with presence of coronary atherosclerosis". — Johansson et al., Eur J Prev Cardiol 2025, doi:10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf424 — [PubMed 40644405](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40644405/)
- SCAPIS, cross-sectional (n = 3,087). Prevalence of CAC > 100 was 4.5% at low-moderate SCORE2 risk, 18.5% at high and 23.0% at very high risk. OR 4.8 (high vs low-moderate) and 6.4 (very high vs low-moderate). — Strömberg et al., Scand J Prim Health Care 2025;43:455–62, doi:10.1080/02813432.2025.2456948 — [PubMed 39853091](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39853091/)

### Inferences
- SCORE2 is an event model in a population without diabetes. Using it for incident plaque is an extrapolation, so both base options (recalibrated score or refitted factors) are defensible, and the pooled-model comparison is needed.
- Mortensen's 0.8 ratio shows that even for events, SCORE2 needs recalibration in CGPS.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal study was found of SCORE2 (or PCE) predicting incident plaque or CAC; MESA incident-CAC papers used individual risk factors rather than a score. No DANCAVAS SCORE2-vs-CAC paper was located.

## 4. Anatomy or geometry as an independent predictor beyond risk factors

### Takeaway
Evidence is sparse, small and mostly cross-sectional in referred or patient samples. Tortuosity tracks hypertension, female sex and age. Studies disagree on whether it goes with more or with less atherosclerosis. The one serial geometry/haemodynamics study (PREDICTION, endothelial shear stress) is lesion-level in ACS patients. No population study adding coronary geometry to a risk score for incident plaque was found.

### Cited Findings
- Danish CATCH sub-study (194 chest-pain patients; CCTA LAD tortuosity as the length/chord ratio). Tortuosity was associated with hypertension (p < 0.001), female sex (p = 0.01) and age (p = 0.045), and **not with calcium score**. — Sharfo et al., Clin Physiol Funct Imaging 2024;44:463–70, doi:10.1111/cpf.12900 — [PubMed 39210716](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39210716/)
- 83 patients with stable angina and normal or near-normal CCTA. Tortuosity was associated with hypertension and with a higher CAC score (adjusted p = 0.022). — El Tahlawi et al., Atherosclerosis 2016;246:334–7, doi:10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2016.01.029 — [PubMed 26828751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26828751/)
- Contradictory. The prevalence of stenosis was lower in tortuous than in non-tortuous segments ("protective"). Small study; the abstract gives no n. — Li et al., J Int Med Res 2018;46:5205–9, doi:10.1177/0300060518804723 — [PubMed 30304979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30304979/)
- PREDICTION (506 ACS patients after PCI; 374 with repeat IVUS at 6–10 months). Decrease in lumen area was independently predicted by large baseline plaque burden and low endothelial shear stress. This is lesion-level, in patients with existing disease, and invasive. — Stone et al., Circulation 2012;126:172–81, doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.112.096438 — [PubMed 22723305](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22723305/)
- Lumen-volume-to-myocardial-mass work (e.g. Taylor et al., JCCT 2017, doi:10.1016/j.jcct.2017.08.001, [PubMed 28789941](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28789941/)) is about FFR and physiology, not incident plaque (title only; abstract not opened).

### Inferences
- The tortuosity–hypertension link means geometry may partly proxy SBP and age, which are already in SCORE2. This is a reason to expect a small increment and to report the geometry HRs adjusted for the base.
- No precedent was found for LM bifurcation angle, taper or diameter ratios as person-level predictors of incident plaque in a population cohort.

### Gaps
- Bifurcation-angle–plaque studies were not retrieved (the PubMed query returned nothing). Coronary dominance and vessel size as outcome predictors were not found in this search. These need a targeted follow-up search if the thesis wants a sentence on them.
