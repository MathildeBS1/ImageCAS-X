## Q1. What is the most defensible way to unify 2D and 3D tortuosity, and which candidate should be chosen?

### Takeaway
Represent the centerline by its complex Bishop curvature ψ(s) = k₁(s) + i·k₂(s), the curvature vector written in a rotation-minimising (parallel-transport) frame. ψ is exactly the planar signed curvature when the curve is planar (so Grisan's constant-sign turns are the sign-constant intervals of ψ), and it is well defined in 3D without Frenet's degeneracies. The chosen descriptor is its bending energy ∫|ψ|² ds, split exactly (by Parseval and a triangle-inequality identity) into four non-negative parts: course/wiggle (wavelength above/below λc) times planar/twist (energy inside/outside the local dominant bending plane over a window W). Classical indices (RMS curvature, Grisan, arc/chord, SRVF distance to a line) turn out to be moments of the same ψ spectrum, which is the unification.

### Cited Findings
- Bishop introduced frames other than Frenet with the same Euclidean invariance, in "There is More than One Way to Frame a Curve", Amer. Math. Monthly 82(3):246 to 251 (1975) ([T&F landing page](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00029890.1975.11993807); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/There-is-More-than-One-Way-to-Frame-a-Curve-Bishop/a9e8a694899d27767df16df1c9668f167a06ddb8)).
- Rotation-minimising frames can be computed on sampled curves; the double-reflection method has fourth-order global error, against second order for the older projection and rotation methods ([Wang, Jüttler, Zheng, Liu 2008, ACM TOG](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/computation-rotation-minimizing-frames/)). This pass used the exact per-step rotation (Rodrigues) of the frame from T_{i-1} to T_i, which is the rotation method.
- On a carotid siphon, a bend detector built on the curvature vector in a parallel-transport frame (Bogunović) had CV below 5 % across segmentations, against 47 % (superior bend) for a torsion-extrema detector (Piccinelli) (as recorded in `reports/Alternative tortuosity merges for coronaries.md`, citing [Kjeldsberg 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626959/)). This is the only direct evidence that parallel-transport curvature components are more stable than Frenet torsion on vessel centerlines.
- Grisan's density is τ = ((n−1)/n)(1/L)Σ(L_i/C_i − 1) over n constant-sign-curvature turns, with a hysteresis band to suppress noise turns (`reports/Merged tortuosity methods for coronaries.md`, from the [Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657) PDF in `tortuosity/`).
- Mokhtarian's curvature and torsion scale-space images compute curvature and torsion of space curves after Gaussian convolution in arc length over a continuum of scales, and are invariant to rotation, uniform scaling and translation ([Mokhtarian 1997, CVIU](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1077314297905440); planar version [Mokhtarian and Mackworth 1992, PAMI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/34.149591)).
- The rotary-component method decomposes a 2D vector time series into clockwise and anticlockwise rotating components, with coefficients invariant under coordinate rotation ([Gonella 1972, Deep-Sea Research 19:833](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1972DSRA...19..833G/abstract)). (k₁, k₂) is such a 2D vector series in arc length, so the same algebra applies to ψ.
- In the square-root-velocity (SRV) representation the elastic metric becomes the L² metric and reparametrisation acts by isometries ([Srivastava, Klassen, Joshi, Jermyn 2011, PAMI 33(7):1415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20921581/)).
- The earlier merged method (slope-chain turning + Grisan with binormal-flip turns) failed on noise bias and arbitrary turn splitting (`reports/Merged tortuosity methods for coronaries.md`; test code `tortuosity/merged.py`, `tortuosity/run_merged_tests.py`); the follow-up found every curvature integral scores S and C bends alike and that κ_r-based ratios (Q) are non-monotone (`reports/Alternative tortuosity merges for coronaries.md`, `reports/Vessel specific tortuosity math and tests.md`).

### Inferences
- **Definition (derivation).** Let r(s), s ∈ [0, L], be arc-length parametrised with unit tangent T. A Bishop frame (T, M₁, M₂) satisfies T′ = k₁M₁ + k₂M₂, M₁′ = −k₁T, M₂′ = −k₂T. Define ψ = k₁ + i·k₂ = κ·e^{iθ}. Then |ψ| = κ, and wherever κ > 0, θ′ = τ (torsion). The frame is unique up to one constant rotation of (M₁, M₂), so ψ is unique up to one global phase e^{iφ₀}; any functional of |ψ|² or |g * ψ²| is therefore frame-independent.
- **Reduction to 2D (derivation).** For a planar curve the plane normal is parallel along the curve, so (up to φ₀) ψ is real and equals the signed planar curvature. Grisan's turns are exactly the maximal intervals where the real ψ keeps its sign. In 3D, the Frenet normal flips by π at every inflection and is undefined where κ = 0; the Bishop ψ just passes through 0 continuously. This is why Bullitt's and the earlier binormal-flip turn rules needed thresholds and the Bishop representation does not.
- **Energy and exact splits (derivation, check).** E = ∫|ψ|² ds = ∫κ² ds (Euler–Bernoulli bending energy). With an orthonormal DCT of ψ and weights |H_L(ω)|² = exp(−ω²σ_c²), |H_H|² = 1 − |H_L|², σ_c = √(ln 2)·λc/(2π), Parseval gives E = E_course + E_wiggle exactly (half-power wavelength λc). Inside a band, let g_W be a Gaussian of sd W. The local 2×2 covariance of (k₁, k₂) over the window has eigenvalues with λ₁ + λ₂ = g_W * |ψ|² and λ₁ − λ₂ = |g_W * ψ²| (ψ² encodes (k₁² − k₂², 2k₁k₂), as in a structure tensor). Define E_planar = ∫|g_W * ψ²| ds and E_twist = ∫(g_W * |ψ|² − |g_W * ψ²|) ds = ∫2λ₂ ds ≥ 0 (triangle inequality). The four parts (course/wiggle × planar/twist) sum to E; the check reproduced this to 6 significant digits (`synthetic.txt` section 8).
- **Key properties that follow (derivation).** (a) Planar curve ⇒ ψ² = |ψ|²e^{2iφ₀} ⇒ E_twist = 0 for any W and any number of inflections. (b) Helix ⇒ ψ = κe^{iτs}, g_W * e^{2iτs} = e^{2iτs}·exp(−2τ²W²) ⇒ f_twist = E_twist/E = 1 − exp(−2τ²W²). (c) ψ² is invariant to ψ → −ψ, so the planar/twist split does not see inflections; inflections move energy between bands only when they change the low-frequency content.
- **Unification of the classical indices as spectral moments of ψ (derivation, partly checked).** Let P(ω) be the power spectrum of ψ. RMS curvature κ_r² = E/L is moment 0. For one constant-curvature turn of length ℓ_t and angle θ = κℓ_t, arc/chord − 1 ≈ θ²/24 = ℓ_t·E_t/24 with E_t = κ²ℓ_t, so Grisan's sum is ≈ (1/24L)Σ_t ℓ_t E_t, bending energy weighted by turn length, a moment near −1 (check: 2 to 8 alternating 90° turns, exact 0.00221 to 0.00886 against the approximation 0.00206 to 0.00822, about 7 % low from θ²/24 at 90°; `synthetic.txt` section 9). Whole-vessel arc/chord TI ≈ V/2 with V the tangent variance (earlier report), and in the small-slope regime T − T̄ ≈ ∫ψ, so TI ≈ (1/2L)Σ P(ω)/ω², moment −2. The SRV function of an arc-length curve is T itself, so the L² SRV distance from a curve to its best-aligned straight line is √(L·V): SRVF shape distance to a line belongs to the same moment −2 family. Mean absolute curvature κ_a is a first absolute moment in space, not a quadratic form, so it has no additive orthogonal decomposition. **The trade-off is structural: only a quadratic (energy) functional splits exactly into planar/twist and course/wiggle parts, and quadratic functionals weight outliers and noise more than κ_a.**
- **Candidates compared (derivation).** (i) Bishop (k₁, k₂) split into dominant-plane and out-of-plane parts: chosen, in the windowed form above, because a single global dominant plane is meaningless over a 100 mm vessel that follows the heart. (ii) Total turning = length of the tangent indicatrix on S² (= ∫κ = L·κ_a): robust, but its "planar vs out-of-plane" analogue (indicatrix geodesic curvature = τ/κ) divides by κ and is ill-conditioned. (iii) 3D turn segmentation by sign changes of the normal-plane curvature vector: a Bishop angle jump of π marks a planar inflection, but on real curves ψ rotates continuously and any split rule needs a threshold; this is the family that already failed (binormal flip) and is not revisited. (iv) Rotary (clockwise/anticlockwise) spectra of ψ: gives an exact chirality split and ∫κ²τ ds = ∫Im(ψ̄ψ′) ds = Σ ω(P₊ − P₋) without dividing by κ, but it counts slow torsion on a planar wiggle as chiral unless bands are wider than 2τ, and chirality has no coronary rationale yet; kept as a documented option, not implemented. (v) Writhe: a double integral over the whole curve, defined for closed curves or with endpoint closure, noise-sensitive; rejected.
- **How BCS differs from the merged method.** No turns, no split points, no hysteresis threshold, no Frenet or binormal flip; a C-shape and a helix score their true energy (merged Grisan scored a C as 0); the noise bias is closed-form, additive and subtractable (next section). What it gives up: like every curvature integral it is sign-blind, so separated same-way and alternating bends score identically (check: 3 × 60° bends R 3 mm, same-way and alternating both kB 0.0782, course 0.0547, wiggle 0.0558), and C versus S differ only slightly through band placement (C: f_wiggle 0.032, S: 0.101 at equal kB 0.040 and 0.039).

### Gaps
- The Bishop, Wang, Mokhtarian, Gonella and Srivastava sources were not read in full in this pass; only their abstracts or landing pages were seen. The derivations above do not depend on anything beyond their abstract-level claims, but they should get a `paper-review` pass before thesis use.
- No source was found that applies Bishop-frame energy decompositions or rotary spectra to coronary (or any vascular) tortuosity; the method is new and unvalidated against expert grading.

## Q2. Which mathematical properties hold, and are they verified numerically?

### Takeaway
All invariances hold to 4 decimals, planar curves give exactly zero twist, the helix twist fraction matches 1 − exp(−2τ²W²), the iid and correlated noise-bias laws match Monte Carlo in the linear regime, and kB is monotone in both amplitude and frequency where κ_a at 5 mm is not. It breaks down under 0.5 mm iid jitter at 0.45 mm spacing, where arc-length parametrisation itself fails and the bias is 30 to 160 times the linear prediction.

### Cited Findings
- κ_a at ℓ 5 mm aliases (falls by 98 % at λ 10 mm) and its analytic truth turns over with amplitude; the iid noise floor for κ_a is √(3π)σ_n/ℓ² and the κ_r² bias is 12σ_n²/ℓ⁴ for chord estimators (`reports/Vessel specific tortuosity math and tests.md`).
- Bullitt's sum-of-angles falls as amplitude rises at fixed frequency (sine SOAM 0.9 → 0.7 → 0.4) (`reports/Alternative tortuosity merges for coronaries.md`, from [Bullitt 2003, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603/)).

### Inferences
All numbers from `synthetic.txt` (defaults σ0 1.25 mm, λc 20 mm, W 5 mm; curves sampled at 0.45 mm).
- **Invariance (check).** Rotation + translation, mirror, reversed direction and resampling at 0.3 mm all give kB 0.1433 and f_twist 0.037 on a 3D test curve. Scaling the curve by 2 with all scales (σ0, λc, W) scaled by 2 gives 2·kB = 0.1434: kB·ℓ_ref is dimensionless and exactly scale-covariant, so absolute values are defined only at a declared (σ0, λc, W).
- **Units and length comparability (derivation).** E is extensive (mm⁻¹); E/L is intensive and kB = √(E/L) has units mm⁻¹, the same as κ_a. Additivity: E over a whole curve equals the sum over a partition up to the smoothing boundary effect (check: halves sum 0.56 % below whole on a 100 mm curve).
- **Line, C, S, helix (check).** Straight line: kB 0.0000 (the fractions are 0/0 and returned NaN; a zero-energy guard is needed before cohort use on straight segments). Helices r 4 to 20 mm: kB within 1 % of the analytic κ, f_twist 0.510/0.088/0.169/0.019 against predicted 0.524/0.088/0.172/0.019; the tight helix r 2, pitch 10 gives 0.877 against 0.949 (end effects over three turns).
- **Monotonicity (check).** Planar sine λ 20 mm, A 0.5 → 12 mm: kB 0.032 → 0.215, monotone, tracking the dense RMS truth 0.035 → 0.241. κ_a@5 over the same sweep rises to 0.145 at A 6 and falls to 0.109 at A 12, because mean |κ| itself turns over (dense truth 0.136 → 0.098). Frequency, A 2 mm, λ 60 → 6 mm: kB 0.015 → 0.592, monotone; κ_a@5 gives 0.000 at λ 10 and 0.065 at λ 6 (aliasing). The energy form is the only one of the three (κ_a, TI, kB) monotone in both sweeps; its attenuation is the known Gaussian factor (e.g. 0.87 of truth at λ 10).
- **Plane hops (check).** Two 90° bends of radius 10 mm in perpendicular planes, 20 mm apart: f_twist 0.011, against 0.000 when coplanar. Twist is local non-planarity at scale W; plane changes between bends further apart than about 2W are not counted.
- **Noise bias, linear regime (derivation, check).** For iid jitter σ_n at raw spacing h, the two normal components give E/L bias b_iid = 3σ_n²h/(4√π σ0⁵). For jitter of per-coordinate SD σ_j that is white noise Gaussian-filtered with sd c along arc, b_corr = 3σ_j²c/(2(c² + σ0²)^{5/2}). Monte Carlo on a 100 mm line, 10 reps: σ_n 0.06 mm gives 7.09e−4 vs 6.85e−4 (σ0 1) and 2.55e−4 vs 2.25e−4 (σ0 1.25); correlated 0.25 mm/1 mm gives 1.68e−2 vs 1.66e−2 (σ0 1); correlated 0.5 mm/1 mm is 1.0 to 1.6 × the prediction for σ0 1 to 2 and 2.2 × at σ0 3. Both biases are additive and depend only on (σ_n or σ_j, c, σ0), so they can be subtracted once the error spectrum is known.
- **Noise bias, nonlinear regime (check).** iid 0.25 and 0.5 mm at 0.45 mm spacing give 8 to 160 times the linear prediction (e.g. σ0 1.25: 0.97 vs 0.016 mm⁻² for 0.5 mm). When jitter exceeds about half the point spacing the polyline doubles back, arc length inflates, and Gaussian smoothing "in noisy arc length" covers much less true length. This affects every arc-length-smoothed metric but hits a second moment hardest.

### Gaps
- No closed-form bias law was derived for the nonlinear (jitter ≥ spacing) regime.
- The TI ≈ moment −2 identity could not be checked numerically on finite curves: on a 100 mm sine (A 2, λ 20) the spectral sum was 0.214 against TI 0.080, from DCT leakage into the lowest frequencies, where 1/ω² amplifies it. That instability is itself the spectral reason TI is length- and truncation-sensitive, but this is an inference, not a demonstrated result.

## Q3. How should σ0, λc and W be chosen from first principles?

### Takeaway
σ0 is bracketed by two closed-form constraints: noise bias ≤ 5 % of signal energy at the measured iid noise (σ0 ≥ about 1.2 mm), and ≥ 70 % curvature retention for the tightest meaningful bend (σ0 ≤ about 1.3 mm). This gave the pre-declared σ0 = 1.25 mm. Under 0.5 mm error correlated over 1 mm, however, the two constraints cannot both be met at any σ0; that is the same wall the earlier reports hit with κ_a. λc and W have no anatomical anchor and are conventions (20 mm and 5 mm); rankings are insensitive to them but levels are not.

### Cited Findings
- Measured centerline residual: median distance to a 1 mm-smoothed curve 0.054 to 0.062 mm, used as σ_n ≈ 0.06 mm; the four scale rules of the earlier report (noise floor, length-bias cancellation, MSE-optimal smoothing, resolving a R 3 mm 90° bend) land between 2 and 5 mm of chord (`reports/Vessel specific tortuosity math and tests.md`).
- Coronary lumen radius about 1.5 to 2 mm (task brief). A tube of radius a can only have a centerline with κ < 1/a without self-intersection (standard tube geometry; derivation).

### Inferences
- **Lower bound (derivation).** Require b_iid ≤ 0.05·κ_r² with σ_n 0.06 mm, h 0.45 mm and a typical κ_r of 0.08 mm⁻¹: σ0 ≥ (3σ_n²h / (4√π · 0.05 · κ_r²))^{1/5} ≈ 1.16 mm. In the cohort the realised bias at σ0 1.25 under 0.06 mm jitter was +3 to +5 % on kB (+4 to +9 % on kB_wiggle), consistent with this (`analysis_train150.txt`, section C).
- **Upper bound (derivation).** Gaussian smoothing multiplies curvature at wavelength λ by exp(−(2πσ0/λ)²/2). A 90° bend of radius 3 mm spans 4.7 mm of arc, i.e. about half of a λ ≈ 9.4 mm oscillation; 70 % retention there needs σ0 ≤ 1.27 mm. The lumen-radius argument points the same way: structure shorter than about one radius (1.5 to 2 mm) is not centerline geometry, so σ0 should not exceed a.
- **The correlated-error wall (derivation).** With σ_j 0.5 mm and c 1 mm, b_corr is 0.036 mm⁻² at σ0 1.25, 0.0067 at 2 and 0.0012 at 3, against a median signal E/L of about 0.006 to 0.011 mm⁻² (kB 0.076 to 0.107 at σ0 1.25). Bias below 20 % of signal needs σ0 ≳ 3 mm, which retains only 13 % of the R 3 mm bend. If extractor error really is of this size, bends tighter than about 5 mm radius are unmeasurable by any curvature method; that is a data limit, not a method limit.
- **λc (convention).** 20 mm half-power wavelength separates bends whose half-wavelength is under 10 mm (a few vessel diameters) from the heart-following course. It matches the 20 mm windows used earlier. Rankings are stable: Spearman of kB_wiggle, kB_course and f_twist at λc 15 or 30 mm vs 20 mm is 0.95 to 1.00 on all three vessels, but levels move (kB_course median LAD 0.072/0.062/0.048 at 15/20/30 mm) (`analysis_train150.txt`, section E).
- **W (convention).** W 5 mm (FWHM ≈ 12 mm, about one bend). f_twist ranking vs W 2.5 or 10 mm: Spearman 0.90 to 0.97, but the median shifts from 0.22 to 0.23 (W 2.5) through 0.32 to 0.35 (W 5) to 0.39 to 0.45 (W 10). f_twist is a scale-indexed quantity and must always be reported with W.

### Gaps
- The extractor-to-extractor error spectrum (σ_j, c) on these data is unknown. It decides σ0, and the whole choice should be revisited once CAS-Net centerlines exist (step 7 of the earlier ladder).
- No anatomical source was found that fixes λc (heart-surface curvature scale) or W.

## Q4. How does BCS behave on ImageCAS-X training centerlines?

### Takeaway
On 150 training scans the magnitude kB is almost the same variable as κ_a@5 (Spearman 0.85 to 0.97). It is less robust than κ_a to 0.5 mm jitter at matched scale, and equally truncation-robust (ICC 0.79 to 0.97). The shape fractions are genuinely new: f_twist and f_wiggle have rank R² of only 0.04 to 0.18 on κ_a plus length. They are reproducible under the measured noise and truncation, but they collapse under 0.5 mm jitter.

### Cited Findings
Baseline earlier numbers: κ_a@5 (σ 1) ICC under 0.5 mm/1 mm correlated jitter 0.87/0.76/0.71 and after a 20 mm distal cut 0.95/0.88/0.93 on P100; κ_a@8 0.87/0.86/0.92 under correlated jitter; TI ρ with length 0.70 to 0.83 (`reports/Vessel specific tortuosity math and tests.md`). All numbers below are checks from `analysis_train150.txt`; cells are LAD/LCx/RCA; ICC is ICC(2,1) absolute agreement across the 3 jitter replicates (orig excluded, as in the earlier P100 analysis), or (orig, truncated) for truncation, with 1000-resample bootstrap 95 % CIs in the file.

## Q5. Verdict: is BCS a clear improvement worth a week, or equivalent to 5 mm mean |κ|?

### Takeaway
Not as a replacement. The BCS magnitude is κ_a in different clothes (ρ 0.85 to 0.97, rank R² 0.83 to 0.89). It is no more truncation-robust, less robust to 0.5 mm iid jitter, and only as robust as κ_a at the equivalent scale under correlated jitter. Its real contributions are theoretical (one representation from which every classical index is a moment, and exact planar/twist and course/wiggle splits) and two new, near-orthogonal shape axes (f_twist, f_wiggle). Those axes are only reproducible at the measured noise level. Worth one to two days to adopt as an exploratory secondary and as a thesis theory section, not a week as a primary.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Keep κ_a@5 (σ 1 mm) as the primary.** BCS's synthetic advantages (monotone in amplitude where κ_a turns over, no aliasing at λ ≤ 2ℓ) are real but concern regimes (A/λ ≥ 0.3, λ ≤ 10 mm) where the cohort signal is small and noise-dominated.
- **What is worth keeping (≈ 1 to 2 days).** (1) The ψ-spectrum section as thesis theory: it explains, in one derivation, why TI is length- and truncation-confounded (moment −2, long wavelengths), why κ_a and κ_r disagree, why Grisan ≈ turn-length-weighted energy, and why curvature integrals cannot see S versus C. (2) f_wiggle@2 (the share of bending energy at wavelengths < 20 mm) and f_twist@2 or @3 (the share outside the local bending plane, W 5 mm), as pre-declared exploratory secondaries. They are nearly orthogonal to κ_a and length, truncation ICC 0.63 to 0.95, measured-noise ICC ≥ 0.96. They must be gated on step 7 (extractor agreement): under 0.5 mm correlated error they fall to 0.15 to 0.60, and they would then be unusable.
- **What not to spend time on.** Debiasing kB (the realistic iid bias is 2 to 5 %), torsion or chirality channels, writhe, and SRVF distances (moment −2, so the TI family and its length confound).
- **Risk statement for the thesis.** Every BCS output is defined at a declared (σ0, λc, W) = (1.25 or 2, 20, 5) mm. f_twist levels move by a factor of about 2 across W 2.5 to 10 mm while rankings hold (ρ ≥ 0.90). Numbers from different scale choices are not comparable.

### Gaps
- Whether f_twist or f_wiggle means anything clinically (plaque, expert-perceived tortuosity) is untested. The Disease column was deliberately not opened, and no expert ranking exists.
- Whether realistic extractor error is closer to the 0.06 mm iid regime (where f_twist is excellent) or the 0.5 mm/1 mm correlated regime (where it fails) is the deciding unknown, as it already was for κ_a's scale.
