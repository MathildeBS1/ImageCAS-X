# Variance partition (eq:method-validity-split) and prespecified decision rules — statistical audit

Section audited: `thesis/methodology/10b_feature_validity.tex`, opening paragraphs (intended profile, lines 35-39), `sec:method-validity-reliability` (lines 61-72), `sec:method-validity-split` (lines 228-242), `sec:method-validity-reading` (lines 244-254).

Notation used below: W = observed feature, X = reproducible ("true") feature, U = measurement error, W = X + U. Z = known determinants (sex, age, BSA, BP, LDL, smoking, family history, dominance). Standardise var(W) = 1. Reliability λ = var(X)/var(W). R²_obs = R² of W on Z; R²_true = R² of X on Z.

## Key Question 1: Is "ICC − R²" the reproducible unexplained share, and is "requires R² ≤ ICC" correct?

### Takeaway
Under classical test theory with error independent of Z and of X, the partition is algebraically correct: R²_obs = λ·R²_true, so ICC − R² = λ(1 − R²_true) is the reproducible variance not explained by Z. The condition R² ≤ ICC is then automatically true in the population (it is a consequence of the model, not an extra requirement), and a sample violation signals sampling noise, error correlated with Z, or an ICC that underestimates reliability (e.g. true change over 10 years). The quantity that actually governs what the LR test can detect is the conditional reliability (ICC − R²)/(1 − R²), not the middle term itself.

### Cited Findings
- The reliability (attenuation) ratio is λ = σ²_x/(σ²_x + σ²_u) and it gives the bias of the slope in linear regression under classical error — [Stata/Carroll measurement-error course slides](https://www.stata.com/merror/call.pdf); textbook source: Carroll, Ruppert, Stefanski & Crainiceanu (2006) *Measurement Error in Nonlinear Models*, 2nd ed., Chapman & Hall/CRC, ISBN 9781584886334 — [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Measurement-Error-in-Nonlinear-Models-A-Modern-Perspective-Second-Edition/Carroll-Ruppert-Stefanski-Crainiceanu/p/book/9781584886334).
- The same three-way partition is established in common factor analysis: total variance = communality (common) + specific + error; when a reliability is available, unique variance can be split into specific and error. Worked example: reliability 0.80, communality 0.64 → specific 0.16, error 0.20 (i.e. specific = reliability − communality) — [Friendly, EFA/CFA handout](https://www.datavis.ca/courses/factor/efacfa-handout2-2x2.pdf).
- "Theoretically, the communality of a variable should not exceed its reliability. Violation of this condition is called a quasi-Heywood case" — [SAS PROC FACTOR documentation](https://support.sas.com/documentation/cdl/en/statug/63962/HTML/default/statug_factor_sect022.htm). This is exactly the thesis's "R² ≤ ICC" condition, with R² in the role of communality.
- Disattenuated (Spearman-corrected) quantities can fall outside their admissible range because of sampling error in the reliability estimate (Heywood-type results), and a corrected value near the bound can be "equally well explained by sampling variability" — [arXiv 1911.01576, Correcting for attenuation due to measurement error](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01576); [Rasch Measurement Transactions, Disattenuating correlation coefficients](https://www.rasch.org/rmt/rmt101g.htm).
- Spearman (1904) proposed correcting an observed correlation by the reliabilities of the measures; *Am J Psychol* 15(1):72–101 — [Revelle reprint](https://www.personality-project.org/revelle/publications/spearman.pdf).

### Derivation (own, from the cited CTT/attenuation results)
Assume (A1) W = X + U, E[U]=0; (A2) U independent of X and of Z (in particular, error variance the same at every value of Z — homoscedastic); (A3) Z measured without error; (A4) the ICC estimates λ for the same population and the same error source that affects the baseline measurement.
- Linear projection of W on Z equals that of X on Z (U is orthogonal to Z), so explained variance is the same: var(Ŵ) = λ·R²_true. Hence R²_obs = λ·R²_true ≤ λ.
- var(X | Z) = λ − R²_obs = λ(1 − R²_true): reproducible, unexplained. var(U) = 1 − λ. So eq. 1 holds term by term.
- var(W | Z) = 1 − R²_obs. Reliability of the residual (conditional reliability) = λ_{X|Z} = (λ − R²_obs)/(1 − R²_obs). This is the attenuation factor of the geometry coefficient in a model that also contains Z (Carroll et al. 2006, chapter on linear regression and attenuation, multiple-covariate case: the attenuation is worse when the mismeasured covariate is correlated with the error-free covariates). Example: ICC = 0.80, R² = 0.40 gives middle term 0.40 but conditional reliability 0.40/0.60 = 0.67; ICC = 0.80, R² = 0.70 gives 0.10 and 0.33.
- When the conditions fail:
  - Error correlated with Z (e.g. narrower vessels in women and with small BSA → noisier centrelines → more apparent curvature, as the section itself argues at lines 201–202): part of R²_obs is error variance, so the "known determinants" term contains error, the middle term is underestimated, and R² > ICC becomes possible even in the population.
  - Heteroscedastic error (larger in women/small vessels): the ICC is a population-average λ; the partition holds only on average and conditional reliability differs by subgroup.
  - Reproducible artefacts (shared image artefacts, tracer biases repeated at both scans) count as "X" — the section already says so (line 241). Correct.
  - Error in Z (e.g. single BP reading, BSA): R²_obs is itself attenuated, inflating the middle term.
  - In-sample R² is optimistic with ~8–10 predictors; adjusted R² (or cross-validated R²) should be used, and both R² and ICC carry sampling error, so "R² < ICC" on point estimates can fail or pass by chance.

### Inferences
- The sentence "It requires R² ≤ ICC, which holds when the known determinants explain only reproducible variance" (line 240) is substantively right but should be framed as: "Under the assumption that the measurement error is independent of the determinants, R² cannot exceed the reliability; an estimate of R² above the ICC would indicate sampling error, error that depends on the determinants, or an ICC that understates reliability." Severity: low–moderate.
- "This partition is introduced in this thesis and is not an established measurement property" (line 239) understates prior art: the identity is the classical-test-theory/factor-analysis partition (common + specific + error, with specific = reliability − communality). Suggested wording: "The partition applies the classical decomposition of variance into common, specific and error variance [Harman/factor analysis; Spearman 1904; Carroll et al. 2006] with R² in place of the communality; it is used here descriptively and is not an established COSMIN measurement property." Severity: moderate (citation/novelty claim).
- Recommend reporting, in addition or instead, the conditional reliability (ICC − R²)/(1 − R²), because that is what attenuates the geometry coefficient and the LR test conditional on the base model.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the exact Carroll et al. (2006) section/page for the multiple-covariate attenuation formula; the formula above is derived directly and should be checked against the book (chapter 3, "Linear regression and attenuation") before citing a page.
- No source found that uses exactly "ICC − R²" in imaging or epidemiology; the factor-analysis analogue is the closest established use.

## Key Question 2: Which ICC belongs in the formula — ICC(A,1) from a 10-year test–retest and from tracer vs Medis?

### Takeaway
The reliability ratio in eq. 1 is a ratio of between-person variances in a single cross-section; it corresponds to a consistency-type ICC from a short-interval replicate, not ICC(A,1) from scans ten years apart. A 10-year ICC is the product of reliability and stability (true change), so "1 − ICC" over-states measurement error and ICC − R² under-states reproducible baseline variance; ICC(A,1) also charges any systematic BL–FU shift (scanner, protocol, nitroglycerin at FU) to "error" even though a constant shift adds no variance at baseline.

### Cited Findings
- Heise (1969) showed that a two-wave test–retest correlation confounds measurement reliability with true-score stability, and proposed a three-wave model to separate them (assuming equal reliability per wave) — *Am Sociol Rev* 34:93–101, [doi:10.2307/2092790](https://doi.org/10.2307/2092790); described in [panelr documentation](https://panelr.jacob-long.com/reference/heise) and [Tufiş/Alwin "On the unreliability of test–retest reliability" (PMC12657207)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12657207/).
- Koo & Li (2016) distinguish absolute-agreement and consistency ICC forms and state that a low ICC can reflect "the lack of variability among the sampled subjects", small samples, or few raters, not only poor agreement — [Koo & Li 2016, J Chiropr Med 15:155–163, PMC4913118](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/).
- Regression-dilution correction uses repeat measurements to estimate the multiplicative correction factor; the appropriate repeat is one that reflects the within-person variability relevant to the exposure — [Frost & Thompson 2000, JRSS A 163:173–189](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssa/v163y2000i2p173-189.html).

### Inferences
- Line 238 ("ICC is the test–retest ICC of Section …") combined with line 236 label "(1 − ICC): measurement error": incorrect labelling for the 10-year design. Fix: label the last term "measurement error and true change between scans" or use a reliability that excludes change. Severity: high (central term mislabelled; direction of bias is known: middle term too small, error term too large, and the ICC ≥ 0.75 rule biased toward rejecting features). The section already notes (line 70) that true change lowers the ICC, but eq. 1 does not carry this through.
- The tracer-vs-Medis ICC is an inter-method agreement on the same image (line 66–69); it shares image artefacts and so overstates reliability. Neither design isolates scan-rescan error. Suggest: state that the two ICCs bracket λ (inter-method ≈ upper, 10-year ≈ lower if change dominates) and compute eq. 1 with both, rather than with the test–retest ICC only.
- ICC form: for eq. 1, ICC(C,1) (consistency) or the between-subject variance share after removing a visit effect is the matching quantity; ICC(A,1) (line 65) is appropriate for interchangeability but is lowered by a systematic BL–FU shift. Report both, or adjust the BL–FU model for visit (and nitroglycerin, line 72). Severity: moderate.
- If new plaque occurred between scans and the ICC is computed "irrespective of new plaque" (line 67), the change component differs between future cases and non-cases; that is fine for blinding but means the ICC is lowest exactly where the outcome lives. Worth one sentence.

### Gaps
- No source found giving typical short-term scan–rescan ICCs for coronary curvature/taper; whether repeat-scan data exist in CGPS is not stated in the section.

## Key Question 3: Does residual reproducible variance imply the feature can add to SCORE2 in an LR test? What does measurement error do to the LR test and ΔC? Is the "null LR with low ICC" sentence right?

### Takeaway
Residual reproducible variance is necessary but not sufficient: the LR test detects a partial association with plaque given the base model; variance unexplained by Z says nothing about whether that variance relates to plaque. Classical non-differential error in the added covariate keeps the test of "no effect" valid but reduces its efficiency roughly in proportion to the (conditional) reliability, and attenuates the coefficient and ΔC; this can be quantified with regression calibration/SIMEX sensitivity analysis rather than left as "indistinguishable".

### Cited Findings
- Random error in an exposure biases regression slopes toward the null (regression dilution) and can cause an association to be underestimated or missed — [Hutcheon, Chiolero & Hanley 2010, BMJ 340:c2289](https://www.bmj.com/content/340/bmj.c2289).
- With a single covariate, measurement error dilutes the coefficient toward the null and reduces power to detect an association — [LSHTM measurement error lecture (Bartlett)](https://web-archive.lshtm.ac.uk/csm.lshtm.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2016/04/Jonathan.pdf); in logistic regression, ignoring covariate error biases estimates and loses power — [Cao & Wong, approximate ML for logistic regression with measurement error](https://researchportal.hkust.edu.hk/en/publications/approximate-maximum-likelihood-estimation-for-logistic-regression/).
- Bias in logistic regression is not exactly the linear attenuation; under-estimation can be stronger than in linear regression for the same parameters — [Chen & Van Ness 2005 (abstract)](https://www.airitilibrary.com/Publication/Index/05296528-200509-43-3-259-280-a).
- Tosteson & Tsiatis (1988) derived the asymptotic relative efficiency of score tests in GLMs when surrogates replace the true covariates, comparing the naive plug-in test with the optimal test — [Biometrika 75:525–534, issue listing](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/issue/75/3).
- STRATOS guidance (Keogh et al. 2020, Stat Med 39:2197–2231) covers classical vs Berkson error, non-differential vs differential error, and regression calibration and SIMEX for continuous covariates, including design of validation/reliability substudies — [PMC7450672](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7450672); regression calibration requires non-differential error and is an approximation for nonlinear outcome models — [Keogh STRATOS talk 2019](https://test1.drupaltest.imbi.uni-freiburg.de/sites/default/files/2021-12/STRATOS_ISCB_RuthKeogh_2019.pdf).
- Regression calibration and SIMEX can be used as sensitivity analyses when the error size is only approximately known from reliability data — [arXiv 2106.04285](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.04285).
- Correction factors from repeat measurements should carry their own uncertainty into the CI of the corrected slope — [Frost & Thompson 2000](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssa/v163y2000i2p173-189.html).
- Predictor measurement error / heterogeneity between development and use changes calibration and discrimination of prediction models — [Luijken et al. 2019, Stat Med (deepai)](https://deepai.org/publication/impact-of-predictor-measurement-heterogeneity-across-settings-on-performance-of-prediction-models-a-measurement-error-perspective); clinical examples: C-statistic differences −0.08 to +0.08, calibration slope 0.50–1.67 — [Luijken et al. 2020, J Clin Epidemiol](https://researchexchange.ocrahope.org/papers/j-jclinepi-2019-11-001).
- Added predictive value beyond AUC: Pencina et al. 2008, Stat Med 27:157–172, doi:10.1002/sim.2929 — [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/952773).
- Incremental validity concerns whether a measure adds prediction beyond existing predictors, and depends on entry order of predictors and size of increment — [Hunsley & Meyer 2003, Psychol Assess 15:446–455](https://www.utoledo.edu/al/psychology/pdfs/meyer/HunsleyMeyer2003IncrementalValidity.pdf).

### Inferences
- Lines 37–38 ("keep substantial variance within those groups … leaving little for the likelihood-ratio test"): the logic is right that the LR test's power depends on the variance of geometry conditional on the base covariates (collinearity reduces information), but: (a) "within-group variance" is not "reproducible within-group variance" — a noisy feature has large within-group variance; (b) only variance conditional on the *base model covariates* (SCORE2 components: age, sex, smoking, SBP, total/HDL cholesterol, as refitted) matters; variance shared with BSA or dominance (not in SCORE2) does not reduce the incremental test; (c) a strong group difference does not prevent the feature adding information so long as conditional variance remains. Suggest: "…should keep reproducible variance after conditioning on the base-model covariates; the information available to the likelihood-ratio test scales with that conditional variance". Severity: moderate. Cite Hunsley & Meyer 2003 for incremental validity and Carroll et al. for conditional attenuation.
- R² in eq. 1 is computed on the Table variables + age (line 238), which differ from the SCORE2 base model (LDL, smoking, family history, BSA, dominance, antihypertensive drugs vs SCORE2's SBP, total/HDL cholesterol, smoking, age, sex). The "known determinants" R² is therefore not the collinearity relevant for the who-question LR test. Suggest computing a second R² on exactly the base-model covariates (and, for the where question, on position + log length + dominance × vessel at segment level). Severity: moderate.
- The middle term is necessary but not sufficient for added prediction: it bounds the variance that *could* carry information; whether it does is the outcome question. The section should not imply that a large middle term predicts a positive LR test. Severity: low (wording).
- Line 242 ("A low ICC would mean that most of the unexplained variance is measurement error, and a null result of the likelihood-ratio test could then not be distinguished from a failure of the extraction"): partly incorrect.
  - First clause: a low ICC means 1 − ICC is large relative to total variance, not necessarily "most of the unexplained variance" — that depends on R² as well (share of error in var(W|Z) = (1 − ICC)/(1 − R²)). Correct form: "a low conditional reliability (ICC − R²)/(1 − R²) would mean most of the residual variance is error."
  - Second clause: under non-differential classical error a null LR test is still a valid test of no association of the *measured* feature; what is lost is power and the ability to bound the true effect. The null is "uninformative" rather than "indistinguishable from extraction failure" (a low ICC is itself evidence about the extraction or about true change). Suggested formulation: "With conditional reliability λ_{X|Z}, the coefficient of the geometry block is attenuated by about λ_{X|Z} (exactly in linear models, approximately in logistic models with small effects), and the information for the test is reduced roughly proportionally; a null result would then be reported with the effect size it could exclude, and a regression-calibration sensitivity analysis using the range of ICC estimates would be reported." Severity: moderate–high.
  - Also: a high ICC does not make a null interpretable either if the error is differential or correlated with Z.
- ΔC: the ΔC estimated with the tracer feature is the ΔC achievable with that measurement (relevant for prediction); ΔC for "true geometry" would be higher and is not identified without the error model. Since ΔC/calibration are estimated, not tested (CLAUDE.md), the section could state that measurement error lowers ΔC and that the reported ΔC refers to the tracer measurement. Severity: low.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact ARE expression from Tosteson & Tsiatis (1988) (commonly cited as approximately the squared correlation between true and surrogate covariate, i.e. the reliability, for small effects) — verify in the paper before stating it as a formula.
- No source found on LR test size/power specifically for a block of several mismeasured covariates in conditional logistic regression (the where question).

## Key Question 4: Is a fixed ICC ≥ 0.75 rule on the point estimate sensible? And R² < ICC on point estimates?

### Takeaway
Koo & Li — the source cited for the 0.75 threshold — explicitly say reliability should be graded on the 95% CI, not the point estimate, and that there are no standard acceptable values; ICC also depends on between-subject heterogeneity, which is restricted in a clean-baseline cohort. The rule should be stated on the CI (or explicitly as a point-estimate rule with CI reported), and the R² < ICC comparison needs both uncertainties.

### Cited Findings
- Koo & Li (2016): reliability should be judged on "the 95% confident interval of the ICC estimate (not the ICC estimate itself)"; the point estimate is "only an expected value of the true ICC"; thresholds <0.5 poor, 0.5–0.75 moderate, 0.75–0.9 good, >0.9 excellent; "there are no standard values for acceptable reliability using ICC"; low ICC can reflect "the lack of variability among the sampled subjects"; recommend ≥30 heterogeneous subjects — [PMC4913118](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/).

### Inferences
- Line 71 (grading) and line 248 (ICC ≥ 0.75) cite Koo & Li but use point estimates, contradicting the cited guideline. Fix: "the lower limit of the 95% CI of the ICC was at least 0.75" or "the point estimate … with the 95% CI reported; Koo and Li recommend grading on the interval" and justify the choice. Severity: moderate.
- ICC depends on the population: the clean-baseline cohort is more homogeneous than mixed cohorts, so ICCs are not transportable and a fixed 0.75 threshold means different error variances in different cohorts. Suggest also reporting the SEM / within-subject SD (absolute error in mm⁻¹ or degrees), which does not depend on heterogeneity. Severity: low–moderate.
- "R² below its ICC" (line 248): under the CTT model this is guaranteed in the population, so as a retention criterion it has almost no discriminating power when error is independent of Z; it fails only from sampling noise, correlated error, or an ICC lowered by true change. Better: prespecify a minimum conditional reliability (ICC − R²)/(1 − R²) or a minimum middle term, use adjusted R², and judge with a bootstrap CI over persons (resampling both R² and ICC jointly). Severity: moderate.
- Choice of 0.75 itself: for a predictor in a regression, the relevant consequence is attenuation (≈ conditional reliability) and power loss (≈ 1/λ more sample size), so a rule framed in terms of conditional reliability ties the threshold to the LR test, which is the stated purpose of the feature. Inference, not from a cited source.
- The 75% construct-validity threshold with few hypotheses per feature is coarse — the section already notes this (line 249). Fine.

### Gaps
- Koo & Li's CI-based recommendation is cited from the PMC full text; their minimum-sample and rater guidance is for inter-rater designs, and applicability to a 2-occasion test–retest design is my inference.

## Key Question 5: Consequences of using a 10-year test–retest ICC in eq. 1 when true change occurred

### Takeaway
If true geometry changes between BL and FU (vessel growth/remodelling, plaque, age-related tortuosity increase, nitroglycerin at FU only), the 10-year ICC estimates reliability × stability, so 1 − ICC includes true change, ICC − R² is biased downwards, and R² > ICC can occur even when the determinants explain only reproducible baseline variance. Any regression-calibration correction using this ICC would over-correct.

### Cited Findings
- Two-wave test–retest correlations confound unreliability with true change; at least three waves (or an independent short-interval replicate) are needed to separate them — [Heise 1969, doi:10.2307/2092790](https://doi.org/10.2307/2092790); [Tufiş et al., PMC12657207](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12657207/).
- Correction factors based on repeat measurements assume the repeats reflect the error relevant to the exposure; long-interval repeats also capture within-person biological change (regression-dilution literature) — [Frost & Thompson 2000](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssa/v163y2000i2p173-189.html); [Berglund 2012, Upsala J Med Sci, PMC3410287](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3410287/).

### Inferences
- Concrete effects on the section:
  - Line 236 label "(1 − ICC) measurement error" — should be "measurement error and change between scans" when the 10-year ICC is used. Severity: high.
  - Line 240 condition — with the 10-year ICC, R² > ICC is an expected possible outcome when features change, not a sign of error in the determinants. Severity: moderate.
  - Line 248 rule (ICC ≥ 0.75 and R² < ICC on the 10-year ICC) — biased toward rejecting features that change with age, which may be exactly the features relevant to plaque. Severity: moderate–high. Suggest: use the inter-method (tracer vs Medis, same scan) ICC as an upper bound and the 10-year ICC as a lower bound, compute eq. 1 for both, and base the rule on the bounds; or estimate a scan-rescan reliability if any short-interval repeat or a phantom noise model (step 1) is available; consider adjusting the BL–FU ICC for visit and FU nitroglycerin (line 72).
  - Conversely, for the who/where prediction from a baseline scan to a 10-year outcome, the 10-year ICC is the relevant "long-term" reliability if the question were whether a single baseline value represents the person's usual geometry over follow-up (the regression-dilution meaning in Frost & Thompson / Hutcheon et al.). The section should say which of the two meanings it intends. Inference.

### Gaps
- No CGPS-specific evidence on how much coronary curvature/taper truly changes over 10 years in plaque-free adults; adult coronary tortuosity is reported to increase with age in the hypothesis literature, but I did not retrieve a longitudinal source for it.
