# Accuracy on synthetic geometry, definitional choices and sensitivity analysis: audit of `thesis/methodology/10b_feature_validity.tex`

Scope: opening numbered list (lines 22-33), sec:method-validity-accuracy (lines 48-59), sec:method-validity-definitions and Table tab:method-validity-definitions (lines 74-107), sec:method-validity-sensitivity (lines 219-226), and the replacement rule in sec:method-validity-reading (line 252).

Severity scale: **error** (wrong or will produce misleading results), **questionable** (a methodologist would object; defensible only with added justification), **fine**.

## Q1. How do QIBA metrology standards assess bias, linearity and precision with reference objects, and does the phantom design match?

### Takeaway
QIBA defines technical performance as bias, linearity and precision (repeatability/reproducibility) over a stated measuring interval, with linearity checked at about 9 to 11 true-value levels. The section records only bias and SD of the error, says nothing about linearity or a measuring interval, and its i.i.d. Gaussian noise "matched to the tracer–Medis distance" is the wrong error model for curvature, taper and ratios.

### Cited Findings
- Raunig et al. (2015) name three primary metrology areas for QIB technical performance: measurement linearity and bias, repeatability, and reproducibility when conditions change; Stat Methods Med Res 24(1):27–67, doi 10.1177/0962280214537344 — [Raunig 2015 record](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/98441)
- Sullivan et al. (2015) define bias as the difference between the mean of repeated measurements and the true value. They define linearity as "the ability to deliver measured values that are directly proportional to the true value of the measurand", recommend checking it with a scatterplot and, against phantom or true data, 9 to 11 true-value levels, and define the "measuring interval" as the range in which bias, linearity and precision are acceptable — [Sullivan 2015, Radiology 277(3):813–825, PMC4666097](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)
- Sullivan et al.: "Pragmatic alternatives include the use of digital or physical phantoms", but phantoms "do not possess all of the characteristics of a human target", and "phantoms do not represent the complexity of human targets; thus, precision is often overestimated". Also: "poor phantom results with respect to bias would most likely be reflected as poor results in patients", but good phantom results "do not guarantee" good results against the true value. They call for "improved realism of phantoms, and hybrid approaches" — [Sullivan 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)
- Sullivan et al. reject "ground truth" and "gold standard" as having no "standardized or unambiguous definition". They require the measurand to be a ratio or interval variable, which excludes ordinal and nominal scales — [Sullivan 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)
- Obuchowski et al. (2015) group algorithm-validation designs into "designs with the true value (e.g. phantoms, digital reference images, and zero-change studies)", designs with a reference standard, and designs without one (agreement, precision). They "propose a series of steps for establishing the performance of a QIB algorithm"; Stat Methods Med Res 24(1):68–106 — [Obuchowski 2015 algorithm comparison](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/esb_facpub/138)
- QIBA describes digital reference objects as synthetic **images** created by computer simulation of a target in its environment, usable to set a minimum performance requirement for QIB algorithms — [QIBA DRO document 2015](https://qibawiki.rsna.org:443/images/1/14/QIBA_DRO_2015_v1.42.pdf) (seen only as a search snippet)

### Inferences (audit items)
- **A1. Only bias and SD of the error are recorded; linearity and the measuring interval are missing (line 57). Severity: questionable.** Curvature error from noise is not constant: it is largest when the true curvature is small and the noise is large, and it shrinks or reverses for tight bends that a 5 mm chord under-resolves. A single bias summed over all arcs, helices and sine curves hides this. Fix: for each definition, plot measured against true value over about 9 to 11 true-value levels per phantom family (for curvature, radii of about 3 to 50 mm), at each noise level. Report bias as a function of the true value, the slope (linearity) and the measuring interval, meaning the range of true values where |bias| and SD stay below a stated limit. The existing "radius of the tightest bend still resolved" is then the lower end of that interval and should be defined by the same limit.
- **A2. Gaussian noise "at levels spanning the distance observed between the tracer and Medis centerlines" (line 56) uses the wrong quantity to calibrate the noise. Severity: error.** The tracer–Medis point distance is dominated by low-frequency offsets: a centerline shifted or slowly displaced relative to the other, plus Medis's own error. A smooth offset of, say, 0.3 mm barely changes curvature. Independent noise of 0.3 mm on each point at sub-millimetre spacing, by contrast, adds turning angles of order σ/chord at every step, inflating the mean turning angle and dominating the maxima and the 90th percentile. So the phantom noise is pessimistic for high-frequency error and blind to the error that actually matters for shape: smooth wandering of the centerline on a wavelength of a few millimetres, for example cutting corners at bends, drifting toward calcium or toward the side-branch ostium. Fix: (i) estimate the noise spectrum. Take the tracer–Medis residual vector along arc length, split it into low- and high-frequency parts (for example with a Gaussian filter at 2 to 5 mm), and report the SD of each part. (ii) Simulate correlated noise, such as a Gaussian process or filtered white noise with that SD and correlation length, as the main condition, keeping i.i.d. noise as a worst case. (iii) Rename the calibration "the residual between tracer and Medis", not an error, in line with the plan's rule that the Medis comparison is never called accuracy or error (comment at line 11).
- **A3. Taper, diameter ratios and bifurcation angle get noise on point positions only (line 56). Severity: error for taper and ratios.** Taper and the ratios are computed from contours or radii, and the main errors there come from radius estimation: blooming and partial volume, which overestimate small lumens, a systematic error that depends on size. A ratio of a smaller branch diameter to the LM diameter is biased by exactly this. Noise on centerline points does not test it at all. Fix: perturb the contour radii as well, with a multiplicative noise term and a size-dependent bias term. Better still, render the tubes into volumes (see Q2). For the Y-tubes, use curved branches, because on straight branches the fitted angle is exact for every fitting length. As written, the phantom cannot inform the "5 vs 10 mm fitting length" alternative in Table tab:method-validity-definitions.
- **A4. Terminology. Severity: questionable, minor.** Line 22 attributes the accuracy step to "the taxonomy of the COSMIN initiative". COSMIN has no accuracy (bias) property; it covers reliability, measurement error, validity and responsiveness. The bias, linearity and precision vocabulary comes from QIBA (Kessler, Raunig, Sullivan). Fix: attribute step 1 to the QIBA metrology terminology and steps 2 to 4 to COSMIN.

- I could not open the full text of Raunig 2015 or Kessler 2015 to quote their exact definitions of "digital reference object" or their recommended number of replicates.
- I found no published validation study of coronary centerline curvature that used a spatially correlated noise model. The claim that real centerline error is low-frequency is my inference from how centerlines are built (from a lumen segmentation, smoothed); it is not a cited finding.

## Q2. Should phantoms be image-based (rendered volumes run through the whole pipeline) rather than point-based? What noise models do vessel curvature studies use?

### Takeaway
The QIBA definition of a digital reference object is a synthetic image, and the vessel literature uses rendered synthetic volumes (VascuSynth) or real data. Published coronary curvature studies (Kashyap 2022, van Zandwijk 2019) did no phantom or noise validation at all, so the point-based phantom is not below the field's standard. It is, however, below what QIBA calls a DRO, and the section is right to say it does not test the tracer (line 58).

### Cited Findings
- Kashyap et al. (2022), Sci Rep 12, doi 10.1038/s41598-022-04796-w: surfaces were Taubin-smoothed in VMTK (passband 0.03, 30 iterations), and VMTK centerlines were resampled at 0.01 mm spacing "to minimize errors that would be introduced based on discretization". No synthetic phantom and no noise analysis of the curvature metrics was reported. Average absolute curvature = ∫|κ|ds / L — [Kashyap 2022, PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)
- van Zandwijk et al. (2019), J Digit Imaging, doi 10.1007/s10278-019-00300-5: local curvature by Menger's formula on points 5 mm apart, averaged along the path. The authors call 5 mm "an adequate and robust option", report no phantom or noise validation, and note that "The centerline creation did not always yield an accurate result" (centerlines were adapted manually). Mean artery curvature was 0.090 mm⁻¹ at end-systole against 0.081 mm⁻¹ at end-diastole (11.1% higher, p = 0.002) — [van Zandwijk 2019, PMC7165136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)
- Bullitt et al. (2003), IEEE TMI 22(9):1163–1171: tested their tortuosity metrics on synthetic curves. They resampled the skeleton at one-voxel spacing to avoid "noise that can appear with sub-voxel sampling", and warned that long sample spacing causes "inaccurate inflection counting" — [Bullitt 2003, PMC2430603](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603)
- Schaap et al. (2009), Med Image Anal 13(5):701–714: a standardized framework of 32 CCTA datasets with a multi-observer consensus reference centerline and "well-defined measures" to evaluate 13 coronary centerline extraction algorithms — [Schaap 2009 record](https://lmi.bwh.harvard.edu/publications/standardized-evaluation-methodology-and-reference-database-evaluating-coronary-artery); [RUG record](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/standardized-evaluation-methodology-and-reference-database-for-ev/)
- VascuSynth (Jassi and Hamarneh) generates synthetic 3D volumes of vascular trees together with their ground truth for segmentation, bifurcation locations, branch radii and hierarchy, "for large scale evaluation studies of medical image segmentation and analysis software" — [VascuSynth, Insight Journal](https://insight-journal.org/browse/publication/794)
- Penalized or free-knot spline fitting has been proposed to make arterial curvature and torsion independent of digitization noise. Kobayashi et al. applied it to aortic and carotid centerlines, and Sangalli et al. found free-knot splines more accurate than local polynomial smoothing for carotid curvature — [Kobayashi, penalized spline](https://pure.fujita-hu.ac.jp/en/publications/a-penalized-spline-fitting-method-to-optimize-geometric-parameter/); [Sangalli et al. 2009 JRSS-C](https://sangalli.faculty.polimi.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2009_Sangalli-Secchi-Vantini-Veneziani-JRSSC_compresso.pdf)
- On a synthetic 3D helix with Gaussian noise, noise increased the variance of curvature estimates and, for some helix parameters, also biased them — [Principal Polynomial Analysis, arXiv 1602.00221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00221)

### Inferences (audit items)
- **A5. A point-based phantom only (lines 52-58). Severity: questionable, not an error, given the stated scope.** Line 58 correctly limits the claim. However, step 1 is still called "accuracy" in the numbered list (line 24) and in the decision rule (line 252, "replaced only on grounds of accuracy"). That wording overstates what a point phantom can show, because it tests the feature computation, not the measurement. Fix: call step 1 "computational accuracy (bias of the feature computation)" in the list and in line 252. Optionally, add a small image-based arm: render the arc, helix, taper and Y-tube phantoms into volumes at CCTA voxel size (about 0.4 to 0.5 mm) with a Gaussian point spread function, contrast and noise, and run them through the full tracer. Even a few configurations would show whether the tracer's own smoothing removes or adds curvature, and whether blooming biases the ratios.
- **A6. Point spacing interacts with the noise (line 56).** Bullitt's finding about sub-voxel sampling and noise means that the result of i.i.d. noise at the tracer's point spacing depends strongly on that spacing. The phantom must therefore use exactly the tracer's spacing and any smoothing the feature code applies (it says so, which is fine), and the tracer's spacing should be stated in the clarify note at line 59.
- **A7. Cardiac phase (outside the accuracy section, relevant to step 2).** van Zandwijk found curvature 9 to 11% higher at end-systole than at end-diastole. If BL and FU scans were reconstructed at different phases, the test–retest ICC (line 67) contains phase variance. Fix: note the reconstruction phase, or add it to the list of reasons the BL–FU ICC is biased downward (lines 68-70).

## Q3. Is choosing a primary definition on accuracy, reliability and literature, then reporting the alternatives as sensitivity, consistent with recommended practice? Is any multiplicity correction needed?

### Takeaway
Fixing one primary definition before testing, on outcome-blind grounds, is the standard defence against analytic flexibility and is fine. The weak point is the sensitivity analysis. It mixes definitions that measure the same construct with different noise trade-offs (chords of 2.5, 5 and 10 mm) and definitions that measure different constructs (bend count, maximum angle), and it reads any loss of significance under an alternative as "dependent on the definition". The multiverse literature says that alternatives which are not equivalent should not be pooled as robustness checks, and that the pattern across specifications needs joint inference, not a vote of significance tests.

### Cited Findings
- Steegen et al. (2016), "Increasing Transparency Through a Multiverse Analysis", Perspect Psychol Sci 11(5):702–712, doi 10.1177/1745691616658637: report results across all reasonable processing choices to show how much they depend on arbitrary choices — [specr README citing Steegen 2016](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/packages/specr/readme/README.html)
- Simonsohn, Simmons and Nelson (2020), "Specification curve analysis", Nat Hum Behav 4:1208–1214: three steps, namely (1) identify "the set of theoretically justified, statistically valid and non-redundant specifications", (2) display the results graphically so that readers can see which decisions matter, and (3) "conduct joint inference across all specifications" — [Simonsohn 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41562-020-0912-z)
- Del Giudice and Gangestad (2021), "A Traveler's Guide to the Multiverse", Adv Methods Pract Psychol Sci 4(1): choices that theory or background knowledge clearly favour are not arbitrary and should not generate alternative specifications. Alternatives that are not equivalent (their "Type N" decisions) should be trimmed or analysed separately rather than mixed into one multiverse. The authors also note that Simonsohn et al. warned against "unambiguously inferior" specifications without saying how to identify them — [APS page for the article](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/ampps/2515245920954925/); summary of Type N/U handling in [arXiv 2607.11345](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11345). The primary PDF was not accessible (HTTP 403), so the Type E/N/U wording rests on secondary summaries.
- A 2026 commentary, citing Auspurg (2025), warns that including many questionable specifications can falsely make a result look non-robust — [arXiv 2605.19745](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19745) (secondary)
- Kashyap et al. found the arc-to-chord tortuosity index unrelated to low TAWSS (adjusted R² = −0.018, p = 0.865 for the whole bifurcation). Average absolute curvature had the highest adjusted R²: LM 0.196, LAD 0.232, LCx 0.061, whole bifurcation 0.112. The 127 patients had symptoms, no obstruction and a zero calcium score — [Kashyap 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)

### Inferences (audit items)
- **A8. Prespecifying the primary definitions and refusing to choose by hypothesis (lines 104-107, 224, 252). Severity: fine.** This matches the "garden of forking paths" logic behind Steegen and Simonsohn. Two refinements:
  - Step 2 (reliability) uses the cohort itself, so a definition chosen for higher BL–FU ICC is still selected on data. It is outcome-blind and hypothesis-blind, which is acceptable, but line 32 ("the hypotheses of step 4 remain an independent test") should say that independence holds with respect to the hypothesis variables, not the data.
  - Line 252 allows a primary definition that failed the hypotheses to be replaced "on grounds of accuracy or reliability". Once a failure is known, re-reading the accuracy and reliability results is itself a forking path. Fix: state the numeric rule in advance, for example "the alternative with the highest ICC above 0.75 and |bias| below X", so that the replacement is determined mechanically.
- **A9. The sensitivity set mixes equivalent and non-equivalent alternatives (Table tab:method-validity-definitions, curvature row; line 225). Severity: questionable, bordering on error for the interpretation rule.** Chords of 2.5 and 10 mm estimate the same construct (mean bending per mm) with a different trade-off between noise and resolution, so they are legitimate robustness alternatives. A count of bends, the 90th percentile and the largest turning angle are different constructs. A count is an integer frequency of discrete events, and a maximum is an extreme-value statistic that grows with vessel length and noise. Line 226 itself shows this: a count and a continuous angle gave opposite associations with CAD in the literature. Under Del Giudice and Gangestad these are Type N alternatives and should not be read as tests of whether the primary result is robust. Fix: split the alternatives into (a) robustness variants of the primary (chord 2.5 and 10 mm, Theil–Sen versus end-ratio taper, fitting length, other sampling distances), where agreement is expected, and (b) related but different features (bend count, P90, maximum, minimum diameter), reported descriptively as separate constructs, with no "dependent on definition" verdict.
- **A10. "Confirmed under the primary but not under the alternatives → dependent on the definition" (line 225) is a vote of significance tests. Severity: questionable.** A CI that just includes zero under the 10 mm chord does not show that the effect differs, so the difference between significant and non-significant is not itself significant. With 5 curvature alternatives × 3 hypothesis rows, some non-confirmations are expected by chance even when the effect is the same. Fix: report each hypothesis as a specification-curve-style panel of standardized estimates (per SD of the feature) with 95% CIs across the variants. Call a result definition-dependent only when the estimates point in opposite directions, or when a prespecified test of the difference between estimates (paired bootstrap, persons resampled) excludes zero. No multiplicity correction is needed as long as the sensitivity analysis stays descriptive and only the primary definition feeds the decision rules. State that explicitly.
- **A11. Citing Kashyap for the primary curvature definition (line 105). Severity: questionable.**
  - Kashyap's "average absolute curvature" is ∫|κ|ds/L on VMTK centerlines from Taubin-smoothed surfaces resampled at 0.01 mm. The thesis primary is a mean turning angle per mm at a 5 mm chord. The two agree only approximately, when bends are much longer than 5 mm, so the citation supports the family of measures, not the 5 mm chord.
  - The criterion in Kashyap is association with a haemodynamic quantity (adjusted R² 0.06 to 0.23), not measurement accuracy or reliability. Choosing a definition because it correlates with low WSS is choosing it for its expected link to plaque, which is close to the "who/where" hypothesis. Say so openly, as relevance rather than validity.
  - Fix: add van Zandwijk 2019 (Menger curvature at 5 mm point spacing, described as "adequate and robust") as the direct precedent for the 5 mm scale, and state that Kashyap supports curvature-based over arc-chord measures.

- I found no source that prescribes a formal multiplicity correction for sensitivity analyses over measurement definitions. The common practice (Simonsohn's joint inference by permutation, or descriptive reporting) is described above.

## Q4. Is "bias" meaningful for summaries such as bend count, maximum or 90th percentile on a helix? Is ground-truth curvature well defined for discrete, noisy curves?

### Takeaway
No, not without defining the measurand per definition. On a helix and an arc the curvature is constant, so a count of bends has no meaningful true value: Bullitt showed that coils contain no inflection points. The chord-based turning angle also has a scale-dependent "true" value that differs from the analytic κ. Bias must therefore be stated against the noiseless value of the same definition (and, separately, against κ), and definitions with different estimands and units cannot be ranked by raw bias and SD.

### Cited Findings
- Bullitt et al.: "As coils do not contain inflection points", the inflection count metric cannot separate coils of different tightness and reduces to the distance metric. Only the sum-of-angles metric handled tight coils in their synthetic tests. Inflection points are detected as large local maxima of ΔN·ΔN (threshold > 1.0) — [Bullitt 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603)
- Bullitt et al.: the sum-of-angles metric sums in-plane and torsional angles over valid points and normalizes by curve length, so it contains torsion, unlike a pure bending measure — [Bullitt 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603)
- Sullivan et al.: a QIB measurand must be a ratio or interval variable — [Sullivan 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)
- Gaussian noise on a helix both increases the variance of curvature estimates and, for some helix pitch and radius combinations, biases them — [arXiv 1602.00221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00221)

### Inferences (audit items)
- **A12. "For every candidate definition ... the bias and the standard deviation of the error against the true value were recorded" (line 57). Severity: error as written.**
  - (a) A "count of bends" on an arc or a helix has no defined true value. A constant-curvature curve has zero inflection points, and a threshold count of turning angles above X° is either 0 or equals the number of chord windows, depending on the threshold. Only sine curves give a true count (one bend per half-wavelength above the threshold).
  - (b) The largest turning angle and the 90th percentile have true values on arcs and helices (both equal the constant angle 2·arcsin(c/2R) for chord c and radius R). Under noise, however, the maximum is biased upward by an amount that grows with the number of points, that is, with vessel length. Its bias is therefore a property of the phantom length, not of the method.
  - (c) The turning angle at chord c measures curvature smoothed at scale c. On a sine curve whose wavelength is close to c, the noiseless value already falls short of the analytic mean |κ|. That is a definitional smoothing bias, not an error.
  - Fix: for each definition, define the measurand as the value of that definition on the noiseless continuous phantom, and report bias against it. That isolates noise and sampling error. Separately, report the deviation from analytic κ for the curvature-per-mm definitions only, to show scale dependence. Use the bend count only on sine phantoms, and report the maximum and P90 at a fixed phantom length equal to the median trunk length.
- **A13. Ranking definitions on accuracy (lines 79, 104) when their estimands and units differ. Severity: error, in logic.** A bias in mm⁻¹ for the mean per mm, in degrees for the maximum and in counts for the bend count cannot be compared, and even across chords the true value differs (scale dependence, A12c). Fix: compare definitions on a scale-free criterion of noise robustness. For example, use the correlation between measured and true value across the phantom family, or the ratio of noise-induced error SD to the between-person SD of that definition in the cohort. The latter is a phantom-based counterpart of an ICC and can be computed outcome-blind. Then state that the primary is the definition with the best robustness within the construct of mean curvature per mm, not "the most accurate feature".
- **A14. Helix phantoms test bending, but torsion adds nothing to the primary.** The section says the feature quantifies bending, not torsion (line 204), which is consistent. If Bullitt's sum-of-angles (which includes torsion) is the reference for the "count of bends" citation, note that the cited source defines tortuosity differently. The citation to bullitt2003 in the Table is for the inflection count, which is appropriate, but the Table should name it "inflection count (Bullitt)", not "count of bends", because a threshold-based bend count (≥ 45°, as in li2011/li2022) is a different metric.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that gives a closed form for the noise bias of a chord-based turning angle under i.i.d. Gaussian noise. The scaling (bias rising as σ/chord) is my own geometric reasoning and should be confirmed by the phantom itself, which is a good reason to run it.

## Q5. Other flaws a methodologist would flag

### Takeaway
Most remaining issues are about what can be concluded from the phantom step, and about stating what the Table rows actually vary.

### Cited Findings
- Sullivan: phantoms tend to overestimate precision, and good phantom results do not guarantee good in vivo performance — [Sullivan 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)
- Simonsohn et al. require the specifications to be "non-redundant" and "statistically valid" — [Simonsohn 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41562-020-0912-z)

### Inferences (audit items)
- **A15. Taper phantom: an exact exponential with noise only on point positions (lines 54, 56).** Theil–Sen on the log radius of an exact exponential returns the set rate with zero error whatever the centerline noise, because point noise does not enter the radius. Unless contour or radius noise is added (A3), the taper "accuracy" result is trivially perfect and gives no basis for preferring Theil–Sen over the end-radius ratio or for the 3 mm and side-branch exclusions in the Table. Severity: error (the step cannot inform the choice it is used for). Fix: add radius noise, focal stenosis-like or side-branch-like bumps (outliers, which are what Theil–Sen is meant to resist), and a size-dependent radius bias.
- **A16. The diameter-ratio and bifurcation-angle rows contain [..] placeholders (Table, lines 94-96), and line 101 states that no study has varied the fitting length.** Fine as a note. It means that the phantom (with curved branches, A3) and reliability are the only grounds for fixing these settings, so they must be fixed before step 4 and the date filled in at line 254.
- **A17. Ramus intermedius "treated as the LCx or excluded" (Table, line 96) is a cohort-definition choice, not a measurement definition.** Excluding people with a ramus changes the sample, so it belongs in a subgroup analysis, not in the sensitivity analysis of the measurement. Severity: questionable. Fix: move it to a separate row, "population variant", or report it as a subgroup.
- **A18. The sensitivity step repeats steps 2 and 4 but not step 6 (line 223).** If a variant definition is more reliable but explains more of its variance by known determinants, the partition of eq. method-validity-split differs. Severity: minor. Fix: also report R² and ICC − R² per variant, or state why that is not needed.
- **A19. "Each such choice changes the number, so a primary definition was fixed" (line 79). Severity: fine.** Optionally add that the reason is to avoid analytic flexibility, citing Steegen 2016 or Simonsohn 2020, which gives the step a methodological anchor.
