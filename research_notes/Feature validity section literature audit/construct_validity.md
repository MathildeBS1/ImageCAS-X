## Q1. Must hypotheses specify magnitude, and is "95% CI excludes the null" an accepted confirmation criterion (n ≈ 1,200)? Is "CI includes the null" acceptable for discriminant hypotheses?

### Takeaway
No. COSMIN requires every hypothesis to state the expected direction AND the expected (absolute or relative) magnitude, and explicitly says p-values / significance should not be used to judge hypotheses because significance depends on sample size. The section does the opposite: magnitudes are "for orientation only" (lines 127-128, table caption line 133) and confirmation is "CI excludes null" (line 125) or "CI includes null" (line 126). Both rules are errors relative to COSMIN, and the discriminant rule is additionally a classic absence-of-evidence fallacy that rewards imprecision.

### Cited Findings
- COSMIN 2012 Box F item 6: "Was the expected absolute or relative magnitude of correlations or mean differences included in the hypotheses?"; items 5-6 explanation: "The expected direction (positive or negative) and magnitude (absolute or relative) of the correlations or differences should have been included in the hypotheses ... Without this specification it is difficult to decide afterwards whether the hypothesis is confirmed or not." Examples given: "a correlation of at least 0.60" or "a mean difference of 10 points ... between two patient groups" — [COSMIN manual 2012](https://faculty.ksu.edu.sa/sites/default/files/cosmin_checklist_manual_v9.pdf)
- COSMIN 2012 Box F item 10: "P-values should be avoided in testing hypotheses, because it is not relevant to examine whether correlations statistically differ from zero. The validity issue is about whether the direction and magnitude of a correlation is similar to what could be expected ... When assessing differences between groups, it is also less relevant whether these differences are statistically significant (which depends on the sample size) than whether these differences are as large as could be expected." — [COSMIN manual 2012](https://faculty.ksu.edu.sa/sites/default/files/cosmin_checklist_manual_v9.pdf)
- The same wording is repeated in the 2018 Risk of Bias box 9a standard 3 ("P-values should not be used in testing hypotheses") and box 9b standard 6 (known-groups: "less relevant whether these differences are statistically significant (which depends on the sample size) than whether these differences are as large as expected") — [COSMIN user manual 2018, sec. 5.7](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)
- COSMIN 2018 sec. 3.4: "The expected direction (positive or negative) and magnitude (absolute or relative) of the correlations or differences should be included in the hypotheses"; hypotheses may be relative, e.g. "PROM A correlates at least 0.10 points higher with ... B than with ... C" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)
- COSMIN 2018 Table 8 generic hypotheses (from de Vet et al.): similar constructs r ≥ 0.50; related but dissimilar 0.30-0.50; unrelated constructs r < 0.30; these should differ by ≥ 0.10; "Meaningful changes between relevant (sub)groups" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf); also in [Prinsen 2018 Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5891568)
- COSMIN 2018 also says: "If it is impossible to formulate a good hypothesis for a specific analysis (e.g. about an expected magnitude of difference between two subgroups) ... the review team can consider ignoring these results, as it is unknown how the results should be interpreted." — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)
- Terwee 2007: an informative hypothesis "should thus include a justification of the expected direction and magnitude of the correlation"; vague hypotheses (e.g. "restriction ... correlates with disability score") are criticised — [Terwee 2007](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2901670/267479.pdf)
- Cronbach & Meehl 1955: validators should "state as definitely as possible the degree of validity the test is presumed to have"; "rejecting the null hypothesis does not finish the job of construct validation" — [Cronbach & Meehl 1955](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Cronbach/construct.htm)
- Lakens 2017: "It is not possible to conclude there is no effect when p > α"; "It is statistically impossible to support the hypothesis that a true effect size is exactly zero"; equivalence tests (TOST) "statistically reject the presence of effects large enough to be considered worthwhile", using bounds set at the smallest effect size of interest (SESOI); "with huge sample sizes, effects can be statistically significant but practically and theoretically irrelevant"; NHST + equivalence give four outcomes (different and not equivalent; different but equivalent/trivial; equivalent; undetermined) — [Lakens 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/)

### Inferences
- **Lines 125, 127-128 and table caption (line 133, "the hypotheses concern the direction") — ERROR.** Direction-plus-significance is exactly what COSMIN item 6/standard 6 rules out. With ~1,200 participants, an SE for a sex difference in a standardized feature is about 0.06 SD, so any difference ≥ ~0.12 SD will "confirm" regardless of whether it is the expected size; a difference of 0.1 SD, far smaller than the ~0.6-1 SD implied by the cited 0.06-0.07 mm⁻¹ (if SD of curvature is of that order), would confirm. Fix: state each hypothesis with an expected magnitude band or minimum (absolute, e.g. "women ≥ 0.2 SD higher curvature", "angle 5-15° wider in men"; or relative, e.g. "|standardized difference| for sex > that for LDL by ≥ 0.1"), and judge confirmation by whether the point estimate (with its CI reported) lies in the predicted band. Drop significance as the criterion. Resolve the \clarify on line 128 in favour of magnitude.
- **Line 126 (discriminant confirmed when CI includes null) — ERROR.** This is absence-of-evidence reasoning (Lakens). It also inverts incentives: a noisier feature (wider CI) passes more easily, and at n ≈ 1,200 a trivially small real association (e.g. 0.08 SD via shared confounding with age/sex) "fails". Fix: define an equivalence margin (SESOI), e.g. |r| < 0.10 or |standardized β| < 0.1 per SD of LDL, or use COSMIN's generic "unrelated constructs r < 0.30" (likely too lax here, see Q2), and confirm when the 90% CI lies entirely within the margin (TOST), or at minimum when the point estimate is inside the margin.
- **Null-type known-groups rows are the same problem — ERROR.** "fractional taper similar" (line 145) and "Diameter ratios: women vs men ... similar" (line 152) are labelled K but are no-difference hypotheses; under line 125 they have no confirmation rule at all (no expected sign), and under line 126 they would inherit the absence-of-evidence flaw. Need explicit equivalence margins.
- **Hypertension row (line 142) — QUESTIONABLE.** The literature magnitude is an odds ratio for a dichotomised tortuosity count; the text itself notes ORs cannot be converted (line 195). That is a reason to state a relative or minimal magnitude derived from reasoning, or, per COSMIN 2018, to admit that no good hypothesis can be formulated and downweight/omit it, rather than to fall back to significance.
- Note: the magnitude requirement is not satisfied by the table's "magnitudes ... serve for orientation" phrasing; COSMIN would rate such hypotheses as lacking item 6.

### Gaps
- de Vet et al. 2011 (Measurement in Medicine, ch. 6) and Streiner & Norman could not be read directly; their positions are known only through the COSMIN reproduction of de Vet's generic hypotheses.
- No source found that gives a recommended equivalence margin for imaging-feature validation specifically; the SESOI must be argued by the author.

## Q2. Are "discriminant", "convergent" and "known-groups" used correctly? Is the Finet ratio a convergent hypothesis?

### Takeaway
In COSMIN, hypotheses testing is split into (9a) comparison with other instruments — "convergent validity" — and (9b) comparison between subgroups — "discriminative or known-groups validity". "Discriminant validity" (Campbell & Fiske; COSMIN/de Vet generic hypothesis 3) means low correlation with measures of different/unrelated constructs, which is roughly what lines 119-120 intend, but the rationale ("no link has been reported") is weak and the variables chosen are not clearly unrelated. The "scaling law / Finet ratio about 0.66" row is not convergent validity in the COSMIN sense (there is no comparator instrument; Variable = "none"); it is a hypothesis about an internal relationship / a nomological law, and it has no defined confirmation criterion.

### Cited Findings
- COSMIN definition: hypotheses testing for construct validity is "the degree to which the scores of a PROM are consistent with hypotheses (for instance with regard to internal relationships, relationships to scores of other instruments, or differences between relevant groups) based on the assumption that the PROM validly measures the construct" — [COSMIN user manual 2018, sec. 5.7](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf); taxonomy origin in Mokkink et al. 2010, J Clin Epidemiol 63:737-45, which subdivides construct validity into structural validity, hypotheses testing and cross-cultural validity — [overview](https://www.clinimetrics.sdu.dk/.\files/01_intro_course/day1/Preparation/The-COSMIN-taxonomy-overview.pdf)
- Box 9 headings: "9a. Comparison with other outcome measurement instruments (convergent validity)"; "9b. Comparison between subgroups (discriminative or known-groups validity)". 9a standards require that "it should be known what the construct is that the comparator instruments measures, and the instrument itself should be of sufficient quality" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)
- COSMIN 2018: "correlations with PROMs measuring similar constructs (i.e. convergent validity) can be considered as providing more evidence than correlations with PROMs measuring dissimilar constructs" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)
- Campbell & Fiske MTMM: convergent = monotrait-heteromethod correlations (same trait, different methods) should be high; discriminant = heterotrait correlations should be lower; heterotrait-monomethod cells expose shared method variance — [Lancaster glossary](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fas/psych/glossary/multitrait-multimethod_matrix/), [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multitrait-multimethod_matrix) (secondary; original not text-extractable)
- Cronbach & Meehl: "If two tests are presumed to measure the same construct, a correlation between them is predicted"; construct validity is supported by "negligible correlations" with unrelated variables; "To validate a claim that a test measures a construct, a nomological net surrounding the concept must exist" (nomological network = "the interlocking system of laws which constitute a theory") — [Cronbach & Meehl 1955](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Cronbach/construct.htm)

### Inferences
- **Line 118 + table row line 153 ("Convergent hypotheses state a value expected from an established relation, such as the scaling of diameters at a bifurcation"; Variable "none") — QUESTIONABLE (terminology) / ERROR (no decision rule).** In COSMIN, convergent = association with another instrument measuring the same/similar construct. The Finet ratio/exponent prediction is a hypothesis about an internal relationship among the feature's own components (COSMIN definition's "internal relationships") or a law in the nomological network (Cronbach & Meehl). Further, lines 125-126 define confirmation only for directional and null hypotheses; for a point-value hypothesis "the null value" is undefined, so this row currently has no confirmation rule. Fix: relabel as "internal-relationship" or "nomological (scaling-law)" hypothesis, and give an acceptance band, e.g. "median Finet ratio within 0.60-0.72" or "exponent within the between-study range of the meta-analysis" (taylor2024murray). Also note it is partly mathematical (ratios of the same diameters), so it tests diameter measurement consistency more than anatomy.
- **A genuine convergent hypothesis is available but placed elsewhere.** Tracer vs Medis features of the same scans (line 66) is the closest COSMIN 9a / MTMM monotrait-heteromethod comparison (different method, same construct). The thesis treats it as reliability/agreement (ICC). That is defensible (COSMIN would call method agreement "measurement error/reliability" when the comparator measures the identical quantity), but a convergent hypothesis with a magnitude (e.g. r ≥ 0.50 or ≥ 0.70 between tracer and Medis curvature) could be added to Table tab:method-validity. FINE as is; optional improvement.
- **Lines 119-120 + row line 143 (discriminant: LDL, smoking, family history; "no link to coronary shape has been reported") — QUESTIONABLE.** (a) Campbell & Fiske / de Vet discriminant hypotheses rest on a theoretical claim that the constructs are different, not on the absence of reports; the text itself admits (line 209) the cited study did not test these associations. (b) LDL and smoking are not unrelated to the grouping variables already in the nomological net (sex, age, BMI/BSA), so an unadjusted association could arise through confounding; the hypothesis must state whether it is adjusted. (c) The stated purpose, detecting a shared error source "such as image quality", is better served by a direct hypothesis on the image-quality variables available at BL (`scan_qualitative_quality`, `bad_quality`, `Image_Quality_reason_*`, `hr_during_scan` only 268 values): e.g. "|association of curvature with image quality| < X" or, in MTMM spirit, "curvature correlates more with sex than with image quality". That is the heterotrait-monomethod logic of Campbell & Fiske. (d) Naming: "discriminant" (Campbell & Fiske) is easily confused with COSMIN's "discriminative" (= known-groups); state explicitly that discriminant here means divergent / unrelated-construct hypotheses.
- **Known-groups rows (sex, hypertension, dominance, ramus, LAD vs RCA) — FINE in type.** These fit COSMIN 9b. COSMIN 9b standard 5 additionally requires describing "important characteristics of the subgroups ... and on which characteristics the groups differ"; this should be reported in results. The LAD vs RCA row (line 146) is a within-person comparison and needs a paired/clustered analysis — minor.
- COSMIN 2018 allows giving convergent evidence more weight than dissimilar-construct evidence, which supports the author's idea of differential weighting (see Q4), but in COSMIN the weighting is by type of hypothesis, not by literature strength.

### Gaps
- Could not verify Campbell & Fiske's exact wording from the original (scanned PDF); claims rest on secondary summaries.
- de Vet et al. 2011's own definitions of convergent/discriminant/known-groups were not read directly.

## Q3. Is the 75% criterion meant per instrument with many hypotheses; is it valid with 2-3 hypotheses per feature; is "all strong confirmed" + 75% coherent?

### Takeaway
The 75% rule (Terwee 2007; Prinsen 2018) is a rating rule for an instrument's whole body of hypothesis results, typically pooled across studies, with sub-groups ≥ 50 patients; COSMIN declined to set a minimum number of hypotheses. Applying it per feature with 2-3 hypotheses makes it degenerate (it equals "all confirmed"), and the count depends on undeclared choices (adjusted vs unadjusted, one or several variables per row, primary vs alternative definitions). The decision rules (lines 248-250) then use a different rule ("all strong confirmed") for retention, with 75% only "considered sufficient", so the two rules can disagree and no tie-break is specified.

### Cited Findings
- Terwee 2007 Table 1 construct validity: "+ Specific hypotheses were formulated AND at least 75% of the results are in accordance with these hypotheses"; "− Less than 75% of hypotheses were confirmed, despite adequate design and methods"; text: "positive rating ... if hypotheses are specified in advance and at least 75% of the results are in correspondence with these hypotheses, in (sub)groups of at least 50 patients" — [Terwee 2007](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2901670/267479.pdf)
- Terwee 2007 introduction: "no criteria have been defined for how many hypotheses should be confirmed to justify that a questionnaire has good validity" (motivation for the 75% rule, which the authors present as a proposal) — [Terwee 2007](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2901670/267479.pdf)
- Prinsen 2018: "When at least 75% of the results are in accordance with the hypotheses, the summary result is rated as 'sufficient'"; Table 1 footnote: "The results of all studies should be taken together" — [Prinsen 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5891568)
- COSMIN v2.0 (2024) Table 1: "+ ≥ 75% of the results is in accordance with predefined hypotheses"; "− ≥ 75% of the results deviates from predefined hypotheses" (i.e. a 25-75% zone is inconsistent, not insufficient) — [Mokkink 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11541334/)
- COSMIN 2012: "The more specific the hypotheses are and the more hypotheses are being tested, the more evidence is gathered ... However, the COSMIN panel considered it not possible to formulate standards for the amount of hypotheses that need to be tested" — [COSMIN manual 2012](https://faculty.ksu.edu.sa/sites/default/files/cosmin_checklist_manual_v9.pdf)
- COSMIN 2018: each result (e.g. each correlation) "could be considered as a single study"; worked example: 11 of 13 results sufficient = 85%; and "We do not grade the evidence per hypothesis or per study design, because otherwise high evidence for hypotheses testing can easily be reached"; reviewers may "put more emphasis on" convergent results when results are inconsistent — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Line 249 (75% per feature, citing terwee2007quality and prinsen2018cosmin) — QUESTIONABLE.** The citation is accurate as to the threshold, and the thesis already concedes coarseness. But with 3 hypotheses, 2/3 = 67% fails; with 2, 1/2 = 50% fails; so 75% = unanimity. The rule is also ill-defined until the unit of counting is fixed: curvature has 3 rows but up to 6 variables (male; antihypertensive + SBP; LDL + smoking + family history), each estimated unadjusted and adjusted (line 124), giving 2-12 "results". Fix: prespecify the counting unit (one result per row, primary estimand only), report the k/n count rather than a percentage, and either drop 75% per feature or apply it across all features' hypotheses pooled (closer to COSMIN's "results taken together"), with per-feature judgement made qualitatively. Alternatively use COSMIN v2.0's three-way rating (+ / inconsistent / −).
- **Lines 248-250 (retain when all strong-graded hypotheses confirmed; 75% "considered sufficient"; failed weak/moderate hypotheses do not lead to rejection) — QUESTIONABLE (incoherent combination).** The retention rule ignores the 75% result; a feature can be "retained unchanged" with 1/3 confirmed if the 1 is the only strong one. Taper's only "strong" row concerns absolute diameters, not taper itself (line 145), so taper's retention could rest on a hypothesis about a different quantity; the angle and diameter ratios have no strong row at all, so their retention reduces to ICC and R². COSMIN supports weighting stronger hypotheses (convergent > dissimilar), so the idea is not foreign, but the thesis should state one rule. Fix: e.g. "construct validity sufficient if all strong hypotheses AND ≥ 75% of all hypotheses for the feature (counted per row, primary estimand) are in accordance; insufficient if ≤ 25%; otherwise inconsistent", and specify what each rating implies for retention.
- Terwee's "(sub)groups of at least 50" is easily met in this cohort except possibly ramus intermedius (line 149) and left dominance (line 151); check cell sizes.

### Gaps
- No methodological source was found that validates the 75% threshold empirically; Terwee 2007 presents it as a proposed criterion and Prinsen 2018 states the guideline was not built on empirical evidence.

## Q4. Is a self-made strong/moderate/weak evidence grade a recognized practice? What established frameworks exist?

### Takeaway
COSMIN does use a graded "quality of evidence" (modified GRADE: high/moderate/low/very low), but it grades confidence in the measurement-property result (downgrading for risk of bias, inconsistency, imprecision, indirectness), not the prior evidence for the hypotheses. Grading the strength of the literature behind each hypothesis is not a COSMIN step; COSMIN only says hypotheses "can be based on literature ... and (clinical) experiences". The author-made grade is therefore a non-standard but transparent device; its criteria overlap with GRADE domains (consistency, indirectness), and borrowing that vocabulary would make it recognisable.

### Cited Findings
- COSMIN 2018: "Using a modified GRADE approach, the quality of the evidence is graded as high, moderate, low, or very low"; four factors: "(1) risk of bias ... (2) inconsistency ... (3) imprecision (i.e. total sample size) ... (4) indirectness (i.e. evidence from different populations than the population of interest)"; publication bias not used "because of a lack of registries"; levels defined as confidence that "the true measurement property lies close to that of the estimate" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf); same in [Prinsen 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5891568)
- COSMIN 2018 on indirectness for construct validity: "One can consider downgrading for indirectness for construct validity ... when the evidence is considered weak. For example when the evidence is only based on comparisons with PROMs measuring different constructs or only based on differences between extremely different groups" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)
- COSMIN 2018: "Hypotheses can be based on literature (including the included studies in the review) and (clinical) experiences of the review team members" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)
- Cronbach & Meehl: when a prediction fails, "any link in the chain may be wrong" — the test, the theory (nomological network), or the design — [Cronbach & Meehl 1955](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Cronbach/construct.htm)

### Inferences
- **Section sec:method-validity-evidence (lines 158-217) — QUESTIONABLE, not an error.** Calling it "Level of Evidence" invites confusion with GRADE/COSMIN quality of evidence (which concerns the validity result, assessed after the data). The section honestly says it is the author's judgement from a non-systematic search (line 165). Fixes: (a) rename to "strength of prior support for each hypothesis"; (b) cite Cronbach & Meehl's point that confidence in the nomological link determines how much a failed prediction says about the instrument versus the theory, which is the real rationale for weighting; (c) express the criteria in GRADE-like domains already implicit in the text: consistency (lines 162-164), indirectness (referral populations, 2D angiography, bend counts vs curvature per mm: lines 192-194), and publication bias (line 196); (d) separately, if the thesis wants a COSMIN-style overall statement, grade the confidence in each feature's validity result after analysis with the modified GRADE domains (risk of bias, inconsistency, imprecision, indirectness).
- **Internal consistency of the grading — QUESTIONABLE.** "Strong" requires ≥ 3 independent cohorts, ≥ 1 CCTA, adjusted, same direction (line 162). For curvature-sex (line 141), Table tab:method-validity-sex lists 2 peer-reviewed CCTA studies with a bends count or arc-chord definition plus 1 non-peer-reviewed CCTA preprint; most are referral cohorts with a different construct definition. Under COSMIN's indirectness concept this would be downgraded. The grade is "consistent direction" not "direct evidence for this measure in this population", which the text acknowledges (lines 191-196), but the label "strong" then drives the retention rule (line 248). Consider a separate indirectness flag.
- Diameter-ratio dominance graded "moderate" while resting on "a single small angiographic study" plus reasoning (line 215) contradicts the stated criterion for moderate (line 163: "mostly replicated ... or one study with a smaller replication"); by the thesis's own rule this is weak. ERROR (internal inconsistency).
- Scaling exponent graded "moderate" from a meta-analysis of 1,070 trees with wide heterogeneity (line 217) — fine under the stated rule.

### Gaps
- Found no published framework specifically for grading prior support of construct-validity hypotheses; this appears to be a gap in the methodological literature rather than a missed standard.

## Q5. When and by whom should hypotheses be formulated; is it acceptable to transfer hypotheses from referral-population literature to a general population?

### Takeaway
COSMIN 2012 and Terwee 2007 require hypotheses formulated a priori ("before data collection"), as specifically as possible, by the investigators; COSMIN 2018 (for reviews) moves formulation to the review team and tolerates hypotheses added later if transparently reported. Literature-based hypotheses are explicitly allowed; transferring them across populations is a recognised source of indirectness that should be reflected in the expected magnitudes and in how failures are interpreted.

### Cited Findings
- COSMIN 2012 item 4: "Were hypotheses regarding correlations or mean differences formulated a priori (i.e. before data collection)?"; "preferably stated in the methods section"; "Without specific hypotheses, the risk of bias is high because retrospectively it is tempting to think up alternative explanations for low correlations instead of concluding that an instrument may not be valid" — [COSMIN manual 2012](https://faculty.ksu.edu.sa/sites/default/files/cosmin_checklist_manual_v9.pdf)
- Terwee 2007: "Construct validity should be assessed by testing predefined hypotheses ... These hypotheses need to be as specific as possible"; "Practically all authors of instrument evaluation studies conclude that their instrument is valid, whereas objective counting of the number of hypotheses that were confirmed frequently indicates otherwise" — [Terwee 2007](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2901670/267479.pdf)
- COSMIN 2018: review team should formulate hypotheses; "We consider it not a problem if the hypotheses are not all defined a priori, as long as the hypotheses are reported in the review, making the methodology transparent" (context: systematic reviews) — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)
- Indirectness = "evidence from different populations than the population of interest" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf); [Mokkink 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11541334/)
- Cronbach & Meehl: altering the network to fit discordant observations "is, in effect, redefining the construct," and the revised interpretation needs fresh data — [Cronbach & Meehl 1955](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Cronbach/construct.htm)

### Inferences
- **Line 115 ("formulated from the literature before the geometric data were available") — FINE in principle**, matches COSMIN item 4. But the claim needs documentation: line 254 ("fixed on [date]") is a placeholder, and the table cites a 2026 study and a revised grading, suggesting the hypotheses were edited over time. Fix: give the date and the location of the frozen version (e.g. a commit hash in the CGPS_statistics- repo or OSF timestamp), and list any hypothesis added or changed after features were computed. Also clarify "before the geometric data were available" vs the decision rules' "before the outcome was linked" (line 247): the first is the relevant one for construct validity.
- **Referral-population evidence applied to a general population (lines 192-196) — FINE to use, QUESTIONABLE as handled.** The text acknowledges indirectness but uses it only to drop magnitude ("only the direction was tested", line 195). COSMIN's logic is the other way round: indirectness lowers confidence in the hypothesis/result; it does not justify dropping the magnitude requirement. Fix: state a population-adjusted expected magnitude (e.g. "smaller than in referral cohorts, at least 0.1 SD") and treat a failure as ambiguous between instrument and transferred theory (Cronbach & Meehl).

### Gaps
- No COSMIN text addresses validation of image-derived (non-PROM) features specifically; applicability is by analogy. The thesis cites kessler2015qib for imaging biomarker terminology; whether QIBA terminology has its own construct-validity guidance was not checked here.

## Q6. Does adjusting for sex, age and BSA change what a known-groups (sex) hypothesis tests?

### Takeaway
Yes. A known-groups hypothesis concerns the difference between groups that are known to differ in the construct; adjusting for a variable on the pathway (body size → vessel size/curvature) changes the estimand from the total sex difference to a direct (body-size-independent) difference, i.e. a different hypothesis. Line 124 also literally prescribes adjusting the sex hypothesis for sex.

### Cited Findings
- Schisterman et al. 2009: overadjustment bias is "control for an intermediate variable (or a descending proxy for an intermediate variable) on a causal path from exposure to outcome"; unnecessary adjustment is control for a variable that "does not affect bias ... but may affect its precision" — [Schisterman 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/2744485)
- COSMIN 9b standard 5: known-groups studies should report "important characteristics of the groups ... and it should be clear on which characteristics the groups differ, for example age, gender, disease characteristics" — [COSMIN user manual 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Line 124 ("Each association was estimated unadjusted and adjusted for sex, age and body surface area") — ERROR in wording.** For the sex hypotheses (lines 141, 145, 148, 152) sex cannot be a covariate; for the BSA hypotheses BSA cannot. Fix: "adjusted for age and, where not the variable of interest, sex and BSA".
- **Two estimands, no rule for which one decides — QUESTIONABLE.** BSA is plausibly a mediator of sex differences in vessel diameter and possibly curvature (the text's own "smaller heart" mechanism, line 200). The unadjusted estimate tests the total group difference (what most cited studies report, e.g. Li 2011/2022 ORs adjusted for risk factors but not BSA); the BSA-adjusted estimate tests a direct effect (matching e.g. Medrano-Gracia's "after adjustment for height", line 148). Per Schisterman this is not "bias" if the direct effect is the intended estimand, but each row must prespecify which estimand constitutes the test, matched to the estimand of the source study, otherwise there are two chances to confirm (forking paths) and the 75% count is ambiguous. Fix: add a column "confirmatory estimand: total / adjusted for X" to Table tab:method-validity; report the other as secondary.
- **Age adjustment — FINE.** Age is plausibly a confounder of sex composition in a cohort and of hypertension; adjusting is reasonable and standard. For the hypertension hypothesis age adjustment is clearly needed (hypertension and tortuosity both rise with age), and this should be the prespecified estimand.
- **Discriminant rows** should likewise state their adjustment set (see Q2): an unadjusted LDL-curvature association may reflect sex/age confounding and would wrongly "fail".

### Gaps
- No COSMIN text addresses covariate adjustment in known-groups analyses explicitly; the argument above rests on general causal-inference principles (Schisterman).
