## Q1. Is tracer vs Medis "reliability" (COSMIN), and what does QIBA call it?

### Takeaway
No. COSMIN defines reliability over repeated measurements with the same instrument (test–retest, inter-rater, intra-rater) in patients who have not changed; comparing two different instruments is either criterion validity (if one is a gold standard) or, without one, a construct-validity hypothesis about the relation to another instrument's scores. In QIBA terms, a different measurement system or analysis algorithm is a reproducibility condition; with two conditions it is usually summarised as agreement (CCC, Bland–Altman). The section calls it "agreement between the two methods" (L66) but files it under "Reliability" and the opening says "reliability, on repeated measurements of the same participants" (L25), which is internally inconsistent.

### Cited Findings
- COSMIN extended definition of reliability: "The extent to which scores for patients who have not changed are the same for repeated measurement under several conditions: for example, using different sets of items from the same HR-PROs (internal consistency), over time (test–retest) by different persons on the same occasion (interrater) or by the same persons ... on different occasions (intrarater)." Reliability (the measurement property) is "The proportion of the total variance in the measurements which is because of 'true' differences among patients." — [Mokkink et al. 2010, J Clin Epidemiol 63:737-745, Table](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2802121/257066.pdf)
- COSMIN criterion validity: "The degree to which the scores of an HR-PRO instrument are an adequate reflection of a 'gold standard'." Construct validity: "The degree to which the scores ... are consistent with hypotheses (for instance with regard to internal relationships, relationships to scores of other instruments, or differences between relevant groups) ..." — [Mokkink et al. 2010](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2802121/257066.pdf)
- COSMIN notes that reproducibility, repeatability, agreement and reliability are used interchangeably in the literature, which is why the taxonomy standardised them — [Mokkink et al. 2010](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2802121/257066.pdf)
- QIBA: "Repeatability refers to the precision of a QIB measured under identical conditions" (same procedure, system, image-analysis algorithm, location, short period); "reproducibility refers to the precision of a QIB measured under different experimental conditions" (e.g. different measurement systems, imaging methods, sites). With two conditions, agreement is summarised by the CCC (Lawrence & Lin) — [Statistical considerations for repeatability and reproducibility of QIBs (review citing Kessler 2015 and Raunig 2015), PMC9667479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9667479/)
- QIBA metrology: "Repeatability is a measurement of precision that occurs with identical or near-identical conditions"; reproducibility applies "when location, operator, measuring system, or other factors differ"; "Correlation is a weak criterion for assessing technical performance", agreement with an existing procedure is "stronger evidence" — [Sullivan et al. 2015, Radiology 277:813-825, PMC4666097](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)
- Bland & Altman: the product-moment correlation between two methods is no indicator of agreement ("It is no such thing"); "Correlation depends on the range of the true quantity in the sample"; limits of agreement d ± 2s; "the repeatabilities of the two methods of measurement limit the amount of agreement which is possible" — [Bland & Altman 1986, Lancet 1:307-310 (author's reprint)](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/ba.htm)

### Inferences
- Design (a) is a method-comparison / inter-method agreement study (QIBA: reproducibility across analysis software). Since the thesis explicitly says Medis is a reference, not ground truth (and the header note forbids calling it "accuracy" or "error"), COSMIN criterion validity does not apply either; the COSMIN-consistent label is a convergent construct-validity hypothesis ("tracer and Medis features agree, ICC ≥ x"), or simply "agreement between methods" reported with Bland–Altman statistics.
- An ICC is acceptable for inter-method agreement (it is not a Pearson r: absolute agreement penalises bias), but Bland–Altman's point that agreement depends on each method's own repeatability means the inter-method ICC cannot exceed what the repeatability of the two methods allows; without an intra-method repeatability estimate, a low inter-method ICC cannot be attributed to either method.

### Gaps
- Kessler 2015 full text (exact definitions incl. "agreement") was not retrieved; definitions above come via the QIBA review and Sullivan 2015, which cite it.
- GRRAS (Kottner 2011) full text not retrieved; its exact definitions of agreement vs reliability could not be verified. Citation: J Clin Epidemiol 64(1):96-106, doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2010.03.002 (also co-published in Int J Nurs Stud 48:661-671) — [record](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/guidelines-for-reporting-reliability-and-agreement-studies-grras-/)

## Q2. Can BL vs FU 10 years apart count as test–retest reliability?

### Takeaway
No. COSMIN's Risk of Bias standards ask whether patients were stable on the construct between the repeated measurements, whether the interval was appropriate, and whether conditions were similar; QIBA test–retest assumes the true value is unchanged and uses "coffee break" intervals of minutes to hours. Ten years with ageing, new plaque and FU-only nitroglycerin fails all three. The correct description is a longitudinal stability / tracking correlation (or "10-year concordance"); its (1 − ICC) is not measurement error.

### Cited Findings
- COSMIN RoB tool design standards: "Were patients stable in the time between the repeated measurements on the construct to be measured?"; "Was the time interval between the repeated measurements appropriate?"; "Were the measurement condition[s] similar for the repeated measurements" — [Mokkink et al. 2020, BMC Med Res Methodol 20:293, Table 5, PMC7712525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712525)
- Under COSMIN's "worst score counts" principle, one inadequate item downgrades the whole reliability study — [COSMIN RoB checklist summary (2018 checklist)](https://cdn-links.lww.com/permalink/cjp/a/cjp_2021_10_01_pate_cjp-d-21-00083_sdc2.pdf)
- COSMIN measurement error: "The systematic and random error of a patient's score that is not attributed to true changes in the construct to be measured" — [Mokkink et al. 2010](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2802121/257066.pdf)
- QIBA: test–retest scans should be done over a short period so the true value does not change; when intervals are long, the model should account for possible true-value changes; in longitudinal settings the correlation between measurements generally falls as the interval grows (Obuchowski 2019) — [PMC9667479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9667479/)
- QIBA: "Precision studies performed with repeatability conditions are sometimes called test-retest studies"; human test–retest are often "coffee break" experiments with "only a short break (eg, on the order of minutes or hours) between scans"; repeatability needs no truth "since they are acquired with an assumption of no change in the measured object" — [Sullivan et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)

### Inferences
- The 10-year BL–FU ICC is a lower bound on measurement reliability (it mixes measurement error, between-person differences in true change, and protocol/scanner differences). It is useful as a stability statistic in its own right (how well baseline geometry tracks 10-year geometry), which is relevant for interpreting a BL-only predictor, but it should not be called test–retest, nor drive the "measurement error" term of Eq. (method-validity-split) or the ICC ≥ 0.75 rule (L248).
- Because the FU scan was taken in people who in part developed plaque, the FU features in them are partly outcome-affected; the section's choice to include everyone (L67) avoids selecting on outcome but does not remove this effect.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that gives a maximum acceptable test–retest interval for CT-derived anatomy; COSMIN leaves "appropriate" to judgment per construct.
- No published repeat-CCTA (short interval) repeatability values for centerline curvature/taper/bifurcation angle were searched in this task.

## Q3. Is ICC(A,1) the right form; consistency vs agreement; sample dependence; what to report alongside

### Takeaway
ICC(A,1) (absolute agreement, single measure) is defensible and matches Koo & Li's advice for test–retest/intra-rater, but the model for both designs is better described as two-way mixed (fixed occasions / the only two methods of interest); the value is identical. Because the ICC depends on between-subject heterogeneity, COSMIN treats measurement error as a separate property and QIBA's primary repeatability metrics are wSD / wCV / RC; Bland–Altman limits of agreement are the standard for method comparison. Reporting the ICC alone is incomplete.

### Cited Findings
- Koo & Li: "2-way mixed-effects model should also be used in test-retest reliability study"; "absolute agreement definition should always be chosen for both test-retest and intrarater reliability studies"; 2-way random is the choice when raters are sampled from a larger population, mixed "if the selected raters are the only raters of interest"; "both ICC estimates and their 95% confidence intervals should be reported"; recommend "at least 30 heterogeneous samples" — [Koo & Li 2016, J Chiropr Med 15:155-163, PMC4913118](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/)
- McGraw & Wong 1996 define ICC forms by model (one-way random, two-way random, two-way fixed/mixed), type (single vs mean of k) and definition (consistency vs absolute agreement); for consistency the column (rater/occasion) variance is excluded from the denominator, for absolute agreement it is not; "the numerical values produced for the two way models are identical for random and mixed models. However, the interpretations under the two models are different" — [IBM/SPSS note on McGraw & Wong](https://www.ibm.com/support/pages/node/418821); [UCLA OARC, Choosing an ICC](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/spss/library/spss-library-choosing-an-intraclass-correlation-coefficient/)
- COSMIN RoB, reliability: "very good" when "ICC calculated; the model or formula was described, and matches the study design and the data"; Pearson/Spearman only "doubtful" without evidence of no systematic difference. Measurement error: "very good" when "SEM, SDC, LoA or CV calculated; the model or formula for the SEM/SDC is described" — [Mokkink et al. 2020, Tables 6-7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712525)
- de Vet et al. 2006: "reliability parameters are highly dependent on the heterogeneity of the study sample, while the agreement parameters, based on measurement error, are more a pure characteristic of the measurement instrument"; reliability parameters fit questions about distinguishing persons, agreement parameters fit measuring change — [de Vet, Terwee, Knol, Bouter 2006, J Clin Epidemiol 59:1033-1039 (record)](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/9794d9c2-488a-4313-bd2c-f2b20f5ce609); quotes via search snippet of [the full text](https://researchgate.net/profile/Lex-Bouter/publication/6814402_When_to_use_agreement_versus_reliability_measures/links/5bdeb3034585150b2b9d4ac5/When-to-use-agreement-versus-reliability-measures.pdf) (full text not opened, 403)
- QIBA: wSD is the most common repeatability metric; wCV when wSD scales with the mean (log-normal model, Raunig 2015); ICC "depends on the between-subject variance ... a sample with greater true-value spread can give a higher ICC even with the same measurement error", so subjects must represent the study population; ICC sampling distribution is complex, bootstrap CIs are often used; Bland–Altman plots show how variability changes across the range — [PMC9667479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9667479/)
- Bland & Altman 1986: "Correlation depends on the range of the true quantity in the sample"; coefficient of repeatability: "We expect 95% of differences to be less than two standard deviations" — [Bland & Altman 1986](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/ba.htm)
- Bland & Altman 1999 (Stat Methods Med Res 8:135-160) extends LoA to replicates, non-constant variance etc. — mentioned in [Bland's citation-history page](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/history.htm)

### Inferences
- In this thesis the features enter regressions after (implicitly) standardisation and the question is ranking of persons/segments, so consistency (ICC(C,1)) is the relevant quantity for attenuation of a coefficient, while absolute agreement (ICC(A,1)) is the relevant one for interchangeability of tracer and Medis. Reporting both plus the bias makes the gap interpretable (e.g. the FU nitro effect, L72, or a uniform age effect over 10 years appears as A < C).
- The clean-baseline cohort is a restricted (plaque-free, mostly lower-risk) population and Medis cases are mostly plaque-at-BL; ICCs from the two are not comparable, and the clean-baseline ICC will be lower than a general-population ICC with the same error. SEM/LoA in feature units are needed to separate "noisy tool" from "homogeneous population".

### Gaps
- McGraw & Wong 1996 (Psychol Methods 1:30-46, with correction p. 390) and Shrout & Fleiss 1979 (Psychol Bull 86:420-428) originals were not retrieved; their content is cited via secondary sources (SPSS/UCLA) and Koo & Li.

## Q4. Koo & Li thresholds: point estimate or CI; is ICC ≥ 0.75 a defensible fixed threshold?

### Takeaway
Koo & Li state explicitly that the bands should be applied to the 95% CI, not the point estimate; the section applies them to an unspecified ICC (presumably the point estimate) and reports no CI. The 0.75 cut is a rule of thumb; COSMIN's criterion for sufficient reliability is 0.70, and acceptability of agreement is a matter of substantive judgment (Bland & Altman), so a fixed threshold needs a stated rationale.

### Cited Findings
- Koo & Li bands: <0.5 poor, 0.5-0.75 moderate, 0.75-0.9 good, >0.90 excellent; "the 95% confident interval of the ICC estimate (not the ICC estimate itself) should be used as the basis" — [Koo & Li 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/)
- COSMIN criteria for good measurement properties (Prinsen 2018, adapted tables): reliability sufficient (+) if ICC or weighted kappa ≥ 0.70; measurement error sufficient if SDC or LoA < MIC — [adapted table, PMC6500280](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6500280/table/T3); original: Prinsen et al. 2018, Qual Life Res 27:1147-1157 (not opened)
- Bland & Altman: "How far apart measurements can be without causing difficulties will be a question of judgment" — [Bland & Altman 1986](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/ba.htm)
- QIBA metrology (for a different quantity, concordance of reference with true value) uses a much stricter 0.99, illustrating that thresholds are purpose-specific — [Sullivan et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)

### Inferences
- The section cites terwee2007quality and prinsen2018cosmin for its 75% hypotheses rule (L249) but uses Koo & Li 0.75 for reliability (L248); a COSMIN-aligned reader would expect 0.70, or a stated reason for 0.75.
- A purpose-based justification exists: under classical measurement error, a slope on a standardised predictor is attenuated by about the reliability ratio, so ICC 0.75 bounds attenuation at ~25% (this is a standard regression-dilution result; no source was retrieved in this task, so cite e.g. Fuller or Carroll et al. if used).
- Sample-size/CI note: with a person-level CI, the lower bound may fall below 0.75 even when the point estimate exceeds it; the rule should say which is used.

### Gaps
- Kottner 2011 GRRAS item wording on statistical uncertainty and on interpretation thresholds not verified (full text not accessed).

## Q5. Other points in the reliability logic a methodologist would flag

### Takeaway
The "biased in opposite directions" argument (L68-70) is reasonable in spirit but overstated: the inter-method ICC leaves out scan-to-scan variation (so it overstates rescan reliability) yet can be lowered by systematic algorithm differences; the 10-year ICC is lowered by true change only if the change differs between persons, and also by protocol/scanner differences; the two estimates come from different populations, so they do not bracket a single true reliability. The variance partition (L235-242) and the decision rule (L248) inherit the test–retest mislabel.

### Cited Findings
- Agreement between two methods is limited by each method's own repeatability (Bland & Altman 1986) — [Bland & Altman](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/ba.htm)
- ICC depends on between-subject variance, so subjects must represent the population (QIBA review) — [PMC9667479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9667479/); reliability depends on sample heterogeneity (de Vet 2006) — [record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/9794d9c2-488a-4313-bd2c-f2b20f5ce609)
- COSMIN's 7-element research question requires stating which components are repeated and which sources of variation are varied (elements 5-6) — [Mokkink et al. 2020, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712525)
- COSMIN RoB asks whether measurement conditions were similar (relevant to FU-only nitroglycerin) — [Mokkink et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712525)
- COSMIN 2020 extends the RoB tool for reliability and measurement error beyond PROMs to clinician-reported, performance-based instruments and laboratory values — [Mokkink et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712525)

### Inferences
- Using COSMIN's element framework would force the section to say, per design, what is varied: design (a) varies the software only (same scan, same day); design (b) varies scan, scanner/protocol, nitro, and 10 years of biology. Writing this out makes clear that neither design isolates measurement repeatability of the tracer.
- Medis was "probably only checked on the contoured part" and its trees are almost all from plaque-at-BL scans (CLAUDE.md / memory); this is a spectrum difference from the clean-baseline target population and should be stated where the inter-method ICC is reported.
- Suggested replacement for L64-71 (sketch): "Measurement precision was examined in three designs, named after the QIBA terminology \cite{kessler2015qib}: repeatability, by rerunning the tracer on the same scan with perturbed start points; reproducibility across software, by comparing tracer and Medis features on the same baseline scans (agreement between methods \cite{bland1986}); and long-term stability, by comparing baseline and follow-up features about ten years apart. Only the first meets the condition of an unchanged true value \cite{mokkink2020rob}. For each design the mean difference, the within-subject SD, the 95% limits of agreement and ICC(A,1) and ICC(C,1) with 95% confidence intervals were reported \cite{koo2016icc, mcgraw1996icc}."

### Gaps
- Whether the tracer is deterministic given a seed (i.e. whether an intra-method rerun design is meaningful) is a codebase question, not checked here.
