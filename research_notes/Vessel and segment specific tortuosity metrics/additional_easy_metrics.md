# Additional Simple Tortuosity Metrics (Beyond the Existing Exclusion List)

## Key Question 1: What are the exact definitions of Bullitt-lineage "Relative Length," "Triangular Index," and "Product-of-Angle-Distance," and how do they differ from SOAM/ICM/DM?

### Takeaway
Beyond SOAM, ICM and the arc/chord ratio (DM), the Bullitt-lineage tortuosity battery includes three further simple, closed-form descriptors with exact formulas: Relative Length (RL, the *reciprocal-style* chord/arc ratio, no "minus 1"), the Triangular Index (TI, a local sliding-triangle straightness average, not a global end-to-end ratio), and Product-of-Angle-Distance (PAD), a composite score defined as SOAM multiplied by RL.

### Cited Findings
- Relative Length: RL = l / lc, where l is the Euclidean (chord) distance between the vessel's two endpoints and lc is the curve (arc) length — i.e., chord/arc, the reciprocal-style companion to the excluded arc/chord DM/TI-ratio metric, reported without the "-1" normalization — [Assessing ML Models for Predicting Age with Intracranial Vessel Tortuosity, PMC10669197](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10669197) (Table 1, fetched directly from the article text).
- Triangular Index: TI = (1/n) · Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ (aᵢ + bᵢ)/cᵢ. For each of n sampled points along the vessel, a triangle is built with an apex at that point and two base vertices at a fixed offset distance ahead/behind on the centerline; aᵢ and bᵢ are the two "sides" from the apex to the base vertices and cᵢ is the base (chord) of that local triangle. The ratio (aᵢ+bᵢ)/cᵢ is ≈1 for a locally straight vessel and increases with local bending; the index is the mean of this ratio over all sampled points — [PMC10669197](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10669197); construction method also described in the [ResearchGate figure summary](https://researchgate.net/figure/The-tortuosity-descriptor-calculation-a-RL-relative-length-b-SOAM-sum-of-angle_fig2_332434245).
- Product-of-Angle-Distance: PAD = SOAM × RL, i.e. the product of the sum-of-angles metric and the relative-length ratio, used as a single combined score in cerebral-aneurysm tortuosity studies — [PMC10669197](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10669197).
- These four descriptors (RL, SOAM, TI, PAD) are reported together as the tortuosity feature set for basilar/middle-cerebral-artery tortuosity-aneurysm association studies — [Computer-aided analysis of middle cerebral artery tortuosity, J Neurosurg 130(5)](https://thejns.org/view/journals/j-neurosurg/130/5/article-p1478.xml); [ResearchGate figure, basilar artery tracking](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/llustration-of-basilar-artery-BA-tracking-and-tortuosity-descriptors-calculation-RL_fig1_341365479).

### Inferences
- RL is mathematically the reciprocal of (1 + DM) where DM is the already-catalogued arc/chord-minus-1 distance metric; it is listed here because it is named and used as a distinct standalone descriptor (feeding into PAD) in this literature, not because its underlying ratio is novel. Report writers should treat RL as "same ratio, different normalization/direction, different name" rather than as fully independent information content from DM.
- TI is genuinely distinct in form from SOAM/DM: it is a *local, triangle-based* straightness average computed over a sliding window of three points, not a global angle-sum or global arc/chord ratio, so it captures a different (more local, smoothed) notion of bending than SOAM's point-to-point turning angles.
- PAD is a simple composite (multiplicative combination) of two other metrics rather than an independently derived geometric quantity; its main value is as a single scalar that jointly penalizes both angular turning and elongation.

### Gaps
- The exact offset distance used to place the two triangle base vertices around each apex point in TI was not found in the sources retrieved (likely a fixed arc-length step, as in the original Bullitt centerline-sampling scheme); the fetched paper's Table 1 gives the formula but not this parameter.
- Could not directly access the original Bullitt et al. papers (e.g. Bullitt et al. 2003, "Measuring Tortuosity of the Intracerebral Vasculature from MRA Images") to confirm these are verbatim quotes vs. a later paper's restatement; the PMC10669197 (2023 MDPI) article was the primary source actually read in full, so these formulas are verified against a secondary/tertiary restatement, not the original Bullitt publication.

## Key Question 2: What is "tortuosity density" in retinal vascular imaging, and what is its exact definition?

### Takeaway
"Tortuosity density" as defined by Grisan et al. and used throughout the retinal-imaging literature is the same construct as the already-catalogued "Grisan et al.'s turn-segmented tortuosity index" (subsegmenting a vessel at successive curvature-sign changes and summing local arc/chord ratios), so it is **not** a genuinely new metric; a handful of closely related but distinctly named variants (angle-tortuosity, distance-tortuosity, inverse-radius-tortuosity) appear in a recent review but could only be sourced from a paraphrased summary, not verified formulas.

### Cited Findings
- Tortuosity Density (TD) was introduced by Grisan et al. to evaluate tortuosity by "calculating the contributions to tortuosity of uniformly convex or concave arcs": the vessel is divided into subsegments at points where curvature changes sign (n "turns"), and TD sums each subsegment's own arc-to-chord ratio (Lcsᵢ/Lxsᵢ), scaled by (n−1) and normalized by the total centerline length Lc — [search-engine-synthesized description citing Grisan et al., surfaced via multiple secondary sources](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-433X/8/10/258); code implementation exists at [github.com/enrigrisan/RET-Tortuosity](https://github.com/enrigrisan/RET-Tortuosity).
- Grisan's TD was reported as the tortuosity measure most accurate at matching ophthalmologists' perceptual tortuosity ratings, ahead of simpler global measures — synthesized from search results, exact paper not independently fetched.
- A 2024 review (PMC11500049, "A New Retest-Stable Tortuosity Metric for Retinal Vessel Analyses") lists a taxonomy of named retinal tortuosity metrics beyond arc/chord and SOAM/ICM: "angle-tortuosity," "distance tortuosity," "inverse-radius tortuosity," "squared curvature tortuosity," "tortuosity density," and a proprietary "VCI" (Vessel/Vascular Curvature Index) — [PMC11500049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500049/).
  - "Distance tortuosity" is described as an arc-over-chord ratio but with dynamic (not fixed start/end) point selection, choosing points at the vessel's inflection points rather than the two endpoints.
  - "Angle-tortuosity" is described with a formula of the form τ(s) = Σᵢ (lᵢ · ΔΘᵢ) / n, where ΔΘᵢ = π − arccos(aᵢ·bᵢ/(|aᵢ||bᵢ|)) is the angular deviation at the i-th inflection point (between direction vectors aᵢ, bᵢ before/after it), lᵢ is the arc length of the vessel segment associated with that inflection point, and n is the number of inflection points — i.e., a length-weighted mean angular deviation evaluated only at inflection points, rather than SOAM's unweighted sum of all point-to-point turning angles normalized by total path length.
  - "Inverse-radius tortuosity" is described as: compute the local radius of curvature at each point along the vessel (via curve fitting), take its inverse (= local curvature) at every point, and average these inverses over the vessel.
  - "VCI" is described only vaguely as "a proprietary metric based on a measurement of change in angular momentum," with a stated form dL/dt = m·v·r′(t); this reads as either a garbled/non-standard description or a genuinely unusual physics-inspired analogy, and could not be confirmed against a primary source.

### Inferences
- Grisan's Tortuosity Density is, on the evidence gathered, the same idea as the exclusion list's "Grisan et al.'s turn-segmented tortuosity index" (same author, same turn-segmentation-then-arc/chord-sum construction) — this should be treated as already covered, not new.
- "Angle-tortuosity" (length-weighted mean deviation at inflection points, normalized by count of inflection points) is mathematically distinct from SOAM (unweighted sum over *all* sampled points, normalized by total arc length) and from ICM (a count multiplied into the arc/chord ratio); it is simple arithmetic on point coordinates and could be considered a genuinely additional, easy-to-compute score if this formula can be corroborated against a primary source.
- "Inverse-radius tortuosity" (mean of 1/local-radius-of-curvature) is conceptually just "mean curvature computed via local circle/spline fitting" and is very likely equivalent in spirit to the already-excluded κ_a (mean absolute curvature), differing mainly in the discretization method (radius-of-curvature fitting vs. discrete chord-turning-angle approximation). This should probably be treated as a computational variant of an excluded metric rather than new information.

### Gaps
- Direct PDF text extraction failed for both the original Hart et al. 1999 paper ("Measurement and classification of retinal vascular tortuosity," Int. J. Med. Inform. 53:239-252) and an arXiv survey (2111.10255); the tool returned only binary/structural PDF content, not readable prose, so Hart et al.'s own exact wording for "integral curvature," "inflection point count," and "fraction of vessel with high curvature" measures could not be directly quoted. These may or may not be genuinely distinct from ICM/curvature-percentile metrics already on the exclusion list; treat as unverified.
- The exact formula and primary source for Grisan's Tortuosity Density were reconstructed from search-engine synthesis of secondary sources, not a directly fetched paper; flagged as moderate-confidence, abstract/summary-level sourcing only.
- The "angle-tortuosity," "distance-tortuosity," and "inverse-radius-tortuosity" formulas above come from a WebFetch-generated paraphrase of PMC11500049's content, not a verbatim quotation confirmed by a second read of the raw article; treat the exact symbols/subscripts as approximate, and the VCI description as unverified/low-confidence.

## Key Question 3: Are there simple curvature-variability or curve-counting metrics (CV of curvature, SD of turning angles, loop/curve counts) used as easy tortuosity-adjacent scores?

### Takeaway
The literature does use curvature-distribution shape statistics (skewness of a curvature-derived index) and root-mean-square / average-squared curvature as simple alternatives to mean absolute curvature, and the Inflection Count Metric's exact formula (found here) is itself a simple count-based score already on the exclusion list; no standalone "coefficient of variation of curvature" or "SD of turning angles" metric with its own name was found as an established, separately-cited score.

### Cited Findings
- A comparative study of tortuosity measures for wall-shear-stress prediction reports several curvature-based descriptors as simple alternatives to the tortuosity/arc-chord index: "average absolute-curvature, root-mean-squared (RMS) curvature, and average squared-derivative-curvature," stating that curvature-based metrics "consistently perform better than the tortuosity index... explaining a significantly larger proportion of the variation in shear stress between patients" — [Accuracy of vascular tortuosity measures using computational modelling, Scientific Reports / PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/).
- A new Vessel Curvature Index (VCI) is reported to have "a somewhat negatively distributed pattern (coefficient of skewness of −0.52)" when compared against other retest-reliability metrics — i.e., skewness of the metric's *distribution across vessels/subjects* is reported as a validation statistic, not as a per-vessel curvature-variability score itself — [A New Retest-Stable Tortuosity Metric for Retinal Vessel Analyses, PMC11500049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500049/).
- Exact formula for the Inflection Count Metric (ICM), surfaced incidentally while searching for count-based metrics: ICM counts the number of inflection points along the curve, adds 1, multiplies by the total path length, and divides by the end-to-end distance — i.e. ICM = (n_inflections + 1) · L / D — [patent filing "Systems, methods... for analysis of vessel attributes," USPTO 8090164](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8090164). (ICM itself is already on the exclusion list; this formula is reported here only because it clarifies that ICM already *is* the standard "count of curvature sign changes" score, so a separate "loop count" or "number of curves" metric found nowhere in the literature searched would likely just be a rediscovery of ICM's inflection count component.)

### Inferences
- No distinctly-named, independently-used "coefficient of variation of curvature" or "standard deviation of turning angles" metric could be located; the field appears to prefer RMS curvature, average-squared-derivative curvature, and skewness-of-metric-distribution (a validation statistic, not a per-vessel score) over a formal CV-of-curvature index. RMS curvature and average-squared-derivative curvature are arguably simple additions in the same spirit as "peak/percentile local curvature" (already excluded) but are distinguishable as *second-moment* (RMS/variance-like) rather than order-statistic (percentile/max) summaries of the curvature sequence along the vessel — this is a defensible genuinely-additional pair of simple metrics: mean-square curvature (κ_rms = sqrt(mean(κᵢ²))) and its variant "average squared-derivative curvature."
- A standalone "loop count" or "number of curves per vessel" metric, if it exists as a distinctly named score, was not surfaced by these searches; ICM already fills that conceptual niche in the literature that was searched.

### Gaps
- Could not find the exact formula for "average squared-derivative curvature" as distinct from RMS curvature (i.e., whether it takes the derivative of curvature along arc length, κ′(s), and squares/averages that, versus squaring curvature itself); the PMC8764056 source names it but a fetch of its methods section was not performed given the tool-call budget, so this remains a named-but-unverified-formula metric.
- No primary source for a dedicated "coefficient of variation of local radius of curvature" was found; this appears to be a gap in the literature rather than a retrieval failure, since multiple searches for CV-of-curvature phrasing returned no matches.

## Key Question 4: Are there other classic, simple, single-vessel tortuosity indices from peripheral, dialysis-access, umbilical-cord, or varicose-vein literature?

### Takeaway
Obstetric ultrasound has a well-established, genuinely simple and distinct metric, the Umbilical Coiling Index (coils per unit cord length), and vascular surgery has a genuinely distinct *qualitative angle-threshold classification* (tortuosity vs. kinking vs. coiling, based on the deflection angle at the sharpest bend) plus a distinct *clinical severity banding* of the ordinary arc/chord tortuosity index (CASS iliac staging); no dedicated tortuosity-specific formula was found for dialysis-access vessels or varicose veins beyond generic severity scoring systems that do not isolate tortuosity mathematically.

### Cited Findings
- Umbilical Coiling Index (UCI): postnatally, UCI = (total number of complete umbilical vascular coils) / (cord length in cm); antenatally (Degani et al.'s formula), UCI = 1 / (distance between two adjacent coils, floating in amniotic fluid) — [The umbilical coiling index, a review of the literature, PubMed 16076615](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16076615/); [Umbilical cord coiling: case report and review, PMC6159302](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6159302/).
- Normal UCI is reported as 0.17 (± 0.009) coils/cm, with hypercoiled defined as UCI above the 90th percentile and hypocoiled as UCI below the 10th percentile — [PMC6159302](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6159302/); [Pathophysiological significance of abnormal umbilical cord coiling index, Sebire 2007](https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/uog.5180).
- Weibel/Paulsen/Fields carotid-artery morphology classification, based on the single largest local deflection angle rather than a continuous arc/chord ratio: "none" if deviation from a straight line is <15°; "tortuosity" if the angle between two adjacent segments is 90-165°; "kinking" if that angle is <90° (with sub-grades: type I 60-90°, type II 30-60°, type III/"sharp" <30°); "coiling" if a full 360° loop/ring is present — [Pathological Tortuosity of Carotid Arteries, wiki.voka.io clinical review](https://wiki.voka.io/diseases/vascular-system/pathological-tortuosity-of-the-carotid-arteries/); corroborated by [Metz's classification of carotid kinking figure, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Metzs-classification-of-carotid-kinking-8-Two-lines-drawn-along-the-axis-of-the_fig1_344230754) and [Carotid artery tortuosity, kinking, coiling: stroke risk factor, marker, or curiosity?, PubMed 16076059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16076059/).
- Calcified Access Severity Score (CASS) iliac tortuosity-index severity bands (a different domain's severity thresholds for the ordinary centerline-length/straight-line-distance ratio than the river-geomorphology sinuosity bands): tortuosity index <1.2 = mild, 1.2-1.29 = moderate, 1.3-1.49 = severe, ≥1.5 = very severe (feeding into an overall CASS stage I-IV, scores 5-20) — [Iliac artery calcification score / CASS discussion, search-synthesized from vascular-surgery literature on iliac access severity scoring](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/15266028241241246); exact tortuosity-index band values as reported in secondary synthesis, primary CASS paper not independently fetched.
- A four-category qualitative "tortuosity phenotype" scheme distinct from Weibel/Paulsen was also found in passing: curving (C-/S-shaped), kinking, looping, and spiralling, used for iliac/aortic access-vessel morphology — noted only from search-engine synthesis, no primary citation captured; flagged as low-confidence.
- Dialysis-access (arteriovenous fistula) literature returned no dedicated tortuosity-specific formula; searches surfaced only general anatomical/flow criteria for fistula maturation (vein diameter ≥6 mm, flow >600 mL/min, depth ≤6 mm) and wall-shear-stress discussion, not a named tortuosity index — [Arteriovenous Vascular Access Selection and Evaluation, PMC5332074](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5332074/).
- Varicose-vein literature returned only the general Venous Clinical Severity Score / revised VCSS (a 9-10-item, 0-3-per-item clinical severity sum covering pain, edema, pigmentation, ulcers, etc.), which does not contain a tortuosity-specific sub-score or formula — [Revision of the venous clinical severity score, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0741521410016381); [Revised Venous Clinical Severity Score calculator, Medscape](https://reference.medscape.com/calculator/333/revised-venous-clinical-severity-score).

### Inferences
- The Umbilical Coiling Index is a strong, clean example of exactly what was asked for: a decades-old, clinically standard, single-number, arithmetic-only tortuosity-adjacent score from a field entirely outside coronary/cerebral/retinal imaging, and it is structurally different from every metric on the exclusion list (it counts discrete coil/loop cycles per unit length, not curvature, angle-sum, or arc/chord ratio).
- The Weibel/Paulsen tortuosity/kinking/coiling scheme is genuinely distinct from the excluded "sinuosity index classification thresholds": the river-geomorphology bands classify a *continuous* global arc/chord ratio, whereas this vascular scheme classifies vessels by the *single sharpest local deflection angle*, with an entirely different set of category names and cutoffs (angle-based, not ratio-based).
- The CASS iliac severity bands, by contrast, are a domain-specific relabeling of the same underlying arc/chord-style ratio as the excluded river bands (just with different numeric thresholds: 1.2/1.3/1.5 vs. 1.05/1.25/1.5), so they are less clearly "new" than the Weibel/Paulsen scheme; report writers should treat this as a weaker, borderline-duplicate addition.
- No genuinely distinct simple formula was found for dialysis-access or varicose-vein tortuosity; both fields appear to rely on qualitative/severity scoring rather than a dedicated geometric tortuosity index, at least within the sources surfaced here.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the primary CASS paper (source of the 1.2/1.3/1.5 tortuosity-index severity thresholds) directly; the values come from a secondary discussion in a lithotripsy outcomes paper, not the CASS-defining publication itself.
- The four-category "curving/kinking/looping/spiralling" phenotype scheme could not be traced to a specific citable paper within the tool-call budget; flagged as an unverified lead rather than a confirmed finding.
- No tortuosity-specific formula for dialysis-access (AV fistula/graft) vessels was found; this may be a genuine gap in that literature (tortuosity is discussed qualitatively, e.g. in ultrasound-mapping guidelines, without a named index) rather than a search failure, but this could not be fully ruled out given the tool-call budget.

## Key Question 5: Are there simple curve-geometry metrics from ecology/GPS-track analysis (path straightness, sinuosity of movement tracks) that could be borrowed?

### Takeaway
Movement ecology has two genuinely simple, well-defined, and distinctly-formulated metrics beyond straightness: the plain Straightness Index (net displacement over path length, mathematically the same *ratio* as the excluded DM/TI but used with inverted convention and different name/field) and Benhamou's (2004) corrected Sinuosity Index, a closed-form combination of mean step length, mean cosine of turning angle, and coefficient of variation of step length, which is a genuinely new formula distinct from everything on the exclusion list.

### Cited Findings
- Straightness Index = D / L, where D is the straight-line ("beeline") distance between the first and last point of a trajectory and L is the total path length traveled; equal to 1 for a perfectly straight path and smaller for more convoluted paths. Also called the "net-to-gross displacement ratio." — [How to reliably estimate the tortuosity of an animal's path, Benhamou 2004, seaturtle.org PDF](http://www.seaturtle.org/library/BenhamouS_2004_JTheorBiol.pdf); [TrajStraightness, trajr R package documentation](https://rdrr.io/cran/trajr/man/TrajStraightness.html).
- The Straightness Index is explicitly noted in the literature as reliable only for oriented/directed paths (path toward a goal), and *not* applicable to random-walk-type trajectories, because for a true random walk expected net displacement scales with the square root of path length rather than linearly, so D/L trends toward zero as L grows even for a "non-tortuous" random searcher — [Benhamou 2004, seaturtle.org PDF](http://www.seaturtle.org/library/BenhamouS_2004_JTheorBiol.pdf).
- Benhamou's (2004) corrected Sinuosity Index: S = 2 · [p · (((1+c)/(1−c)) + b²)]^(−0.5), where p is the mean step length (mean distance between consecutive points), c is the mean cosine of the turning angle between consecutive steps, and b is the coefficient of variation of the step length. This is stated as a correction (Benhamou's eq. 8) to the earlier Bovet & Benhamou (1988) sinuosity index, extending it to be valid for a wider range of turning-angle distributions and non-constant step lengths — [Benhamou 2004, seaturtle.org PDF](http://www.seaturtle.org/library/BenhamouS_2004_JTheorBiol.pdf); [TrajSinuosity2, trajr R package documentation](https://rdrr.io/cran/trajr/man/TrajSinuosity2.html).
- The R package `trajr`, built specifically for animal-trajectory tortuosity analysis, implements both TrajStraightness (D/L) and TrajSinuosity2 (Benhamou's corrected formula above) as its two headline tortuosity measures, alongside fractal-dimension estimation — [trajr vignette, CRAN](https://cran.rstudio.com/web/packages/trajr/vignettes/trajr-vignette.html); [trajr: An R package for characterisation of animal trajectories, Ethology 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/eth.12739).

### Inferences
- The plain Straightness Index (D/L) is mathematically equivalent to 1/(1+DM) where DM is the already-excluded arc/chord-minus-1 metric (since D/L = chord/arc), so on pure formula content it is not new information. It is included here only because (a) it is the standard name and convention used in an entirely different field (movement ecology, not vascular imaging), (b) it is used with the opposite semantic direction (an *efficiency*/directedness score where higher = straighter, vs. tortuosity metrics where higher = more tortuous), and (c) the literature explicitly documents a known failure mode (breaks down for non-directed/random paths) that is not discussed for the vascular-imaging arc/chord ratio. Report writers should weight this low as "new metric" but potentially useful as "a caveat/insight borrowed from another field."
- Benhamou's Sinuosity Index is a genuinely additional, simple (pure arithmetic on point coordinates: distances and turning-angle cosines) formula not equivalent to SOAM, DM, or any excluded metric: it combines three summary statistics (mean step length, mean cosine of turning angle, CV of step length) in a specific nonlinear closed form, and it is specifically designed to remain valid when segment ("step") lengths are irregular, i.e. it is a variance-aware turning-angle metric with no counterpart on the exclusion list.
- Fractal dimension of a movement path (also implemented in `trajr`) is very likely the same box-counting/Hausdorff-dimension concept already excluded (there for "branching structures"), just applied to a single open curve instead of a branching tree; this is flagged as a likely duplicate rather than reported as new.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether Benhamou's sinuosity formula has ever been applied directly to 3D vascular centerlines in the published literature (it was designed for 2D animal GPS/observation tracks); its direct applicability and interpretation for a fixed anatomical vessel rather than a stochastic search path is untested in the sources found, and this is a genuine open question for anyone considering borrowing it.
- No GPS-track-specific "path efficiency" metric distinct from the Straightness Index (D/L) was found in these searches; the ecology literature appears to treat straightness index and sinuosity index as the two dominant simple metrics, with fractal dimension as a third (excluded-duplicate) option, rather than offering further distinct simple formulas.
