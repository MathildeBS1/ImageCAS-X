# Add indexed lumen size, not another angle

Yes, the current set is missing one clearly relevant feature, and it is **lumen size normalised to the territory it supplies**: coronary lumen volume divided by left ventricular myocardial mass (V/M), or lumen volume or reference diameter indexed to body surface area when no myocardium mask is available. It is the only whole-tree geometric descriptor with outcome data and demonstrated incremental value over plaque and CT-FFR, and in the largest human CT-based flow study diameter-type features explained more wall shear stress (WSS) variance than bifurcation angle or curvature. The apparent disagreement in the notes, where one note ranks position along the vessel (distance from the ostium) as the top missing feature, dissolves once the question is split in two: position along the vessel is the strongest predictor of **where** in a tree plaque forms, while indexed lumen size is the strongest candidate for **who** develops it. Since the thesis asks who develops plaque in the Herlev–Østerbro cohort, indexed lumen size belongs in the feature set and arc length from the ostium belongs in the analysis as a covariate or stratifier for aggregating local features. Diameter-based features carry a real reverse-causation risk, because plaque narrows or outwardly remodels the lumen, so they must be measured at plaque-free reference segments or analysed in participants without plaque at baseline. The recommended minimal set is four features: bifurcation diameter ratio, mean absolute turning angle, indexed lumen size, and bifurcation angle as the first one to drop. No study in any general-population or plaque-free cohort has tested baseline lumen geometry against later plaque, so every recommendation here rests on symptomatic or diseased cohorts and on simulation.

## Two questions hide inside "predicting plaque"

Most of the coronary geometry literature asks where plaque sits within an artery, not which person gets it. The two questions reward different features. A feature that varies strongly along one tree (distance from the ostium, local curvature at a bend, the flow divider of one bifurcation) is excellent at locating plaque but says nothing about a person unless it is aggregated, and once aggregated it can lose most of its signal. A feature that varies mainly between people (total lumen calibre relative to demand, overall tree shape) is what a person-level risk model needs.

Position along the vessel is the clearest "where" feature in the notes. In 208 STEMI patients, occlusions clustered in the proximal third of each artery, and **each 10 mm further from the ostium cut occlusion risk by 13% in the RCA, 30% in the LAD and 26% in the LCx** ([Wang et al., Circulation 2004](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.0000135468.67850.f4)). In PARADIGM's serial CCTA registry, proximal lesions progressed faster and carried more low-attenuation plaque ([PARADIGM review, JCCT](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1934592521004640)), and among 334 plaque-free patients 35% developed new lesions, most often in the LAD (60%), with no geometric predictors reported ([EHJ CVI 2026 abstract](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)). This evidence is strong and cheap to exploit, but every person has a proximal LAD, so arc length cannot rank individuals. Its value for this thesis is methodological: when bifurcation and turning-angle features are summarised per person, a sharp bend 10 mm from the ostium and one 120 mm distally should not count equally. Arc length is therefore the weighting or stratifying variable for aggregation, not a fifth predictor.

The same split sorts most other candidates. Dominance shifts where calcium lands (left-dominant systems hold 10.1% more of their calcium in the LAD) ([Circulation 2023 abstract](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/abstract-14844-coronary-artery-system-dominance-influences-the-distribution-of-calcified-plaques-in-coronary-tree/)) but was null for MI, mortality and revascularisation in the prospective CONFIRM registry of 6,382 patients over five years ([CONFIRM, eScholarship](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7fg730bm)). Myocardial bridging spares the tunnelled segment and concentrates plaque just proximal to it ([PMC10870693](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870693/)), yet was not a significant patient-level risk factor against traditional risk factors ([EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/24232)). Left main length, the LM to LAD take-off angle, and the RCA take-off angle all come from small, symptomatic, cross-sectional studies whose outcome is a proximal lesion ([Moon et al., QIMS 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10644144); [J Clin Med 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm12031051)). All of these are "where" features and can be left out of a minimal person-level set.

## Lumen size relative to demand is the best-evidenced "who" feature

V/M is total epicardial lumen volume divided by LV myocardial mass, both segmented from CCTA, and is framed as a supply to demand mismatch ([Frontiers Cardiovasc Med 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2025.1449148/full)). In 238 NXT patients, those with low V/M had more plaque, greater stenosis and **lower FFR (0.80 vs 0.87, P < 0.0001)**, and V/M predicted FFR ≤ 0.80 independently of plaque measures ([Taylor et al., JCCT 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28789941)). In a retrospective cohort of 306 people with type 2 diabetes followed for a median 3.8 years, V/M had an adjusted **HR of 0.899 per mm³/g** after accounting for obstructive CAD, total plaque volume and CT-FFR, and adding it improved reclassification (NRI 0.173, IDI 0.057) ([PMC13151284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13151284/)). A related coronary artery volume index below 28 mm³/g was associated with roughly four times more events in 325 patients ([Diagnostics 2022 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9497479/)). No other geometric descriptor in the notes has incremental-value data of this kind.

The flow-simulation evidence points the same way from the mechanistic side. In 127 CTCA-segmented left main bifurcations from patients without significant stenosis, a model of 11 shape features explained **79% of the variance in low time-averaged WSS area**; Finet's ratio dominated low WSS, branch diameters dominated oscillatory shear, and curvature and bifurcation angle were secondary ([Gharleghi et al., arXiv 2401.12504](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12504v1); [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-73490-w)). In 39 left coronary trees, the only geometry to haemodynamics correlations surviving multiple-comparison correction were diameter ones (marginal diameter vs average TAESS, r = -0.85), while curvature and torsion did not survive ([Shen et al., arXiv 2502.06161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)). The existing bifurcation diameter ratio already captures the relative split, which is the strongest single WSS term; what the set lacks is absolute calibre.

Absolute calibre cannot be used raw. Body surface area and sex independently predict coronary size, and women have smaller proximal LAD and RCA areas on CT ([PubMed 20043337](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20043337/)). Unnormalised diameter in a cohort that is 57% women would largely re-encode sex. V/M solves this by normalising to the myocardium the arteries serve; lumen volume per BSA is the fallback when no LV mask exists, and it is cheaper because the lumen tree is already segmented.

## Diameter features are the most exposed to reverse causation

Plaque changes the lumen it sits in. It narrows the lumen at stenoses, and compensatory or excessive expansive remodelling can preserve or enlarge it ([PREDICTION, PMC5508525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5508525)). In a single baseline scan, low V/M is therefore partly a plaque-burden readout; the authors of the ACS comparison attribute the stepwise fall of V/M from stable angina to MI to lower lumen volume that may reflect plaque ([PMC11865212](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11865212)). The same bias reaches the bifurcation diameter ratio whenever a daughter vessel carries plaque near its origin, which is exactly where bifurcation plaque forms.

The Herlev–Østerbro cohort offers a direct fix. Of 9,533 asymptomatic adults scanned, **54% had no atherosclerosis** ([Fuchs et al., Ann Intern Med 2023](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M22-3027); [TCTMD summary](https://www.tctmd.com/news/mi-risk-subclinical-cad-both-extent-and-stenosis-severity-matter)), so a large plaque-free subgroup exists in which lumen size cannot yet be a consequence of plaque. Three mitigations follow. First, measure diameters at plaque-free reference segments or use a proximal reference diameter for the ratio, rather than lesion-level values. Second, adjust person-level models for total plaque volume when the outcome is extent rather than presence. Third, if any participants have a repeat CCTA, the cleanest design is baseline geometry in plaque-free people against incident plaque, which is the one design no published study has run. Curvature and bifurcation angle are mostly set by heart shape and branching and are less altered by plaque, which favours them as exposures in a cross-sectional analysis. This is an inference: no study has measured how much plaque shifts them.

## Turning angle and bifurcation angle need different handling

The turning angle is a discretised curvature, and the best human evidence supports that choice over chord-based tortuosity. In the same 127-patient cohort, **average absolute curvature** had the highest association with low-WSS area across all left main branches (p < 0.001), whereas the arc to chord tortuosity index was not significant (p = 0.86) ([Kashyap et al., Sci Rep 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8764056/)). Aggregate the turning angle as a mean absolute value per arc length, at a fixed resampling step and smoothing scale, and report sensitivity to both, because bend detection in vascular centrelines can change tenfold with resolution ([PMC8626959](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8626959/)).

At person level, tortuosity carries a confounding trap. In 1,010 angiography patients, tortuosity was inversely associated with CAD (OR 0.755) and positively with female sex (OR 2.603) and hypertension (OR 1.533), with no MACE difference over 2 to 4 years ([Li et al., PLoS One 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/)). A machine-learning analysis of 38,691 angiograms found the opposite sign after adjustment (OR 1.05 per SD for CAD), while confirming the links to female sex and hypertension ([JACC Advances 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.102829)). The sign depends on adjustment, so turning angle must enter a model that already contains sex, age and blood pressure, or it will look protective for the wrong reasons.

Bifurcation angle is the weakest of the three current features on mechanistic grounds. Wider LM angles go with lateral-wall plaque in small series (≥ 88.5° in 63% of patients with LAD disease) ([Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10554-006-9144-1)), but in human CFD it contributed less than diameter ratio and branch diameters ([Gharleghi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12504v1)), and in carotid bifurcations, area expansion and axis colinearity explained the apparent lack of any branch-angle effect on WSS ([J Biomech Eng 2010](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4002538)). It is, however, the most reproducible of the three, with 3D cylinder fits giving inter-observer differences near 2° ([UFU repository](https://repositorio.ufu.br/handle/123456789/18107)). That makes it worth keeping as a secondary feature and the first to drop if the set must be three.

Torsion does not earn a place. Its effect on WSS runs through helical flow, which curvature also produces and which is atheroprotective ([De Nisco et al., PoliTo](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/2726967/235290/The%20Atheroprotective%20Nature%20of%20Helical%20Flow%20in%20Coronary%20Arteries.pdf)); its plaque signals are isolated and unadjusted ([Zhang et al., arXiv 2312.00257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00257)); and as a third derivative it is the least reliable to estimate from CCTA centrelines. Eccentricity, segment length, side-branch density and Murray or Finet residuals have no direct plaque evidence. A ramus intermedius flag is a cheap optional extra, since it was the strongest risk factor for left main and proximal plaque in 1,380 CCTA patients ([Clin Radiol 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crad.2026.107299)), but that finding is again cross-sectional and localising.

## The recommended minimal set

| Feature | Where or who | Strongest evidence | Reverse-causation exposure | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bifurcation diameter ratio | Both | Dominant WSS term, 127 human CFD bifurcations | High near daughter origins | Keep; use reference diameters |
| Mean absolute turning angle | Where, weak who | Best curvature metric for low WSS | Low | Keep; adjust for sex, age, BP |
| Indexed lumen size (V/M, or lumen volume per BSA) | Who | Only geometric feature with incremental outcome value | High | Add; test in plaque-free subgroup |
| Bifurcation angle | Where | Small XS series; secondary in CFD | Low | Keep as secondary; drop first |
| Distance from ostium | Where | 13% to 30% lower occlusion risk per 10 mm | None | Covariate for aggregation, not a predictor |
| Torsion, dominance, bridging, eccentricity, Murray residual | Where or null | Weak, null or no direct evidence | Varies | Leave out |

## Conclusion

The literature gives no ready-made answer for the thesis question, because no study has tested baseline coronary geometry against later plaque in a general or plaque-free population, and no published model has run formal feature selection on geometry-only predictors in such a cohort. That gap is the thesis's opportunity rather than a weakness: the Herlev–Østerbro plaque-free majority is precisely the population where indexed lumen size can be separated from its own reverse-causation problem. The defensible design treats plaque presence or extent as the outcome (71 MIs give too little power for events), fits a baseline model of age, sex, BSA, lipids, blood pressure, smoking and diabetes, and then reports the increment from the four geometric features.

Expectations should be modest. Even the prospective evidence for CFD-derived low WSS, which geometry only approximates, reaches a positive predictive value of 41% for progression in diseased patients ([PREDICTION, Circulation 2012](https://doi.org/10.1161/circulationaha.112.096438)), and plaque extent itself carries relative risks near 8 to 9 for MI in this cohort. A small, clearly reported gain from indexed lumen size in plaque-free people would be a novel and publishable result; a null result, properly adjusted for sex and body size, would still be the first general-population test of the hypothesis.
