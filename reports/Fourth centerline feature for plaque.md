# Add normalised lumen taper as the fourth feature

The fourth feature should be **normalised lumen taper**: the fractional change in lumen radius per unit arc length within each proximal, mid and distal window, computed as the robust slope of log radius against centerline arc length. Of the three candidates the researchers converged on, it is the only one that is grounded in the dominant geometric driver of wall shear stress (WSS), applies the same way to the LAD, CX and RCA, and is not already encoded by curvature, bifurcation angle, daughter diameter ratio or the window positions. Lumen radius is the strongest geometric determinant of WSS in human CT-based flow studies: in 349 plaque-free vessels, segments under 2.6 mm had more than three times the minimal shear of segments over 3.2 mm ([Schultz et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10554-022-02739-0)). The most recent expert review names "diameter and its variation" as important shape factors "that have yet to be studied in detail" ([Shen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17109)). The log-slope form cancels body size and sex scaling, so it describes shape rather than calibre, which keeps it inside the supervisor's "geometry, not flow" scope. Curvature-weighted torsion is the runner-up. It is orthogonal to curvature and has a protective hypothesis behind it, but it was null in the only human test and is the noisiest quantity a CCTA centerline yields. Left main length comes third: it has the oldest direct plaque evidence but does not exist for the RCA. No study has tested any of the three as a predictor of new plaque in plaque-free trunks on serial CCTA, so whichever feature is chosen, the thesis will be the first test of it.

## The three chosen features leave lumen calibre change uncovered

The current set describes bending (mean turning angle) and branching (bifurcation angle, daughter diameter ratio). It contains no information about how the lumen of the trunk itself changes size, and calibre is where the geometry-to-WSS literature puts most of the weight. In a U-net WSS surrogate trained on real and synthetic coronaries, integrated-gradient attribution found that **"radius and inlet velocity are the most important features"** ([Alamir et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11345599/)). In 39 human left coronary trees, the only geometry-haemodynamics correlations that survived multiple-comparison correction involved diameter (marginal diameter against average time-averaged endothelial shear stress, **r = -0.85**), while the curvature and torsion correlations did not ([Shen et al., arXiv 2502.06161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)). In 127 CT-segmented left main bifurcations without significant stenosis, Finet's ratio and branch diameters dominated low and oscillatory shear, and curvature and bifurcation angle were secondary ([Gharleghi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12504v1)). Even the older patient-specific RCA work found "a strong correlation between WSS and reciprocal radius and a weaker correlation between high curvature and extremes of WSS" ([Johnston & Johnston 2007](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9155/52/9/013)).

Raw radius, however, cannot be the feature. It largely encodes body size, sex and dominance, and in the Schultz cohort men had lower shear than women even after adjustment for diameter ([Schultz et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10554-022-02739-0)). Absolute lumen size relative to the myocardium it supplies is also the flow-proxy route (lumen volume to myocardial mass) that was deliberately set aside. The physics points to a shape-only alternative. Poiseuille shear scales as Q/r³, and flow in a trunk falls at every side branch. A trunk that tapers less than its branching would demand is relatively dilated for the flow it still carries, and should therefore have lower WSS ([Gijsen et al. 2019](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/40/41/3421/5576147) describe flow dividing by branch diameter "accompanied by tapering of the parent lumen"). Taper measured as a change in log radius is dimensionless per unit length and does not change when the whole tree is scaled up or down, so it isolates this shape signal from calibre. This mechanistic chain is an inference. No human study has isolated taper rate as a WSS or plaque predictor, and targeted searches returned only stent and non-coronary papers.

## Torsion and left main length each fail one test

The table compares the three finalists, plus the features ruled out as redundant, against the constraints of the thesis design.

| Candidate | Works for LAD, CX and RCA | Per-window | Redundancy with existing features or windows | Best evidence | Robustness at 0.4 to 0.5 mm voxels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Normalised lumen taper** | Yes | Yes | Low against curvature and angles; partial against daughter ratio in proximal LAD/CX | Radius is the top geometric WSS determinant (human CT-CFD); no plaque-outcome study | Moderate; a slope over many cross-sections |
| Curvature-weighted torsion | Yes | Yes | Low by construction, but curvature also drives helical flow | Swine only; null in 39 human trees | Poor; third derivative |
| Left main length | No (no RCA analogue) | No (patient-level constant) | Low | Old postmortem and angiographic series; null in one CCTA study | Good |
| Distance from ostium | Yes | Already the window index | High | Best prospective lesion-level evidence | Good |
| Murray, Finet or expansion-ratio deviation | Bifurcations only | Proximal windows only | High against daughter ratio | Idealised CFD; one IVUS composition study | Poor near the carina |
| Tortuosity index | Yes | Yes | High against mean turning angle | Contradictory | Moderate |

Torsion is the most intellectually attractive alternative because its hypothesis has the opposite sign to curvature. In swine, torsion correlated with helical flow (**r = 0.64**), and helicity was strongly inversely related to unfavourable WSS (**r = -0.91**) ([De Nisco et al. 2019](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/112774)). In a longitudinal minipig study, arteries in the highest tertile of near-wall helicity grew wall thickness more slowly (0.032 against 0.040 mm per month in the lowest tertile) ([De Nisco et al. 2020](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/125151/Repub_125151.pdf)). The human evidence, though, is weak. In the only human test, mean torsion did not separate stenosed from non-stenosed trees (**p = 0.247**), while curvature did ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.00257)). In a helical-tube model, a change in curvature moved WSS by up to 22% and a change in torsion by 3% ([J Biomech Eng 2012](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/biomechanical/article-abstract/134/7/071005/464806/Investigation-of-the-Effects-of-Dynamic-Change-in)). In human CFD, helical flow shrank the low-shear area but enlarged the high-shear area ([Shen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)). Torsion also needs the third derivative of a stair-stepped centerline and is undefined where curvature approaches zero. Groups that report it fit free-knot regression splines first ([De Nisco et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2021.731924/full)), and no CCTA reproducibility data exist. With few events per artery, the thesis cannot afford a feature that may be mostly noise.

Left main length has the most direct plaque evidence of any candidate, and all of it is old and small. In 204 postmortem hearts, short trunks went with earlier and faster LAD and CX atherosclerosis, and in subjects over 50 branch atherosclerosis correlated inversely with trunk length ([Gazetopoulos et al. 1976](https://heart.bmj.com/content/38/11/1160)). A 43-patient angiographic series found shorter trunks in patients with CAD ([Gazetopoulos et al. 1976](https://doi.org/10.1136/hrt.38.2.180)). The only CCTA study (n = 62) found no length difference, at 10.6 against 11.3 mm ([Cademartiri et al.](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/assessment-of-left-main-coronary-artery-atherosclerotic-burden-us/)). The decisive problem is structural rather than evidential. The RCA has no left main, so the RCA model would need a different fourth feature, and the three per-artery models would no longer share one feature set. Left main length is also one number per patient and cannot vary across windows, and separate ostia or a trifurcation need an ad hoc rule. It remains a sensible LAD/CX-only sensitivity analysis.

The redundant candidates are easier to dismiss. Short distance from the ostium is the best-evidenced geometric predictor of future culprit lesions (**HR 2.71** against non-culprit lesions in ICONIC) ([Han et al. 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2788006)). The proximal, mid and distal windows already encode it, so a continuous version would be close to collinear with the window index. Murray, Finet and expansion-ratio deviations are functions of the same mother and daughter radii as the daughter diameter ratio, and they apply only at bifurcations. In the 127-bifurcation cohort, average absolute curvature was associated with low-shear area (p < 0.001) and the arc-to-chord tortuosity index was not (**p = 0.86**) ([Kashyap et al. 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8764056/)), so tortuosity adds nothing to a turning-angle feature computed over the same window.

## Compute taper as a robust log-radius slope over each window

The definition below follows from the measurement problem. Normal coronaries taper by about **0.25 mm in diameter per 10 mm** distal to bifurcations ([Medrano-Gracia et al.](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). Across a 30 mm window that is a radius change of roughly 0.4 mm, about one voxel. The signal is therefore at the resolution limit of any single measurement, and the feature only becomes reliable by pooling many cross-sections and by resisting the radius jumps at side-branch ostia. A two-point ratio of inlet to outlet radius, or a pointwise derivative dr/ds, would be dominated by noise.

The recommended pipeline is as follows. First, resample the trunk centerline at a fixed step of 0.5 mm and smooth it with the same physical kernel already used for the turning angle, so that the two features share one centerline. At each sample, cut the lumen segmentation with a plane orthogonal to the smoothed tangent and compute the cross-sectional area A(s). Then take the equivalent radius r(s) = √(A/π). Area-equivalent radius is preferable to the VMTK maximum-inscribed-sphere radius, which follows the minor axis of non-circular sections and is more sensitive to single-voxel boundary noise. Second, mask about one parent diameter on either side of every side-branch origin, which the bifurcation-angle pipeline already locates, along with 3 mm at the ostium and any sample whose cross-section touches another vessel. Third, within each window w of length L_w, fit ln r(s) = a_w + b_w · s with a Theil–Sen or Huber estimator, and report

  **T_w = −b_w × 10 mm**, the fractional loss of radius per 10 mm (≈ 0.04 to 0.08 in normal trunks if the atlas taper holds).

A positive T_w means normal tapering. Values near zero or negative mean that the window holds its calibre or expands, which is the hypothesised low-WSS state. Require at least 20 mm and 60% unmasked samples per window, and set the window to missing otherwise. Within each artery-specific model, standardise T_w per window, because proximal LAD taper and distal RCA taper have different normal ranges. Fit `ct_cag_dominance` (BL and FU) as a covariate in the RCA and CX models, since dominance changes how much flow those trunks carry. `male` and `clin_bsa` (BL and FU) enter through the SCORE2 and baseline covariates already planned, and the log-slope form should make the feature largely independent of them. That independence is an expectation to check in the data, not a result from the literature.

Three checks belong in the methods. The first is a test-retest estimate: every trunk that stays plaque-free at follow-up has two CCTAs years apart and essentially unchanged anatomy, so the baseline-to-follow-up intraclass correlation of T_w in non-event trunks gives a reproducibility figure that no published study has reported. The second is a sensitivity analysis over smoothing scale and branch-mask width. The third is a collinearity check of T_w against the daughter diameter ratio in the proximal LAD and CX windows, where the LM bifurcation feeds both. Elsewhere along the trunk the two features measure different things, since the ratio describes how flow splits between daughters and taper describes how the parent lumen responds to losing flow. Expect some correlation with curvature as well. Curvature correlated with absolute diameter at r = -0.474 ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.00257)), although a dimensionless slope should share less of that size effect.

## Plaque-free trunks protect taper from reverse causation, not from diffuse disease

Diameter features are the geometric descriptors most exposed to reverse causation, because plaque narrows the lumen or remodels it outward. Requiring the whole trunk, and the left main for the LAD and CX, to be plaque-free and evaluable at baseline removes most of this problem, which is why taper suits this design better than it suits the cross-sectional studies behind it. Two residual risks remain. Sub-threshold, reader-invisible wall thickening could still change the lumen. More importantly, the sign of the association is not settled in advance. The WSS hypothesis predicts that **less** taper (relative dilation, low shear) leads to more new plaque. Diffuse early atherosclerosis would instead make a trunk taper **more** steeply before any focal plaque is graded, and that predicts the opposite sign. This competing reading is an inference rather than a sourced finding, but it is enough to justify a pre-specified two-sided test, and a negative coefficient on T_w could be interpreted as the WSS mechanism while a positive one could be interpreted as preclinical disease.

The thin evidence is also a reason to keep the feature simple. Prospective CFD-derived low WSS, which taper only approximates, reached a positive predictive value of 41% for progression even in diseased patients ([PREDICTION, Circulation 2012](https://doi.org/10.1161/circulationaha.112.096438)). A geometric proxy in plaque-free trunks should therefore be expected to add modestly, not dramatically, to SCORE2 variables and calcium.

## Conclusion

Comparing the three shortlisted features against one another, the decisive constraints were the thesis design, not the strength of the literature. Left main length fails because the RCA model needs the same feature. Torsion fails because few events cannot absorb a third-derivative estimate that has never been validated on CCTA. Distance from the ostium, Murray deviation and tortuosity fail because the windows, the daughter ratio and the turning angle already carry their information. Normalised taper survives each of these tests while adding the one dimension, calibre change along the trunk, that the human CFD literature weights most and that the current set does not contain.

The feature also lets the thesis report two results that do not yet exist in the literature: the first scan-rescan reproducibility of coronary taper on CCTA, from paired baseline and follow-up scans in non-event trunks, and the first test of whether taper in a plaque-free trunk predicts where new plaque appears. If the per-window slope proves too noisy in the reproducibility check, the fallback is the same log-radius fit over the whole trunk, which gives one value per artery. Torsion should only be considered if the event count turns out larger than expected, and then as a pre-registered interaction with curvature rather than as a main effect.
