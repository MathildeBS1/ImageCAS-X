# Fix the yardsticks before testing the features

The feature-validity section (`thesis/methodology/10b_feature_validity.tex`) has a sound outline: an outcome-blind order of steps, primary definitions fixed in advance, hypotheses formulated before the data, and repeated warnings about differential measurement error. The yardsticks it applies inside that outline, however, mostly deviate from the methodological sources it cites. **Five errors go to the core.** First, the 10-year baseline-to-follow-up ICC is called "test–retest", and it then drives both the "measurement error" term of the variance partition and the ICC ≥ 0.75 retention rule. COSMIN and QIBA both require that the true value does not change between the repeats ([Mokkink 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712525); [Sullivan 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097)). Second, construct-validity hypotheses state only a direction and are confirmed by "95% CI excludes the null", whereas COSMIN requires an expected magnitude and explicitly rules out significance as the criterion ([COSMIN 2012 manual](https://faculty.ksu.edu.sa/sites/default/files/cosmin_checklist_manual_v9.pdf)). Third, discriminant and "similar" hypotheses are confirmed when the CI includes the null, which is the absence-of-evidence fallacy ([Lakens 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/)). Fourth, the Koo & Li bands are applied to a point estimate, although Koo & Li say they apply to the 95% CI ([Koo & Li 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/)). Fifth, the phantom design cannot inform the choices it is meant to inform: point noise never reaches taper or the ratios, and "bias" is undefined for bend counts. Most fixes are wording plus one or two extra analyses, such as an intra-method rerun of the tracer and Bland–Altman statistics. None requires new data, and none touches the outcome. The decision rules need the most rewriting, since they combine three yardsticks that are each slightly wrong: a 10-year ICC, a point-estimate threshold and a per-feature 75% rule with 2–3 hypotheses.

Severity scale used throughout: **error** means wrong, or will produce misleading results. **Questionable** means a methodologist would object, and it is defensible only with an added justification. **Fine** means consistent with the literature. Line numbers refer to the `.tex` file as of 2026-10-31. I checked them against the file.

## Steps 1 and 3: the phantoms test less than the text claims

### Accuracy on synthetic geometry (lines 22–24, 48–59)

QIBA defines the technical performance of an imaging biomarker as bias, linearity and precision over a stated measuring interval. Sullivan et al. recommend checking linearity with 9 to 11 true-value levels against phantom or true data. They define the measuring interval as the range in which bias, linearity and precision stay acceptable ([Sullivan 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097); [Raunig 2015](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/98441)). They also warn that phantoms "do not represent the complexity of human targets; thus, precision is often overestimated." Poor phantom bias predicts poor performance in patients, but good phantom results "do not guarantee" good performance. QIBA's own digital reference objects are synthetic *images* ([Obuchowski 2015](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/esb_facpub/138)), and vessel-tree simulators such as VascuSynth render volumes together with their ground truth ([VascuSynth](https://insight-journal.org/browse/publication/794)). For context, the coronary curvature studies the thesis relies on ran no phantom or noise validation at all ([Kashyap 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/); [van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)). A point-based phantom is therefore above the field's usual practice, although it falls short of what QIBA calls a reference object.

## Step 2: tracer–Medis agreement and the 10-year ICC are not reliability

### Reliability (lines 61–72)

This subsection carries most of the reconciliation work. Three sets of notes (reliability, variance partition, construct validity) each discussed the two ICC designs from a different angle.

**What the literature defines.** COSMIN defines reliability as "the extent to which scores for patients who have not changed are the same for repeated measurement" with the same instrument: test–retest, inter-rater or intra-rater ([Mokkink 2010](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2802121/257066.pdf)). Its Risk of Bias tool asks whether patients were "stable in the time between the repeated measurements", whether the interval was appropriate and whether "the measurement condition[s] [were] similar" ([Mokkink 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7712525)). QIBA calls precision under identical conditions *repeatability*. Its human test–retest studies are "coffee break" experiments, minutes to hours apart, which assume an unchanged true value. A different measurement system or algorithm is a *reproducibility* condition ([Sullivan 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666097); [QIBA review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9667479/)). Bland and Altman add that two methods can agree no better than each method's own repeatability allows ([Bland & Altman 1986](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/ba.htm)).

**Reconciled verdict on tracer vs Medis (line 66).** The notes offered three labels: reliability, method agreement and convergent validity. Only "reliability" is wrong. The design varies the software on the same scan, which makes it a method-comparison study, or "reproducibility across analysis software" in QIBA terms. Medis is a reference, not a gold standard, so COSMIN criterion validity does not apply either. A convergent construct-validity hypothesis with a magnitude (for example, tracer–Medis curvature r ≥ 0.70) is a legitimate *additional* reading in the multitrait–multimethod sense ([Campbell & Fiske, secondary](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fas/psych/glossary/multitrait-multimethod_matrix/)). It is optional. **Recommendation:** move the design out of "reliability" and call it "agreement between methods". Report it with Bland–Altman statistics plus ICC(A,1) and ICC(C,1). Do not call it accuracy or error, and state that the Medis trees come almost entirely from scans with plaque at baseline, which is a different population.

**Reconciled verdict on BL vs FU (line 67).** The reliability, variance-partition and accuracy notes all reach the same conclusion. Ten years of ageing, new plaque, possible remodeling, nitroglycerin at FU only, a different scanner or protocol and possibly a different cardiac phase fail COSMIN's stability and similar-conditions items. Curvature alone was 9–11% higher at end-systole than at end-diastole ([van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)). Heise showed that a two-wave correlation confounds reliability with true-score stability ([Heise 1969](https://doi.org/10.2307/2092790); [Tufiş et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12657207/)). **Recommendation:** rename this design "10-year stability (tracking)". It is a lower bound on reliability, not test–retest. It still has a legitimate use: it says how well one baseline value represents a person's geometry over follow-up, which is the "usual value" sense of regression dilution ([Frost & Thompson 2000](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssa/v163y2000i2p173-189.html)). The section should state that it intends this meaning when it reports the 10-year ICC.

**The missing design.** Neither existing design measures the same thing twice with the same instrument. The cheapest true repeatability design is to rerun the tracer on the same baseline scans with perturbed seeds or start points. That is an intra-method repeatability study, and it needs no new data. Its ICC is the one that belongs in the partition and in the decision rule. *Unverified:* whether the tracer is deterministic given a seed, which would decide whether a rerun is informative, was not checked in the code.

## Steps 4 and the evidence grading: hypotheses need magnitudes, not p-values

### Construct validity (lines 109–129, Table `tab:method-validity`)

COSMIN is unambiguous on two points. Every hypothesis must include "the expected direction (positive or negative) and magnitude (absolute or relative)", because without it "it is difficult to decide afterwards whether the hypothesis is confirmed". And "P-values should be avoided in testing hypotheses … it is less relevant whether these differences are statistically significant (which depends on the sample size) than whether these differences are as large as could be expected" ([COSMIN 2012 manual, Box F items 5, 6 and 10](https://faculty.ksu.edu.sa/sites/default/files/cosmin_checklist_manual_v9.pdf); repeated in [COSMIN 2018 manual, box 9](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf)). Cronbach and Meehl had already noted that "rejecting the null hypothesis does not finish the job of construct validation" ([Cronbach & Meehl 1955](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Cronbach/construct.htm)). For "no association" claims, "it is statistically impossible to support the hypothesis that a true effect size is exactly zero". Equivalence tests against a smallest effect size of interest are the tool for that ([Lakens 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/)).

### Level of evidence (lines 158–217)

COSMIN does grade evidence, with a modified GRADE (high, moderate, low, very low). It grades confidence in the *measurement-property result*, downgrading for risk of bias, inconsistency, imprecision and indirectness, and it does not grade the prior support for each hypothesis. COSMIN says only that hypotheses "can be based on literature … and (clinical) experiences" ([COSMIN 2018](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-syst-review-for-PROMs-manual_version-1_feb-2018.pdf); [Prinsen 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5891568)). No published framework for grading prior support of construct-validity hypotheses was found. The author-made grade is therefore non-standard but transparent, and it fills a real gap.

## Steps 5 and 6: sensitivity needs joint reading, the partition needs the right ICC

### Sensitivity to definitional choices (lines 219–226)

Specification-curve analysis asks for "theoretically justified, statistically valid and non-redundant specifications", a graphical display, and "joint inference across all specifications" ([Simonsohn 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41562-020-0912-z)). ### Variance not explained by known determinants (lines 228–242)

Under classical test theory, with W = X + U and the error U independent of the true value X and of the determinants Z, the partition is algebraically correct. The observed R² equals the reliability λ times the true R², so ICC − R² = λ(1 − R²_true) is reproducible unexplained variance. The reliability ratio λ = σ²ₓ/(σ²ₓ + σ²ᵤ) is the standard attenuation factor ([Carroll et al. slides](https://www.stata.com/merror/call.pdf); [Carroll et al. 2006](https://www.routledge.com/Measurement-Error-in-Nonlinear-Models-A-Modern-Perspective-Second-Edition/Carroll-Ruppert-Stefanski-Crainiceanu/p/book/9781584886334)). The same three-way split is established in factor analysis as common + specific + error, with specific = reliability − communality ([Friendly](https://www.datavis.ca/courses/factor/efacfa-handout2-2x2.pdf)). "The communality of a variable should not exceed its reliability. Violation of this condition is called a quasi-Heywood case" ([SAS PROC FACTOR](https://support.sas.com/documentation/cdl/en/statug/63962/HTML/default/statug_factor_sect022.htm)). That is exactly the thesis's R² ≤ ICC condition.

## Decision rules: one coherent rule from three inconsistent ones

### Prespecified decision rules (lines 244–254)

The rules combine the issues above. The notes converge on one rewrite.

**The 0.75 vs 0.70 threshold, reconciled.** The section cites Koo & Li for ICC ≥ 0.75 (line 248) but Terwee and Prinsen for the 75% hypotheses rule (line 249). COSMIN's criterion for sufficient reliability is ICC ≥ 0.70 ([Prinsen 2018 criteria, adapted table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6500280/table/T3)). Koo & Li themselves say "there are no standard values for acceptable reliability" ([Koo & Li 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/)), and Bland and Altman call acceptability "a question of judgment" ([Bland & Altman 1986](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/ba.htm)). Both 0.70 and 0.75 are rules of thumb. The defensible threshold is the one tied to the feature's purpose, which is to be a regression predictor. **Recommendation:** keep 0.75 if you prefer, but justify it by attenuation (a reliability of 0.75 bounds the dilution of a coefficient at about 25%, per Carroll et al.). Apply it to the *repeatability* ICC, not the 10-year ICC. Apply it to the **lower 95% bound**, which follows Koo & Li. If you apply it to the point estimate instead, state the deviation. Add a second, purpose-based criterion on the conditional reliability (ICC − R²)/(1 − R²) against base-model covariates, and judge it with a person-level bootstrap that resamples R² and ICC jointly. Drop "R² < ICC" as a criterion: under independent error it is automatically true, so it discriminates nothing.

**The 75% construct rule.** Terwee's 75% rule applies to an instrument's whole body of results, in subgroups of at least 50, and Prinsen adds that "the results of all studies should be taken together" ([Terwee 2007](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2901670/267479.pdf); [Prinsen 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5891568)). COSMIN declined to set a minimum number of hypotheses ([COSMIN 2012](https://faculty.ksu.edu.sa/sites/default/files/cosmin_checklist_manual_v9.pdf)). With 2–3 hypotheses per feature, 75% means unanimity, since 2/3 = 67% fails. The count is also undefined: curvature has 3 rows but up to 6 variables, each estimated unadjusted and adjusted, which gives 2–12 "results". COSMIN v2.0 rates ≥ 75% in accordance as sufficient, ≥ 75% deviating as insufficient, and everything in between as inconsistent ([Mokkink 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11541334/)).

## What could not be verified

Several claims behind the recommendations rest on secondary sources or on the researchers' own reasoning, and should be checked before they are cited in the thesis:

- **Original sources not read.** McGraw & Wong 1996 and Shrout & Fleiss 1979 were not retrieved; their ICC forms come via SPSS and UCLA documentation and Koo & Li. Kessler 2015 and Raunig 2015 were not opened in full. The de Vet 2006 full text returned 403. Kottner's GRRAS wording was not checked.
- **Secondary or partial readings.** Campbell & Fiske was read only through summaries. Del Giudice & Gangestad's Type E/N/U wording comes from secondary summaries. The QIBA DRO definition was seen only as a search snippet.
- **Formulas without an exact source.** The multiple-covariate attenuation formula and the "ICC 0.75 bounds attenuation at about 25%" argument need a page reference in Carroll et al. 2006. The asymptotic relative efficiency of Tosteson & Tsiatis 1988 was not retrieved.
- **The researchers' own inferences.** The claim that real centerline error is low-frequency was inferred from how centerlines are built, as was the σ/chord scaling of noise bias. No CCTA phantom source was found for the blooming and partial-volume bias of small-vessel diameters.
- **Questions for the data and the code.** No source gives short-interval repeat-CCTA ICCs for curvature, taper or bifurcation angle, or the true 10-year change in plaque-free adults. Whether the tracer is deterministic given a seed was not checked.
- **Bibliography.** The `.bib` entries for `mokkink2010cosmin`, `prinsen2018cosmin` and `terwee2007quality` were not checked.

## Conclusion

The section's weaknesses share one root: it borrows the *names* of measurement properties from COSMIN and QIBA but not their *conditions*. Test–retest assumes no change, hypotheses testing assumes stated magnitudes, Koo & Li's bands assume a CI, and a phantom bias assumes a defined measurand. Restoring those conditions changes the logic only in two places, the retention rule and the variance partition. Both currently lean on a 10-year ICC that mixes anatomy with error. Once an intra-method repeatability ICC and the conditional reliability against the *base-model* covariates replace it, the section can say what the thesis actually needs to know: how much of each feature's baseline variance, beyond SCORE2 or anatomical position, is reproducible. It also tells the reader how much a null likelihood-ratio test could have missed. That turns step 6 from a descriptive curiosity into the quantitative link between measurement and the who/where tests.

The 10-year stability ICC, demoted from reliability, gains a clearer role of its own: it shows how well one baseline scan stands for a person's geometry across the follow-up period. That is a separate argument the thesis can make openly.
