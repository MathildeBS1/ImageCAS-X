# Choose arc/chord plus curvature, not turns

**The thesis should declare a two-metric primary panel computed from one smoothed, resampled centerline: the arc/chord tortuosity index TI = L/D minus 1, and total absolute turning per unit length (mean absolute curvature, kappa_a, from chord angles). TI is the anchor at whole-vessel scale (LAD, LCx, RCA); kappa_a, with its dimensionless companion K (total turning in radians), is the anchor at branch scale for graph nodes.** This reverses two recommendations of the earlier reports. The concentration ratio Q = (kappa_r/kappa_a)^2 drops from co-primary to an exploratory secondary gated by a noise floor, and the turn layer (Grisan-style bend segmentation) drops from "challenger" to a sensitivity analysis. Arc/chord, which the earlier report treated as a mere comparator, is promoted because it is the only metric with reproducibility evidence (carotid inter-package ICC 0.95 to 0.99, aorta interobserver ICC 0.97 to 0.98, cardiac-phase shift of only 1.8 to 2.3 %) and the only continuous CCTA metric with an outcome-adjacent association (LAD arc/chord with hypertension, sex and age). Kappa_a is kept because it is the only metric that tracked a haemodynamic reference on coronary CTA (R^2 0.06 to 0.23 against low wall shear stress in 10 mm pieces, where arc/chord showed nothing), is length-normalised, and is compositional, so a vessel value equals the length-weighted mean of its branch values. Bend counts, torsion, 3D chain-code torsion, Mateos tau3D, fractal dimension and radius-weighted variants are dropped. The supervisor's warning that curvature is fragile is supported in part (phase, sampling and definition dependence) but has never been formally tested on coronary CTA, so the descriptors enter as an ablation on top of a tokenised tree baseline, behind pre-registered reliability gates. The measured 0.5 mm ImageCAS-X centerlines add a hard constraint: raw turning angles are about three times the real curvature, so nothing may be computed without smoothing and a chord of several millimetres.

## What the supervisor and the outcome literature license

Bjorn's email asks for three things that bear directly on the choice: interpretable hand-crafted descriptors, a baseline without heavy feature engineering with descriptors added as ablation, and distances normalised so that "purely the tree-structure remaining". Kit adds that the end goal is matching descriptors to outcome in the Herlev-Osterbro cohort, with ImageCAS used to develop descriptors and compute statistics first (`from_superviser.tex`). Bjorn also reports that curvature and tortuosity were "a fragile measurement" in his left atrium work. Two implications follow. The panel must be small, because every added descriptor is a feature-engineering step that the baseline is supposed to do without. And each metric must earn its place on reliability first, since the outcome evidence cannot rank them.

The outcome evidence is thin and mostly the wrong kind. Nearly all of it is invasive angiography with categorical bend-count definitions (three or more bends of 45 degrees or more). In 1,010 chest-pain patients that definition gave prevalence 39.1 %, female OR 2.60, hypertension OR 1.53, coronary atherosclerosis OR 0.755, and no MACE difference over 2 to 4 years ([Li 2011](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3164184/)). Tortuosity is strongly tied to spontaneous coronary artery dissection (78 % vs 17 %) ([Eleid 2014, abstract via snippet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25138034/)) and to ischemia without obstructive disease, where an arc/chord index matched ischemia territory for LCx and LAD but not RCA ([Diagnostics 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/)). Neither phenotype can be recovered from ImageCAS. On CCTA, the only continuous single-metric result is LAD arc/chord associated with hypertension (p < 0.001), sex (p = 0.01) and age (p = 0.045) in 194 patients; effect sizes were not retrievable ([Sharfo 2024, abstract via snippet](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cpf.12900)). A CCTA MACE model (CArTI, 992 patients, HR 2.69 high vs low risk) ranked curvature and tortuosity features among its top predictors, but it is a multi-feature abstract without definitions ([Circulation 2025 abstract](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4365988)). The largest continuous study, 22,334 RCA angiograms with a turning-angle score, found effects of OR 1.05 to 1.09 per SD for CAD and AUC 0.60 for the global score against 0.67 for a local-profile transformer ([PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)).

Three consequences matter for the choice. The sign of the CAD association flips with definition and cohort (negative for bend counts, weakly positive for the turning-angle sum), so validating a descriptor against CAD severity says nothing about whether it is correct; `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md` already fixes the hypothesis as association with non-obstructive disease. The robust, definition-independent associations are female sex, age and hypertension, which are the right sanity checks and also the confounders that any Danish analysis must adjust for. And the expected effects are small, so the Danish study should plan for modest signal. No study attached tortuosity to outcome at tree level, and none used ImageCAS.

## Robustness on 0.5 mm centerlines rules out the fragile family

No coronary CTA study reports an ICC, CV or Bland-Altman figure for any tortuosity or curvature metric; that gap is confirmed in three independent note sets and is itself a thesis contribution. What exists is indirect. Across 9,000 vessels including coronary trees, measurements at voxel-level sampling generally underestimated tortuosity metrics, changes were under 5 % between 10x and 100x oversampling, torsion varied far more than curvature, and the curvature-minimum inflection count rose by up to 439 % in coronaries as sampling densified ([Frenet-Serret study, arXiv 1911.12316](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12316)). Carotid arc/chord agreed across four commercial packages at ICC 0.95 to 0.99, but in patients with a tortuosity index near 1.3 to 1.4, far above the healthy coronary range, so that figure must not be quoted as coronary reproducibility ([PMC6348067](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6348067/)). Thoracic aorta interobserver ICC fell from 0.97 to 0.98 for arc/chord to 0.82 to 0.84 for fitted-circle curvature and 0.39 to 0.75 for angles ([aorta 2018, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29705085/)). In 71 patients, end-systolic curvature was 11.1 % higher at artery level and inflection points were higher in systole (0.63 vs 0.51 per segment), while segment arc/chord moved from 1.10 to 1.12 ([van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)). The stability ranking is therefore arc/chord, then curvature, then inflection or bend counts, and ImageCAS cannot test phase at all because each scan has one.

The repo data sharpen this. On 20 test cases (40 sides, 61 main vessels, a small sample from the test split only), the delivered centerlines have a median point spacing of **0.45 mm** (5th to 95th percentile 0.34 to 0.61 mm, so density varies by a factor of about 2), no radius array, and a median angle of **5.9 degrees** between consecutive raw steps (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`, `topology/radius.py`, measured in the repo research pass). My arithmetic on those figures: a mean raw turn of 6.7 degrees (0.117 rad) per 0.45 mm step is about **0.26 rad/mm**, roughly three times the 0.08 mm^-1 curvature that van Zandwijk measured at a 5 mm scale. Summing raw turning on a 100 mm vessel gives about 1,470 degrees on a nearly straight course. A naive per-point turning-angle score in the style of the RCA transformer study would mostly measure discretisation and local point density. The Frenet study's "underestimate at native sampling" and this "overestimate from staircase noise" point in opposite directions; which dominates depends on how the centerline was built, and these ones are noise-dominated. This is my inference and the experiments below test it. Smoothing has its own bias: Gaussian smoothing at sigma 1 mm shortens arc length by a median 2.1 % and at 2 mm by 4.2 %, so L/D is biased low and scale-dependent, and the same sigma must be used in every analysis. Raw L/D on the main vessels had a median of 1.45 (5th to 95th percentile 1.13 to 2.35), higher than van Zandwijk's artery-level 1.33 to 1.36; whether that gap comes from noise inflating raw length or from vessel definition is unresolved.

Kjeldsberg's carotid work reports that resolution, not smoothing, drives bend counts, and that the parallel-transport detector had CV below 5 % while the torsion-extrema detector had 47 % for one bend ([Kjeldsberg 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626959/)). That is a carotid siphon result with model-specific units, not a coronary validation, so it argues against building on inflection detection rather than for any particular detector. The noise-versus-radius trade-off from the earlier report survives: noise control needs a chord or smoothing scale of about 4 to 5 mm for second-moment metrics, while radius sensitivity needs a chord below the bend radius, so bends tighter than about 3 to 5 mm cannot be resolved by any metric here (derivation, not measurement).

## Where the new notes overturn the earlier reports

The earlier reports ranked (kappa_a, Q) first, a parallel-transport turn layer second, and arc/chord as a comparator. The new evidence changes each. First, Q: it is the ratio of second to first curvature moments, and second-moment curvature is the quantity that inflates under noise; spurious energy scales as 12 sigma^2 / ell^4 against 3 sigma / ell^2 for kappa_a, and Q tends to about 4/pi on pure noise (earlier report, derivation). The one check in the earlier report that Q is monotone in amplitude and frequency was a noise-free numpy computation outside the repo, and has not been rerun inside `tortuosity/`. Q also grows with straight padding and may be raised by plaque deflection. It remains the only threshold-free way to separate one wide arc from several tight bends, so it stays as an exploratory secondary. It is not co-primary, and it is computed only above a noise-floor mask.

Second, the turn layer. The earlier report already flagged phase exposure; the new numbers add the 439 % sampling swing for curvature-minimum counts, and the earlier report's own observation that mean inflections per segment are below one, so many segments would score Grisan tau = 0 (a floor-effect risk). The one advantage the layer keeps, distinguishing S-bends from C-bends, is a definitional question the literature does not settle, and the supervisor's steer toward a small interpretable panel points away from a tunable detector. It becomes a sensitivity analysis, and it is kept in the thesis only if it carries information beyond the primary pair at a pre-set bar.

Third, arc/chord. The earlier report ranked it lowest because it failed against wall shear stress (R^2 minus 0.018, p = 0.86, in a 10 mm left main bifurcation piece) and cannot tell a gentle C-shape from a tortuous vessel ([Kashyap 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/), [Bullitt 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603/)). Those are haemodynamic-relevance and construct-validity results, not reproducibility results, and the two criteria were conflated. For the thesis question, which is phenotyping and matching to outcome across two cohorts extracted by different pipelines, reproducibility and comparability with prior CCTA and angiography work weigh more. The Kashyap regions are also 10 mm pieces, where arc/chord has almost no dynamic range; it says little about whole-vessel behaviour. Fourth, radius: the graph note proposes curvature times local radius as a scale-free node feature. I side with the earlier report and drop it. The only direct test found (Hart 1999, retina) had diameter-weighted measures performing slightly worse than their plain counterparts ([Hart 1999](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)), and radius here comes from the mask distance transform, which over-reads by up to half a voxel (0.25 mm) against distal radii near 1 mm. Radius stays a confounder to test and a separate node feature, not part of the index.

## How the descriptor enters the tree

No coronary graph or tree-transformer paper ablates tortuosity, so this section is inference from adjacent fields. Graph datasets attach tortuosity to edges or branch nodes as pre-aggregated scalars: VesselGraph puts curvature and tortuosity on edges ([snippet only](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354235479_Whole_Brain_Vessel_Graphs_A_Dataset_and_Benchmark_for_Graph_Learning_and_Neuroscience_VesselGraph)), a retinal graph model makes each inter-bifurcation segment a node with length, volume and curvature ([arXiv 2502.16697](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16697)), and airway phenotyping computes arc/chord per branch and aggregates with summary statistics per lobe ([PMC13419480](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13419480/)). The one coronary per-vertex precedent feeds an ordered turning-angle vector to a local-attention transformer ([PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)), which is the noise-exposed form, and needs the data volume Bjorn warns about for positional encodings.

The correspondence concern in the supervisor's email points the same way. Features attached to sequence position inherit the correspondence problem; features attached to a junction-to-junction branch carrying an anatomical label do not. The repo already provides that structure: `topology.graph` builds rooted trees for 1,584 of 1,600 sides, with `Segment` (junction to junction, already storing length, chord and arc/chord) and `Vessel` (same-named segments chained proximal to distal). Two repo facts constrain branch scale. Junction-to-junction segments are short: 26 of 255 are under 10 mm, 74 under 20 mm and 112 under 30 mm, and the LM has a median length of 10.1 mm with 19 of 20 under 20 mm. Chained main vessels are long enough for whole-vessel values: LAD median 127.8 mm (minimum 20.9), LCx 81.0 (37.2), RCA 107.0 (70.5). So the LM cannot carry a shape descriptor at any useful scale, and branch-level TI on short segments has almost no dynamic range (segment arc/chord is about 1.10). Kappa_a and K degrade more gracefully on short segments because they are length-normalised or additive, and compositionality means the tree-level value is a length-weighted mean of branch values, with no double counting. The supervisor also wants distances normalised. TI and K (radians) are dimensionless; kappa_a in mm^-1 is not, so the graph should carry TI and K as node features with branch length (as a fraction of tree length) as a separate feature, letting the network recover density, while the vessel-level statistics report kappa_a in mm^-1 for comparability with Kashyap. The branch length and vessel definition inherit a non-anatomical tie-break (the child with the longest same-name downstream run continues), which needs its own sensitivity check. Whether tortuosity leaks heart size is untested; total tree length is the natural proxy to regress against.

## The decision

| Item | Choice | Basis and status |
|---|---|---|
| Primary 1 | TI = L/D minus 1 on the smoothed, resampled polyline, per chained main vessel (LAD, LCx, RCA) | Best reproducibility and phase stability; only continuous CCTA outcome-adjacent association (Sharfo, abstract-level); comparable to angiography index studies |
| Primary 2 | kappa_a = total absolute turning / length from chord angles, per main vessel and per junction-to-junction branch; dimensionless K also stored per branch | Only coronary CTA winner against a haemodynamic reference (Kashyap, 10 mm pieces, R^2 0.06 to 0.23); compositional; length-normalised |
| Scale | Named main vessel for statistics and Danish outcome matching; branch node for graph features; LM reported NaN with reason; branches below a length floor NaN plus a mask flag; per-vertex only as second ablation | Prior art is vessel level; branch level is the graph norm; segment lengths measured |
| Smoothing to validate | Arc-length resample to 0.25 mm, Gaussian sigma in {0.5, 1, 2} mm, default 1 mm (prior-art module value; length shrink 2.1 %) | Native spacing is 0.45 mm and noisy |
| Chord to validate | ell in {2, 3, 5, 8, 10} mm, provisional default 5 mm, locked only after noise calibration | 5 mm is the one coronary precedent (Menger, van Zandwijk); noise bound needs about 4 to 5 mm |
| Length floor | Roughly 5 to 10 chords per measured unit, provisional 25 mm at ell 5 mm, set empirically by truncation | No validated minimum exists; a design choice |
| Secondary | Q above a noise-floor mask; a high-percentile or windowed-maximum curvature; per-vertex turning profile; bends of 45 degrees or more per 100 mm for angiography comparison only; parallel-transport turn layer as sensitivity | Q unvalidated on real data; bends and turns fragile |
| Dropped | Torsion and 3D-SCC torsion, Mateos tau3D, Bullitt SOAM as a feature, fractal dimension, writhe, radius-weighted indices, blended composites, Q as co-primary | Ill-conditioned on 0.5 mm points, wrong object, no gain in published composites, or unvalidated |

The two primaries are reported side by side and never blended. The earlier report notes composites only beat single metrics when they added non-tortuosity information, and Bjorn wants interpretability, so an unweighted structural pair is the maximum defensible complexity. If the thesis must name one metric for the Danish outcome analysis, name TI at vessel level, and state that this reverses the student default of curvature as primary in `docs_thesis/supervisor_questions.md`, on the ground that reproducibility is the binding criterion. The reversal is provisional: if kappa_a passes the same gates and TI floors out on healthy vessels, kappa_a takes the role.

## Evidence quality and open gaps

The evidence is weaker than the confident structure above suggests. The outcome notes are mostly abstract or snippet level; Sharfo effect sizes, CArTI definitions and ICONIC's tortuosity effect were not retrieved, and the notes warn that fetch summaries came from a small model, so numbers need spot-checking before thesis citation. The robustness notes found no coronary ICC anywhere; the carotid, aorta and retinal figures are cross-organ analogies. The graph note found no coronary tree study with a tortuosity ablation, so the branch-scale layout is an inference from VesselGraph, retinal and airway practice. Many Q, noise and amplitude claims rest on the earlier report's numpy checks that live outside the repo, and its noise bound assumed sigma 0.25 mm and independent jitter, likely pessimistic for correlated centerline error. The repo measurements come from 20 test cases only, and the Kashyap precedent covers 10 mm proximal pieces under Taubin smoothing at 0.01 mm resampling, which is not the regime of this thesis.

## Conclusion

The choice turns on a reframing: the question is which descriptor survives the pipeline, not which best characterises tortuosity in theory. On that test the simple, older indices win. Arc/chord is the most reproducible and phase-stable but insensitive to bend structure; turning density adds bend information at the cost of noise sensitivity that the measured 0.45 mm centerlines make severe. The more elaborate metrics, Q and the turn layer, each fix a weakness of the pair but pay for it in derivative order or detector thresholds, and nothing yet shows the extra information survives.

The most useful thesis output may therefore be the reliability characterisation itself: a documented operating point with ICC and rank stability on ImageCAS-X, per named vessel, where the literature has none for any coronary CTA metric. That result would let the Herlev-Osterbro matching claim a descriptor whose measurement error is known, which serves Bjorn's fragility concern better than a bigger feature set would.
