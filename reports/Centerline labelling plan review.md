# Recalibrate pruning, rescore branches, re-anchor the labeller

The plan's architecture holds up: recovering week3 `skeleton.py`, scoring against the delivered trees, and labelling junction-to-junction segments with a GNN are all sound choices. Its numbers would not be trustworthy as written, though, for three reasons. First, **two operational blockers stop it before any science happens**. The imagecasx venv now holds a CPU-only torch (`2.14.1+cpu`), so the Stage 0 resubmission of CAS-Net inference would need about 68 more CPU hours. The planned `skan` install through `uv sync` would also uninstall torch altogether. Second, **the spur-pruning calibration is ill-posed**. Lee skeletons of the GT masks have no excess terminals (11 of 11 val cases have at most the delivered terminal count), and no delivered terminal is shorter than 2 mm. A grid search on GT masks with thresholds capped at 5 mm will therefore choose "prune almost nothing", and that choice tells you nothing about CAS-Net masks, where spurs and fragments do appear. Third, **several scoring and labelling rules are miscounted against what the data actually contain**. Bifurcation matching without one-to-one assignment double counts on 20% of sides. The "spurious terminal" rule almost never fires. Label 14 never occurs on the right side, label 0 needs an explicit remap, and a hard transition decode forces errors on 7 test segments and 2 right trees. EAGMN, the paper the plan names as the design anchor for Stage 3, is a 2D angiography method with 5 labels; the CCTA analogues (Hampe 2024, TopoLab, CPR-GCN) are the right references, and they put realistic F1 at about 0.74 on automatic trees against 0.91 to 0.95 on reference trees. None of these faults needs a redesign. Each one is a local change, and they are collected in the change list at the end.

## Two prerequisites block Stage 0 before any centerline is drawn

### Blocking: inference runs on CPU and a dependency sync would remove torch

CLAUDE.md says the venv has torch 2.11.0+cu128. The installed package is actually **`torch 2.14.1+cpu`** with `torch.version.cuda = None`, and its dist-info is dated 2026-10-02. The last inference job, `logs/cas_net_infer_29585272`, logged `device=cpu`. It ran at about 1790 s per case and was killed at the 12 h walltime after **24/160** cases. That is why the plan's "only 24/160 exist" count stands. Resubmitting `jobs/infer_eval_cas_net.sh` unchanged would need about six more 12 h runs ([codebase audit, sec. 0](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)). The skan step makes this worse. `uv.lock` has no torch entry, and `pyproject.toml` deliberately leaves torch out. A dry run of `uv sync --frozen` reports "Would uninstall ... torch==2.14.1+cpu". So "add `skan` to `pyproject.toml`" followed by the usual sync would delete torch from the venv. Install skan with `uv add --no-sync skan` or `uv pip install skan`. A dry run shows that this adds only numba, llvmlite and toolz and leaves numpy 2.4.6 alone. Reinstall a CUDA torch wheel before any GPU job ([codebase audit, secs. 0 and 7](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)).

### Blocking: nothing exists to calibrate on except test

`cas_net_pretrained/predictions/` intersects train 0, val 0 and test 24, and no `predictions_val/` directory survives on /work3. Every choice that should be tuned on predicted masks, including the pruning setting, the ostium distance cap and the label-transfer tolerance, can therefore only be tuned on test, and that leaks into the headline result ([extraction notes, Q3](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/extraction_stage.md); [codebase audit, sec. 6](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)). Stage 0 needs a val inference run (`inference.py --split val`, 80 cases) alongside the test completion.

### Blocking for Stages 2 and 3: the radius cache covers 16 cases

`load_centerline` reads radius from `$RESULTS/centerline_radius/`, which holds 32 files for **16 train cases** and nothing from test. Without it, the "mean and proximal radius" node features are `None` for nearly every delivered tree, and the radius-based scoring tolerance has no GT radius to read. `python -m bifurcation.radius --split {train,val,test}` takes about 1 s per case, so roughly 15 minutes as a CPU job ([codebase audit, sec. 5](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)).

### Wrong reasoning, right action: the TotalSegmentator venv

TotalSegmentator v2.18.0 requires `torch>=2.1.2` and `nnunetv2>=2.3.1`. It does not pin its own torch, so the plan's stated reason for a separate venv is inaccurate ([TotalSegmentator setup.py](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator/blob/master/setup.py)). A separate venv is still sensible, to isolate nnunetv2's roughly 20 dependencies. The real hazard is storage. Weights default to `~/.totalsegmentator`, which is on /zhome at 25.9/30 GB. Set `TOTALSEG_HOME_DIR` to a /work3 path and pre-download with `totalseg_download_weights` on the login node, since compute-node internet access is unverified ([TotalSegmentator README](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator); [extraction notes, Q8](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/extraction_stage.md)). Record the package and weights version in each case JSON so the aorta masks stay reproducible. The aorta job should also cover **train**, not only val and test, because the ostium distance cap has to be fitted on train start points (see below).

## Stage 1 extraction is faithful in geometry but wrong in pruning and rooting

### What the plan gets right, and a sharper replication test

Bransby et al. skeletonised on the native anisotropic grid. **96.4% of delivered end points and 97.2% of bifurcation points sit exactly on a native voxel centre**, against 1.5% of interior points. The delivered vertex step (median 0.458 mm, 5th to 95th percentile 0.345 to 0.615 mm) matches 26-neighbour steps on a 0.35 x 0.35 x 0.5 mm grid and not on a 0.5 mm isotropic one. On-grid ends and junctions with off-grid interiors confirm that junctions and ends were held fixed during smoothing, which is exactly what week3 `smooth()` does ([extraction notes, Q1 and Q2](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/extraction_stage.md)). Anisotropy of about 1.4:1 is not a topology risk: Drees et al. found "no trend towards positive or negative impact" on topology for anisotropy factors from 1 to 16 ([Drees et al., arXiv 2102.03444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03444)). The data also confirm the plan's order (smooth, then prune, then merge degree-2 joints). On 12 of 80 sides the delivered trees contain degree-2 joints, 61.5% of them on-grid, which is the signature of a junction that stayed unsmoothed and was then demoted when a spur was removed. Re-smoothing after pruning would therefore be a deviation.

These on-grid points also give a better verification step than the plan's "mean distance ≲0.3 mm". Measure what fraction of delivered junction and end voxels appear in `skeletonize(GT mask)`. A value near 100% shows that skimage's Lee implementation is the skeletoniser Bransby used. A 0.3 mm distance gate cannot show this, because a vertex-to-vertex KD-tree distance already reads up to about 0.22 mm for a point lying exactly on the curve ([scoring notes](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/scoring_stage.md)).

### Wrong result: the pruning calibration selects "no pruning" and cannot transfer

This is the most consequential fault in Stage 1. The plan calibrates on GT val masks because "the delivered centerlines are themselves the skeleton of those masks". That premise is true, and it is exactly why the calibration is empty. The GT masks were voxelised from traced centerlines and corrected slice by slice ([Bransby et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30404)), so their skeletons are already clean. On 11 val cases the GT skeleton had **fewer or equal degree-1 voxels than delivered terminals in every case** (for example 10/12, 8/10, 14/14). On 8 CAS-Net test predictions the skeleton exceeded the delivered count in 6 cases (for example 19/13, 21/15), mostly from fragments ([codebase audit, sec. 8](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)). The objective "fewest spurious + missed" on GT masks is therefore dominated by missed terminals, which pruning cannot fix. The grid will pick its weakest setting.

The grid is also out of range. Delivered terminal lengths have **1st, 5th and 10th percentiles of 7.8, 11.6 and 16.2 mm** over 1179 terminals; none is under 2 mm and 2 are under 5 mm ([extraction notes, Q3](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/extraction_stage.md)). The tracing protocol skipped side branches thinner than 1 mm distally, as well as septals, acute marginals and nodal arteries ([Bransby et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30404)). With min_mm ≤ 5 and k ≤ 3 (about 4.5 mm at r = 1.5 mm), spurs of 5 to 8 mm on predictions all survive. And CAS-Net can legitimately segment exactly the untraced anatomy (septals, conus, sub-millimetre distal branches), which produces long branches that no GT-calibrated length rule should remove. The fix has two parts. Calibrate on **CAS-Net val predictions** against delivered val centerlines, and report a spurious-versus-missed trade-off curve rather than a single minimum. Then report pruning sensitivity on test as a secondary analysis.

### Wrong result: the pruning rule itself

Three defects in `prune_spurs` compound the calibration problem ([extraction notes, Q3](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/extraction_stage.md)). The **normaliser is wrong**. `radius.radius_at` at a junction gives the maximal inscribed sphere, which is larger than either branch. A skeleton branch's length also includes about one parent radius inside the parent lumen. Drees et al. define bulge size as (length minus inner length plus tip radius) divided by the branch's own mean radius. They recommend 1.5 for irregular vessels, and say "3.0 or higher can be chosen confidently" for healthy vessels. They also warn that pure minimum-length pruning "is problematic if vessels of different scales are present" ([Drees et al., arXiv 2102.03444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03444)). The second defect is **cascade erosion**. "Repeatedly delete terminal polylines shorter than" a threshold, with no guard, can delete both children of a short terminal fork in one pass, after which the parent becomes a terminal and is pruned too. Drees iterates to a fixed point but retains two edges per node; pruning the shortest spur first and recomputing achieves the same thing. Third, `radius_at` over-reads by up to half a voxel. That is acceptable for features, but it is biased exactly at the 1 mm protocol cutoff.

### Wrong result: missing junction contraction and arbitrary cycle breaking

skan replaces clustered junction pixels with their minimum spanning tree, which yields short junction-to-junction paths ([skan csr.py](https://github.com/jni/skan/blob/main/src/skan/csr.py)). The delivered data have only **2 of 575 internal segments under 1 mm** and 1.2% of bifurcations with degree ≥ 4, so Bransby's pipeline did not leave such pairs ([extraction notes, Q4](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/extraction_stage.md)). Without contracting junction-to-junction paths shorter than about 1 to 2 voxels, bifurcation counts inflate, Stage 2 matching is biased, and the GNN sees fake 0.4 mm segments. Separately, `build_tree` drops whichever closing edge BFS reaches second (`graph.py:431-433`). Predictions will contain cycles from touching vessels and from lumen cavities. Fill enclosed cavities before thinning (`ndi.binary_fill_holes`), and break each remaining cycle at the chain with the smallest minimum radius.

### Wrong result: ostium rule, side rule and dropped components can each delete a whole tree

The **5 mm aorta rule was set for a sub-millimetre heart-chambers aorta**. The `total` model runs at 1.5 mm and is reported to leave "a gap between the left ventricle and the aorta", so it "does not completely cover the aortic outflow region" ([Brosig et al. 2024, J Med Imaging 11(4):044504](https://journals.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/journal-of-medical-imaging/volume-11/issue-04/044504/Learning-three-dimensional-aortic-root-assessment-based-on-sparse-annotations/10.1117/1.JMI.11.4.044504.full); [TotalSegmentator README](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator)). Lee thinning also retracts a blunt end by about one vessel radius. Together these exceed the 5 mm budget, and under the plan a missed threshold drops an entire LCA or RCA. Fit the cap on train (delivered start point to `total` aorta distance), and add a fallback: for a side without an ostium, take the degree-1 tip of the largest component nearest the aorta surface and log it. There is a useful check on the other side of the ledger: CAS-Net covers the GT start points, with 46 of 48 inside the predicted mask on 24 test cases ([extraction notes, Q5](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/extraction_stage.md)).

The **"larger LPS x is left" rule fails 4/560 train and 2/160 test**, by 0.7 to 6.8 mm. Ostium y (left more posterior; Δy 14.9 to 44.1 mm) and tree-centroid x were both **perfect on all 800 cases** ([codebase audit, sec. 3](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)). An earlier 150-case check reported 150/150 for x; the full-split count supersedes it. Use centroid x, which tolerates a mislocated ostium, with y as a tie-break. The rule must also allow **two left ostia** (8 train, 2 val and 1 test cases have separate LAD and LCx origins). A single predicted component that fuses both trees through a leak gets only one ostium under "nearest per component", so log it as a side failure.

**Dropping ostium-less components discards real vessel.** Of 293 fragments that keep-2-largest removed from CAS-Net test predictions, **53.9% overlap GT by at least 50%** ([CGPS CAS-Net centerline biomarker plan, keep2 section](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/knowledge/reports/CGPS%20CASNet%20centerline%20biomarker%20plan.md)). In 3 of 24 test predictions the two largest components hold under 80% of the volume. `build_tree` already roots a component without a start point at its nearest node and warns rather than dropping it (`graph.py:398-418`). So keeping orphans costs almost nothing: assign each component above a length floor to the nearest rooted side and write it with no start point. Scoring then separates "never extracted" from "extracted but orphaned" ([extraction notes, Q7](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/extraction_stage.md)). One code note: if the plan does keep the drop rule, it must apply it in `extract.py`, because `build_tree` would otherwise silently root the orphan.

## Stage 2 scoring reinvents existing metrics and miscounts branches

### Wrong result: bifurcation and terminal-branch counts

"Number matched within 3 mm" is ambiguous on this data. Across the 320 delivered test sides, **19.6% of sides with at least two bifurcations have a pair closer than 3 mm** (44 pairs), and a skeleton often splits one trifurcation into two nearby degree-3 nodes. Without one-to-one assignment the count double credits. Use Hungarian matching (`scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment`) with a gate. Report TP, FP, FN, precision, recall and F1, plus the matched-distance distribution, over a 1/2/3/5 mm sweep, because no coronary paper fixes a principled bifurcation tolerance ([scoring notes](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/scoring_stage.md)). The spurious-terminal rule ("no delivered point within tolerance") will almost never fire, because a spur's proximal points lie against the parent centerline. ATM'22 counts a branch as detected "only if more than 80% of centerline voxels ... are within the ground-truth" ([Zhang et al., ATM'22, arXiv 2303.05745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05745)). Apply that 80% coverage rule in both directions for spurious and missed branches, and report their lengths, since real terminals are almost always at least 10 mm.

### Wrong result: distances that mix accuracy with capability, and vertex-weighting bias

Mean distance and HD95 both move when one 20 mm spur appears, so they conflate tracking accuracy with tracking capability. CAT08 keeps these apart. Its overlap OV counts TP and FP/FN against the annotated radius, OF measures overlap until the first error with a 5 mm ostial grace zone, and OT restricts to the part with radius ≥ 0.75 mm. Its accuracy measure AI averages only connections shorter than the radius, because "in case of a tracking failure the magnitude of the distance ... should not be included in the accuracy measure" ([Schaap et al. 2009, via Metz thesis ch. 3](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/23756/110629_Metz,%20Cornelis%20Thimotheus.pdf)). The plan's two radius-gated fractions are CAT08's TPR and TPM ingredients, but OV, OT, an OF analogue per ostium-to-terminus path, and AI are all missing. Two definitions need pinning down. The tolerance should be the **GT-mask radius at the nearest delivered point**, in both directions; radius measured on the prediction would reward over-segmentation. It also needs a floor of about 0.35 mm, since the 5th percentile GT radius is 0.51 mm. Second, **resample both curves to 0.05 to 0.1 mm arc-length spacing before any nearest-neighbour query**. Delivered spacing is about 0.45 mm, so vertex-to-vertex distances carry up to 0.22 mm of pure sampling error, and point counts weight the means instead of length ([scoring notes](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/scoring_stage.md)).

### Wrong framing: what the `gt` source does and does not separate

The `gt` source is not an observer reference. The delivered centerlines are the skeleton of the same masks, so `gt` error measures implementation differences: skeletoniser, smoothing and the gap between automatic and manual pruning. The paper's inter-observer reference (centerline clDice 95.4, ASSD 0.53 mm, HD95 4.58 mm; CAS-Net 93.3, 0.73 mm, 5.75 mm) came from blinded re-annotation of every test case ([Bransby et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30404)). Those second-annotator files are not in the local data, although `configs/inter_observer.json` anticipates them. Also, because pruning behaves differently on bumpier predicted masks, `cas_net_pretrained` minus `gt` is not a clean subtraction of segmentation error. Report the two sources side by side, with a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test per patient, which is the paper's own convention.

### Improvement: reuse, anchor, stratify

`utils/metrics.py:compute_centerline_point_metrics` already computes symmetric MD and HD95 on two LPS point sets with the benchmark's aggregation, and `evaluate.py` already uses it per segment label. Call it on the resampled curves rather than writing a cKDTree version. Also run the raw-skeleton `compute_centerline_metrics` on the same CAS-Net cases. That ties Stage 2 to Table 2's 0.73 mm and 5.75 mm and shows what smoothing and pruning buy. Per-label recall needs no labeller, because every delivered point carries `segment_label`, and it is the most clinically readable output. Stratify by radius bin, distance from the ostium and the Descriptors.xlsx groups. Keep failures (no ostium, swapped side, empty side) as counted rows rather than NaN drops ([scoring notes](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/scoring_stage.md)). One documentation conflict should be settled with the user before any thesis sentence relies on it. CLAUDE.md says the paper's ASSD 0.73 mm "cannot be reproduced". The README maps `centerline_md` to exactly that centerline ASSD. Only the surface ASSD is genuinely absent.

## Stage 3 labeller rests on the wrong anchor paper and misreads the label set

### Blocking: label 0 and label 14 handling

`_majority_label` treats 0 and 14 as ordinary integers. Label 0 occurs only in case 272 (test, left side, one 8 mm segment), and it means "not attributed", as 14 does. A 14-way head indexed by `label - 1` would turn that segment into index -1. Remap 0 to 14 (or to ignore) in one explicit place ([codebase audit, secs. 1 and 2](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)).

### Wrong result: the side masks and the decode constraints do not match the data

"14 'Other' is allowed on both" is factually wrong. Label 14 has 5849 train points on the left and **zero on the right in every split**. The right side uses exactly {9, 10, 11}, on trees with a median of 3 segments, while the left is an 11-class problem with a median of 10 segments. Allowing 14 on the right is harmless but unsupported, and the plan should say the right head is 3-way. The decode examples are not strict either. Train contains LM to D1 (1), LM to IM (55), IM to LAD (19), IM to LCX (15) and R-PLA to R-PDA (41), and the left root is LAD or LCX in 16 trees. **A hard decode built from train transitions mislabels 7 test segments by construction, plus 2 right trees whose true root is R-PDA** ([codebase audit, sec. 4](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)). Greedy top-down decoding adds a propagation risk: a wrong LM or LAD near the root forces every descendant into an allowed but wrong label, which CPR-GCN cites as the reason to avoid hard rules ([CPR-GCN, arXiv 2003.08560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08560)). Max-product (Viterbi) decoding over the rooted tree is exact and needs no more code. Use GNN log-probabilities as unaries and soft transition penalties from train counts (Laplace-smoothed, so rare valid variants survive). Report TopoLab's violation rate for both the raw and decoded outputs. TopoLab reports 22% against CPR-GCN's 54% on orCaScore ([TopoLab, arXiv 2307.11959](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.11959v1)). No CCTA paper reports Viterbi decoding with numbers, so this recommendation rests on reasoning rather than an ablation.

### Wrong result: segment-majority transfer hides the predicted-tree failure mode

On predicted trees, a missed side branch merges two GT segments into one node that spans two labels. Majority transfer plus segment-level scoring then scores the larger half as correct and hides the error. Hampe et al. built multi-resolution graphs (segments capped at 5, 10, 20 and 30 points) and averaged probabilities back to points precisely because of this label ambiguity. They score a node as TP when its nearest reference node lies within the local radius, not within a fixed 3 mm ([Hampe et al. 2024, PMC11095121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/)). Make length-weighted point accuracy primary, which the plan already lists, and use radius-based transfer. A split-long-segments graph (for example 10 to 20 mm caps) is the cheap version of Hampe's ensemble. The augmentation should also follow CPR-GCN's robustness test. That test deleted 20% of branches (1123 of 6760 segments affected), and F1 fell from 0.955 to 0.929. So the augmentation should delete whole internal subtrees and add synthetic spurs, since predicted trees err in both directions, not just remove short terminals ([CPR-GCN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08560)).

### Improvement: change the reading anchor, the coordinate frame and the architecture

EAGMN is **2D invasive angiography with 263 images and 5 labels** (LMA, LAD, LCX, D, OM). It works by template graph matching on 121 hand-crafted features ([EAGMN, PMC11073582](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11073582/)). HAGMN-UQ is the same setting ([arXiv 2308.10320](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10320)). Neither should fix a CCTA feature list. The right reads are the following.

- **Hampe 2024**: CCTA, 10 classes, 3-layer GAT ensembles. F1 is **0.74 on automatic trees** and 0.91 to 0.95 on reference trees.
- **TopoLab**: 14 classes, which is the same scale as this plan. F1 is 87.2% on orCaScore and 92.2% in-house.
- **CPR-GCN**: 11 classes, mean F1 0.955 on 511 subjects ([Hampe 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/); [TopoLab](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.11959v1); [CPR-GCN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08560)).

These set the expected gap between the plan's two test arms: about 15 to 20 F1 points between delivered and predicted trees is what the literature shows.

Location dominates every ablation. In Hampe's ablation, removing location drops automatic-tree F1 from 0.74 to 0.59, while removing orientation, geometry or appearance each costs 2 to 3 points ([Hampe 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/)). The strong CCTA systems anchor location to the heart. Hampe uses the LV myocardium centre, Gülsün the four chamber positions, and Ren LA and LV distances to find the LCX ([Ren 2023, PMC10626726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10626726/)). An ostium-only frame with ±15° jitter leaves cardiac orientation to the scanner. The plan already runs TotalSegmentator, so adding the heart chambers to `--roi_subset` buys three things: an LV-centred origin, a long-axis frame, and per-segment distances to the LV and septum. Those are exactly the cues for LAD versus D, LCX versus OM, and PDA. Keep the ostium-relative features as well. Image features add only about 2 to 3 points, mostly on D and OM (CPR-GCN 0.955 against 0.934 without them), so a geometry-only first version is defensible.

On architecture, small-data evidence argues against four plain GAT layers. CPR-GCN saw no gain from 3 to 4 layers on trees mostly under depth 4, and needed residual connections (0.947 without them, 0.955 with). Li et al. found GAT at 0.642 against GraphSAGE at 0.805 on 141 patients ([Li et al., ISBI 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.00386)). Use 2 to 3 layers with separate self and neighbour weights or a residual connection. Report a **zero-layer ablation** too: the same head on node features alone. This stays within "GNN only", because it is the same model with no message passing, and it is the only way to claim the graph matters. A plain MLP with rules reports the best orCaScore F1 (93.3%), ahead of every GNN ([LWT-ARTERY-LABEL, arXiv 2508.06874](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06874v1)), and Hampe's self-connection-only model is just 2 points behind its full GNN.

### Improvement: imbalance, dominance and ordinal labels

Train segment counts run from LAD 1546 down to L-PDA 37 and L-PLA 53, and each of those rare left labels has only about 10 to 13 test segments ([codebase audit, sec. 4](../research_notes/Centerline%20labelling%20plan%20review/codebase_audit.md)). Use a class-weighted or focal loss (as LWT does), and report per-class support next to macro F1. Dominance is where every published method is weakest. Cao's atlas needs dominance known in advance, and expert agreement is 97.6% on right-dominant trees but only 87.6% on left-dominant ones ([Cao et al. 2017, PMC5677991](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5677991/)). Score L-PDA and L-PLA as a separate tree-level dominance readout. Ordinal labels (D1/D2, OM1/OM2) should be renamed after decoding by take-off order along the parent, which is effectively what Cao and LWT do.

## Conclusion

The common thread is that the plan calibrates and verifies against the cleanest available target, GT masks and delivered trees, and then deploys on the messiest one, CAS-Net predictions. The data show the two differ in exactly the dimensions the plan tunes: spur count, fragment count and ostium coverage. Every tuning step should therefore move onto CAS-Net val predictions, and the GT-mask arm should be demoted from calibration source to replication check, where its near-perfect on-grid junctions make it unusually sharp. The labeller faces the same split from the other side. Delivered-tree accuracy is an upper bound that the literature places 15 to 20 F1 points above automatic-tree accuracy. The thesis-relevant number is the predicted-tree arm scored at point level with radius-based transfer, and the heart-frame features are the cheapest lever on it.
