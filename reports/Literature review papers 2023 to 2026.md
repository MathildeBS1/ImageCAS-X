# Two 2026 papers belong in the review

Two recent papers belong in the literature review, and they share a weakness the thesis can turn into an argument: neither is widely cited yet. **Malmqvist et al. 2026 (SCAPIS, JACC Cardiovascular Imaging)** is the only 2023–2026 study found that adds a CCTA measure to SCORE2 in a random population sample. **Avigdor et al. 2026 (SCOT-HEART post hoc, European Radiology)** is the only full paper found that follows people with normal coronary arteries for about ten years. Both go in `sec:lit-risk`. Malmqvist should replace Hageman 2023 for the "imaging beyond SCORE2" point. Avigdor joins Won 2022 as the plaque-free-baseline study with the longest follow-up. Four more are worth a sentence each, in this order: Zhang R et al. 2025 (person-level plaque prediction from a plaque-free scan, using fat radiomics), Griffo et al. 2025 (whole-LAD shape against later infarction, which forces a narrower wording at the end of `sec:lit-measure`), Gharleghi et al. 2025 (patient CTCA in which the bifurcation diameter ratio, not curvature, led the low-WSS correlations) and, outside the three files, the 2024 ESC chronic coronary syndromes guideline in place of `knuuti2019esc`. The most cited 2023–2026 papers near the topic are endothelial-biology reviews, ORFAN, Cleerly-type AI plaque staging and EMERALD II. None of them holds a concept the review lacks, so all can be left out. **No 2023–2026 paper weakens the gap as stated.** Every near miss is missing at least one of these: geometry, a plaque-free baseline, a population sample, or plaque at follow-up as the outcome. Two sentences around the gap still need adjusting: the closing claim of `sec:lit-measure` and the count "all three" in `sec:lit-risk`. Most verdicts rest on abstracts only. Counts are OpenAlex (OA), retrieved 2026-11-08, with Semantic Scholar (S2) where available. Google Scholar was not queried and usually runs 1.3 to 2 times higher. For papers only months old, citations per year mean little.

The search covered PubMed, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC and the web, and was targeted rather than systematic. Recent work already in `refs.bib` was checked before anything was proposed: Kashyap 2022, Zhang 2024, Garcha 2025, Tello Ayala 2026, Nannini 2024, Tommasino 2024, Chen 2024, Lee 2024, Hageman 2023, Good 2026 and Fuchs 2023. CAS-Net and `shen2026geometry` were left out, as instructed. Suggested wording below keeps numbers out of the prose.

## SCAPIS 2026 and SCOT-HEART 2026 change `sec:lit-risk`, not the gap

**Malmqvist et al. 2026** quantified total plaque volume with deep learning in **23,314 randomly selected Swedes aged 50–64 without ASCVD** and followed them for a median of **7.8 years** for coronary death or MI (287 events). Adding plaque volume to SCORE2 plus the segment involvement score raised the C-statistic from **0.783 to 0.798**. Risk rose with plaque volume **even with a calcium score of zero and in people without manually detected atherosclerosis** ([PubMed 42663364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42663364/); [JACC CVI](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004)). It was an ESC 2026 late-breaker with simultaneous publication ([ACC summary](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2026/08/24/12/30/sat-jacc-studies-esc-2026)). It has only **4 citations (OA, 2026-11-08; online 21 Aug 2026)**, so its standing comes from venue and design, not from citations. No cited source holds this result. Hageman 2023 shows that calcium adds little to SCORE2. Malmqvist shows the same for a CCTA plaque measure in the kind of sample the thesis argues for, with the base score the thesis uses. Under the one-source rule it should **replace `hageman2023score2`** in the sentence "for cardiovascular events Hageman et al. (2023) found that coronary artery calcium improved SCORE2 only slightly". Keep Hageman only if the thesis needs the calcium version of the point somewhere else. The second finding, risk-bearing plaque in visually plaque-free people, is the strongest published support for the thesis rule against "first ever" and for the "plaque first detected at follow-up" definition. It fits the Discussion or the outcome definition better than the review. Wording sketch, without numbers: *"In a random population sample, Malmqvist et al. (2026) found that plaque volume quantified on CCTA improved prediction of coronary events beyond SCORE2 only modestly."* The full text was not read: the software, any industry role and calibration are unknown. Its outcome is events and its baseline is mixed, so it is a benchmark for the expected ΔC, not a near miss to the gap. It also adds a third SCAPIS paper to a review that already leans on that cohort.

**Avigdor et al. 2026** followed **524 SCOT-HEART patients whose baseline CCTA showed normal coronary arteries** for a median of **9.3 years**. Repeat imaging was done only when clinically indicated: 31 had CCTA and 162 had chest CT. CAD appeared on 15 of the 31 CCTAs and 41 of the 162 chest CTs. The authors conclude that about one third develop CAD on clinically indicated CT within ten years ([PubMed 41781727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41781727/)). It has **1 citation (OA, 2026-11-08), about 1 per year**. It is the closest published analogue to the thesis outcome and holds a point no cited source does: development of disease from a normal baseline over a decade. Its weaknesses are the ones the review already argues against: referred chest-pain patients, a small and selected repeat-imaging subset, mostly non-contrast CT, and no geometry. Place it right after Won 2022: *"Avigdor et al. (2026) followed patients with normal coronary arteries in the SCOT-HEART trial for about a decade and found that a considerable share developed coronary disease, although repeat imaging was done only when clinically indicated."* It does not weaken the gap, since it measures no geometry. It does mean the thesis cannot present a ten-year plaque-free-baseline follow-up as new in itself. The novelty has to be stated as the combination of a population sample, a plaque-free baseline, a planned repeat CCTA and geometry. Adding it also makes the sentence "but all three used patients scanned for a clinical reason" wrong in its count. Change it to "all of them". Abstract only.

**Zhang R et al. 2025 (Int J Cardiol)** studied **947 patients with a normal first CCTA and a second CCTA**. A model combining pericoronary-fat radiomics with clinical factors predicted who developed plaque better than either alone, with AUCs of **0.99 in training and 0.93 in validation** ([PubMed 40812623](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40812623/)). It has **4 citations (OA, 2026-11-08), about 3 per year**. By design it is the nearest miss of all: a whole person plaque-free at baseline, person-level incident plaque, and features added to risk factors, which is the thesis's own "who" structure. It still differs in feature type (fat texture, not artery geometry), population (referred) and interval (not stated in the abstract). Lee 2024 already holds the concept "radiomics on a plaque-free scan adds to risk factors". Zhang adds the person-level, wholly plaque-free version, which can be argued to be a distinct point, so it can **join rather than replace Lee**. If the one-source rule is read strictly, keep Lee: it is larger, is already in the table and its C values were confirmed. The AUCs suggest overfitting (279 candidate features, retrospective, a single group). The full text was blocked, so the centres, interval, reason for the repeat scan and validation scheme are unverified. Do not cite it before reading it. Its descriptive companion paper (Eur J Radiol 2025, [PubMed 40450797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40450797/)) is not needed.

## Griffo and Gharleghi bear on two closing sentences, not on the argument

**Griffo et al. 2025** built a PCA statistical shape model of **69 whole LADs from invasive 3D-QCA**. With discriminant analysis it separated arteries whose lesions later caused MI within five years from the others (p<0.0001), even after the main lesion was removed virtually ([PubMed 40561693](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40561693/)). It has **2 citations (OA, 2026-11-08)**. It is the only 2023–2026 coronary study found that relates the shape of a whole artery, beyond single summaries, to a later outcome. That puts pressure on the final clause of `sec:lit-measure`, "in the coronary arteries this has so far been shown in only one study, on invasive angiograms" (Tello Ayala 2026). The clause stays true if it is read as being about a *curvature profile*, because Griffo models 3D shape, not a profile. A reviewer could still read it as wrong. There are two fixes, and both satisfy the rule. Either narrow the sentence to "the curvature profile", or add Griffo as the shape-model counterpart: *"Griffo et al. (2025) found that a statistical model of the whole LAD shape separated arteries that later caused myocardial infarction, although in diseased arteries on invasive angiograms."* It must not be called FPCA. Its arteries were diseased, its sample small, and it had no external validation. Neither of these papers weakens the overall gap, since Griffo's arteries had plaque at baseline. No 2023–2026 FPCA of coronary centerlines was found ([OpenAlex query](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=title_and_abstract.search:functional%20principal%20component%20coronary,from_publication_date:2023-01-01)).

**Gharleghi et al. 2025 (Sci Rep)** simulated flow in **127 left main bifurcations from CTCA of patients with suspected CAD and no significant stenosis**. Of 11 shape features, Finet's ratio correlated most strongly with the area of low time-averaged WSS (p<0.001), while diameters and curvature correlated with oscillatory and multidirectional indices ([PubMed 39747107](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39747107/)). It has **11 citations (OA and S2, 2026-11-08), about 6 per year**. Earlier notes filed it as an arXiv preprint. It is now a peer-reviewed article, so the rule against citing preprints no longer applies. Today `sec:lit-wss` supports "the division of diameter at the bifurcation" only with Garcha's synthetic trees and Zhang's null result for Finet's ratio. Gharleghi supplies the patient-data version. In the closing sentence it would join Garcha rather than replace it, because Garcha holds the interaction between features and Gharleghi holds the patient evidence. Gharleghi is the same Beier/UNSW group and probably the same 127 patients as Kashyap 2022, so it is a second analysis of one dataset, not a replication. "No significant stenosis" also does not mean plaque-free. Check the overlap with Kashyap in the full text before citing. The group's sex-difference paper on the same patients (IEEE TBME 2025, [PubMed 40031780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40031780/)) is not needed.

**Candreva et al. 2024** pooled **6 intravascular studies (136 patients, 31,385 segments)**. Lower baseline WSS went with lumen loss, larger plaque burden and more necrotic core at follow-up ([PubMed 38641263](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38641263/)). It is the only recent evidence synthesis that low WSS precedes progression in humans. The earlier landmark report recommended PREDICTION (`stone2012prediction`) for exactly this concept, so the rule allows one of the two. Choose Candreva if a recent source is preferred. Choose PREDICTION for a single prospective cohort, worded carefully (low ESS predicted lumen narrowing, not plaque growth). Do not use Candreva to replace `chatzizisis2007ess` in the opening sentence, which also carries the link from geometry to WSS. Candreva's sample is small and all of its patients had disease. No 2023–2026 ESC, EAPCI or SCCT consensus on WSS or geometry exists, so `gijsen2019wss` remains the latest consensus ([2019 EHJ recommendations](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/153395)).

## The gap survives, but its wording needs two adjustments

The gap sentence reads: no study "related the geometry of the three main coronary arteries on a plaque-free baseline scan to plaque development at follow-up". **No 2023–2026 paper found meets all of its conditions.** The near misses each lack at least one:

| Paper | Geometry | Plaque-free baseline | Population sample | Plaque at follow-up as outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zhang R 2025 | No (fat radiomics) | Yes, whole person | No (referred) | Yes, person level |
| Avigdor 2026 | No | Yes | No (trial, chest pain) | CAD on clinically indicated CT |
| Malmqvist 2026 | No | Mixed | Yes | No (events) |
| Griffo 2025 | Yes, LAD shape | No | No | No (MI) |
| Gharleghi 2025 | Yes, LM bifurcation | No significant stenosis, not plaque-free | No | None (cross-sectional) |
| Lee SE 2026 PARADIGM PVAT ([PubMed 42900033](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42900033/)) | No | Plaque-free *segments*, all patients had plaque | No | Plaque progression |

Two neighbouring sentences need adjusting even though the gap survives. With Avigdor and possibly Zhang added, the review should no longer imply that plaque-free-baseline follow-up exists only over short intervals. "All three" must become "all of them", and the novelty has to rest on geometry together with the population sample. Lee SE 2026 is also worth noting. In PARADIGM, **baseline** perivascular fat did not predict plaque progression (all P>0.50), and only its change did. That is a published null for a well-known non-geometric CCTA marker measured at baseline, and it belongs in the Discussion if anywhere. One registered study could close the gap from the hospital side. **APPLE (NCT06498830)** is a recruiting Chinese retrospective cohort that enrols only people without plaque at baseline who had at least two CCTAs, with any plaque formation as the primary outcome. Its registration does not mention geometry ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06498830)). No CGPS serial CCTA paper had been published as of 2026-11-08. Re-check APPLE, CArTI and the CGPS group's output right before submission.

## Conclusion

The recent literature narrows the thesis's claim of novelty rather than removing it. Ten-year follow-up from a normal scan now exists (Avigdor), person-level prediction of plaque from a plaque-free scan exists (Zhang R), and imaging beyond SCORE2 in a random population sample exists (Malmqvist). Each of them lacks geometry, and only the last is a population sample. The thesis's contribution is therefore the conjunction of these elements, and the review should say so explicitly rather than rely on any single element being new. Malmqvist also sets the expectation: if a direct plaque measure adds only a little to SCORE2 for events, a geometry block on plaque-free baselines should be expected to add less. That supports the plan's choice to estimate ΔC rather than test it.

Every addition above was read at abstract level only, and Zhang R 2025 needs its full text most, given its implausibly high AUCs and its unknown validation scheme. Under the one-source rule the net change is small: two replacements (Malmqvist for Hageman, Vrints for Knuuti), two or three new clauses (Avigdor, plus optionally Zhang R and Griffo or a narrower curvature-profile sentence) and one optional join (Gharleghi). The review grows by a few sentences, not a paragraph.
