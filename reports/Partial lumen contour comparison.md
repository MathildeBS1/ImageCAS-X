# Clip, align and compare each Medis stretch

The best comparison treats **each contiguous run of Medis lumen rings as its own reference stretch**. Locate it on the tracer centerline with the ring centroids themselves, which needs no Medis tree. Pair every Medis ring with one tracer step by an order-preserving alignment. Recompute every lumen feature from both methods on exactly that window, and summarise agreement with Bland–Altman limits taken from a mixed model with person and stretch as random effects. The geometric core follows the published coronary centerline benchmark, which clips the test centerline with a disc at the reference start and pairs points with a monotone minimum-distance correspondence ([Schaap 2009](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/cn69mc016)). Two parts are my own adaptations, and the thesis must say so: the distal disc, and using ring centroids in place of a reference centerline. The statistical core follows Bland and Altman's method for unequal numbers of observations per subject when the true value varies within the subject ([Bland & Altman 2007](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/3411/1/Bland_2006_Agreement.pdf)). Three features behave differently on short, diseased windows. Per-ring equivalent radius is well determined. Stretch taper is dominated by noise below about 20 mm and is distorted by the plaque that defined the stretch. The LM→LAD and LM→LCx ratios can be compared directly only where Medis contoured both the parent and the daughter. However, their expected agreement can be derived from the per-ring radius model. **What the comparison measures is agreement with a reader-edited reference in diseased lumen of people who already had plaque.** The literature does not support calling that agreement an upper or a lower bound for the plaque-free study cohort.

## Ring centroids, not a Medis tree, mark where the stretch begins and ends

The contour scans do not need the Medis centerline trees. In this delivery each contour comes as lumen and outer-wall rings, with a per-contour centerline that runs along the whole vessel. The project code therefore takes the contoured stretch from the ring centres, not from that centerline (`CGPS_data/thesis_final/split_check.py`). Both the tracer and Medis live in the same LPS mm volume. Every ring centroid is therefore already a 3D landmark on the tracer's coordinate frame, and no registration is needed. Clinical CT–IVUS work matches stretches with side branches and calcifications as fiducials because the two modalities have different frames ([Athanasiou 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4719213/)). That machinery solves a problem this comparison does not have. Its only useful lesson here is to express position along the stretch as arc length once the two ends are fixed.

**Endpoint finding is a three-step procedure.**

1. **Choose the tracer path.** Pick the labelled path (LAD, LCx or RCA trunk, or the LM) that minimises the mean distance to the stretch's ring centroids, so a centroid cannot snap to a diagonal or marginal branch passing nearby.
2. **Clip with discs at both ends.** Place a disc at the first ring and another at the last ring. Each disc is centred on the ring centroid, its normal is the ring's plane normal (fitted from the ring points), and its radius is twice the ring's equivalent radius. Discard tracer points before the first crossing of the proximal disc and after the crossing of the distal disc.
3. **Check the result.** Record the clipped tracer length against the Medis stretch length as a coverage check.

The proximal disc is published: CAT08 clips the evaluated centerline with a disc of radius twice the annotated radius, normal to the reference tangent, and ignores "every point before the first intersection" ([Schaap 2009 §4.3](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/cn69mc016)). **The distal disc is an extension.** CAT08 handled the distal end through the correspondence and a radius cut-off on the reference ([Schaap 2009 §4.4.2](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/cn69mc016)), because its reference ran to the vessel end. A Medis stretch stops in mid-vessel, so a second clip is needed. Describe it as an adaptation of Schaap's disc, not as a standard.

**The finite radius does real work.** A disc limited to 2r fires only where the tracer passes through the lumen. An infinite plane could fire where a tortuous vessel or a neighbouring branch recrosses it. This is reasoning, not a tested result.

**Three failure modes need prespecified handling (reasoning).**
- If the tracer ends before the distal ring, the distal disc is never crossed. Fall back to the tracer's end, and flag the stretch as truncated.
- If the tracer enters a side branch near a stretch end, it can cross the disc off-centre or miss it. The path-selection step guards against this.
- If a ring is badly placed, the disc normal is wrong. Taking the normal from the ring plane rather than a finite-difference tangent of a few centroids avoids most of this.

Schaap's own rule for defining reference ends gives a simple, citable fallback when a disc fails: take the first point, walking in from each end, that lies within the local radius of the other line ([Schaap 2009 §4.2](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/cn69mc016)). Report how often each fallback fired.

## Order-preserving alignment pairs rings with tracer steps

**Once both ends are clipped, pair the rings with tracer steps by a monotone alignment.** The pairing that suits this is Schaap's minimum-total-length, order-preserving correspondence between the Medis centroid sequence and the clipped tracer points. It is solved by Dijkstra on the distance matrix and is structurally dynamic time warping ([Schaap 2009 §4.3](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/cn69mc016)). It must run *after* clipping, because it forces start-to-start and end-to-end connections. Unclipped tails would otherwise produce long many-to-one connections. IVUS alignment work found that a one-to-one correspondence "may not be always possible" and added a penalty favouring diagonal steps to limit plateaus ([Tsiknakis 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8394087/)). The same penalty, or a simple one-tracer-step-per-ring rule after alignment, is reasonable here.

**Why not the alternatives.** Independent nearest-point projection is the method in the superseded draft (`thesis/methodology/10_medis_validation.tex`). It has no ordering guarantee. At the LM bifurcation, the LAD–diagonal and the LCx–OM origins it can jump between vessels or map consecutive rings back and forth. At a stretch end beyond the traced extent, every remaining ring piles onto the tracer's last point. Plain arc length from the ostium drifts distally whenever the two lines differ in smoothing or start point. Normalising arc length between the two clipped ends limits the drift to the stretch. **The research found no study that compares these three schemes head to head on coronary CT centerlines**, so the choice rests on Schaap's precedent and on the failure modes above, not on evidence of superior accuracy.

**Gate the pairs before computing anything.** CAT08 counts distance accuracy only on connections shorter than the reference radius, because "in case of a tracking failure the magnitude of the distance … is no longer relevant" ([Schaap 2009 §4.4.3](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/cn69mc016)). The same logic applies here. Flag as a mismatch any pair whose centroid distance exceeds the ring's equivalent radius, and report it separately rather than averaging it into lumen error. Add a gate on the angle between the tracer's cutting plane and the ring plane, because an oblique cut inflates area by roughly 1/cos θ. The cleaner alternative is to **cut the tracer lumen on the Medis ring plane**. That removes centerline-orientation error from the radius comparison entirely. Neither threshold has a published value, so fix them before looking at the results.

## Recompute each feature on the shared window, and expect taper to be noisy

**Every lumen feature must be recomputed from both methods on the same window.** Comparing the tracer's whole-trunk taper with a Medis taper over a 15 mm stretch would confound measurement disagreement with a change in what is measured. Proximal segments taper more, and the stretch may or may not include branch points and plaque. Both published CT-vs-IVUS designs compare only over the stretch the reference covers. Papadopoulou et al. used 1,364 co-registered 1 mm cross-sections and 255 segments of 5 mm ([Papadopoulou 2011](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/detection-and-quantification-of-coronary-atherosclerotic-plaque-b/)). Luo et al. compared lumen area point pair by point pair, and they noted length misalignment at the end points ([Luo 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906085/)). **Restricting the comparison to the Medis stretch is therefore the standard design, not a compromise.**

### Equivalent radius per ring

Compute the equivalent radius r = √(A/π) from each Medis lumen ring and from the paired tracer section. This matches the thesis taper code, which uses an area-equivalent radius (`tortuosity/taper.py`). Analyse it on the log scale: differences in ln r are relative errors, and they stay comparable from the proximal LM to small distal lumens. For scale, CT lumen against IVUS at matched positions gave a diameter normalised RMSE of 9.5% and a CSA normalised RMSE of 16.2% ([Luo 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906085/)). Expect tracer–Medis differences of a similar order, since both read the same CT.

Exclude rings with very small lumen. TAG and normal-taper studies stopped where CSA fell below 2.0 mm² or diameter below 2 mm, the partial-volume zone ([PMC4104747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4104747)). The thesis taper code already drops samples near junctions and in the first 3 mm (`tortuosity/taper.py`). Apply the same exclusions to both methods.

### Stretch taper

The thesis defines taper as minus ten times the Theil–Sen slope of ln r against arc length. It is fitted over a whole trunk and declared missing below 20 mm of usable arc (`tortuosity/taper.py`). Two consequences follow.

**A log-scale slope is unchanged by any proportional bias.** If the tracer reads every radius k times the Medis value, both slopes agree exactly. This is algebra, and it makes the per-ring log-radius bias and the taper agreement two separate questions.

**Taper over a short stretch is mostly noise.** For an OLS slope through independent sections of residual SD σ at density n per mm over length L, the slope variance is about 12σ²/(nL³). This is a textbook regression result, not a coronary finding, and Theil–Sen is somewhat less efficient. Take σ = 0.1 mm in radius at n = 1/mm, an assumed figure of the order of the CT error above. The slope SD is then about 0.31 mm per 10 mm at L = 5 mm, 0.11 at 10 mm and 0.04 at 20 mm. Normal taper is only about 0.11 mm of radius per 10 mm, half of a reported 0.22 mm of diameter ([e-century IJCEM](https://e-century.us/files/ijcem/9/3/ijcem0016899.pdf); snippet-level source, verify before citing). **Below roughly 10 mm the noise is as large as the signal, and at 20 mm it is still about a third of it.** Correlated errors between neighbouring tracer steps make the effective n smaller still.

Plaque inside the stretch adds a bias. A proximal lesion flattens or reverses the fitted slope, and a distal one steepens it. Because the Medis stretches exist because of plaque, taper agreement on them tests segmentation agreement in diseased lumen. It does not validate taper as a feature.

Four practical rules follow:
- keep the thesis's own 20 mm minimum as the primary threshold;
- add a 10 mm sensitivity analysis;
- refit with sections near the minimal lumen excluded, by analogy with the TAG studies that removed calcified lesions from the regression ([PMC4104747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4104747));
- report the stretch-level taper LoA next to the expected noise SD, so that wide limits are not read as tracer failure.

### Diameter ratios

The LM→LAD and LM→LCx ratios can be compared directly only where Medis contoured both the LM window and the proximal daughter window at the thesis's fixed offsets. That subset will be small, so report its n. The per-ring model offers an indirect route, which is reasoning rather than a published method. The log-ratio difference between the methods is d(ln r_LM) − d(ln r_daughter). In a model d = μ + u_person + v_stretch + ε, the person effect cancels. The variance of the log-ratio difference is then about 2σ²_ε, plus 2σ²_v when the two radii come from different stretches. **This gives an expected LoA for the ratios even for people with only one window contoured.** It rests on two assumptions: the errors at the parent and the daughter are independent beyond the person effect, and errors near the carina behave like errors elsewhere. Bifurcation work excludes a ±5 mm carina zone partly because of exactly that doubt ([Radunović 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2023.1292517/full)). Check the derived limits against the direct subset wherever it is large enough.

## Clustered limits treat the stretch as the unit and the person as the cluster

**Pooling rings, or stretches, as if they were independent makes the limits of agreement too narrow.** Averaging to one value per person makes them too narrow as well, because the result then describes an average of several measurements rather than a single one ([Bland & Altman 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17613642/)). Their "true value varies" method fits a one-way ANOVA of the paired differences on subject. The between-subject component is (MS_subjects − MS_residual)/D, with D = [(Σmᵢ)² − Σmᵢ²]/[(n−1)Σmᵢ]. The LoA SD is the square root of the within-subject plus between-subject variance, and the bias is the plain mean of all differences. The 1999 paper wrongly multiplied by this factor instead of dividing ([Bland & Altman 2007, author PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/3411/1/Bland_2006_Agreement.pdf)).

The natural generalisation for rings nested in stretches nested in persons is a mixed model, d = μ + u_person + v_stretch + ε, with LoA = μ ± 1.96√(σ²_u + σ²_v + σ²_ε). This variance-component formulation goes back to Carstensen and colleagues, who distinguish exchangeable from linked replicates ([Carstensen 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/ijbist/v4y2008i1n16.html); abstract only, formulas not verified). It is implemented in `SimplyAgree::loa_lme` ([CRAN](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/SimplyAgree/html/loa_lme.html)).

**Autocorrelation is the one assumption the method cannot absorb.** Bland and Altman call within-subject independence "rather strong" and suggest plotting the differences against order ([author PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/3411/1/Bland_2006_Agreement.pdf)). Neighbouring tracer steps share image content, so thin the pairs to about one per 1 mm, the spacing Papadopoulou used ([Papadopoulou 2011](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/detection-and-quantification-of-coronary-atherosclerotic-plaque-b/)), or fit an AR(1) residual. Get confidence intervals by a person-level cluster bootstrap, which matches the thesis's person-split cross-validation (reasoning).

**Disjoint stretches stay separate.** A person can have several disjoint stretches in one artery, for example contoured proximal and distal LAD lesions with an uncontoured gap. Do not fit one taper across them: that fits a slope over unmeasured vessel and often across a branch point. Treat each stretch as one stretch-level observation with a person random effect, so the LoA describes a single stretch measurement. If an artery-level summary is wanted, take a length-weighted mean for mean radius. For slopes, inverse-variance weighting under the formula above implies weights proportional to L³ (reasoning).

**Report counts and both versions of the limits.** Give the number of persons, stretches and rings at every gate, and show the naive pooled LoA next to the clustered one. Most CT-vs-IVUS papers pool points as if they were independent; Luo pooled 1,314 pairs from five patients ([Luo 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906085/)). Showing both lets a reader see how much clustering widens the limits.

**No published minimum stretch length exists** for comparing lumen features. The published units are 1 mm sections and 5 mm segments ([Papadopoulou 2011](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/detection-and-quantification-of-coronary-atherosclerotic-plaque-b/)), 1 cm sampling for normal CT diameters ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221834308_Coronary_artery_lumen_diameter_and_bifurcation_angle_derived_from_CT_coronary_angiographic_image_in_healthy_people)), and a 20 mm window for defining marked taper ([e-century IJCEM](https://e-century.us/files/ijcem/9/3/ijcem0016899.pdf)). Two rules are defensible and should be fixed in advance, with the number of stretches each one excludes reported:
- per-ring radius needs no minimum length;
- stretch mean radius and taper need at least 20 mm and at least 60% usable samples, the same rule as the feature itself, with 10 mm as a sensitivity analysis.

## A plaque-only reference cannot bound error in plaque-free trunks

**The literature does not support calling diseased-segment agreement either conservative or optimistic.** In the Rotterdam lumen benchmark, Dice was a few points lower in diseased than in healthy segments, but mean and maximum surface distances were similar or lower. For example, the best automatic method had Dice 70 vs 73% and MSD 0.40 vs 0.39 mm. The authors attribute the lower distances partly to "the smaller scale of the diseased vessel" ([Kirişli thesis, Table 4.11 and §4.5.3](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/40314/130614_Kirisli,%20Hortensa%20Ayla.pdf)). The benchmark's "healthy" means no stenosis of 50% or more, not plaque-free ([Kirişli thesis §4.3.6](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/40314/130614_Kirisli,%20Hortensa%20Ayla.pdf)). Against IVUS, the CT lumen difference "tended to be larger among coronary segments without atherosclerotic plaque" ([Falcão 2015](https://scielo.br/j/abc/a/mcMsDcm8xntCkcnR7BJRmxc/?lang=en)). In calcified sections, CT lumen accuracy was limited by "high variance and not … systematic error", and was worst in the smallest lumens ([Noll 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3915947)). This corrects the earlier report's working hypothesis that plaque-free trunks are easier and that the Medis agreement is therefore an upper bound on error. **The error mechanisms differ; the direction is unknown.**

Plaque stretches present blooming, soft-plaque–lumen boundaries and lesions that pull a contour, none of which the plaque-free cohort has. They under-sample the generic problems that dominate there: partial volume, which overestimates area in small lumens ([Freiman, arXiv 1906.09763](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.09763), preprint version; journal venue not verified), plus branch ostia and centerline drift (reasoning).

The reference itself was validated mainly at lesions. QAngio CT against IVUS reported minimal lumen area r = 0.75 over 146 lesions, from abstract-level data ([Boogers 2012](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/64020)). No QAngio lumen validation in normal segments was found. Any disagreement therefore mixes tracer error with Medis and reader error, and must be called **agreement with a reference, not accuracy**.

**What the comparison can say:**
- whether the tracer finds the lumen boundary as Medis does where plaque is present;
- whether its radius bias is constant, proportional or position-dependent;
- whether taper and ratios computed from it reproduce Medis's on the same window.

**What it cannot say:** what the error is in the plaque-free trunks of the prediction cohort. Those trunks differ in lumen size, calcium and position along the tree, and the people differ in age and image quality.

**Three steps narrow that gap without closing it.**
1. **Stratify** by ring-level plaque status. A ring whose lumen and outer wall nearly coincide is the closest thing to plaque-free vessel in the data. Also stratify by the visual grade from the BL read (`_stenosis`, `_plaque_type`) and by reference radius, as the benchmark and IVUS studies stratify rather than pool ([Noll 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3915947)).
2. **Report minimal and mild grades separately**, because those segments sit nearest the target cohort.
3. **Use the whole-tree Medis centerline comparison** for the curvature and angle features, because those trees are not limited to diseased segments.

Any statement that agreement transfers to plaque-free people is an assumption, and the thesis should label it as one.

## Conclusion

The partial coverage changes less than it first appears for the radius and more than it appears for taper. Per-ring radius agreement is well posed on any stretch, and the mixed-model LoA turns it into the main lumen result. The same variance components also predict the ratio agreement where direct LM-plus-daughter pairs are rare. Taper is the weak point. On Medis stretches it is short, and it is diseased by construction. Its agreement mostly measures noise and plaque distortion, not the feature as used in the plaque-free model. The honest presentation sets the taper LoA beside its expected noise, and does not offer it as validation of the feature.

The recommended method, in two sentences of thesis text: *Each contiguous run of Medis lumen contours was located on the tracer centerline of the same artery by clipping it with discs at the first and last contour (centred on the contour, normal to its plane, radius twice its equivalent radius; adapted from Schaap et al. 2009), and the contours were paired with tracer cross-sections by an order-preserving minimum-distance correspondence, with pairs further apart than one lumen radius excluded. The equivalent radius, taper and LM→LAD/LCx diameter ratios were recomputed from both methods on the same window, and agreement was summarised as Bland–Altman bias and 95% limits of agreement from a mixed model with person and stretch as random effects, with taper restricted to stretches of at least 20 mm and all results reported separately by plaque status of the cross-section.*
