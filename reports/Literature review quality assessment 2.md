## Priority 1: seven sentences an examiner can check against the cited paper

These come first because each one can be falsified from a single cited source, and two of them (the tortuosity synthesis and the "not tested" sentence) sit at the chapter's argumentative peaks. All replacements keep author and year as subject, use no numbers, put nothing between two commas, define nothing, and cite every element of any synthesis.

**The tortuosity reconciliation (LR:96-109) is wrong, and the Zebić Mihić sentence feeding it is wrong in part.** The chapter says "counts of discrete bends related inversely to obstructive disease and continuous measures related positively to disease or ischemia". The Zebić Mihić full text reports that both methods found more tortuosity in non-obstructive than in obstructive disease: "TI was higher in NOCAD compared to OCAD ... (median LCX 1.30 vs. 1.15, p < 0.001; LAD 1.26 vs. 1.18, p < 0.001; RCA 2.00 vs. 1.87, p = 0.014)". The angle method also found the LCX-lateral ischemia association (OR 4.9, p = 0.046). Only the LAD-anterior association was index-only ([PMC10795752](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/)). The study's ischemia analysis was confined to non-obstructive patients. Nannini's CCTA score is itself a bend count, and it found tortuosity inversely related to calcium volume ([PMC10807932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10807932/)). The split therefore runs by endpoint, not by measure, which is what the author's own knowledge bank already says: "They agree once the endpoint is stated precisely" (`find_research.md` lines 116-119). Tello Ayala remains a genuine positive finding for stenosis (OR 1.05 per SD for any RCA stenosis) ([PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). The honest synthesis states that one conflict survives and names the features the studies share. Groves also did not count bends. Its criterion was "two consecutive 180 degrees turns by visual estimation" ([Europe PMC 19585899](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/19585899)). Li's criterion (at least three bends of at least 45 degrees) is therefore not "similar", and Li's follow-up comparison was made within CAD patients ([PMC3164184](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/)).

> Groves et al.\ (2009) \cite{groves2009tortuosity} graded tortuosity visually on the invasive angiograms of consecutive patients and found severe tortuosity to be associated with less obstructive disease.
> Li et al.\ (2011) \cite{li2011tortuosity} reported the same inverse association with a bend count, and tortuous patients with CAD fared no worse at follow-up.
> Zebi\'c Mihi\'c et al.\ (2024) \cite{zebicmihic2024index} compared a continuous tortuosity index with the bend count in the same patients.
> Both measures were higher in non-obstructive than in obstructive disease, and the index related to myocardial ischemia in more arterial territories than the count.
> [Tello Ayala sentence unchanged.]
> Collectively, these studies related tortuosity inversely to obstructive disease whether it was graded, counted or measured continuously \cite{groves2009tortuosity,li2011tortuosity,zebicmihic2024index}, and positively to ischemia without obstructive disease \cite{zebicmihic2024index}.
> Only Tello Ayala et al.\ (2026) \cite{telloayala2026tortuosity} found the opposite direction for stenosis, and all of these studies measured tortuosity on projection angiograms of referred patients.

The last sentence also raises item 8 (projection angiography, which bears on what transfers to CCTA). To raise item 6 as well, one cited sentence can add the confounders that both Li and Tello Ayala report: "Li et al.\ (2011) and Tello Ayala et al.\ (2026) also found tortuosity more common in women \cite{li2011tortuosity,telloayala2026tortuosity}." The basis is the Li refs.bib note ("far more common in women") and the Tello Ayala note (female sex β 0.17) (`papers/telloayala2026tortuosity.md`). This should be confirmed in the Li full text before it goes in.

**"Geometry has not been tested in this role" (LR:162-163) is contradicted inside the chapter.** Tommasino's CLAP score combined the left main angle with clinical variables into a patient-level score that predicted later events ([PMC11595042](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11595042/fullTextXML)). Han related geometry to later acute coronary syndromes ([PMC8792800](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8792800/)). Outside the chapter, CArTI extracted automated tortuosity, curvature and torsion features from CCTA and predicted five-year MACE ([Circulation 2025 abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4365988)). The coverage note reports CArTI's population as unstated. The fix narrows the claim to what no cited study has done:

> Imaging can therefore refine an individual estimate beyond systemic factors.
> Geometry has been related to later events only in referred patients \cite{han2022plaque,tommasino2024clap}, and not to the onset of plaque in people without known disease.

Once CArTI has been read, it can be added to that citation if its cohort proves clinical. Until then, "in clinical cohorts" is the safer phrase if CArTI is cited.

**Han "came closest to a prospective design" (LR:142).** The two notes disagree here. The citation note marked the Han row correct. The appraisal note calls the phrase inaccurate, because Han is "a nested case-control substudy of ICONIC" and the paragraph then cites serial cohorts that are more prospective. The appraisal is right on the wording, and it also identifies the limitation that matters for an individual-risk thesis. The main comparison was culprit precursors against non-culprit lesions in the same patients, so the design shows where events arise, not who develops plaque (`papers/han2022plaque.md`). The citation note's facts still hold: a median of 29 days from scan to event, and normal-segment culprits excluded.

> Han et al.\ (2022) \cite{han2022plaque} nested a case-control comparison in a registry of patients referred for suspected CAD.
> They compared lesions that later caused an acute coronary syndrome with other lesions in the same patients and found that culprit lesions lay nearer the ostium and more often at bifurcations.
> However, the typical event followed within weeks of the scan, and patients whose culprit lay in a segment that was normal on CCTA were excluded, so the study concerned where events arise in existing plaque rather than who develops plaque.

**Griffo's design sentence (LR:78-79) over-extends the infarction subset.** The 748 patients pooled FAME 2, FIRE and a future-culprit series. Only the 187-vessel prognostic test set came from patients who all later had an infarction (`papers/griffo2026wss.md`).

> Both estimates discriminated only moderately, and the prognostic test set consisted of single vessels reconstructed from invasive angiograms of patients who all went on to have an infarction.

**Tommasino (LR:130-133) has two wording errors.** Most events were imaging-detected progression (42 progression, 12 target-lesion revascularizations, 5 MI, 3 cardiovascular deaths), with imaging performed "when clinically indicated". The point estimate does reproduce from Table 2 (exp(1.499) = 4.48 against a reported 4.474). The interval does not (2.99 to 6.71 against a reported 3.799 to 6.701), and neither do two p-values. Table 2 is also labelled logistic regression while the text calls the estimates hazard ratios ([PMC11595042](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11595042/fullTextXML); `papers/tommasino2024clap.md`). The appraisal note's "accurate" verdict predates this re-read, so the citation note wins.

> In a single center, Tommasino et al.\ (2024) \cite{tommasino2024clap} reported that a wide left main bifurcation angle accompanied proximal LAD disease and its progression within a year.
> Most of their events were progression on imaging that clinicians had requested, and the reported confidence intervals and p-values cannot be reproduced from their own regression table.

**Nannini (LR:112-113) describes the tool, not the study.** The appraisal note, working from `literature.md`, called Nannini's measure "continuous three-dimensional". The full text says the score was "the count of branches with three or more bends with change in direction forming an angle >45°" and that "all statistical analyses pertaining to CorT and CAC relied solely on manual segmentations" ([PMC10807932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10807932/)). The full text wins, and it makes Nannini a CCTA bend count with an inverse finding, which supports the endpoint reconciliation above.

> Nannini et al.\ (2024) \cite{nannini2024characterization} combined deep learning segmentation with centerline extraction to count tortuous branches on CCTA, although their reported tortuosity analyses used manual segmentations.
> They found tortuosity inversely related to the volume of coronary calcium.

**Small citation corrections.** Temov reports only the LAD-LCX bifurcation angle, so "how they branch and bend" (LR:28) should read "how they branch", or bending needs its own citation ([Europe PMC 27076223](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27076223)). Zebić Mihić is "2024" in the text, but the bib entry `zebicmihic2024index` has `year = {2023}` (journal issue 2024, epub December 2023), so the printed year will disagree with the prose. The table needs three cell edits. Stone should read "plaque-area growth predicted by baseline plaque", because low WSS also predicted the exploratory increase in plaque burden ([Europe PMC 22723305](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22723305)). Li's "no event difference" holds within CAD patients. Chan's incremental-value finding comes from the 3,393-patient validation cohort, not the 40,091 ([PMC11664027](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11664027/)). Sakellarios's daughter-branch advantage was stated for lumen reduction, not plaque progression generally ([Europe PMC 28606882](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28606882)). The Bransby bib entry needs the preprint's actual title and `eprint = {2608.30404}`.

## Priority 2: the gap holds as a conjunction but must name its near misses

The coverage search found **no study that measured whole-tree geometry automatically in a general population and related it to new plaque on a later scan**. Every near miss fails at least two of the four qualifiers (`coverage_gaps.md` Q3). The two notes disagree only on whether to qualify the claim. Coverage says it "holds as written"; the appraisal wants "identified in this search". Both are right. The claim is true against everything found, but the search statement names no database or window, so a universal "No study has" is warranted only by the search the chapter does not document. Qualifying costs nothing and shifts the burden onto a completed search statement.

The near misses now need to appear in the chapter, because an examiner can name them. **Chen 2024** (EHJ-CVI) followed 295 referred patients with an LAD myocardial bridge and no proximal plaque to a repeat CCTA. A cross-sectional vascular radiomics model predicted new proximal plaque (external AUC 0.75) and raised a clinical and anatomical model from 0.56 to 0.75 ([Europe PMC 38781436](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38781436)). It is paywalled and unread, and whether its "vascular radiomics" includes centreline shape cannot be confirmed, so it must not be called geometry until read. **Lee 2024** (JCCT, PARADIGM) predicted new plaque in 9,583 plaque-free segments from 1,162 registry patients. Radiomics combined with clinical factors reached a C-index of 0.767, against 0.696 for clinical factors alone ([Europe PMC 38378314](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38378314)). It comes from the same registry as Won, so the sentence "Plaque-free cohorts with repeat CCTA have come from clinical registries without geometry \cite{won2022paradigm}" invites the obvious counterexample. **CArTI** (Circulation 2025 abstract) is the automated CCTA tortuosity-to-MACE study discussed above ([Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4365988)). The **SCAPIS re-examination** is re-scanning about 15,000 general-population participants a median of 8.1 years after baseline, with the stated aim to "quantify and explain the development of atherosclerosis" ([Europe PMC 41558989](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41558989)). It is the one competing cohort that could close the gap, and naming it makes the thesis look aware rather than lucky. The abstract does not confirm that contrast CCTA was repeated, so the sentence should say "repeat examination", not "repeat CCTA", until the full text has been checked.

One tension with the house exclusion deserves a decision. LR:33 excludes "plaque radiomics without vessel geometry". Lee 2024 is radiomics of plaque-free segments, not of plaque, so the exclusion does not cover it literally. Chen 2024 may contain centreline features. Citing both as near misses in the gap paragraph is stronger than leaning on the exclusion. Replacement for LR:168-179, with new keys marked and to be read before use:

> Taken together, the evidence leaves one combination untested.
> Studies relating geometry to disease have been cross-sectional or have followed referred patients whose plaque was already present \cite{li2011tortuosity,telloayala2026tortuosity,han2022plaque}.
> Studies linking geometry to flow have examined single bifurcations, single vessels, small sets of trees or synthetic trees \cite{kashyap2022tortuosity,zhang2024curvature,griffo2026wss,garcha2025sensitivity}.
> Plaque-free cohorts with repeat CCTA have come from clinical registries \cite{won2022paradigm,lee2024radiomics}, and the closest designs related radiomic features of a single segment \cite{chen2024bridge} or of plaque-free segments \cite{lee2024radiomics} to new plaque.
> General-population CCTA cohorts have reported plaque without geometry \cite{bergstrom2021scapis}, and a repeat examination of the same cohort is under way \cite{good2026scapis}.
> No study identified in this search has measured the geometry of the whole coronary tree automatically in a general population and related it to new plaque on a later scan.
> Closing this gap would show whether geometry adds to age and sex in identifying who develops plaque, using CCTA scans that are already acquired.

Adding Garcha makes the flow clause exhaustive. The alternative is to drop the clause, since the thesis computes angles, radius ratios and curvature without simulating flow. Changing "systemic factors" to "age and sex" aligns the significance sentence with the introduction's "baseline model of age and sex" (`thesis/introduction/01_introduction.tex` line 56). Citing `fuchs2023cgps` once here, as the cohort that can close the gap, would tie the review to the design. It is currently cited only in the introduction. If Chen 2024 cannot be read before submission, its clause should be dropped rather than cited from the abstract.

## Priority 3: four aims still arrive without literature behind them

An examiner reading the introduction's aims will look for prior work on each named feature. Angles are covered (Givehchi, Cui, Juan, Tommasino, Bekirçavuşoğlu), curvature is covered (Kashyap, Zhang, Tello Ayala), and the transformer against logistic regression is the best-motivated aim in the chapter. Four aims are not covered, and each needs one or two sentences in the existing strands.

**Artery labelling** is a named pipeline stage with no literature at all. Hampe 2024 performs joint extraction and labelling on CCTA with graph networks and is already in refs.bib as `hampe2024gnn` ([Europe PMC 38756439](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38756439)). Wu 2019 TreeLab-Net was the first tree-structured CCTA labeller ([doi:10.1007/s11548-018-1884-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11548-018-1884-6)). Bekirçavuşoğlu's ramus intermedius result and Cui's LAD-LCX angle both presuppose labelled branches, which gives a cited bridge. For the extraction paragraph, after Bransby:

> Assigning the extracted branches to named arteries has been automated on CCTA with tree and graph networks \cite{wu2019treelab,hampe2024gnn}.

**Branch radius ratios** rest only on Garcha's synthetic trees. Taylor 2024 is a systematic review of Murray's law in the coronary circulation and is in refs.bib as `taylor2024murray` ([Europe PMC 38787386](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38787386)). A Europe PMC query found no clinical study relating radius ratio or Murray deviation to plaque (357 hits, all PCI, QCA or physiology). The absence is itself part of the gap. After the Garcha sentences:

> Branch radius ratios rest on scaling laws \cite{taylor2024murray} and simulation \cite{garcha2025sensitivity}, and no clinical study identified in this search has related them to plaque.

**The age-and-sex baseline** is not motivated, and the gap's "systemic factors" contradicts it. Tello Ayala's transformer and logistic regression both included age and sex (`papers/telloayala2026tortuosity.md`). Saying so motivates the baseline at no extra length:

> Tello Ayala et al.\ (2026) also showed that a transformer reading the full turning-angle profile with age and sex identified CAD better than a logistic regression on the mean angle with the same covariates.

**Per-artery models "since shear acts locally"** have no supporting synthesis, although the elements are already cited. Tello Ayala is RCA-only, Garcha and Zhang are left-tree-only, and Bergström found plaque "most frequent in the proximal and mid LAD" (`papers/bergstrom2021scapis.md`). One fully cited sentence, closing the measurement section, would do:

> These studies examined the right coronary artery or the left tree alone \cite{telloayala2026tortuosity,garcha2025sensitivity,zhang2024curvature}, although plaque is distributed unevenly across the arteries \cite{bergstrom2021scapis}.

**Calcium scoring** lifts item 3. One sentence beside Chan, citing the 2021 ESC guideline ([Europe PMC 34558602](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34558602)) or the open MESA paper by Detrano 2008 ([doi:10.1056/nejmoa072100](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa072100)), positions geometry against the established imaging refiner. EMERALD ([Europe PMC 29550316](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29550316)) and PROSPECT ESS ([Europe PMC 28917684](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28917684)) would balance Stone's partly negative result with CCTA- and IVUS-derived WSS that predicted future culprits. They are should-cites, not threats to novelty, and both are paywalled and unread. Dominance belongs in methods, not the review.

The rewrite changed the kind of problem the chapter has. Round one failed on structure and on errors any examiner could spot. This version fails mostly where it tried to do the work of a grade 12 review, offering its own reconciliation and a sharp gap, and overreached by a clause each time. That is a better failure. The corrections need no new reading, because the knowledge bank already contains the endpoint-based reconciliation, the Han design, the Griffo subset and the Tommasino table.
