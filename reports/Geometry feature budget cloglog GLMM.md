# Count person-level terms, then spend five

**The pooled cloglog GLMM can support the planned 5-df linear geometry block, and the 7-df Mundlak version, at an anticipated full-model C of 0.70, but only if the budget counts the parameters that vary between people. Under the strict count of every fixed effect it cannot.** Riley's shrinkage criterion at person level (n = 1,076, 452 events, 42 %) gives P_max ≈ **8 / 15 / 18.5 / 24 parameters at C = 0.65 / 0.70 / 0.72 / 0.75**. The person-varying base costs 3 df with recalibrated SCORE2 (log follow-up, SCORE2, dominance) and 8 df with the refitted factors. That leaves **12 and 7 geometry df at C = 0.70**. If the 8 segment dummies and log length are also counted (12 and 17 df), only 3 df remain with the recalibrated base, the refitted base is already over budget, and the 5-df block fits only at C ≥ 0.72 or 0.75. A synthetic simulation of the actual pooled model sides with the person-varying count. At a true base C of about 0.70, the mean out-of-sample calibration slope of the aggregated person risk stayed **≥ 0.92 at 5 geometry df and ≥ 0.90 up to 12 df** in all eight scenarios. Single datasets were much less reassuring: **up to 40 %** of them had a slope below 0.9 at 5 df. The thesis should cite Riley's criteria with C = 0.70 as the primary anticipated value, 0.65 as the conservative bound, and state the two-level count openly as a judgement no published rule covers. Three numbers are still missing and must be obtained before outcome and geometry are linked: a real pmsampsize run, a count of segment events in the clean cohort, and σ̂ from the null GLMM.

## Riley's shrinkage criterion binds, and the anticipated C moves the budget threefold

Riley's three binary-outcome criteria are an expected shrinkage of S ≥ 0.9, an optimism of at most 0.05 in Nagelkerke R², and a mean risk estimated within ±0.05 ([Riley et al. 2020, BMJ](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m441); [pmsampsize manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/pmsampsize/refman/pmsampsize.html)). Criterion (i) solved for the number of parameters is P_max = n(S − 1)ln(1 − R²_CS/S), with R²_CS obtained from an anticipated C by assuming a normal linear predictor with equal variance in events and non-events ([Riley, Van Calster & Collins 2021, Stat Med](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/a-note-on-estimating-the-cox-snell-rsub2sub-from-a-reported-c-sta)). At 42 % prevalence the maximum R²_CS is 0.744 ([Riley et al. 2019, Part II](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6519266)). Two independent reimplementations [own calc] agree on the numbers below and reproduce the draft's "≈ 15 at C = 0.70". Criterion (ii) never binds (P ≈ 42–45), and criterion (iii) needs only n ≥ 374.

| anticipated C | R²_CS | P_max, n = 1,076 | P_max, 1,041 complete cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.65 | 0.068 | 8.4 | 8.1 |
| 0.68 | 0.096 | 12.2 | – |
| **0.70** | **0.118** | **15.1** | **14.6** |
| 0.72 | 0.142 | 18.5 | 17.8 |
| 0.75 | 0.181 | 24.2 | 23.3 |
| Riley default, 15 % of max R²_CS | 0.112 | 14.2 | 13.8 |

The budget depends almost entirely on the anticipated C: ±0.05 moves it from 8 to 24. Three external anchors support **C = 0.70** as the primary value. First, Riley's generic default of a Nagelkerke R² of 0.15 ([Riley et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6519266)) lands at C ≈ 0.70 at this prevalence [own calc]. Second, the closest incident-outcome analogue is MESA, where traditional risk factors predicted conversion from CAC = 0 to CAC > 0 with an **apparent C of 0.688** ([Okwuosa et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3310187)). That study had low-risk participants, 2.5 years of follow-up and a CAC outcome, so its biases point in opposite directions. Third, cross-sectional C for *prevalent* CAC > 0 is 0.75–0.79 ([Pletcher et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3840900)), and for plaque in PESA about 0.69 against 0.78 for CAC ([ACC summary of PESA](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2017/11/13/15/51/established-and-new-risk-scores-both-accurately-predict-subclinical-atherosclerosis)). These are an upper reference that a cohort restricted to plaque-free people should fall below. No source reports a C for SCORE2 or any score predicting incident CCTA plaque, or a C or ΔC for geometry predicting incident plaque. C = 0.75 therefore has no support. The draft's PARADIGM value (0.68) comes from a referral cohort with progression of existing plaque as the outcome, was not verified, and should be dropped or demoted.

The project's own base model is a data-informed check. Its out-of-fold C is 0.687 (recalibrated) and 0.686 (refitted), and its out-of-fold log-loss converts to **R²_CS ≈ 0.099 and 0.087** [own calc from [score2_honesty.md](file:///Users/mathildebrinchsorensen/Desktop/ImageCAS-X/CGPS_data/score2_honesty.md)]. Both lie between the C = 0.65 and 0.70 rows. Riley treats an existing model's optimism-adjusted R² as a lower bound for a model that adds predictors ([Riley et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6519266)), so a full-model C of 0.70 is consistent with it. Two caveats apply. The anchor uses this cohort's outcome. It also comes from the person-level `score2_honesty.py` run, which by project rule is not the pooled model. If it is quoted, the draft's sentence that the budget "used the number of participants and events only" must change.

## The counting rule decides the answer, and no published rule covers it

pmsampsize assumes independent individuals. No published extension of the Riley criteria to clustered or multilevel development data was found, and a 2026 methods overview lists hierarchical data as future work ([Shamsutdinova et al., arXiv 2602.23507, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23507)). The clustered-data simulations that exist concern patients clustered in a few large centres ([Wynants et al. 2015](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/91572)). They do not fit 1,076 small clusters in which the cluster, the person, is the prediction target, so their finding that clustering hardly matters should not be carried over [inference].

The algebra gives a defensible two-level rule [inference]. A person-constant covariate, such as SCORE2 or its factors, log follow-up, dominance, the LM angle or the LM→LAD and LM→LCx ratios, takes its information from between-person contrasts and should be counted against the person-level Riley budget. The LM features are entered only on some rows but are still one number per person. The segment dummies are different. When every person contributes all 9 segments, Σⱼ exp(αⱼ + xᵢβ) = exp(xᵢβ)Σⱼ exp(αⱼ), so the 8 dummies collapse into one constant in the aggregated person risk. They are identified from *where* the events fall, not from *who* has them. Log length behaves almost the same way. For a regression coefficient under clustering, the Scott–Holt design factor ≈ 1 + (m − 1)ρₓρ_y depends on the covariate's own within-cluster correlation ([McMaster seminar slides on Scott & Holt 1982](https://math.mcmaster.ca/canty/seminars/0102/april3sem.pdf)). A segment-varying covariate with low ρₓ therefore pays little clustering penalty. This formula is for linear and survey estimation and was not verified for a conditional cloglog GLMM.

The counting rule for the thesis follows from this. **Person-level df = person-constant base terms + person-level geometry + 1 per shared-slope segment feature (curvature, taper).** The segment features are counted conservatively, because the between-person part of their slope is what answers "who". The dummies and log length go into a separate segment-level count. Two smaller items count as well. The random-intercept SD is identified from the overdispersion of segment events per person, so counting it as 1 person-level df is conservative and cheap. Riley counts candidate parameters, not only the ones retained ([Riley et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m441)), and the open choice between recalibrated SCORE2 and the refitted factors is to be made by comparing the two in the outcome data. That comparison is a selection step, so the conservative count charges the larger base (8 df) even if the 1-df recalibrated score is kept [inference]. Penalisation cannot buy extra df for the confirmatory block. Ridge and lasso are least reliable exactly where overfitting is large ([Riley et al. 2021, J Clin Epidemiol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8026952/)), and the LR test of the geometry block needs unpenalised ML.

At the primary C = 0.70, the person-varying count supports the 5-df block with either base and the 7-df Mundlak block with the recalibrated base. With the refitted base, the Mundlak block has a margin of 0.1 df. That margin disappears if the random-intercept SD is counted (6.1 left) or if diabetes joins the factors. At the conservative bound C = 0.65, only the recalibrated base with 5 df fits. With the refitted base nothing fits at C = 0.65, because the base alone uses the whole budget. The recalibrated base leaves 5 more df than the refitted base under either count. The budget therefore favours it, and the ΔC of −0.001 between the two in [score2_honesty.md](file:///Users/mathildebrinchsorensen/Desktop/ImageCAS-X/CGPS_data/score2_honesty.md) points the same way. That comparison was person-level, though, so it does not settle the open decision, which has to be made in the pooled model.

A segment-level check gives a second, independent count. Segment events are **estimated, not counted**: 452 people × 2.45 plaque segments per affected person = 1,107 events in 9,684 rows. The 2.45 comes from the old 1,352-person table ([plaque_location.md](file:///Users/mathildebrinchsorensen/Desktop/ImageCAS-X/CGPS_data/plaque_location.md)). Harrell's rule of at most m/15 df ([Harrell, RMS ch. 4](https://hbiostat.org/rmsc/multivar)), with m divided by the design effect 1 + 8ρ_obs, gives **54, 39 and 23 total df at latent ICC 0.1, 0.3 and 0.55** [own calc]. The total model is 17 df with the recalibrated base plus 5 geometry df, and 22 df with the refitted base. Both fit even at ICC 0.55, the refitted model with 1 df to spare. Under this count the segment-level budget is about as tight as the person-level one at high ICC, so neither level should be ignored.

## Two disagreements the thesis has to state, not hide

**Strict Riley count against the simulation.** The strict count says the refitted base is over budget at C = 0.70 before any geometry is added. The simulation fitted the exact model: a Gauss–Hermite cloglog GLMM with 9 segments, segment dummies and log length, aggregated to P = ∫[1 − exp(−e^b Σⱼ e^ηᵢⱼ)]φ(b)db and evaluated on 20,000 independent synthetic people [own calc]. In it the refitted base at k = 0 (17 df) already had a mean slope of 0.94–0.98. Each added geometry df cost only about 0.003–0.006 in slope.

The two answers differ for a clear reason. Riley charges every parameter against person-level information, while the dummies and log length are paid for by the within-person allocation of about 1,100 segment events. The simulation's numbers sit close to the person-varying count, not the strict one. They do not overturn the strict count, for four reasons. They come from one family of data generators with 40 replicates per cell (MC SE 0.009–0.015). The predictors are independent apart from the stated correlations, and the geometry has no measurement error. Every person-level feature acts on all 9 rows, which differs from `design()`, where the LM angle acts on the pLAD and pCX rows only. And the simulation includes no optimism from feature selection or coding choices. The recalibrated base's slope above 1 at ICC 0.1 reflects a misspecified SCORE2 proxy, not an absence of overfitting. The mean slope also hides how much single fits vary. Pavlou et al. warn that the realised calibration slope is highly variable with fewer than 10 predictors, even when the Riley formula is met ([Pavlou et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11234534/)). Here 12–40 % of datasets fell below 0.9 at k = 5. The segment-level heuristic shrinkage 1 − p/LR (0.94–0.97) overstated the person-level slope by 0.02–0.03 for the refitted base at low ICC, so it should not be reported as the person-level shrinkage. The honest position: **the strict count is the conservative, citable rule; the person-varying count is the working rule; the simulation explains why the second is adequate under stated assumptions.**

**Two Harrell formulas at segment level.** Harrell's limiting sample size for a binary outcome is m = min(events, non-events). The general ordinal formula is m = n − n⁻²Σnᵢ³ ([Harrell, RMS ch. 4](https://hbiostat.org/rmsc/multivar)). Applied to two categories, the ordinal formula reduces exactly to 3nq(1 − q) [own calc], which is about 3 × min when the outcome is unbalanced. At q = 0.114 it gives m = 2,942 instead of 1,107, and the budget becomes **62–144 df instead of 23–54**. The prior project report's "35–60 df" used 3nq(1 − q). One of the two research notes for this report also applied it at person level (m = 786 → 52 df, against 452/15 ≈ 30 with min). For a binary outcome the min form is Harrell's binary rule and the one to use. Both notes quote the RMS table from memory without re-fetching it, so the exact wording should be checked before it is cited.

**A third, smaller disagreement concerns the ICC.** Earlier planning assumed a latent ICC of 0.1–0.3. At 42 % person risk, the synthetic generator reproduces 2.45 plaque segments per affected person only at a **latent ICC of about 0.55 (σ ≈ 1.4)**, which corresponds to a design effect of about 3.2 [own calc]. At ICC 0.1–0.3 it gives only 660–780 segment events per 1,076 people, not 1,107. If the 2.45 holds in the clean cohort, the outcome is strongly clustered. A larger σ also dilutes how much segment-level effects show in the person risk. The assumed segment-level HRs of 1.10–1.20 raised the person-level C by only **+0.007 to +0.014**, while the 5-df LR test had power 1.00 in every scenario [own calc]. That test is driven mostly by within-person allocation, so its power is not power for the "who" claim. At a fixed noncentrality of 10, power falls from 0.68 at 5 df to 0.61 at 7 and 0.50 at 12 [own calc]. Mundlak costs about 0.003–0.01 in calibration slope but some power.

## What the methods section should assume, and what must still be measured

The methods text in [03_model_size.tex](file:///Users/mathildebrinchsorensen/Desktop/ImageCAS-X/thesis/methodology/03_model_size.tex) should state the following as assumptions. The Riley criteria are applied at person level with n = participants and events = people who developed trunk plaque. They are an approximation, because the pooled model is fitted on segment rows and aggregated nonlinearly. The anticipated C is 0.70, with 0.65 and 0.72 as sensitivity values, and is justified by Riley's default, MESA and the cross-sectional upper reference, not by PARADIGM. The conversion from C to R²_CS follows Riley, Van Calster and Collins 2021, which has to be added to refs.bib. Parameters are counted at two levels as described above, and the text should say plainly that this is a judgement. The base choice is charged at its larger size if it is made by outcome comparison. A Riley derivation for the cloglog link does not exist. The shrinkage heuristic is likelihood-based and should carry over to cloglog [inference], but the C-to-R² map assumes a logistic model.

Five things must still be checked, and all of them can be done before geometry meets the outcome. First, **run pmsampsize** (binary, `cstatistic = 0.65 / 0.70 / 0.72`, prevalence 0.42, n = 1,076 and the complete-case 1,041). The quadrature results here were not checked against it, because R is not installed. Record the version. Second, **count the segment events** in the 1,076-person clean cohort as an aggregate, replacing the 2.45 × 452 estimate from the old table, and report events per segment type with MIN_CELL = 5. Third, **fit the null pooled GLMM** (base only) and report σ̂ with the latent ICC σ²/(σ² + π²/6). The π²/6 analogue of the logit π²/3 is an inference. Rerun the simulation's Harrell row at that ICC. Fourth, **estimate ρₓ for curvature and taper** within person from geometry alone, which shows how much of each slope is between-person. Fifth, **update the code**: `who_where_readiness.py` still uses events-per-parameter rules of 10 and 20. `progression_who_pooled.py` currently fits a 2-df geometry block (curvature and LM angle) on the Medis progression cohort, with BL-grade dummies that drop out in a plaque-free cohort. Under the project rule, the methods text must describe what the code does, so it cannot yet claim a 5-df block on the tracer cohort. Whether an indicator for an absent LM (+1 df) belongs to the block is still open. If added, it takes the refitted-base Mundlak scenario below budget at C = 0.70.

## Conclusion

The budget question is really a question about the estimand. Most of the pooled model's segment-level parameters buy *where* information, which costs almost nothing in person-level overfitting. The person-level budget is spent on terms that separate people: the risk-factor base and geometry summarised per person. Seen this way, the recalibrated score is the budget-efficient base. A 5-df geometry block is comfortably affordable at C = 0.70, and 7 df is affordable only with the recalibrated score. Anything beyond that, such as splines, per-artery slopes or extra features, would rely on the simulation's optimism rather than on any citable rule. The weaker link is not the number of parameters but how small the person-level gain is. Segment-level geometry effects that a block test detects with certainty move person-level C by about 0.01, so ΔC and calibration, which the plan already estimates rather than tests, will carry the real "who" message. Once pmsampsize, the segment count and σ̂ are in, the scenario table above shrinks to one row, and the thesis can state its budget as fixed before the outcome is linked.
