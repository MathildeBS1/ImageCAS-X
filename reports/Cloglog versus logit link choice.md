# Cloglog wins on structure, not fit

The complementary log-log (cloglog) link was chosen for the segment-level GLMM of incident coronary plaque mainly for one structural reason. Under cloglog, a person's nine segment hazards add into one person hazard, so the pooled person risk reduces to a one-dimensional integral over the person random intercept. A logit link has no such form. Two further reasons are recorded: cloglog is the grouped-time proportional-hazards model, so exp(β) is a hazard ratio rather than an odds ratio, and SCORE2 and follow-up time sit naturally on its log-cumulative-hazard scale. The choice was right for this design, but the reason is interpretive, not empirical. The project's own notes concede that model fit cannot tell the links apart at segment level, and no fitted logit-versus-cloglog comparison exists anywhere in the project. Where the link does matter is the person risk. About **42% of plaque-free participants developed plaque in an artery segment**, and at that level odds ratios and hazard ratios differ by roughly 15% for a moderate effect (my own computation). Three wording issues need fixing before the methods text is written. Log follow-up enters the code as a covariate with a free coefficient, not as an offset. The hazard ratios are conditional on the person frailty. Proportional hazards cannot be tested with one follow-up scan. The script read here is also the Medis progression version, not the final plaque-free incident-plaque model.

## Three reasons appear in the project files, and one carries the weight

Three sources of evidence are kept apart below. The project files say what was decided and why. The outside literature, thin and mostly secondary, supports the theory. Numbers marked as my own computation come from neither; I reproduced each one in this session.

**The pooling collapse is the decisive reason.** Under cloglog, the conditional segment survival is 1 − p_ij(b) = exp(−e^b e^{η_ij}), so the product over segments collapses to exp(−e^b S_i), where S_i is the sum of the segment hazards. "The logit link has neither property, because a product of logistic survival terms is not logistic" ([Segment model to person risk](../../Speciale/CGPS_statistics-/reports/Segment%20model%20to%20person%20risk.md)). The same point is made in `experiments/who/pooled_score2/pooling.md` and in [Plaque risk model choice](Plaque%20risk%20model%20choice.md) ("the rolled-up model is again cloglog"). The code builds exactly this. `CGPS_data/progression_who_pooled.py` (lines 11–14) fits a cloglog GLMM with a normal person intercept and pools to P_i = 1 − ∫ exp(−e^b Σ_j exp(η_ij)) φ(b) db by quadrature. I confirmed this in the file. The code integrates over b. It does not use the naive product 1 − ∏(1 − p̄) of marginal risks, which the project's own check puts at about **0.63–0.69 instead of 0.49** (old-table prevalence, `pooling.md`).

**The hazard-ratio reading is the second reason.** The thesis draft (`Thesis_coronary/content/technical/01_prediction_model.tex`, lines 272–287) states that exp(β_k) is the hazard ratio and that it "does not depend on how long the window is", citing Prentice and Gloeckler (1978). **The third reason is the SCORE2 scale.** `CGPS_data/score2_honesty.md` regresses plaque cloglog on cloglog(SCORE2 risk), "both log cumulative hazards", and gets a slope of **0.722 (0.569–0.874)**.

The project is candid about the limits. The thesis notes (lines 95–99 of the same `.tex` file) say to "say honestly that fit cannot tell the links apart at segment level; the reason for cloglog is interpretive". [Pooled cloglog GLMM evaluation](Pooled%20cloglog%20GLMM%20evaluation.md) rates the link's impact "Low for coefficients and the test; matters for the person risk". It lists a logit refit as sensitivity analysis 6 and finds no coronary paper whose methods were read that uses cloglog. That absence rests on about eight full texts and was not a targeted search, so it is weak evidence of a gap, nothing more. The choice also reversed over time. The earlier [Experiment setup who and where](../../Speciale/CGPS_statistics-/reports/Experiment%20setup%20who%20and%20where.md) made **person-level logistic regression the primary model** and cloglog the sensitivity analysis. Cloglog became primary only once the pooled segment model was adopted, which supports the reading that the pooling, not the hazard ratios, drove the switch.

## Theory backs cloglog as the two-scan proportional-hazards model

The core equivalence is standard. If plaque onset follows a continuous-time proportional-hazards model and is observed only as "by the follow-up scan or not", then P(event) = 1 − exp(−Λ0(t)e^{xβ}). That is a binary regression with a cloglog link, and β is the log hazard ratio. SAS documentation states that the grouped-data model is "equivalent to the binary response model with complementary log-log link function" and that its β "is identical to that of the continuous-time proportional hazards model" ([SAS PROC LOGISTIC](https://go.documentation.sas.com/api/docsets/statug/v_023/content/statug_logistic_examples19.htm)). Rodríguez's GLM notes add that cloglog "would seem more appropriate" when time is continuous but observed grouped, and that logit is used more often largely because of software ([Rodríguez](https://grodri.github.io/glms/notes/c7s6)). Both are reliable secondary sources. **The primary paper, Prentice and Gloeckler 1978, was not opened** ([record only](https://scilit.com/publications/994d0738ce096f9417692845dca7b7f0)), and neither were Allison 1982 or the textbook treatments. The thesis cites Prentice and Gloeckler for this claim, so someone should read it before submission. The substantive story also fits: plaque first appearing somewhere along an artery segment over ten years is the textbook case of "at least one event of a process accumulating over time and length", which is what the inverse cloglog link describes.

How much the odds-ratio/hazard-ratio gap matters depends on the risk level. The figures below are **my own computation, assuming proportional hazards with HR 1.5**. They were reproduced in this session.

| Baseline risk | Implied odds ratio | Context |
|---|---|---|
| 2% | 1.51 | plausible segment-level risk |
| 6% | 1.52 | plausible segment-level risk |
| 15% | 1.56 | upper segment-level range |
| 42% | 1.75 | observed person-level risk |

Odds ratios approximate risk and hazard ratios only when the outcome is rare ([VanderWeele 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/biomet/v76y2020i3p746-752.html), record only). The segment-level event rate has **never been tabulated** on the 1,801-person table ([Pooled cloglog GLMM evaluation](Pooled%20cloglog%20GLMM%20evaluation.md)). If it is below about 15%, segment coefficients barely differ between links. The person risk is different. Of 1,077 plaque-free participants, 483 (44.8%) developed plaque anywhere and 452 of 1,076 (42.0%) in one of the nine artery segments (`CGPS_data/score2_honesty.md`), so the person level sits where the scales diverge. The interval-length argument is real but small here. Follow-up has an IQR of 9.7–12.2 years. Holding HR 1.5 and a constant hazard reaching 42% at 11 years, the implied odds ratio drifts from 1.71 to 1.78 across that range (my computation), about 4%.

## The hazard ratio is conditional, and the time form is not an offset

Two qualifiers attach to "exp(β) is a hazard ratio" in this model. **First, it is conditional on the person intercept.** e^{b_i} acts as a lognormal shared frailty multiplying all nine segment hazards. Once b is averaged out, the marginal model is no longer exactly proportional hazards with the same β, and marginal effects are attenuated. The familiar attenuation formula β_m ≈ β_c/√(c²σ² + 1) is a logit result ([Pavlou et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4525751/)), and the project plan already flags that it does not transfer to cloglog (`CGPS_statistics-/CLAUDE.md`, open item 10). For person-level geometry such as the LM bifurcation angle, "within-person" has no meaning. β is between-person information adjusted for frailty, consistent with the root `CLAUDE.md` note that β mixes the two. The discrete-time frailty framing has precedent in Scheike and Jensen 1997 ([record only](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Item:Q4351029)) and in the udder-quarter infection models of Goethals et al. 2009 ([record](https://research.itg.be/en/publications/modeling-interval-censored-clustered-cow-udder-quarter-infection-/)). Neither was read in full.

**Second, log follow-up is a free covariate, not an offset.** I confirmed in `progression_who_pooled.py` that `design()` puts `np.log(d["years"])` in as an ordinary column named "log follow-up". Log length is treated the same way, and no offset enters the fixed-effect design. Under proportional hazards, a free coefficient κ on log t implies Λ0(t) ∝ t^κ, a grouped Weibull baseline. The thesis notes say this too but mark it "VERIFY in prentice1978grouped before writing", and I derived it rather than finding it in a source. Several local notes still say "log(time) as offset". By the root `CLAUDE.md` rule, the methods text must follow the code. A rough plausibility check, mine and assumption-laden: if log follow-up is near normal, its SD is about log(12.2/9.7)/1.349 ≈ 0.17. The person-level HR of 1.19 per SD for log follow-up (`score2_honesty.md`) then implies κ ≈ 1.0, which is what a constant hazard predicts. This is only indicative. The SD is not reported, the model is person-level rather than the GLMM, and follow-up may be confounded with recruitment date or age.

Proportional hazards itself is untestable here. With one follow-up scan per person, the baseline shape, the time effect and any covariate-by-time interaction are confounded except through the narrow spread in follow-up. The thesis notes already say "PH cannot be tested with one follow-up scan; say so."

## The test and C survive a link swap, while calibration may not

The prespecified LR test of the geometry block and ΔC should be close to link-robust. Under link misspecification with roughly elliptical regressors, maximum-likelihood slopes are consistent up to a common scale factor ([Li and Duan 1989](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-17/issue-3/Regression-Analysis-Under-Link-Violation/10.1214/aos/1176347254.short), abstract and secondary descriptions only). The direction and signal-to-noise of β_geometry should therefore change little. Czado and Santner (1992) warn that a wrong link, especially one with the wrong skewness, can bias coefficients, but the notes rely on a secondary description of that paper. The person risk ranks people by S_i under cloglog, and a logit-pooled risk would rank them almost identically, so C should hardly move. **Calibration is the exposed quantity.** At 42% person risk the scales diverge, and σ² sits on a different latent scale (residual variance π²/6 for cloglog versus π²/3 for logit), so the size of the frailty and hence the integrated person risk can shift. A logit GLMM can still be pooled with a one-dimensional quadrature over b. The project's phrase "no closed form" is correct, but it does not mean logit pooling is impossible. What logit loses is the summed-hazard reading and a person-level model of the same family.

Two alternatives fall away. A log-link or Poisson model can predict probabilities above 1 at this person risk and is only a rare-event approximation to cloglog, as the earlier setup report already concluded. The Aranda-Ordaz family nests logit and cloglog and can be profiled (R [gcloglog](https://cran.r-universe.dev/gcloglog/doc/manual.html)). That gives a descriptive link check, not a decision test, and no standard goodness-of-link test was found. A preprint claims cloglog approximates the risk ratio better than the odds ratio across that family ([Tsubota and Beppu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00889v1)). **It is not peer-reviewed** and should not be cited as settled. No empirical study comparing cloglog and logit GLMMs on clustered imaging outcomes was found.

## Conclusion

The case for cloglog is stronger than "it gives hazard ratios" and weaker than "it fits better". It is the only common link under which the nine segment risks add up to a person risk of the same family, and that property is what the pooled who model is built on. Hazard ratios that can be compared with SCORE2's cumulative-hazard scale are a real but secondary benefit, and interval-length invariance buys little when follow-up varies by only ±12%. The defence an examiner will accept is therefore structural. Prentice and Gloeckler should be read before it is cited, and the defence should state openly that the data cannot choose the link.

What remains is concrete. Run the already-listed logit refit of B2 and G02 on the same person-split folds and report the LR statistic, ΔC and person-level calibration. Report κ, the log follow-up coefficient, against 1. Tabulate the segment-level event rate and the number of people with two or more positive segments. Write the methods to match the code: a free log follow-up coefficient (grouped Weibull baseline), conditional hazard ratios, and untestable proportional hazards. Never compare these hazard ratios numerically with published odds ratios. All of this must be checked again on the final plaque-free incident-plaque script, which may differ from the Medis progression script read here. The model scripts are not yet in `CGPS_data/thesis_final/`, which holds only the feature modules.
