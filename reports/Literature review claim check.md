# Fix six overstatements; the gap holds

Most of what the three literature review sections say is supported by their sources, but about a dozen sentences describe a study more strongly or more broadly than the paper does, and three syntheses claim more than their citations allow. The most serious problems are these. The closing synthesis of `sec:lit-wss` ranks the bifurcation angle among the features "most closely tied" to low WSS, yet no cited study ranked features against each other, the angle effect in Garcha and Grande Gutiérrez (2025) was weak at an even branch split and reversed sign with dominance, and Zhang et al. (2024) related curvature to stenosis, not to low WSS. Johnston and Johnston (2009) cannot show that taper and curvature carry "complementary rather than redundant information": that is a statement about people, and the paper simulated two idealised arteries. In `sec:lit-measure`, Grisan (2008) is cited for a coronary claim but studied retinal vessels; Li (2011) did not count bends but used a yes/no rule; the "tortuosity index" half of the Tello Ayala (2026) sentence turns a model with the index as its outcome into a CAD model; and "the choice of summary has even decided the direction" is a two-study contrast confounded by country, modality and CAD definition. In `sec:lit-risk`, Chen (2024) measured lumen and centreline geometry, not "image texture"; three of the five studies said to be "in patients who already had coronary disease" compared patients with and without it; and "random population samples avoid this selection" should be "reduce". The research gap holds. A targeted search found no study that related named geometric features measured in plaque-free people to later plaque, but the gap paragraph should name Chen (2024) as the nearest miss and say "identified in this search" until a documented database search exists. Several verdicts rest on abstracts only (listed in the last section), and those sentences should not be strengthened until the full texts are read.

All suggested wording below follows the chapter rules recorded in `00_chapter_intro.tex` and the project `CLAUDE.md`: author and year as subject with `\cite` after the year, no numbers in the prose (sizes go in the table), no lists, no em dashes, no inserted clauses between two commas, a synthesis only when every element is cited, and never CAS-Net or `shen2026geometry`. It also follows the citation rule added during this check. When one source holds everything about a concept, cite that source every time and do not add a second paper for the same point. A second source is cited only for what the first does not contain. Every addition proposed below was checked against that rule, and where a note suggested a second source for the same point, the report says to drop it.

## Section 1 (sec:lit-wss): five sentences overreach and the synthesis ranks what nobody ranked

The descriptive core is sound. Kashyap et al. (2022) did find that average absolute curvature had the strongest, weak but significant, relation to the share of the left main bifurcation with low time-averaged WSS, while the tortuosity index, "the most commonly used tortuosity metric", was unrelated ([Kashyap 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)). Zhang et al. (2024) did find that "absolute average curvature was the only anatomical parameter significantly higher in the stenosed whole trees" ([Zhang 2024](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.241267)). Johnston and Johnston (2009) did find, in tapered S-shaped models, a region of consistently low WSS proximally that is "not observed in constant radius artery models" ([MODSIM09](https://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim09/B4/johnston_bm.pdf)). The problems are scope and verbs. There are four main ones. Van der Giessen et al. (2009) studied only bifurcations and **assigned WSS from the geometry rather than computing it** ([EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study)). Kashyap's patients were **symptomatic referrals with zero calcium and no obstruction**, and the analysis is a correlation, not a prediction. Garcha's 230 models are **all variants of one repository patient model**, and the diameter ratio **changed the sign of the angle effect**, not just its size ([Garcha 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11770140/)). Zhang's curvature difference held over the whole tree and between bifurcations but **not at the bifurcations**, where the angle and Finet's ratio were tested and no anatomical feature differed. Zhang's low-WSS area also did not separate stenosed from non-stenosed trees.

## Section 2 (sec:lit-measure): a retinal source and two reframed results

This section's argument is right: curvature summaries differ, they can point in opposite directions, and whole-profile methods exist. Three sentences misdescribe their sources. **Li (2011) used a binary rule**, an artery with at least three bends of 45 degrees or more seen in systole and diastole, not a bend count. Its CAD was obstructive (at least 50% stenosis), and the regression had tortuosity as the outcome with CAD as a predictor. The adjusted estimate is borderline and printed inconsistently between its Tables 2 and 3 ([Li 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0024232)). In **Tello Ayala (2026) the tortuosity-index result comes from a linear model with the index as the outcome**, where RCA stenosis had a weak inverse coefficient (P = 0.042, supplementary table). The mean-turning-angle result is a properly adjusted logistic model ([Tello Ayala 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). The paper is a peer-reviewed JACC: Advances original article, not a preprint. Its population is a catheterisation registry that **included patients with prior PCI or bypass**, and its CAD label came from the same angiogram. **Grisan (2008) is retinal** ([IEEE TMI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)), so it cannot support a claim "for curvature in coronary arteries". A fourth issue comes from the one-source rule rather than the sources: the section cites `ramsay2005fda` for FPCA, while the rest of the thesis uses `wang2016fda` as its single FPCA source, so the FPCA citation here should change to Wang.

## Section 3 (sec:lit-risk): Chen measured geometry, not texture, and the gap survives

The longitudinal paragraph is well chosen, and Won et al. (2022) is now described correctly. The 402 plaque-free PARADIGM patients were followed for a median of 3.6 years. Newly developed plaque appeared in about a third, and the baseline risk factors "were not significantly associated with the risk of RPP", while HbA1c measured at follow-up was associated with the annual change in percent atheroma volume ([Won 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36371222)). The caveat is power: rapid progression occurred in only 4.2%. The sentence that matters most is the one on Chen et al. (2024). Their "vascular radiomics" were **16 cross-section metrics and 17 centreline parameters, such as curvature and arc length, over the cardiac cycle**, and the patients had **no plaque in the LAD** at baseline ([Chen 2024](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/25/10/1462/7676581)). Chen is therefore the one plaque-free-baseline study that measured geometry. Calling it "image texture" hides the nearest miss to the gap. By contrast, Lee et al. (2024) is texture-type radiomics of plaque-free segments, read at abstract level only ([Lee 2024](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38378314)).

### What the gap search found: near misses, none that break it

No study found measured curvature, bifurcation angle, diameter ratios or taper in plaque-free people and related them to later plaque. Every geometry study with follow-up is in referred or diseased patients, covers one segment or bifurcation, or uses events as the outcome. Medrano-Gracia et al. (2017) is the closest plaque-free geometry study. It is a cross-sectional statistical shape model of bifurcations in patients with zero calcium and no stenosis, in which hypertension, smoking and diabetes showed no apparent association with shape ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12265-016-9720-2); `medranogracia2017bifurcation` is already in `refs.bib`). It supports, weakly, the idea that geometry is not a proxy for risk factors, and it could carry one sentence. No population-based serial CCTA cohort has published geometry. Several studies would sharpen the context without breaking the gap, and each makes a point no cited source already holds. The MESA "warranty period" of zero calcium is the closest population analogue of incident disease from a zero baseline over about ten years ([Dzaye 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8076346)). The Miami Heart Study found non-calcified plaque in asymptomatic adults with zero calcium ([Houston Methodist record](https://scholars.houstonmethodist.org/en/publications/coronary-atherosclerosis-in-an-asymptomatic-us-population-miami-h/)). A SCAPIS deep-learning plaque-volume paper reports gains over SCORE2 for coronary events ([JACC 2026](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2026.08.004)). That is a different marker from Hageman's calcium, so citing both is consistent with the one-source rule, but only a search snippet was seen. A Dutch per-protocol ten-year serial CCTA in referred patients ([ESC365 abstract](https://esc365.escardio.org/presentation/266447)) shows that a ten-year interval alone is not new. The novelty of the thesis lies in the random population sample and the plaque-free baseline, and the review should not imply otherwise. CArTI ([AHA 2025](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4365988)) and a 2026 PARADIGM new-lesion abstract ([Tantawy 2026](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)) are conference abstracts and should stay out of the prose. No study tests coronary geometry against SCORE2 or any other risk score, so the review can say that "beyond the established risk factors" is untested for geometry.

Two limits keep the gap from being called systematic. The counter-example search was a set of targeted searches, not a documented database search. The TODO in `00_chapter_intro.tex` (databases, time window, search date, inclusion criterion, a reason per exclusion) is still owed. In addition, the SCAPIS re-examination and the CGPS group may publish soon, so both should be re-checked before submission.

## Conclusion

The pattern across the three sections is consistent. The review is right about *what* each study found and drifts on *how far* the finding reaches. The drift shows in verbs ("showed", "predicted", "depended", "decided"), in populations widened past the sample ("individuals without CAD", "patients who already had disease"), and in syntheses that rank or generalise beyond a single design. Fixing it does not weaken the thesis argument. It strengthens it. Once each study is scoped honestly, the evidence that geometry relates to low WSS is weak and simulation-based, the clinical associations are cross-sectional in referred patients, and the one plaque-free geometry study covers a single LAD segment. That is precisely the gap a ten-year random-population study of three arteries fills.

Two decisions remain with the author. The first is whether the bifurcation angle stays in the `sec:lit-wss` synthesis at all: the honest version says its effect depends on the branch widths, which also motivates modelling the angle together with the LM→LAD and LM→LCx ratios. The second is whether to spend scarce space on the population "zero-baseline" literature (MESA, Miami Heart, the SCAPIS SCORE2 paper), which is the best defence of "beyond SCORE2" but has been read only at abstract or snippet level so far.
