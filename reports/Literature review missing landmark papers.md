# Add four landmarks; leave the famous trials out

Yes, a few big and widely cited papers are missing, but fewer than the citation counts suggest. Only four papers would leave a gap a reviewer would notice, and two of them are already in `refs.bib`. **Caro, Fitz-Gerald and Schroter (1971)** is where the low-shear hypothesis starts, and as things stand `sec:lit-wss` calls Asakura and Karino (1990) "early foundational work". **Friedman et al. (1993)** is the "geometric risk factor" study that relates the LM bifurcation angle to early lesions in the proximal LAD and LCx. That makes it the direct ancestor of this thesis's question, and its direction is the opposite of Tommasino's. **PREDICTION (Stone 2012, already in `refs.bib`)** is the landmark prospective human study of low shear stress and progression. **MESA incident calcium (Kronmal 2007)** is the population evidence on who develops coronary atherosclerosis from a disease-free baseline. Three cheaper fixes also count as must-do: cite `fuchs2023cgps` (this cohort, already in the bib) in `sec:lit-risk` next to SCAPIS, add the PARADIGM design paper or drop "for clinical reasons", and add Zhu et al. (2009), the only per-segment curvature catalogue of normal coronary arteries, to `sec:lit-measure`. The most cited CCTA papers are Motoyama 2009, SCOT-HEART, CONFIRM, PROMISE and ICONIC, and the most cited WSS classic is Ku 1985. All of them can be left out of the review. They study events in symptomatic or diseased patients, or a cited source already holds their point. The notes found three bibliography problems, and two of them are confirmed: `schaap2009cat08` is cited but has no `refs.bib` entry, and "Finet's ratio" is named with no citation. The third was wrong as stated. `rampidis2022geometry` has no DOI at all, and the Circulation DOI the notes blamed belongs to `seiler1992structure`, where it is correct. Many verdicts below rest on abstracts or titles only, and the tables flag those papers so that none is cited before its text is read.

The searches behind this report were targeted, not systematic. Citation counts come from two databases: Semantic Scholar (S2) for the WSS and CCTA notes, and OpenAlex (OA) for the measurement note. All were retrieved on 2026-11-08. Google Scholar could not be queried and usually runs about 1.3 to 2 times higher, so read the counts as a ranking. Every recommendation was checked against the thesis rules. A paper is added only for a concept no cited source holds, a duplicate is resolved to one paper, numbers stay in tables and out of the prose, and CAS-Net and `shen2026geometry` are never mentioned. The prose read for this report (working tree, 2026-11-08) does not yet include the wording fixes from the claim check. For example, Chen is still described by "image texture" and "litterature" is still misspelled. The placement advice below assumes those fixes will be made.

## Section 1 (sec:lit-wss) lacks the origin of the low-shear hypothesis

Section 1 now cites Chatzizisis 2007 for the premise, Asakura 1990 and van der Giessen 2009 for where plaque sits, Gijsen 2019 for why WSS must be simulated, and then the simulation studies (Kashyap, Zhang, Garcha, Johnston). The gap a biomechanics reviewer would notice is the start of the story. **Caro et al. (1971, Proc R Soc B; S2 1,473)** proposed from human autopsy arteries that early atheroma sits where wall shear is low. That is about two decades before Asakura, and Caro was answering Fry's competing high-shear hypothesis ([PubMed 4396262](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4396262/); [Fry 1968](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.RES.22.2.165)). Chatzizisis is a 2007 review and does not hold the origin of the hypothesis, so adding Caro does not break the one-source rule. Two ways satisfy the rule equally well: cite Caro in one clause before Asakura, or remove "early foundational" from the Asakura sentence. PubMed holds no abstract for Caro 1971, and the one-line summary comes from the title and the paper's well-known content. Read it before citing. The 1969 Nature letter (S2 676) is not needed once the 1971 paper is cited ([PubMed 5810692](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5810692/)).

The second candidate holds a concept the thesis leans on without a source. **Lee, Antiga, Spence and Steinman (2008, Stroke; S2 380, OA 365)** simulated flow in 50 carotid bifurcations of 25 young adults. Disturbed-flow exposure varied between people and followed the proximal area ratio and the bifurcation tortuosity, but not the bifurcation angle. The authors conclude that geometric features are "robust surrogate markers", so that large imaging studies can proceed without flow simulation ([PubMed 18556585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18556585/)). That is the reason the thesis can measure geometry and skip CFD. Kashyap holds only the coronary curvature version of the idea. Lee is carotid and simulation-only, with no plaque outcome, and the text must name the vascular bed. Morbiducci et al. (2016, S2 238, OA 267) states the same surrogate idea at review level ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1160/th15-07-0597)). Under the one-source rule, choose Lee as the primary study and leave Morbiducci out.

Zarins 1983 and Chatzizisis already hold its points in `sec:wss`, so it is at most a swap for Zarins in the background, not an addition ([PubMed 3994585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3994585/)). Myers 2001, Soulis 2006 and Wellnhofer 2010 are simulation-method papers on single geometries. Wang 2004 (proximal clustering of MI occlusions, S2 376) belongs in the Discussion of the segment intercepts, if anywhere.

## Section 2 (sec:lit-measure) misses the one normal-coronary curvature catalogue

The section argues that curvature summaries differ, can point in opposite directions, and can be replaced by whole-profile methods. Its coronary evidence is Li 2011, Kashyap 2022 and Tello Ayala 2026, all of them in referred patients with disease on the same scan. **Zhu, Ding, Piana, Gehrig and Friedman (2009, Int J Cardiol; S2 66, OA 54)** measured curvature, torsion and tortuosity per proximal, mid and distal segment in 32 LADs and 35 RCAs of 52 patients with angiographically normal arteries. Two factors expressed more than 80 per cent of the geometric variability ([PubMed 18597872](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18597872/)). It is the only coronary source found that describes curvature per segment, the thesis's unit, in normal arteries. It is also the only coronary example of reducing several curvature descriptors jointly, which fits the bridge from single values to whole profiles. Citing it for both points stays within the one-source rule, since each point appears only in Zhu. It is not FPCA and must not be called that. Its patients were referred for angiography, the reconstruction was biplane, and it had no outcome. Its title calls it "a potential tool for predicting risk", which ties it to the Friedman programme discussed under Section 3.

For the sentence that no curvature summary "has been agreed", the claim check found that Grisan is retinal and Kashyap only implies the point. A tortuosity review would be the first source that states it outright. **Ciurică et al. 2019 (Hypertension, OA 206)** is the stronger choice and **Kahe et al. 2020 (OA 48)** the coronary-specific alternative, but neither abstract confirms the statement. Ciurică's abstract was not even available ([PubMed 30852920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30852920/); [Kahe DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MCA.0000000000000769)). Cite one only if its full text says it. Otherwise rephrase the sentence as "agree poorly", which Tello Ayala reports directly.

## Section 3 (sec:lit-risk) lacks population incidence and its own cohort

Section 3 argues that geometry–disease studies used one scan of referred patients, that the few plaque-free-baseline studies were hospital cohorts, and that random population samples have not related geometry to later plaque. Four additions strengthen this argument. One already sits in the bib, and two are factual repairs.

**Fuchs et al. 2023 (Ann Intern Med; S2 63; `fuchs2023cgps`, cited only in the Introduction)** is the CGPS baseline CCTA paper: a large asymptomatic Copenhagen sample with one scan and myocardial infarction as the outcome ([AuntMinnie summary](https://www.auntminnieeurope.com/clinical-news/article/15657736/subclinical-atherosclerosis-increases-heart-attack-risk-8-fold)). The paragraph on random population samples names only SCAPIS, and a reviewer from the Copenhagen group will expect the thesis's own cohort there. Under the rule of citing the one source every time a concept appears, citing it again is required rather than redundant. It is also the cleanest single reply to "why no CCTA prognosis trials", in an asymptomatic Danish population.

**Kronmal et al. 2007 (Circulation; S2 546)** followed a community MESA sample without clinical CVD over repeat calcium scans. Incident calcium was common, and age, sex, ethnicity, hypertension, BMI, diabetes, glucose and family history were associated with it ([catalogue record](https://ersearch2.cvtisr.sk/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c419t-cb6c3411af72e50b03713630eee18ba9e33e9b42797a70d16cc501dd1420e6f52/UserComments?lng=en)). No cited source holds population evidence on who develops coronary atherosclerosis from zero. It is the natural counterweight to Won 2022, where risk factors did not predict rapid progression in a referred cohort. It also shows why geometry has to be tested beyond SCORE2: the risk factors do predict incidence in a population. **Dzaye 2021 (S2 105)** is the alternative and makes a different point: how many years a zero calcium score lasts before converting, which frames the ten-year interval ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8076346)). The rule allows one, chosen by the point the sentence makes. Choose Kronmal for "beyond the risk factors", or Dzaye for the interval. Both measure calcium, not CCTA plaque, in a US cohort, and Kronmal was read only through abstract fragments.

**PREDICTION (Stone 2012, Circulation; S2 607; `stone2012prediction`, in the bib but uncited)** profiled ESS by IVUS in ACS patients after PCI and re-imaged most of them within a year ([PubMed 22723305](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22723305/)). It is the landmark prospective human evidence that low shear precedes progression, which Chatzizisis, a review drawing on animal and cross-sectional data, does not hold. All three notes and the claim check agree on including it. The claim check placed it in `sec:lit-risk` and the WSS note left the choice open. Place it in `sec:lit-risk`, where its diseased population sharpens the reverse-causality argument, rather than in the geometry-to-WSS section. The wording needs care. **Low ESS independently predicted lumen narrowing, the secondary end point. Plaque area growth, the primary end point, was predicted by plaque burden alone.** The sentence must not say that low ESS predicted plaque growth. PROSPECT ESS (Stone 2018, S2 169) is a nested case-control with a MACE outcome and adds nothing PREDICTION does not hold for this thesis ([PubMed 28917684](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28917684/)).

**Friedman, Brinkman, Qin and Seed 1993 (Atherosclerosis; S2 81, OA 80)** found in 15 autopsy hearts that a smaller LM branch angle went with relatively more early sudanophilic lesions in the proximal LAD and LCx. They called the small angle a "geometric risk factor" ([PubMed 7681291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7681291/)). It is the only pathology evidence on one of the five thesis features. It concerns early, pre-stenotic lesions, and its normalised index separates the local geometric effect from systemic factors. Its direction (small angle worse) is the opposite of Tommasino's wide-angle result. Together the two show that the expected sign of the angle effect is not settled, which supports a two-sided test of the geometry block. The best place is right after Glagov 1987, the other autopsy study, as the counterpart that relates geometry to the earliest lesions rather than to mature plaque. It is low-cited, small and read at abstract level, and the text must say that it is cross-sectional.

## Conclusion

The citation counts mislead in one particular way. Most of the famous papers missing from the review are famous for answering a different question: whether visible disease or high-risk plaque predicts events in symptomatic patients. Leaving them out is a design argument, and Fuchs 2023 alone answers the reviewer who asks about them. The papers that are really missing are less cited but closer to the thesis. They form a lineage that runs from Caro's low-shear proposal through Friedman's "geometric risk factor" and Zhu's normal-coronary catalogue to PREDICTION. That line already frames geometry as a person-level, measurable predisposition, and placing the thesis at its end makes the gap sharper: none of these studies started from plaque-free arteries in a population. Friedman 1993 does more than fill a gap. Its small-angle direction, set against Tommasino's wide angle, turns an unsettled sign into an argument for testing the geometry block two-sided.

Two cautions hold for everything above. First, Caro 1971, Friedman 1993, Zhu 2009, Kronmal 2007, PREDICTION and the PARADIGM design paper were all seen at abstract, fragment or title level, so each needs a full-text read before its sentence is written, and the PREDICTION sentence is the easiest to get wrong. Second, these were targeted searches, so the chapter's own TODO still applies: a documented database search, and a re-check of SCAPIS and the CGPS group's publications before submission.
