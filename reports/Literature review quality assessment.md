# Literature review reads as a solid 7, not yet 12

The chapter at `thesis/literature_review/literature_review.tex` is **not yet at grade 12 standard; on its own it sits in the 7 band, with a real risk of being read as a 4 if the external examiner checks one specific citation**. The prose is clean, the house rules are followed, every cohort size in the table is correct, and the two gaps point in the right direction. What holds it down falls into three groups. First, there is one outright misattribution of the home group's own benchmark paper (Bransby et al. 2026). Second, the gap statements are contradicted by papers that the author already holds in `refs.bib`, and in two cases by papers cited in the same chapter or in the introduction. Third, the review mostly summarises one study per paragraph and never notices that two of its own sources (Li 2011 and Tello Ayala 2026) disagree on the sign of the tortuosity association. The DTU descriptors separate 12 from 10 by command of "all aspects" versus "most aspects" of the material, so a review that omits clinical evidence for two of the thesis's three computed features cannot reach 12. That holds however good its sentences are. The fixes are cheap. Most must-cite papers are already in the bibliography, and every structural fix can be made without breaking the author's rules. Fixing the factual errors, the gaps and the must-cite omissions would plausibly lift the chapter to 10. Reaching 12 also needs real synthesis and per-study appraisal, which means adding perhaps 300 to 500 words.

## Three rubric items score at the bottom, and they decide the band

Grade 12 is officially "an excellent performance displaying a high level of command of all aspects of the relevant material, with no or only a few minor weaknesses". Grade 7 is "good command of the relevant material but also some weaknesses", and 4 involves "some major weaknesses" ([UFS grading system](https://ufsn.dk/english/education/the-danish-education-system/grading-system/)). DTU publishes no review-specific rubric. It requires only that a thesis "must include elements of literature studies and criticism", and it weights academic content above formulation ([DTU MSc thesis rules](https://student.dtu.dk/en/rules/afsluttende-projekter/kandidatspeciale)). The most operational instrument is Boote and Beile's 12-criterion rubric, in which level 3 ("critically examined", "resolved ambiguities", "offered new perspective") marks the top band ([Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)). The 15-item scoring below extends that rubric with currency, a related-work comparison and citation practice. Its mapping (mostly 3s for 12, mostly 2s for 7, several 1s for 4) is an inference, not an official DTU conversion.

The criteria where Boote and Beile found dissertations weakest (inclusion criteria, synthesis, methods critique, scholarly significance) are the ones where this chapter is also weakest ([Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)). Two caveats apply. DTU grading is holistic over the report and defence, so the chapter does not fix the thesis grade on its own. And the review's length is not the problem. At about **880 words of prose, 37 sentences and 13 citations** it closely matches the model thesis section (about 1,020 words, 17 citations) the author is following. Danish guidance accepts state-of-the-art sections from "half a page to ten" pages ([SDU Library](https://mitsdu.dk/da/service/bibliotek/vejledninger/opgaveskrivning/state-of-the-art?sc_lang=en)). Brevity simply raises the density every paragraph must carry.

## Factual errors come first because an examiner can verify them in minutes

The most serious error is the **Bransby et al. (2026) misattribution**. The chapter says the best methods "approached human agreement, with the most accurate segmentations in the main branches of the tree", and the table repeats it. The per-segment result is the inter-observer agreement of the human analysts: "the main branches (LM, LAD, LCx and RCA) were the best segmented (DSC 84.8 to 95.3)", from a table titled "Inter-observer segmentation agreement per coronary segment". No per-segment breakdown for automated methods exists in the paper. The same paper reports that CAS-Net (DSC 91.2) differs significantly from inter-observer agreement (92.8) and concludes that "automated methods do not yet match human-level agreement" (local PDF, arXiv:2608.30404v1, `papers/`). Because the author list includes the DTU home group, the examiner is likely to know this paper well. The error also brings down the following sentence: "Automated segmentation is therefore most reliable in the arteries that matter most" joins a misread benchmark to Wang et al. (2004). Wang covers **208 STEMI patients**, not acute myocardial infarction in general, and maps the "proximal third" of each artery, which is a different unit from "main branches" ([Europe PMC 15249505](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15249505)).

The second error is that **van der Giessen et al. (2009) never computed WSS**. The study divided cross-sections into quarters and assumed the shear pattern: "High WSS is assumed in part IV ... in part I ... low WSS is expected". It reports plaque in 72% of outer-wall parts versus 31% of flow-divider parts in 28 patients ([EuroIntervention full text](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study)). Paired with Chatzizisis et al. (2007), a narrative review that calls remodelling mechanisms "incompletely understood" ([Europe PMC 17599600](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/17599600)), it cannot carry "consistently located plaque at these sites in living patients and identified low WSS as the underlying cause". The next sentence's "established", resting on five post-mortem trees plus these two sources, compounds the overreach.

Five smaller errors follow the same pattern of wording that outruns the source. Kashyap et al. (2022) found curvature "significantly" but weakly related to low WSS, with full-bifurcation **R² of 0.112, 0.101 and 0.093**, so "clearly related" overstates it ([PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)). Sakellarios et al. (2017) do not state that every analysed segment had plaque at baseline, and their significant results belong to the Murray's-law daughter-branch model, so "growth of existing plaque" should become "plaque progression in patients with established disease" ([EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/the-effect-of-coronary-bifurcation-and-haemodynamics-in-prediction-of-atherosclerotic-plaque-development-a-serial-computed-tomographic-coronary-angiographic-study)). "Current risk scores combine systemic risk factors only" is plural on a single SCORE2 citation, and SCORE2 predicts 10-year CVD events, not plaque ([Europe PMC 34120177](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34120177)). Li et al. (2011) are introduced as "early clinical work", although Groves (2009, n = 1221) is earlier and is in `refs.bib`. Li's table cell also says "cross-sectional", which omits the 2 to 4 year MACE follow-up that found no difference ([PMC3164184](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/)). Finally, the "Collectively" sentence claims measure dependence from two studies, but Li used a single bend count and Kashyap's association is with simulated WSS, not disease, so only Kashyap supports it. Two housekeeping items do not reach the examiner but should be fixed: the Bransby bib entry has no arXiv identifier and its title differs from the PDF, and the chapter's header comment still lists Zebic Mihic, Zhang and Stone, which are no longer cited.

## Both gap statements are contradicted by papers already in refs.bib

Gap 1 says studies related geometry "either to WSS in computer models or to disease that was already present". **Han et al. (2022, ICONIC)** related baseline CCTA geometry to *future* ACS culprit lesions in 116 patients and 548 lesions. Culprit precursors sat closer to the ostium, more often at bifurcations (73.3% vs 38.9%) and more often in tortuous segments ([PMC8792800](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8792800)). Han is in `refs.bib` and cited in the introduction for the very same gap, which makes its absence from the review look selective. Tommasino et al. (2024, CLAP) also linked left main bifurcation angle above 80 degrees to later MACE in 499 CCTA patients ([PMC11595042](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11595042/)). Its reported confidence interval looks inconsistent and needs checking before it is quoted. The clause "those that related geometry to flow in patients included at most several hundred individuals" is contradicted inside the chapter: Griffo et al. (2026), cited two paragraphs earlier, used **748 patients and 1078 arteries** (`knowledge/docs_thesis/papers/griffo2026wss.md`). The clause also places the size limitation in flow studies, a branch the thesis does not extend. Its aim computes angles, radius ratios and curvature and predicts plaque without simulating flow. "Single region" is the strongest of the three claims. It holds for Kashyap (left main) and Tello Ayala (RCA), and Zhang et al. (2024) is a partial whole-left-tree exception at n = 39 ([Europe PMC 39309260](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39309260)).

Gap 2's core claim survives: no study has combined consistent tree-wide geometry with follow-up of a *general population* from a plaque-free state to *new* plaque. Nothing found as of October 2026 contradicts that conjunction. General-population CCTA cohorts such as SCAPIS (over 25,000 adults, atherosclerosis in 42.1%) have published no geometry ([Europe PMC 34543072](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34543072)). The sentences around the claim are weaker. "The few longitudinal studies available have followed patients who already had coronary disease" rests on one abstract-level citation and is partly false. **PARADIGM followed 402 patients with no plaque at baseline**, 35.6% of whom developed new plaque, and baseline traditional risk factors did not predict rapid progression ([Europe PMC 36371222](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36371222)). **Chen et al. (2024)** followed 295 patients who were plaque-free proximal to an LAD myocardial bridge, and a centreline and cross-section radiomics model predicted new plaque with external AUC 0.75 ([Europe PMC 38781436](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38781436)). Chen is the paper most likely to be raised against the novelty claim at the defence. It differs from the thesis in being a single site in referred patients and using radiomics rather than interpretable geometry, and the review should say so before the examiner does. The "therefore" in the absence sentence is a non sequitur, because one diseased-cohort study cannot imply that no study exists.

The risk-score sentence has two further problems. It ignores imaging that already refines individual risk. The 2021 ESC guideline lists CAC as a risk modifier ([Europe PMC 34558602](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34558602)). **ORFAN** showed in 40,091 CCTA patients that a pericoronary inflammation score adds to QRISK3 and CAD-RADS, even without CT-detected disease ([Europe PMC 38823406](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38823406)). ORFAN is the direct precedent for the role the thesis proposes for geometry. The sentence also justifies an individual-risk claim with a localisation argument ("plaque forms where its geometry lowers WSS"), against the author's own rule that localisation appears only as mechanism. And it sets up SCORE2, while the thesis baseline is age and sex. Both gap paragraphs are also uncited, which breaks the author's own style guide ("Each subsection ends on a gap that a cited source stated, not one we inferred", `thesis/style_guide_sota_aida.md` §12). Not citing shen2026geometry is legitimate, but then the primary studies must carry the gap. At present they contradict it in places.

**Reworded gaps that stay within the author's rules.** Gap 1 could read: large geometry and disease cohorts exist, but they are referred and either cross-sectional or event-based in patients with established disease (Li, Tello Ayala, Han, Tommasino). The studies that ground geometry mechanistically in flow are small or confined to one region (Kashyap, Zhang, Sakellarios). Griffo is named as the large exception, built on invasive angiography in single vessels of patients who all had MI. Gap 2 could read: plaque-free cohorts with repeat CCTA exist (PARADIGM, Chen) but are referred and either measured no geometry or examined one site. No study has measured tree-wide geometry automatically in a general-population cohort and related it to new plaque at a repeat scan. Every clause there is backed by a citation, so the author's no-own-conclusions rule is respected without Shen. The risk sentence becomes: risk estimates use systemic factors, refined in imaged patients by calcium and inflammation markers, but not by geometry. Its individual-level premise is that geometry varies between people (Temov 2016, already cited in the introduction).

## Synthesis and appraisal are where grade 12 is won

The chapter's architecture is one principal study per paragraph: Asakura, Li plus Kashyap, Garcha, Bransby, Tello Ayala, Sakellarios. Boote and Beile describe the worst reviews as "disjointed summaries", and they rank synthesis (mean 1.42 of 3) and methods critique (1.92) among the criteria dissertations score worst on ([Boote & Beile 2005](https://courses.washington.edu/rdesign/Boote_Biele2005.pdf)). The chapter has two genuine synthesis moves: the Glagov caveat on cross-sectional data, and the Bransby to Wang join, which is the one that is wrong. What it lacks is the paragraph that would earn a 3 on criterion G.

That paragraph already exists in the evidence. **Li 2011 reports an inverse tortuosity and CAD association (OR 0.755, CI upper bound 0.994), while Tello Ayala 2026 reports a positive one (adjusted OR 1.05 per SD)**, and the chapter cites both without noting that they point in opposite directions. With Groves 2009 (inverse), Zebić Mihić 2023 and 2024 (positive for non-obstructive disease) and Li M 2022 (null after adjustment, [Europe PMC 35197040](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35197040)), the pattern is measure- and endpoint-dependent. Discrete 2D bend counts in referred angiography populations find inverse links with obstructive disease. Continuous or 3D measures find positive or territory-specific links. Female sex and hypertension are over-represented in tortuous vessels in both. Written as a comparison with each claim cited, this replaces the self-confirming "Collectively" sentence. It also justifies two of the thesis's own design choices, continuous curvature and the age-and-sex baseline. It stays within the no-own-conclusions rule because each element is a cited finding.

Per-study appraisal needs one concessive limitation clause for each principal study, a form the model thesis allows. The material is already in the author's bib notes and full-text notes. For Tello Ayala, the clause would say: modest discrimination (AUROC 0.67 vs 0.60, no significance test), single-plane 2D projection, and a CAD label read from the same frames. For Kashyap and Garcha, it would say that the outcome is simulated WSS and that Garcha's geometries are synthetic. Griffo should be cited for its actual result rather than for the cost of simulation, because geometry-only WSS estimation at scale is the review's central bridge and Griffo is the near miss to name and distinguish. The WSS premise should also be stated honestly as consistent but modest. PREDICTION missed its primary endpoint, SMARTool lost WSS once LDL entered the model, and FAME II found high WSS predictive of MI ([Europe PMC 30309470](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30309470)). Saying this pre-empts the obvious defence question of why geometry should predict plaque when WSS itself predicts only modestly. Two structural changes make the argument visible. One is a sentence after the opening that names the strands (what geometry is, how it is obtained, what it has been used for), or two or three restored headings. The other is moving the segmentation paragraph up so the clinical-use studies run together into the gap. Finally, a short search statement (databases, time window, what was excluded and why) would raise criterion 1 from 1 to at least 2 at the cost of two sentences.

## House rules are coherent, but three of them need something to replace what they remove

The **author-and-year-as-subject** rule tends to produce author-centric paragraphs. The fix is a field-level topic sentence that groups three or more studies, with author-year subjects inside it, as the model thesis does. The **no-numbers** rule leaves claims such as "at most several hundred" impossible to check in the prose. Adding Population, Modality and Outcome columns to the table (referred or general, CCTA or invasive, WSS or present CAD or progression or onset) would make every gap visible at a glance. Calibrated design words such as "single-centre", "synthetic" or "two-dimensional" carry appraisal weight without using numbers. The **no-concept-explanations** rule is the costliest. A grep of `thesis/clinical_background/` finds no definition of tortuosity index, curvature or turning angle, so the reader cannot see why Kashyap's result matters. Those definitions need a home in the clinical background, which keeps the rule intact. The **individual-risk focus** is breached three times: the opening sentence ("beginning with the question of where plaque forms"), the Wang paragraph, and the "although" clause of the risk sentence. Each can be reframed around between-person variation. **Not citing shen2026geometry** costs only what the primary studies fail to rebuild, which the reworded gaps above address. The **no-em-dash** rule costs nothing. One tension goes beyond the listed rules: the style guide's "no conclusions of our own" caps criterion G at level 2 if read strictly. Reading it as "every element of a synthesis must be cited", rather than "no synthesis", unlocks the Li versus Tello Ayala paragraph.

## Writing fixes are small and mostly mechanical

Sentence quality is not the problem. The mean sentence length is 23.7 words against the model's 24, and author-year subjects and the no-em-dash rule are followed throughout. The fixable points are these. The gap opener "Despite these advancements, a significant gap remains" is near-verbatim from the model and follows a paragraph that ended on a limitation. The frame "Using ... relies on ..." is used twice. "Large populations" or "at scale" appears three times, and "Their work demonstrated" twice. The Garcha sentence runs to 46 words, above the guide's 45-word ceiling. The opening field claim is uncited. "These two elements" in the closing has no clear referent. The chapter also duplicates the introduction, which already states the referred-cohort, remodelling and measurement gaps. The author should decide which chapter owns the evidence, and the model has the review own it while the introduction names the problem. Asakura appears in the introduction, in `05_hemodynamics.tex` with "five" trees in the prose, and again as the review's opening. The review could open on the clinical question and cite Asakura in one clause as the mechanistic starting point.

## Conclusion

The chapter's problem is not effort or style but evidence management. The author's own knowledge bank already contains the papers that contradict the gaps (Han, Griffo, Groves) and the papers that would make them unassailable (PARADIGM, Chen, Zhang), and the gaps were written against a narrower set. The useful reframing is that the thesis's novelty does not need the overstated claims. The conjunction of general population, plaque-free baseline, automated tree-wide geometry and new plaque at a repeat scan survives every counterexample found. Stated with its near misses named, it is a stronger and more defensible claim than the current version.

Several things remain uncertain. The Chen 2024, SMARTool, Tommasino and most angle-study details are abstract-level. The EuroIntervention quotations were read through a summariser and should be confirmed by opening the articles. Garcha's "narrower branch" finding was not re-verified against the full text in this pass. No search was exhaustive enough to rule out a conference abstract that does exactly what the thesis does, which is one more reason to put a search statement in the chapter. The grade estimate is an inference from public descriptors and a published rubric, not from DTU grading practice, which is not public.
