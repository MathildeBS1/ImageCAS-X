# Fix the regions, declare one test, then freeze

> **Verdict**
>
> **(1) Paper-ready: no.** The methods PDF is a good descriptor definition. It is not yet a protocol for a serial-CCTA plaque paper. Four things block it. First, it has **no reliability evidence at its own operating point**: every quoted ICC was computed with σ = 1 mm extra smoothing or on another estimator, and the one figure it cites (κ_a "held 0.74 to 0.81" under 0.5 mm correlated jitter) misses the earlier reports' own gate. Reference versus CAS-Net agreement has never been measured. Second, it **states no hypothesis**: "primary in every plaque-relevant region" means 6 to 9 untargeted tests with no direction or multiplicity rule. Third, **regional κ_a is biased in the code as written**: the LAD and LCx proximal regions start at the LM, where no turning angle can be attributed, but the sum is still divided by the full region length. Fourth, it **cannot reach the two places where first plaque concentrates**, the LM bifurcation and the first 5 mm of the LAD and LCx, and it has no plan for heart size, cardiac phase, measurement error or power. Fixing all four needs about two weeks on ImageCAS-X alone. The outcome analysis itself needs CGPS data that nobody on the project has seen yet.

> **(3) CT-FFR: no. Reduced-order haemodynamics: conditional yes, as a covariate.** CT-FFR is a stenosis index. In a plaque-free tree it reduces to a smooth decline governed by length, radius and flow allocation, and every prognostic result for it comes from existing lesions. Low wall shear stress is the quantity with a mechanism for plaque onset, but 3D CFD cannot run on a whole cohort in 12 weeks. The feasible minimum is a steady Poiseuille network on each lumen tree, giving a per-segment pressure drop, a delta pseudo-FFR and a tree-propagated WSS index. Add lumen volume to myocardial mass (V/M) if LV mass is available. Validate on 20 to 40 trees against steady 3D CFD. Enter the result as a pre-declared adjustment and secondary exposure, never as a primary endpoint or as "CT-FFR". It must not delay the tortuosity freeze.

The main risk to the paper is not the tortuosity estimator. It is everything around it. κ_a at a 5 mm chord is length-insensitive, survives distal truncation and matches the outcome literature's preference for local mean curvature over arc/chord. That part can be frozen. What cannot yet be frozen is how κ_a becomes a hypothesis test: which regions, which single estimand, which reliability bar, which covariates, and how measurement error and selective return for the ten-year rescan enter the model. The design facts are sobering. CGPS rescans are not described in any public paper. The effect sizes seen in the largest continuous tortuosity study are far below what any plausible rescan subset can detect. Adding a full CCTA plaque read to risk factors plus calcium raised the C-statistic by only 0.015 in a 24,791-person cohort. The realistic contribution is therefore a registered, reliability-qualified measurement with population centiles, plus a well-powered test for a moderate effect (OR about 1.3 per SD). Haemodynamics belongs in as a physically motivated covariate, not a second headline.

## κ_a is sound, but three statements in the PDF are wrong

The estimator core should be kept. On 560 training scans, κ_a correlates with vessel length at Spearman 0.20, 0.26 and 0.32 (LAD, LCx, RCA). Whole-vessel arc/chord correlates at 0.80, 0.84 and 0.69, and shifts with dominance (RCA 1.30 left-dominant versus 1.79 right-dominant, p = 4e-13) ([normal ranges report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Centerline%20tortuosity%20normal%20ranges.md)). After a 20 mm distal cut κ_a keeps ICC 0.95, 0.88 and 0.93, where arc/chord falls to 0.60 to 0.83 ([vessel-specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)). The only head-to-head outcome comparison favours the same kind of measure. In 127 CCTA patients without obstruction, average absolute curvature explained low WSS with adjusted R² 0.196 in the LM and 0.232 in the LAD, while the arc/chord index explained about nothing ([Kashyap 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-04796-w)). The two-RCA case study showed the same thing by eye: two length-matched RCAs differ 16 % in arc/chord but twofold in κ_a (0.035 versus 0.068 rad/mm), and the renders side with κ_a ([case study](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Two%20centerline%20tortuosity%20case%20study.md)). The regional independence table (whole-LAD κ_a tracks the distal LAD at ρ 0.89) is the right argument for going regional ([methods PDF](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/tortuosity/research/tortuosity_methods.tex)).

**The regional denominator is biased, and I confirmed it in the code.** In `regional.py`, chord vertices start at the path origin, so the first turning angle sits about 5 mm along the path. The LAD and LCx proximal regions are defined as `(0, d1)`, from the LM to the first side branch. Regional κ_a is computed as `tt / len3`, the attributed turning divided by the full region length ([regional.py](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/tortuosity/research/case_study/regional.py)). My own derivation from that code follows. Each θ_k summarises bending over roughly ℓ/2 either side of its vertex. A region starting at 0 therefore never sees its first 2.5 mm, and on average it is biased down by about 2.5/|R|: about 14 % at the 18 mm median D1 distance and 25 % at the 10 mm floor. On top of that there is a sawtooth quantisation. A region just over 10 mm long holds one angle but is divided by 10 mm, a 50 % shortfall. The bias depends on where D1 sits, so it brings back exactly the length dependence κ_a was chosen to avoid. The RCA escapes only because its proximal window starts at 5 mm. There are two fixes: start every proximal window at 5 mm, as the RCA already does, or divide by 5 mm times the number of attributed vertices. Either way, state that the first 5 mm of the LAD and LCx is blind by construction.

**The quoted reliability does not belong to the adopted recipe.** The PDF adopts "no extra smoothing". Yet the vessel-specific, regional and priorities reports all computed their ICC tables at σ = 1 mm, and ICC rises monotonically with σ and ℓ under every jitter family ([vessel-specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md); [priorities report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports.md)). Even at σ = 1 mm, κ_a failed a 0.90 gate under 0.5 mm error correlated over 1 mm, with point ICCs 0.87, 0.76 and 0.71. The PDF's "held 0.74 to 0.81" misses the normal-ranges report's own point-ICC gate of 0.75 on the LCx and RCA ([normal ranges report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Centerline%20tortuosity%20normal%20ranges.md)). Adding σ = 1 mm changed κ_a medians by only 2 to 4 % (Spearman 0.98 to 0.99). Switching to σ = 1 mm therefore costs almost nothing in value and probably buys reliability. Whichever σ is chosen, the battery must be rerun there.

**Two further claims overstate the evidence.** The first is the "added information" table. It credits f_twist with 94 to 99 % of rank variance not explained by κ_a and length, but whole-vessel f_twist has ICC 0.15 to 0.32 under 0.5 mm correlated jitter, so most of that "new information" is noise. The adoption rule in the earlier report was reliable variance unexplained ([normal ranges report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Centerline%20tortuosity%20normal%20ranges.md)). The second is "not affected by dominance (all p ≥ 0.2)". That rests on 32 left-dominant and 15 co-dominant training scans, which cannot show absence of an effect. It should be reported as an estimate with a confidence interval. The PDF also contradicts itself on windows: its region table uses landmark windows (LM to D1, LM to OM1), while its text recommends fixed proximal windows as primary. The regional report used 2 to 17 mm windows instead ([regional descriptors report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors.md)). One scheme has to be chosen, on reliability, before freezing.

## Five additions turn the descriptor note into a lockable protocol

**Reliability gates and extractor agreement come first, because they decide whether a null is interpretable.** The normal-ranges report already sets gates for κ_a to stay primary: per-vessel ICC lower bound ≥ 0.90 at measured noise and at 0.25 mm correlated jitter, point ICC ≥ 0.75 at 0.5 mm correlated jitter, ≥ 0.85 after a 20 mm cut, and |ρ| with length ≤ 0.30. If it fails, move once to ℓ = 8 mm and stop tuning. The vessel-specific report adds a bias cap of 10 % on phantoms, so that "maximise ICC" cannot turn into "maximise smoothing", and a lag diagnostic that tells correlated from white centerline error ([vessel-specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)). None of this is in the PDF. Every earlier report names reference versus CAS-Net agreement on the 160 test scans as the single untested bottleneck ([priorities report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports.md)). A reviewer will also want agreement, not only reliability. ICC depends on between-subject spread, so an ICC from a referred cohort with about half plaque will not transfer to a healthier CGPS population. Per-region Bland-Altman limits, within-subject CV and the repeatability coefficient do transfer. That is QIBA practice ([Raunig 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0962280214537344)). The sample size, independence and blinding of the reliability substudy should be reported as GRRAS items ([GRRAS](https://www.acilci.net/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Guidelines-for-reporting-reliability-and-agreement-studies-GRRAS-were-proposed.pdf)). No coronary CTA study has yet reported an ICC or Bland-Altman figure for any tortuosity metric, so this substudy is itself publishable ([descriptor choice report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Choosing%20a%20coronary%20tortuosity%20descriptor.md)).

**One primary estimand replaces "primary in every region".** The cleanest form is a single mixed model: vessel region as a fixed factor, a κ_a-by-region interaction and a patient random effect. The global test of the κ_a terms is then the one pre-specified primary test, and it absorbs the multiplicity ([vessel-specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)). For a simpler headline, use the OR per SD of a patient-level proximal κ_a z-score for any new plaque. The hypothesis must be two-sided. The CAD association changes sign across definitions and cohorts (OR 0.755 in [Li 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024232)). In a 39-tree CFD study, the Marginal curvature correlated negatively with low-WSS area (r = -0.60), consistent with curvature-driven helical flow ([Shen 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)). The largest bend angle, currently the secondary, needs the same gates as κ_a before it can hold that role. Peaks are the least reliable statistic of a noisy curve, and in the case study the maximum bend was decided by a 127° ostial hook ([case study](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Two%20centerline%20tortuosity%20case%20study.md)).

**The bifurcation family is how the protocol reaches the onset sites.** In PARADIGM, 35 % of 334 patients with no plaque at baseline developed new lesions over a median 3.3 years, and 60 % of those lesions were in the LAD ([PARADIGM new plaque](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)). One MSCT study placed more than 90 % of LM plaques opposite the flow divider ([PubMed 17028928](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17028928/)). κ_a cannot measure the LM (median 7.3 mm) and is blind to the first 5 mm of each daughter. On ImageCAS-X the fitted daughter angle stayed at 78.0 to 80.8° across smoothing, where a 2 mm secant drifted from 98.6 to 63.2°. The LM Finet ratio had method ICC 0.85 ([regional descriptors report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors.md)). Objective 7 in the project plan names bifurcation angles next to tortuosity ([project plan](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/thesis/project_plan.tex)). The PDF should declare the LM daughter angle as a co-primary under Holm, or as the lead secondary, and record which. A ramus flag is required, because the LM angle averages 89 ± 21° with an intermediate artery and 75 ± 23° without ([regional descriptors report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors.md)).

**Heart size and cardiac phase are biases, not noise.** κ_a is in rad/mm, so it scales as 1/s when a tree is scaled by s. Small hearts are more tortuous: LV mass index was 77.6 versus 121.0 g/m² in tortuous versus non-tortuous hearts, and female sex raises tortuosity (OR 1.68 to 2.60) ([Oehler 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5912164/); [Tello Ayala 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). Size should enter as a covariate, not a divisor, because ratio indexing creates spurious associations. CGPS has already published LV mass from the same 320-detector scans, so a direct size covariate probably exists ([Fuchs 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/290432000_Normal_values_of_left_ventricular_mass_and_cardiac_chamber_volumes_assessed_by_320-detector_computed_tomography_angiography_in_the_Copenhagen_General_Population_Study)). Systole raises CCTA curvature by 11.1 % ([van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)). The CGPS protocol picked the phase automatically with PhaseXact, so phase varies with heart rate ([CGPS reproducibility study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6294364)). Phase and heart rate should come from the DICOM headers, enter the model as covariates, and define a single-phase sensitivity subset.

**Bookkeeping rules reviewers will check.** Fix an ostium rule for every energy-type or peak descriptor. Keep one definition of "the LAD": the repo has two, `tortuosity/vessels.py` and `topology.graph.Vessel` ([priorities report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports.md)). Derive the length floor from an ICC-versus-remaining-length curve, not the current unexplained 10 mm. Treat an absent OM1 (a fifth of trees) as an anatomical variable rather than silent NaN. Map regions to SCCT 18-segment units, because CGPS read plaque per SCCT segment ([SCCT 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25301040/)). The pre-registration should also say plainly that the Disease column was opened in an earlier session and that confirmation can only be the 80-scan validation transfer test ([priorities report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports.md)).

## The serial analysis turns on who returns and what counts as normal

**CGPS supplies two outcomes with different denominators.** About 10,000 participants aged 40 or older opted into a research CCTA from 2010. Registry outcomes (MI, death, revascularisation) now have a median 6.0 years of follow-up in 10,003 participants ([EHJ ehag612](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/advance-article/doi/10.1093/eurheartj/ehag612/8759473)). New plaque exists only in those who returned for the rescan, and I found no public paper on the size, invitation rule or reading protocol of that subset. Those details must come from the supervisors, and the design cannot be finalised without them. The recommended primary outcome follows PARADIGM: new plaque in units that were plaque-free at baseline. PARADIGM defined it as total plaque volume ≥ 1 mm³ at follow-up in 9,583 normal SCCT segments ([PARADIGM](https://www.journalofcardiovascularct.com/article/S1934-5925(24)00032-7/abstract)). Per-patient "any new plaque" among plaque-free returners is the simplest and best-powered version. Per-region new plaque is the mechanistic secondary. Segments with baseline plaque leave the risk set rather than being modelled as progression, because progression is driven by baseline plaque composition. In the only published ten-year serial CCTA cohort, baseline non-calcified plaque was the sole plaque predictor ([Amsterdam 10-year cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12590748/)). Follow-up scanners are more sensitive, so the detection threshold must be fixed before unblinding, with calcium conversion as a scanner-robust sensitivity outcome. Registry events belong with the whole cohort as an exploratory analysis: 71 MI in 3.5 years among 9,533 participants leaves power only for large hazard ratios ([Fuchs 2023](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M22-3027)).

**"Healthy at both scans" cannot define normal.** That group is selected on survival, on returning and on the outcome itself. Building the reference from it and then testing whether deviation predicts new plaque is circular, because future non-cases set the centre and cases land in the tails by construction. Two references should be declared before any outcome is linked. Reference A is all baseline CCTA participants. Reference B is a prescriptive healthy standard: baseline plaque-free, no diabetes, not smoking, no statin, with hypertension handled as a conditioning variable because it raises tortuosity. Both are fitted as GAMLSS or LMS centiles in age, sex and heart size, the method WHO chose for growth standards ([Borghi 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sim.2227); [Cole 2009](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sim.3504)). The both-scans-healthy group stays descriptive. It is the right group for measuring how much geometry changes per decade without disease, against the measurement-error band. The z-score from reference A or B is the exposure in the outcome model and the basis of the top-5 % flag. "Abnormal" then means statistically unusual for age, sex and size, not harmful, because no outcome-anchored cut-off exists.

**Adjustment and return bias.** The primary model is logistic, or complementary log-log with a log-interval offset if intervals span 8 to 12 years. Its terms: baseline κ_a z-score, age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, LDL, BMI, LV mass, baseline scanner, phase or heart rate, interval and follow-up scanner. The Amsterdam study handled scanner change the same way, by adjusting for follow-up kV and scanner ([PMC12590748](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12590748/)). Statin start between scans lies partly on the causal path, and may itself respond to the baseline scan result. Adjust for baseline statin use only, and add between-scan statins as a sensitivity analysis. For per-region outcomes, the within-between (Mundlak) random-effects model separates between-patient from within-patient effects and puts the "no segment versus segment" rule into statistics ([Bell 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11135-018-0802-x)). Return after ten years is strongly selected: in Amsterdam, 299 of 539 invited patients were rescanned ([PMC12590748](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12590748/)). Model P(return) from baseline covariates, baseline plaque and baseline geometry, and weight returners by its stabilised inverse. Treat death as a competing event and name the estimand as the survivor population. As a check, test whether geometry predicts non-return, using registry data on the non-returners. Imputing plaque outcomes for non-returners is not credible, because nothing about their plaque is observed after baseline.

**Measurement error changes both the estimate and the required n, and the two earlier notes disagree on how.** Classical error with reliability λ attenuates the per-unit slope to λβ. Per SD of the observed variable, the effect shrinks by √λ. The required n therefore scales by 1/λ when the target effect is stated per SD of the true descriptor, not by 1/λ² as the regional longitudinal note assumed ([longitudinal note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors/longitudinal_design.md)). Pre-register effects per SD of the measured z-score, and report the regression-calibrated OR with a bootstrap CI as secondary ([Frost and Thompson 2000](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-985X.00164)). λ must come from same-scan repeats: reference versus CAS-Net on the ImageCAS-X test split, or two segmentations or two phases of one CGPS scan. The ten-year pairs confound error with real change and cannot supply it. The plaque outcome has error too. CGPS plaque-volume reproducibility was inter-observer CV 27 %, and it worsened with higher risk profile ([PMC6294364](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6294364)). That makes outcome misclassification differential by risk, which reviewers will ask about.

**Power allows a moderate effect, never the literature's small one.** From calcium data, per-patient incidence among plaque-free participants is probably 0.3 to 0.5 over ten years. In MESA, 53 % of people with CAC = 0 at baseline had CAC > 0 by ten years ([Dzaye 2021](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2020.06.048)). Using Hsieh's formula ([Hsieh 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9699234/)) with two-sided α 0.05, 80 % power, covariate R² 0.1 and a 1/λ penalty, the serial-design note gives these minimum detectable ORs per SD ([serial design note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Tortuosity%20protocol%20readiness%20and%20CT%20FFR/serial_cohort_design.md)):

| Plaque-free returners analysed | p 0.3, λ 1.0 | p 0.3, λ 0.7 | p 0.5, λ 1.0 | p 0.5, λ 0.7 |
|-|-|-|-|-|
| 300 | 1.45 | 1.56 | 1.41 | 1.50 |
| 500 | 1.33 | 1.41 | 1.30 | 1.37 |
| 1,000 | 1.23 | 1.28 | 1.21 | 1.25 |
| 2,000 | 1.15 | 1.19 | 1.14 | 1.17 |

About half of CGPS has plaque at baseline, so the analysed n is roughly half the rescan subset, before any inverse-weighting design effect. The largest continuous angiographic study found per-SD ORs of only 1.05 to 1.09 for CAD ([Tello Ayala 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). That size of effect is out of reach for any plausible rescan subset. The protocol should therefore declare OR 1.3 per SD as the smallest effect of interest and report the achieved minimum detectable effect. A null then reads as "effects larger than X excluded", which is publishable. A prediction claim should stay a secondary, TRIPOD+AI-reported analysis ([TRIPOD+AI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11025451/)). In SCAPIS, adding a full CCTA read to risk factors plus CAC raised the C-statistic by only 0.015 ([SCAPIS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12598578/)). A geometric proxy will add less.

## CT-FFR measures stenosis physiology that healthy trees do not have

**CT-FFR does vary in normal vessels, but mostly as geometry the thesis can measure directly.** In normal vessels it falls from about 0.96 to 0.99 at the ostium to 0.86 to 0.90 distally ([JACC 2018 abstract](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/S0735-1097(18)32115-6)). In 59 stenosis-free asymptomatic marathon runners the distal LAD read 0.81 ± 0.10, and 32 % were at or below 0.80 ([PMC8589749](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8589749/)). The known determinants are lumen calibre, length and lumen volume relative to myocardial mass. Low V/M gave invasive FFR 0.80 versus 0.87 even in non-obstructive disease ([Taylor 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28789941)). Tortuosity itself moves pressure little without a stenosis. Idealised simulations gave 115 to 285 Pa (about 1 to 2 mmHg) of extra drop at resting flow over one to five bends ([Li 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3419180/)). A 1D CT-FFR model sees tortuosity mostly through path length. A CT-FFR "association" with tortuosity would therefore largely be the length effect that κ_a was chosen to remove. The distal value is also the least reproducible CT-FFR readout across repeat scans; delta-FFR is the most reproducible ([interscan FFRCT study](https://www.journalofcardiovascularct.com/article/S1934-5925(24)00523-9/fulltext)).

**The prognostic evidence does not transfer to plaque-free baselines.** EMERALD and EMERALD II, FAME II substudies and the 2026 WSS surrogate work all ask which existing lesion becomes an ACS culprit within one to three years. The measured quantities are delta-FFR, high WSS and axial plaque stress at a lesion shoulder, and none of these exists in a healthy tree ([EMERALD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29550316/); [EMERALD II](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2024.03.015)). Their horizon is also short: EMERALD II discrimination fell from AUC 0.851 for events within one year to 0.741 at two to three years ([EMERALD II time frame](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2025.02.003)). The evidence that bears on onset is low WSS. Low ESS predicted lumen loss and plaque-burden increase in PREDICTION ([Stone 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22723305/)). Low WSS was linked to progression even in wall classified as plaque-free, though from invasive imaging in patients who already had disease and a search summary only ([PMC10153640](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10153640/)). No study was found that computes CT-derived haemodynamics at a plaque-free baseline and follows it for new plaque beyond about five years. A CGPS analysis would be novel, but there is no effect size to power it against.

**The minimal defensible package fits in about two to three weeks, alongside tortuosity.** The first part is a steady 0D Poiseuille network on each lumen tree. Inlet flow comes from an allometric diameter law, flow splits by daughter diameter ratio, and each segment has resistance 8μL/(πr⁴) with r smoothed over 2 to 5 mm. It returns per-segment pressure drop, a pseudo-FFR at each node and delta pseudo-FFR per named segment, plus a tree-propagated WSS index 4μQ/(πr³) normalised to the root. It is a sparse linear solve, seconds per tree, so it runs on every ImageCAS-X and CGPS tree. Reduced-order models are credible surrogates: 1D FFR agreed with 3D at 0.00 ± 0.03 ([Blanco 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11472)), and steady and pulsatile 3D FFR correlated at r = 0.988 ([arXiv 2408.16496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16496)). The second part is a steady 3D CFD check on 20 to 40 ImageCAS-X trees (OpenFOAM or SimVascular), with the same boundary conditions. It reports agreement for pressure drop and segment-mean WSS, and asks how much of the inner-bend low-WSS area κ_a and the WSS index explain together. That is the experiment that makes the tortuosity-WSS link concrete. The third part is V/M, if LV mass can be obtained: it is the best-supported haemodynamic scalar for healthy trees and needs no simulation. Two cautions apply. Radius enters at the fourth power, so a 10 % radius error gives about 40 % error in segment pressure drop. The inputs therefore need the same reference-versus-CAS-Net agreement test as the centerlines, and normalised or delta quantities should be preferred. Transient CFD for OSI and helicity at cohort scale, and commercial CT-FFR (HeartFlow, Siemens cFFR, DEEPVESSEL), are out of scope in 12 weeks.

**Its role is adjustment, not a second headline.** Declare in the protocol that the κ_a analysis will be repeated with the WSS index and V/M as covariates. The question then becomes whether any tortuosity association survives adjustment for flow-resistance geometry. Declare the WSS index as a secondary exposure under the same multiplicity rule. Do not call the output CT-FFR and do not claim ischaemia relevance. If time runs short, drop the 3D validation before the network, and drop the network before any part of the tortuosity freeze.

## Conclusion

The descriptor was never the weak point. The PDF's measurement choices hold up. What the earlier reports built (gates, a single estimand, the bifurcation family, error correction, size and phase covariates) never reached the document that is about to be frozen, and one regional formula is wrong in the running code. The order of work follows from that. Fix the denominator and the operating point. Run the reliability and extractor battery against written gates. Declare one two-sided primary with OR 1.3 per SD as the smallest effect of interest. Specify reference groups A and B, and the return-weighted model, before any CGPS outcome is linked. Everything in that list can be done on ImageCAS-X in the next two to three weeks. It is also what makes a null in CGPS publishable rather than uninterpretable.

The haemodynamic question comes out more clearly than expected. In a healthy tree, CT-FFR is mostly a nonlinear restatement of length, radius and heart size, and its prognostic record belongs to existing lesions. Low WSS is mechanistically right for onset but is only partly predicted by curvature. A cheap network model plus a small 3D check is therefore worth more than any CT-FFR run: it tests how far κ_a stands in for local shear, and it lets the paper show that a tortuosity effect, if one exists, is not simply flow-resistance geometry. The questions that decide the paper can only be answered by the supervisors: how many CGPS participants were rescanned, how they were chosen, and how their plaque was read.
