# Curvature panels earn their keep with guardrails

Tello Ayala et al.'s single-feature CAD model is not really evidence for using fewer geometric descriptors; it is evidence for representing the *one* descriptor they chose more richly, since their transformer reading the full per-point tortuosity profile beat a logistic-regression model built on the identical metric's scalar mean, AUROC 0.67 (95% CI 0.65-0.69) versus 0.60 (95% CI 0.58-0.63) ([Tello Ayala et al. 2026, PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). That result argues for depth of representation, not narrowness of feature choice, and it does not settle whether adding independent geometric domains, such as torsion or bifurcation angle, on top of curvature would help. The wider literature gives a consistent but qualified answer: combining features from *different* information domains (geometry plus plaque burden, geometry plus clinical variables) produces the largest documented discrimination gains, while stacking multiple pure-geometry features on top of each other tends to produce only modest gains of roughly 0.02-0.03 AUC, some of which is likely shared information rather than independent signal ([Moon et al., QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/117032/html); [CLAP score, MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2308-3425/11/11/338)). Whether this thesis's own four-metric locked panel (mean absolute curvature, RMS curvature, torsion/non-planarity, bifurcation angle with branch diameter) is worth the added complexity therefore depends less on a philosophical single-vs-multi-feature stance and more on two concrete, checkable things: whether the panel's components carry genuinely independent information, and whether the available cohort has enough outcome events to support that many predictors without the "gain" being overfitting in a coat. On current evidence, a compact, correlation-screened, regularized version of the multi-feature panel is defensible and likely to outperform a bare single scalar, but an unregularized five-feature model fit without matching sample-size planning is the riskier of the two extremes, not the safer one.

## Tello Ayala's "one feature" is a richly represented profile, not a minimal one

The instinct to read Tello Ayala et al. as an argument for minimalism misreads what was actually compared. Their transformer did not compress the right coronary artery to a single number the way a logistic-regression baseline does; it consumed the full local curvature profile along the vessel, point by point, and only then combined that sequence representation with age and sex, reaching AUROC 0.67 against a logistic-regression baseline using the same underlying metric collapsed to its patient-level mean, AUROC 0.60 ([PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). The 0.07 AUC gap is therefore a representation effect, keeping *where* along the vessel a bend sits rather than averaging it away, not a feature-count effect: both models use exactly one geometric domain (discrete curvature), and the richer of the two wins. No other study in cardiology, retinal, or cerebrovascular imaging was found to replicate this specific profile-versus-scalar contrast for any geometric metric, so it stands as a single, well-quantified data point rather than a general law, but the implication for a "one feature versus many" argument is clear: the honest single-feature baseline this thesis should compare against is a curvature *profile*, per the thesis's own fifth locked decision to keep $\kappa(s)$ un-collapsed, not a single averaged curvature scalar. Comparing a multi-feature panel against a deliberately weakened scalar-mean baseline would overstate the case for adding more features; comparing it against the profile-based baseline is the fairer test, and is also the test the profile-versus-panel literature has not yet run for coronary geometry specifically.

## Pure-geometry combinations buy little; cross-domain combinations buy a lot

Where the literature does directly test combining geometric features, the pattern is that stacking more geometry onto geometry has a low ceiling. Moon et al. combined proximal-LAD tortuosity (d20) with the LAD bifurcation angle (cos θ) into one product term predicting severe stenosis: d20 alone reached AUC 0.634, cos θ alone reached 0.743, and the combined term reached only 0.767, with neither individual component surviving multivariable adjustment once the product term was included ([Moon et al., QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/117032/html)). A 0.024 absolute AUC gain from adding a second geometric term to an already-decent single geometric term is a real but small effect, and it is consistent with substantial shared information between tortuosity and angle rather than fully additive, independent contributions. The same pattern appears at the score level: SYNTAX's roughly ten anatomical morphology items outperformed Gensini's two-feature severity-times-location design for heart-team decision prediction, AUC 0.759 versus 0.680 ([PMC8942066](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8942066/)), but Gensini actually beat SYNTAX specifically in patients with only one or two diseased vessels, where the extra complexity features had nothing to describe ([Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-85191-z)). By contrast, the scores with the strongest discrimination in this literature, CLAP (AUC 0.91 development, 0.85 validation) and the retinal CAD-detection study (AUROC rising from 0.694 for fundus geometry alone to 0.802 once clinical risk factors were added), get there by mixing geometry with a categorically different domain, plaque burden, calcium score, comorbidity, or traditional risk factors, rather than by adding more geometric terms of the same kind ([CLAP score](https://www.mdpi.com/2308-3425/11/11/338); [retinal CAD detection, PMC13052816](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13052816/)). For this thesis's locked panel, the honest expectation is therefore that adding bifurcation angle and branch diameter on top of curvature should behave like the Moon et al. case, a real but modest incremental gain, not like CLAP's much larger jump, because all four locked metrics live inside the same geometric domain rather than crossing into plaque or clinical information.

## The panel's own components are not obviously redundant, but that has barely been tested

The strongest primary evidence bearing directly on redundancy within a tortuosity/curvature panel is Kashyap et al.'s 127-patient CFD study, which is also the source this thesis's own locked-panel note already leans on. It found the classic tortuosity index explained essentially none of the variance in low wall shear stress area (R² ≈ -0.018, p = 0.86), while mean absolute curvature explained the most (R² = 0.112 to 0.196 across vessels, p < 0.001) and RMS curvature explained a similar but slightly smaller amount (R² = 0.093 to 0.164) ([Kashyap et al. 2022, PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)). Two things follow from these numbers. First, curvature-family metrics are clearly not interchangeable with the older tortuosity index with respect to hemodynamic relevance, so replacing the index with curvature, as this thesis's panel already does, is well supported. Second, mean absolute curvature and RMS curvature explain similarly-sized, overlapping shares of the same outcome (0.112 versus 0.093 at the left main), which is circumstantial evidence that these two locked measures carry substantially shared information about the same underlying bending, exactly the kind of correlated pair that the SYNTAX and Moon et al. results above suggest gives only a modest combined benefit. No study, including Kashyap's, was found to report a direct pairwise correlation coefficient between curvature and torsion, or between bifurcation angle and either curvature measure, in the same coronary cohort ([multi-metric evidence notes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)). The best indirect evidence for bifurcation angle and branch diameter specifically is that Murray's-law-optimal diameter geometry and shear-stress-minimizing angle geometry were shown in one computational study not to coincide, implying angle and diameter carry at least partly independent atherogenic information rather than one substituting for the other ([angle-rule CFD study, PubMed 22365499](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22365499/); deviation from Murray's law independently tracked plaque and calcium volume after adjustment, [Atherosclerosis 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22261173/)). Torsion is the weakest-evidenced member of the panel: its own locked-metrics note already flags that the claim it affects wall shear stress is drawn from a review table summarizing other groups' simulations rather than primary measurement, and no source in this research pass found a direct torsion-versus-outcome correlation of the kind Kashyap ran for curvature. The panel is therefore a mix: two measures (curvature and angle-with-diameter) with reasonable indirect evidence of adding distinct information, one pair within the panel (mean absolute and RMS curvature) with a real redundancy signal, and one measure (torsion) that is mechanistically motivated but empirically unverified for this exact use.

## Every added predictor raises the sample-size floor, and this cohort's ceiling has not been checked against it

The clinical-prediction-modeling literature converges on a specific, quantifiable warning that applies regardless of how independent the panel's features turn out to be: the number of outcome events available, not the number of features that seem theoretically justified, is what should set the feature budget. The classical rule of at least 10 events per candidate predictor, counted before any selection is performed, is now considered too permissive on its own; current guidance (Riley, van Smeden, Steyerberg) instead targets explicit overfitting criteria, a global shrinkage factor of at least 0.9 and less than a 0.05 gap between apparent and optimism-corrected R² ([Riley et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/sim.7992); [PMC12106283](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12106283/)). Critically, the "safe" EPV threshold itself rises as the candidate predictor count rises: van Smeden et al.'s simulations found EPV = 10 eliminated coefficient bias for 3- and 5-predictor models, but the same low-bias level required EPV = 20 once the model had 7, 10, or 12 predictors, and at EPV = 2 in a 12-predictor model, only 7 of 1,000 simulated models even converged ([van Smeden et al., PMC5045274](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5045274/)). TRIPOD and PROBAST both treat predictor-count inflation and outcome-driven predictor selection on the same data used for evaluation as the primary, avoidable cause of optimistic apparent performance that fails to replicate externally, and both require candidate predictor counts and selection procedures to be reported transparently precisely because this failure mode is otherwise invisible to readers ([TRIPOD E&E](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M14-0698); [PROBAST E&E](https://www.probast.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/aime201901010-m181377.pdf)). This matters concretely here because Tello Ayala et al. validated their single-feature approach on 22,334 angiography patients ([PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)); any coronary imaging cohort assembled for this thesis's own CAD-outcome prediction task is very unlikely to reach that scale, and the four-to-six candidate predictors in the locked panel (curvature, RMS curvature, torsion, angle, and one or two diameter terms) sit exactly in the range where van Smeden's results show the required EPV climbing from 10 toward 20. Whichever feature count is chosen, the number of outcome events actually available should be checked against this arithmetic before the model is built, not after its apparent AUC is seen, since diminishing-returns evidence elsewhere in this literature (age-alone versus full Framingham screening reaching AUC 0.89 versus 0.90-0.91 on 500,000 simulated individuals, a 0.01-0.02 gain for a large increase in model complexity) shows that small apparent AUC bumps of the size a geometry panel might plausibly produce are exactly the size that overfitting alone can manufacture with zero true signal ([PMC3087724](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3087724/)).

## If more than one feature is kept, the panel needs correlation-aware regularization, not a plain multivariable fit

Given a real, if partial, redundancy signal between the panel's curvature measures, the standard multivariable regression that simply enters all four locked features as covariates is the wrong tool: plain LASSO, the default feature-selector in imaging prediction modeling, handles correlated predictor groups poorly, arbitrarily retaining one member of a correlated cluster and zeroing the rest, a selection that is unstable across resamples ([LASSO and correlated features](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.1605)). Elastic net was built specifically to fix this failure mode via its "grouping effect," keeping correlated predictors in or out of the model together rather than letting one win by chance ([Zou & Hastie 2005](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/67/2/301/7109482); [grouping effect of elastic net](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167715213001752)). The standard pre-modeling diagnostic sequence in quantitative-imaging methodology is to report the full pairwise correlation matrix among candidate features up front, compute variance inflation factors and flag anything above roughly 5 to 10, and only then let elastic net (or an equivalent shrinkage method) perform final selection, rather than doing manual stepwise elimination, which the methodology literature explicitly calls an invalid way to resolve collinearity ([VIF thresholds](https://editverse.com/understanding-variance-inflation-factor-in-clinical-research/); [stepwise regression flagged as inappropriate, PMC11093476](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11093476/)). A single non-negotiable requirement threads through every source that touches this: whatever selection or dimensionality-reduction step is used has to be fit inside each training fold and only applied, never refit, to the corresponding held-out fold, because feature selection performed once on the whole dataset before cross-validation is a well-documented source of data leakage, with reported inflation on the order of 0.15 AUC in the most directly relevant estimate found ([radiomics feature-selection leakage](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13244-021-01115-1)). Finally, interpretability is not a purely aesthetic cost of a larger panel: the clinical-adoption literature independently converges on the same conclusion the statistics does, that complex multivariable models do not reliably outperform simple, transparent scores in practice, and that clinicians extend more trust and workflow integration to models they can audit by eye ([interpretability-performance tradeoff](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21934); [VOCAL-Penn score, PMC12668580](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12668580/)).

## Conclusion

The evidence does not support treating this as a binary choice between Tello Ayala's one feature and the thesis's full four-measure panel. It supports a middle path: keep the curvature profile representation Tello Ayala's result actually validates, add bifurcation angle with branch diameter as a second, evidence-backed geometric domain since Murray's-law and shear-optimization studies suggest it is not redundant with curvature, treat mean absolute curvature and RMS curvature as a correlated pair to be merged or shrunk together rather than entered as two independent predictors, and hold torsion out as an exploratory addition pending its own direct outcome correlation, since it is currently supported only by review-level mechanistic argument rather than measured association. Whichever final feature count is chosen, the deciding factor should be the number of CAD outcome events this thesis's own cohort actually provides, checked against the EPV arithmetic above, not an a priori preference for parsimony or for completeness; a five-feature model on a cohort with too few events to support even three predictors at EPV ≥ 10 will look better in-sample and generalize worse, which is precisely the failure mode TRIPOD, PROBAST, and the van Smeden simulations were built to catch. What the literature genuinely does not yet contain, and what this thesis would be contributing if it does the correlation-matrix-and-elastic-net analysis properly, is a formal multicollinearity study of a coronary tortuosity/curvature/torsion/angle panel of exactly this kind; no coronary or retinal vascular-geometry paper found in this research pass reports that analysis, which is both a gap and an opportunity to make the feature-count decision on this thesis's own data rather than by analogy to unrelated radiomics or SYNTAX literature.
