# Keep five features, prespecify one more

The literature does not justify widening the primary geometry block. Keep curvature, taper, the LM bifurcation angle and the LM→LAD and LM→LCx diameter ratios as the single prespecified LR test for each question, and prespecify **at most one or two** extra features as a labelled exploratory secondary analysis. The extra feature with the best mechanistic support is **side-branch flow loss per trunk segment**, best expressed as the mismatch between observed taper and the taper that branch outflow would require. It is the only candidate that adds a WSS mechanism the block does not already hold: blood leaving through side branches lowers main-vessel WSS, and modelling the branches roughly halved computed mean time-averaged WSS in one study, although that study is an unreviewed preprint of 19 diseased vessels. A second option, for the who question only, is segment lumen size adjusted for body size and sex. The other candidates the researchers examined fail at least one test. Torsion, eccentricity and side-branch counts are too noisy or depend on detection thresholds. Side-branch and ostial angles and myocardial bridging lack consistent mechanistic or clinical support. Tortuosity index, distance from the ostium and Finet's ratio largely re-express curvature, segment position or the existing LM ratios. Above all, **no human study has tested any of these features, or the current five, as predictors of incident coronary plaque in people whose baseline CCTA was plaque-free**. Every effect size in this field comes from computational models, diseased vessels or ACS case-control designs. Expect small effects. Each feature that adds degrees of freedom without independent signal costs the confirmatory test power.

## No study links any extra feature to plaque developing in plaque-free people

The clinical evidence for geometry beyond the current block sits almost entirely in populations that already had plaque. ICONIC found that adverse geometric characteristics, meaning a short distance from the ostium, bifurcation involvement and tortuosity, raised discrimination of ACS culprit lesions from **AUC 0.733 to 0.766** over plaque features, with bifurcation involvement at HR 2.14 ([JAMA Cardiol 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2788006)). That was a post hoc nested case-control study of lesions in ACS patients, and tortuosity occurred in under 5% of lesions. EMERALD, also an ACS case-control design, found CT-derived hemodynamic indices that were **significantly intercorrelated** but each added similar information over plaque features ([Front Cardiovasc Med 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2021.713835/pdf)). That pattern argues for overlapping rather than cumulative information from many correlated geometric terms. PROSPECT-ESS reported low endothelial shear stress at a propensity-adjusted HR 4.34 for nonculprit events ([Mount Sinai record](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/role-of-low-endothelial-shear-stress-and-plaque-characteristics-i-2/)), but in nonculprit lesions of ACS patients, measured with invasive imaging.

Only two designs come close to the CGPS setting, and neither supports named geometric features. In a multicentre **retrospective** serial-CCTA study of 295 patients with an LAD myocardial bridge and no plaque proximal to it at baseline, a radiomics model of the proximal cross-section predicted later proximal plaque with external AUC 0.75. The **clinical plus anatomical model reached only 0.56** externally ([Chen et al. 2024, PubMed 38781436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38781436)). A PARADIGM radiomics study selected baseline plaque-free segments, of which about 9.8% developed plaque, and found that radiomics added to clinical risk factors. Its full text was not read, so whether geometry was tested is unknown ([JCCT 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1934592524000327)). A PARADIGM sub-analysis, available only as a **conference abstract** from a referral population, found that 35% of people without baseline plaque developed lesions over a median 3.3 years, most often in the LAD (60%). Its adjusted predictors were baseline plaque, diabetes and BMI, and **no geometric predictor was reported** ([EHJ-CVI 2026 abstract](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)).

The where design already matches the lesson from the invasive serial-imaging work. In 10,205 IVUS sectors, low-WSS sectors progressed more at population level, but absolute WSS varied so much between patients that **absolute thresholds were inappropriate per patient** ([Hartman et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8586146)). Within-person comparison, which the conditional logistic where model makes, is the right frame. It also means that position, log length and dominance × vessel in the where base will absorb much of what features such as distance from the ostium carry.

## Side-branch flow loss is the one mechanism the block lacks

The CFD literature names a single descriptor that is both mechanistically distinct from curvature, taper, the LM angle and the LM ratios and supported in the same direction by several groups: **how much flow a trunk segment loses to side branches**. Adding side branches to patient-specific models lowered mean time-averaged WSS from 1.698 to 0.821 Pa in IVUS-based models and from 1.325 to 0.682 Pa in OCT-based models, and WSS from the branched models predicted progression better (AUC 0.712 vs 0.554) ([Ramasamy et al., bioRxiv 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.05.535789.full.pdf)). This is a **preprint** of 19 diseased IVUS vessels and 13 OCT vessels. Whether it has since been peer-reviewed was not checked. Work from the Stone and Coskun group found that leaving side branches out greatly reduced the ability to detect regions with pathological shear stress, though only the search summary was read ([PMC4983229](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4983229/)). The direction also follows from the flow-split literature. In six patient-specific CT coronary trees, Murray's law underestimated side-branch flow, so it gave about 8% higher average WSS than the empirical diameter-based split, with a range of −5% to +27% ([van der Giessen et al. 2011](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/33468)).

The physics explains why this is the non-redundant candidate. Poiseuille WSS scales as Q/r³. Measured coronary flow splits with an exponent near 2.3 to 2.4, not Murray's 3. A meta-analysis of 1,070 trees pooled 2.39 ([Taylor et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11380967/)). If flow is lost at that rate, a trunk that narrows from 4 to 3 mm raises Poiseuille WSS by only about 21%. The same narrowing without branch flow loss would raise it 2.4-fold. This is the researchers' own arithmetic. The WSS-relevant quantity is therefore the **mismatch between observed taper and the taper that branch outflow justifies**. A segment that stays wide while a large branch takes flow should carry low WSS. Raw taper mixes this mismatch with the flow-matched narrowing that leaves WSS unchanged, so the taper in the block is an attenuated proxy for it.

Three caveats keep this out of the primary block. First, the evidence comes from invasive imaging of diseased and often stented vessels, and no human outcome study uses a branch-flow geometric index. Second, a simple branch count per segment partly re-encodes the segment split, because `split_trunks.py` places the LAD, LCx and RCA boundaries at labelled side branches (D1/D2, OM, PDA) or at side branches at least 15 mm long. Counting branches in a segment would partly test where the boundaries were drawn. The flow-weighted mismatch avoids this but needs side-branch **diameters**. The tracer tree gives side-branch positions and lengths, since the split rule uses them. Whether the branch diameters near the ostia are reliable has not been checked. Third, the mismatch overlaps with taper by construction. It would replace the decided taper definition, a robust log-radius slope masked around side branches, rather than add a new axis, so it belongs in a sensitivity or secondary analysis flagged as a change of feature definition.

## Diameter dominates computed WSS, and the block already holds its best form

Where studies regress computed WSS on named geometric descriptors, diameter-type terms explain most of the variance and shape terms little. In 127 symptomatic patients with zero calcium and no obstruction, each curvature metric explained only **about 6% to 23%** of the variance in the percentage of LM-bifurcation area with time-averaged WSS below 0.4 Pa. The tortuosity index (arc length over chord) explained essentially none of it (p = 0.66 to 0.87) ([Kashyap et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)). In the same cohort, **Finet's ratio** had a standardised β of −0.84 for low-shear area, the curvatures and angles were not significant, and a step-down model reached R² = 0.79 ([Gharleghi et al., arXiv 2401.12504](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12504v1)). These numbers come with three limits. They were read from the arXiv version. The R² is in-sample after selection from 11 candidates. And with uniform inlet velocity and 0 Pa outlets, the daughter diameters set the flow split, so diameter predicts computed WSS partly by construction.

That evidence supports the current block rather than an extension of it. Finet's ratio, D_LM / (D_LAD + D_LCx), is a re-expression of the LM→LAD and LM→LCx ratios already in it, so adding it would spend a degree of freedom on information the block already holds. Absolute lumen size is the other candidate this literature points to, and here the gain is much less clear. The researchers' own derivation combines Poiseuille WSS with the empirical flow law Q = 1.43·D^2.55 and gives a mean WSS proportional to about D^−0.45, which is nearly flat across vessel sizes. Under that scaling, raw diameter carries little mean-WSS information. Local deviation from the expected diameter carries more. The derivation is not validated against CFD ([Zhang et al., arXiv 2312.00257](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.00257) for the flow law). Absolute lumen size is also strongly confounded. In 201 CT subjects without atherosclerosis, lumen areas depended on sex and dominance ([Am J Cardiol 2018](https://em-consulte.com/article/1208261/article/measurements-of-lumen-areas-and-diameters-of-proxi)). Coronary volume tracked LV mass independently of BSA and sex ([Danish CT study, KU](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/volume-and-dimensions-of-angiographically-normal-coronary-arterie)).

The two questions therefore treat lumen size differently. In the where model, person-level confounders cancel within the stratum, but a segment's absolute area is tied to its position and dominance × vessel, which the base already holds, so little is left to test. In the who model, segment lumen size could carry person-level information that SCORE2 lacks, but it also re-enters sex and body size, which makes any likelihood gain hard to read as geometry. If it is used, adjust it for BSA and sex in the model (`clin_bsa` and `male` exist in BL), and treat it as exploratory. V/M, the version with outcome data in the earlier reports, needs LV myocardium segmentation that the tracer does not provide, and its evidence is cross-sectional and concerns ischaemia, not incident plaque ([Leiden thesis on V/M](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3640299/download)).

Deep-learning WSS surrogates do not change this ranking. They take the full centerline and radius, or point clouds, as input and report overall error, not the importance of named descriptors. Several were trained largely on **synthetic** geometries: 3,302 of 3,429 cases in one ([Alamir et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11345599/)), and 1,800 idealised LM bifurcations in another ([Sarkhosh et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11920710/)). They show that tracer-type output contains what generic-boundary CFD uses. They do not show that a few scalar segment summaries keep that information.

## Most other candidates fail on noise, feasibility or redundancy

The rejections rest on specific findings. Bifurcation angles of side branches had only a minor effect next to curvature in **idealised** LAD/D1 models, read at abstract level ([Chiastra et al. 2017, PoliMi](https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1029992)), and a review table notes that patient-specific models did not confirm the angle–WSS correlation ([PMC9457427](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9457427/table/jcm-11-05143-t002)). Myocardial bridging is contradictory. Moving-boundary CFD found lower proximal LAD WSS with a bridge, but only abstracts were read ([Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/abnormal-shear-stress-and-residence-time-are-associated-with-prox/)). A static CCTA CFD study of 36 bridges found no proximal WSS difference (0.94 vs 1.06 Pa, p = 0.56) and no WSS difference between arteries with and without plaque ([Giannopoulos et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870693/)). A single-phase CCTA lumen cannot capture systolic compression, and detecting a bridge needs the myocardium. On eccentricity, a circular cross-section gave adequate WSS accuracy in three human RCAs with mean radius ratio about 1.13 ([PMC1513234](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1513234/)). On a plaque-free CCTA contour, ovality would mostly measure segmentation and partial-volume noise. Helicity and torsion have the most interesting protective hypothesis, but the evidence is in **swine**, read from snippets ([De Nisco et al., PoliTo](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2726967)), and curvature and torsion estimates depend strongly on centerline resolution and smoothing ([BioMed Eng Online 2021](https://0-biomedical--engineering--online-biomedcentral-com.brum.beds.ac.uk/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12938-021-00957-6.pdf)). The WSS topological skeleton index is associated with wall-thickness growth ([Mazzi et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10439-021-02829-5)), but it is a CFD output, not a geometric descriptor. No source maps a centerline feature onto it.

Reliability evidence is thin for every candidate. No coronary-CCTA test–retest or interobserver ICC was found for curvature, torsion, tortuosity index, eccentricity, side-branch counts or V/M. The nearest analogue is carotid CTA tortuosity in 12 patients, with interobserver ICC 0.72 to 0.99 ([de Vries et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6348067/)). For the tracer, the Medis comparison gives only an indirect agreement estimate, on the LAD, LCx and RCA, in people with plaque at baseline. It cannot establish reliability in the plaque-free cohort. The paired baseline and follow-up scans of trunks that remained plaque-free give a scan–rescan estimate within CGPS. Measure any secondary feature that way before relying on it.

## Every added degree of freedom taxes the single test

The person-level budget is not what binds. With 1,077 plaque-free participants of whom 483 (44.8%) developed plaque, these counts are taken before the image-quality exclusion and must be recomputed on the final cohort (`thesis/results/00_cohort.tex`). Harrell's limiting sample size m = 3nq(1−q) gives about 800, or roughly 53 candidate parameters at m/15 ([Harrell, RMS ch. 4](https://hbiostat.org/rmsc/multivar)). What binds is the power of the one prespecified LR test. In a noncentral χ² approximation at α = 0.05, a 5-df block with 80% power falls to about **74% with two added null df and 68% with five**. Each added df must bring about 6% of the block's whole signal, independent of the existing features, just to break even. This is the researchers' own calculation, a large-sample approximation not checked in the cloglog GLMM or the conditional model. Correlated additions bring less than their df cost. Noisy ones bring less still, because measurement error shrinks the noncentrality roughly with the squared attenuated effect. Harrell's guidance points the same way. Decide the df first, test the prespecified group with one P-value, prefer data reduction masked to the outcome over selection, and never feed results back into the primary set ([Harrell, RMS ch. 4](https://hbiostat.org/rmsc/multivar)). Penalisation does not remove the cost, because its tuning is least reliable exactly where samples are small ([Riley et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8026952/)).

That fixes the structure. The primary block stays as decided: five features, one LR test per question. A secondary analysis, written down before any outcome is seen and labelled exploratory, adds the **taper–branch mismatch**, if side-branch diameters prove usable, as a replacement-definition sensitivity analysis of taper in both models. For the who question it can add **BSA- and sex-adjusted segment lumen size**. Each is reported as a coefficient with a confidence interval and the change in log-likelihood, not as a new confirmatory claim. Before unblinding, run a redundancy check masked to the outcome, such as variable clustering of the baseline features, and the scan–rescan check on non-event trunks. Drop any candidate that correlates strongly with curvature, taper or the LM ratios, or that fails to reproduce. The project notes say tracer geometry for about 1,200 plaque-free people is coming, so this feature list has to be frozen before the outcome is linked, together with the methods.

## Conclusion

The search for more low-WSS features mostly reaffirms the current block. The two descriptors with the strongest computational WSS support, diameter ratios at the LM and curvature, are already in it, and taper is a crude form of the third, branch flow loss. What the literature adds is a sharper reading of taper, not a new axis. The physically meaningful signal is taper relative to the flow a segment loses, and the thesis can measure it because the tracer already locates side branches. Whether their diameters are reliable enough is the open practical question, and it should be settled before any outcome is linked.

Two consequences follow for interpretation. A null result for the block would not refute the WSS hypothesis, because segment summaries discard the circumferential inner-wall signal, single-phase CCTA loses the oscillatory component, and raw taper dilutes the branch-flow effect, and each of these biases toward the null. A positive result would be the first evidence in a general-population, plaque-free cohort. The relevant human studies are radiomics and clinical models, and in the closest design anatomy alone reached an external AUC of 0.56. Against that background, a modest, well-prespecified increment from five interpretable features is a more credible result than a larger increment from a block widened after looking at the literature.
