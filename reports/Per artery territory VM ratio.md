# Territory V/M is feasible but unproven for plaque prediction

The proposed feature is buildable with open tools and has a real basis. Define it per artery as V = lumen volume of the trunk plus all branches it gives off, pruned to a fixed minimum diameter, and M = 1.05 g/mL × the volume of the LV myocardium voxels whose nearest coronary lumen voxel carries that artery's label. Voronoi territory mass has been checked against swine histology, SPECT and MR perfusion, and it has a 948-patient reference study. **Two claims in the original proposal are wrong.** Voronoi is not "the standard method in the V/M literature": that literature reports one whole-heart ratio per patient. The few territory-level V/M studies used three different ways to get mass, and only one of them (Choi 2020) used Voronoi. Voronoi also does not "handle dominance automatically". It shows dominance only if the tree has already been labelled correctly, and in left-dominant hearts the RCA often supplies no LV myocardium at all, so the RCA ratio becomes undefined. The bigger problem is the evidence. **No published study, global or territorial, has related baseline V/M to future plaque.** The hypothesis is new, and the direction of the effect cannot be predicted in advance. With 4, 10 and 9 parameters available for LAD, CX and RCA, the feature can only enter as one pre-specified term per model, tested two-sided, and treated as exploratory.

## The V/M literature is a per-patient ratio, not a territory method

Taylor and colleagues introduced V/M in 2017 in an NXT-trial substudy (238 patients, 438 vessels). They defined it as **total epicardial coronary lumen volume (mm³) divided by LV myocardial mass (g)**. Low V/M was associated with lower invasive FFR independently of stenosis and plaque (median 18.57 mm³/g; FFR 0.80 vs 0.87 below vs above the median) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28789941)). Mass is LV myocardial volume × **1.05 g/mL**. Only the LV is used, the RV is excluded, and the lumen is traced down to vessels of about 1 mm ([Ihdayhid 2022 review](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3567756/view)). The same review states plainly that "the reporting of V/M remains limited to being a per-patient measure". It names "the lack of validated CT techniques to quantify fractional myocardial mass in relation to patient-specific coronary anatomy" as the obstacle, and lists regional V/M as future work ([Ihdayhid 2022](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3567756/view)). The large cohorts that define typical values are all global and all from HeartFlow: ADVANCE (n=3,110) reports mean V 3,003 mm³, M 122 g and V/M 25.2 mm³/g, with a range of 5.6–62.5 ([Ihdayhid 2022](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3567756/view)).

Only three groups have computed a ratio that resembles the thesis feature, and each derived territory mass differently:

| Study | Territory mass method | Main finding | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liu 2021, 205 pts / 241 stenosed vessels | Allometric estimate from vessel diameter, no spatial map | Vessel-specific VR/MR AUC 0.89 vs global V/M 0.62 for FFR ≤0.80 | Mass is derived from the same vessel as V, so the ratio is partly circular; the D–M exponent used conflicts with Kassab theory ([PMC9597209](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9597209)) |
| Sun 2025, 23 cases vs 25 controls | Fixed AHA 17-segment template | LAD and LCX V/M lower in microvascular angina; RCA not | "Some outliers, especially in some right dominant cases, were excluded" ([PMC12302837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12302837/)) |
| Choi 2020, 43 pts / 638 segments | 3D Voronoi (Fujifilm Synapse Vincent) | V scales with M at an exponent of about 1.03–1.08; exponents differ by artery | Allometry study, not a biomarker study ([PMC7387886](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)) |

So the proposal's claim needs restating. **Voronoi is the most widely used and best-validated method for vessel-specific myocardial mass and area at risk. It is not the standard in the V/M literature, which has no territory standard.** Choosing it is still the right call. It is the only one of the three methods that follows each patient's anatomy and is spatially explicit. The AHA template misassigns segments in most patients: in a hybrid PET/CT study, **72% of 71 patients had at least one reassigned segment** (about 9% of all segments), and most reassignments were RCA→LCx or LCx→LAD ([Javadi 2010, J Nucl Med](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/51/2/198)). Those errors fall on exactly the boundary that separates the CX and RCA denominators.

## Voronoi validation is real but small, and none of it is longitudinal

The method gives each LV voxel to the nearest coronary lumen voxel and then sums, for each vessel, the voxels assigned to the tree distal to a seed. Kurata and colleagues introduced it for area at risk. In 34 patients, CT area at risk correlated with Tl-201 SPECT at **r = 0.49–0.81**, depending on the SPECT score used ([Kurata 2015, Eur Radiol](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00330-014-3388-2)). Ide and colleagues reported that Voronoi mass was "substantially correlated" with ex vivo vessel-specific mass in swine hearts. The coefficients were not retrievable; the finding is as cited in [Stalikas 2025](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/id/eprint/118057/2/stalikas-et-al-2025-vessel-specific-myocardial-mass-in-patients-with-stable-coronary-artery-disease.pdf). A pig study found that **direct 3D Voronoi was more accurate than projecting the arteries onto the epicardial surface first**. The same paper noted that Voronoi segmentation "has been considered controversial due to lack of validation" ([Informatics in Medicine Unlocked 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352914819304253)). Keulards 2020 is often cited as validation: CT territorial mass agreed with invasive thermodilution perfusion in 35 patients (ICC 0.90; mean differences LAD 5.3%, LCx −2.0%, RCA −3.2%) ([Heart](https://heart.bmj.com/content/106/19/1489)). However, Stalikas cites Keulards as the *allometric, diameter-derived* approach rather than Voronoi ([Stalikas 2025](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/id/eprint/118057/2/stalikas-et-al-2025-vessel-specific-myocardial-mass-in-patients-with-stable-coronary-artery-disease.pdf)), so the thesis should not count it as Voronoi validation unless the full text confirms it.

The strongest practical anchor is **Stalikas et al. (JAHA 2025, n=948)**. The paper documents the reference implementation in enough detail to copy. It uses **all side branches >1.5 mm**, seeds at the LAD, LCx and RCA ostia, gives LM no territory of its own, treats the ramus intermedius as a separate branch (present in 26%), segments septal perforators, and defines dominance by PDA origin. It reports median LV mass 141 g with territory fractions **LAD 42.5%, LCx 28.8%, RCA 26.4%**. Septal branches alone supplied 10.2% of LV mass ([Stalikas 2025](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/id/eprint/118057/2/stalikas-et-al-2025-vessel-specific-myocardial-mass-in-patients-with-stable-coronary-artery-disease.pdf)). These fractions are the obvious plausibility check for a CGPS pipeline. Every validation cohort is small (n ≈ 6–35) or cross-sectional. **No inter-observer or scan–rescan reproducibility of Voronoi territory mass has been published.** The only reproducibility figure for V/M of any kind is a modest intra-/inter-observer ICC of **0.78/0.76** for global V/M from a semi-manual workstation ([Ding 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2025.1449148/full)).

## Dominance is encoded in labelling, not solved by Voronoi

The geometry does show dominance if the tree is labelled correctly: a dominant LCx that runs down the inferior wall will be nearest to the inferior myocardium. But Voronoi only returns "nearest lumen voxel". The *artery label* on that voxel comes from the branch-labelling step. **If a PDA from a dominant LCx is labelled as RCA, Voronoi gives the inferior wall to the wrong denominator**, and the ratio does nothing to correct it. Stalikas had to state an explicit rule: in left dominance, "the mass supplied by the PDA included the most distal side branch of the dominant LCX". Left dominance occurred in 11%. In those hearts LCx mass rose to **47.5%** of the LV (vs 27.7% in right dominance), and **the RCA supplied no LV mass at all in 77 of 103 left-dominant patients** ([Stalikas 2025](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/id/eprint/118057/2/stalikas-et-al-2025-vessel-specific-myocardial-mass-in-patients-with-stable-coronary-artery-disease.pdf)). The proposal's wording "RCA+PDA/PLA" is therefore wrong for about one in ten people. The numerator has to be defined as "the trunk plus whatever branches arise from it, PDA/PLB included wherever they arise". In left-dominant patients the RCA ratio is near 0/0 and should be excluded from the RCA model or handled with a pre-specified minimum territory mass. Both CGPS files contain `ct_cag_dominance`, which can be used to check the CT-derived labelling. Disagreement between the two should be treated as a labelling error, not as biology.

There is also a second blind spot in the same direction. **The LV-only convention understates RCA supply** because the RV free wall is excluded ([Stalikas 2025](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/id/eprint/118057/2/stalikas-et-al-2025-vessel-specific-myocardial-mass-in-patients-with-stable-coronary-artery-disease.pdf)). Human data also show that the RCA has less lumen volume per gram of territory than the left arteries, and that the scaling exponents differ significantly between LAD, LCX and RCA ([Choi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)). Both points support separate per-artery models and rule out comparing absolute V/M values across arteries.

## A concrete definition the pipeline can compute

The recommended specification below follows Stalikas and the V/M convention. The choices marked "thesis rule" are not established in the literature; they are decisions to fix in advance and state.

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Lumen mask | In-house ImageCAS-trained model, at the same reconstruction phase as the myocardium (best diastolic) |
| Branch labels | LAD tree = LAD trunk + diagonals + septals (if segmented); CX tree = CX trunk + OMs; RCA tree = RCA trunk + its branches; PDA/PLB go to whichever trunk they arise from. LM is excluded from both V and M; ramus is assigned by a fixed rule to the same artery in V and M (thesis rule) |
| Distal cutoff | Prune centreline points below 1.5 mm diameter (matches Stalikas); sensitivity analyses at 1.0 and 2.0 mm ([1 mm is the V/M convention](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3567756/view)) |
| V_artery | Volume of the pruned, labelled lumen (mesh or voxel), in mm³ |
| LV myocardium | TotalSegmentator `heartchambers_highres` (free non-commercial licence, runs locally) ([GitHub](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator)); state how papillary muscles are handled |
| Territory | 3D Euclidean distance transform on the labelled lumen mask; each myocardial voxel takes the label of its nearest lumen voxel. A geodesic variant restricted to the myocardium is a sensitivity analysis that stops the distance from shortcutting across the cavity |
| M_territory | Assigned voxel volume × 1.05 g/mL |
| Feature entered in model | log(V/M), or better, the per-artery residual of log V on log M (thesis rule; the ratio forces the exponent to 1) |

The Voronoi step itself is a few lines of code: `scipy.ndimage.distance_transform_edt` with `return_indices=True`. **No maintained open-source territory tool exists.** The validated implementation is commercial (Synapse Vincent), and VMTK and Slicer provide only the building blocks ([Stalikas 2025](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/id/eprint/118057/2/stalikas-et-al-2025-vessel-specific-myocardial-mass-in-patients-with-stable-coronary-artery-disease.pdf); [VMTK](https://github.com/vmtk/vmtk/blob/master/doc/joss-paper/paper.md)). The hard part is branch labelling. Automated labelling is 87–100% accurate for LAD, RCA and LM but weaker for LCx and side branches ([PMC10626726](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10626726/)). The ImageCAS-X segment labels (CC BY 4.0) are the natural training set for a labeller ([arXiv 2608.30404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30404)).

Three sources of error deserve their own checks. First, **uneven distal truncation moves boundary myocardium toward whichever artery is segmented further**. In an early implementation, apical over-segmentation inflated the LAD territory ([Termeer poster](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2009/termeer-2009-scmr/termeer-2009-scmr-poster.pdf)), and no study has quantified this effect. A common diameter cutoff limits it, and the cutoff sensitivity analysis measures it. Second, nitroglycerin changes V substantially. In 200 Copenhagen volunteers, coronary volume was **4.33 mL with nitroglycerin vs 2.55 mL without** ([Sørensen 2017](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/volume-and-dimensions-of-angiographically-normal-coronary-arterie)). CGPS records `scandata_nitro` only at follow-up, so the baseline protocol has to be assumed or recovered. This matters less for a baseline-only predictor, provided the baseline protocol was uniform. Third, CT LV mass agrees with MRI on average, but the limits of agreement are **about ±28 g** ([Kaniewska 2017 meta-analysis](https://www.springermedicine.com/noninvasive-evaluation-of-global-and-regional-left-ventricular-f/20864202)). CGPS-specific LV-mass reference values from 569 healthy participants give a cohort-matched plausibility check ([Fuchs 2016](https://research.regionh.dk/da/publications/normal-values-of-left-ventricular-mass-and-cardiac-chamber-volume/)). The territory fractions should also be compared with Stalikas's 42/29/26% split, stratified by `ct_cag_dominance`.

## The predictor is coupled by construction and its effect direction is unknown

Two properties limit what V/M can show. The first is coupling by construction. Under Voronoi, an artery with a longer and larger tree claims more myocardium, so V and M rise together; Choi found a V–M exponent of about 1 ([Choi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)). The ratio therefore varies less than either part, which is useful for size normalisation but leaves less independent signal. The same coupling probably cancels part of the segmentation-depth error, since a missed distal branch lowers both V and M. That is an inference, and the cutoff sensitivity analysis should test it. Global V/M also partly tracks LV mass. Women's higher V/M (26.2 vs 24.8 mm³/g) is driven by lower mass. Diabetics have lower V/M through lower volume (23.9 vs 25.7). After adjustment, V/M does not differ in hypertension because volume grows in step with mass ([Ihdayhid 2022](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3567756/view); [van Rosendael 2023](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3759937/download)). All of these covariates (`male`, diabetes history and medication, HbA1c, `clin_bsa`) are available in both CGPS visits. Several already enter through SCORE2, so V/M has to show added value beyond them.

The second property is that the mechanism points both ways. Under allometric flow scaling (flow ∝ M^¾, the assumption behind FFR-CT), low V/M means more flow per unit lumen and higher wall shear stress, which would argue *against* new plaque. Low V/M also tracks diabetes and microvascular dysfunction, which would argue *for* it ([Ihdayhid 2022](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3567756/view)). Every cross-sectional association, including more plaque, more stenosis and lower FFR at low V/M, is confounded by existing plaque narrowing the lumen. In serial CCTA, each 1 mm³ of new plaque removed about 0.29 mm³ of lumen ([Leiden repository](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3638655/view)). The plaque-free-trunk design removes most of this reverse causation from the trunk. It does not remove it from the *branches* included in V, so baseline branch plaque (`ct_cag_<segment>_plaque_type` for d1/d2/om1/om2/pda/pla at BL) should be an exclusion criterion or a covariate.

The parameter budgets set the final form. With about 4 parameters for LAD, the model cannot afford V and M as separate terms, sex interactions, or splines on V/M. **Territory V/M has to enter as one linear term on the log scale, chosen before the outcome is seen**, or be folded into the compressed geometry score that the thesis design already plans. CX (~10) and RCA (~9) have room for one further term, such as separate log V and log M or a dominance indicator, but not much more. Measurement error makes this worse: a reliability around 0.77 attenuates the coefficient roughly in proportion, and the thesis should estimate its own reliability, for example with ±1-voxel perturbations and cutoff shifts. Predictor measurement that differs across settings also degrades calibration, with observed calibration slopes of 0.50–1.67 ([Luijken 2020](https://researchexchange.ocrahope.org/papers/j-jclinepi-2019-11-001)).

## Conclusion

Territory V/M is a sound and feasible feature in a narrow sense. It can be computed reproducibly from tools the thesis already has, it rests on validated ideas (V ∝ M allometry, Voronoi vessel-specific mass), and per-artery modelling is the correct frame for it. It is not an established predictor of anything longitudinal. The thesis would be the first to test whether baseline territory lumen supply relative to demand predicts new trunk plaque, and both a positive and a null result would be informative. The proposal should recast its claims accordingly. Voronoi should be presented as the best-validated *vessel-specific mass* method that the thesis adapts to the V/M concept, not as the V/M standard. Dominance should be described as something handled by explicit branch-to-trunk labelling rules checked against `ct_cag_dominance`, with left-dominant RCAs excluded.

The most useful contribution may be methodological rather than predictive. No one has published how deep-learning lumen truncation propagates into Voronoi territory mass, or any reproducibility figures for territory V/M. A pre-specified sensitivity analysis on CGPS (1.0/1.5/2.0 mm cutoffs, Euclidean vs geodesic distance, territory fractions against Stalikas by dominance) would fill a documented gap, whatever the plaque models show.
