# Sex and anatomy, not risk factors, validate geometry

The best external checks for all four features are **sex and anatomical variants (dominance, ramus intermedius)**, not cardiovascular risk factors. Your extraction is grouping people correctly if, in the plaque-free BL scans, women have **higher proximal LM/LAD/LCx curvature** (about +0.06–0.07 mm⁻¹), men have a **wider LM bifurcation angle B** (about +10°), the angle is **about 14° wider when a ramus intermedius is present**, LCx-based diameter ratios **fall in left-dominant hearts**, and **ratios and fractional taper barely change with sex or body size**, while absolute diameters do. Hypertension is a weaker second check for curvature only. Age, BMI and diabetes are supporting checks at best. Lipids, smoking and family history are null in the curvature literature and work as negative controls. Two features need richer extraction before they can be trusted. The LM angle needs ramus/trifurcation detection and a fixed fitting depth: a ramus mislabelled as LCx moves the angle more than sex does. Taper needs a robust log-diameter slope that skips the branch ostia, not an endpoint ratio. Curvature and the diameter ratios are well specified as they stand, provided the ratios are sampled outside the bifurcation flare. Clustering is defensible only as a descriptive add-on. No study has shown that discrete coronary shape types exist, the largest coronary geometry clustering study has 76 patients, and in diabetes, clusters built from continuous traits predicted worse than the traits themselves. Cardiac phase and BL nitroglycerin are not recorded in the BL headers. They are the largest unmeasured sources of error for curvature, angle and diameter.

## How to read the checks below

Each feature section ends with a ranked table. "Strong" means replicated across independent cohorts, "moderate" means mostly replicated with some nulls, and "weak" means a single study or a disease-enriched sample. Figures marked **[secondary]** come from a search summary, review table or secondary citation, not from the primary paper. Every check should be run first on the clean cohort restricted to scans with `ct_cag_visual_read` set. Unread BL segments are filled with "none", so an unread scan can look plaque-free without being so. Report each check twice: unadjusted, and adjusted for `male`, `scan_age` and `clin_bsa`. That separates an extraction artefact from confounding. Most of the literature comes from referral or chest-pain cohorts, so expect the directions to carry over to the Danish general population and the absolute values to shift.

The CGPS columns used throughout are `male`, `scan_age`, `clin_height`, `clin_weight`, `clin_bmi`, `clin_bsa`, `clin_bp_sys_mean` and `clin_bp_dia_mean`, `preparation_heartrate` (BL and FU) and `hr_during_scan` (only 268 BL values). Also used are `med_antihypertensive_any`, `demo_hypertension_deriv` (**FU only**), `diabetes`, `hba1c`, `ldl`, `hdl`, `tot_cholesterol`, `hist_smoking_current`, `egfr`, `ct_cag_dominance`, `ct_cacs_lm/lad/cx/rca/sum`, `score2`, `scan_qualitative_quality` and `bad_quality` (BL), and `scandata_nitro` (**FU only**). No LV mass, echo or reconstruction-phase variable exists in the tables. LV mass could be derived from the CT volumes. Phase must come from the DICOM/NIfTI metadata, if it is stored there at all.

## Curvature: women are the reference group, and your chord metric is the right family

**Female sex is the most replicated correlate of coronary tortuosity.** It holds across invasive angiography, CCTA, visual bend counts and continuous curvature. In 1,010 angiography patients, female sex gave an adjusted OR of 2.60 for visual tortuosity ([Li 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024232)). In 1,280 CCTA patients the OR was 1.68 ([Chen 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12893-022-01470-w)). Continuous ML-derived RCA tortuosity in 22,334 patients was 0.17 SD higher in women ([JACC Adv 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). The closest analogue to your pipeline is a 127-patient CCTA cohort with no stenosis and zero calcium, measuring mean centreline curvature within 10 mm of the LM bifurcation. There, women exceeded men by **+0.062 mm⁻¹ (LM), +0.063 mm⁻¹ (LAD) and +0.072 mm⁻¹ (LCx)**, all p<0.001 after Bonferroni correction, with coefficients of variation of about 24–26% ([Gharleghi 2024, arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12504)). That paper's table means could not be extracted, so the effect cannot be stated in SD units. The researchers' estimate of roughly 0.5–1 SD is an inference. Even at a fraction of that size, ~1,200 clean scans give ample power, so **a null or reversed sex effect in CGPS is a red flag** for the extraction.

**Hypertension is positive but small.** Adjusted ORs are 1.35–1.53 for binary visual tortuosity ([Li 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024232); [Chen 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12893-022-01470-w)), and the continuous effect is β = 0.06 SD ([JACC Adv 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). A Danish CATCH-trial CCTA arc/chord study also found it, at adjusted p<0.001 ([Sharfo 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39210716/)). Two studies were null ([Groves 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19585899/); [Gharleghi 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12504)). Every study used a binary diagnosis, so no benchmark exists for continuous BP. At BL in CGPS the best proxy is `med_antihypertensive_any`, with `clin_bp_sys_mean` as a continuous secondary. `demo_hypertension_deriv` exists only at FU and describes a person ten years after the scan. **BMI is inverse** in the one study that tested it (OR 0.46 for BMI >28 vs <24) ([Chen 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12893-022-01470-w)). Diabetes is inverse in the 22k cohort (β −0.11 SD) and null elsewhere ([JACC Adv 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). Age is weakly positive (OR 1.02 per year in [Chen 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12893-022-01470-w)) or null after adjustment ([JACC Adv 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). Hyperlipidaemia, smoking and family history were null across studies ([Groves 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19585899/)). That makes them **negative controls**: a curvature metric that strongly separates people by LDL or smoking is suspect.

Two things to settle before reading the sex effect. First, the sex effect partly reflects scale. Women have smaller hearts and vessels, and an artery wrapped around a smaller surface curves more. Run the sex contrast with and without `clin_bsa` or height adjustment. This is a research inference, not something any study has tested. Second, **plaque vs no plaque is not a validation check**. Visual bend counts lean inverse to CAD (OR 0.755 in [Li 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024232)). Continuous curvature leans weakly positive (OR 1.05 per SD in [JACC Adv 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)), and arc/chord in the same 22k data was weakly inverse. Both directions are also confounded by sex.

**Your metric choice is supported.** Chord-based turning angle per mm is a version of mean absolute curvature. In 127 CCTA scans without CAD, mean absolute curvature had the highest R² with low time-averaged wall shear stress. The arc/chord tortuosity index, which is Bullitt's distance metric (DM), correlated worst ([Kashyap 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-04796-w)). Bullitt's inflection-count metric was best overall on cerebral MRA, and the sum-of-angles metric (SOAM) was best for tight coils ([Bullitt 2003](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2003.816964)). Expect only a moderate correlation between T5 and DM. A weak correlation is not a bug, and SOAM and ICM should track visual "≥3 bends of ≥45°" definitions more closely than mean curvature does.

**Phase is the main threat.** In biplane cineangiography, LAD curvature changed by **0.025–0.18 mm⁻¹ within one cardiac cycle**, depending on the site ([Gross 1996](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/684552)). That is as large as the sex difference. This is also why the clinical definitions require a bend in both systole and diastole ([Li 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024232)). No multi-patient CCTA study of curvature by phase exists. No study reports normal curvature by SCCT/AHA segment either, so CGPS clean scans would provide the first per-segment reference.

## Taper: a slope on log diameter, checked against vessel order rather than risk factors

**No published CCTA study gives normal per-segment taper in a general population**, so the plaque-free CGPS scans would be the reference. Existing taper values use incompatible definitions. The normal CTA atlas reports the LAD tapering about **0.25 mm per 10 mm** beyond its bifurcations ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). Plaque-free IVUS shows about 30% less lumen area from proximal to mid LAD, about 16% less diameter ([Ge 1994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1025457/)). Angiographic tapering indices of about 18% (proximal LAD), 30% (distal LAD) and about 8–9% (RCA) **[secondary]** come only from a summary of Zubaid 2002 ([ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11090643_Normal_angiographic_tapering_of_the_coronary_arteries)). A 2026 angiographic study gives LAD about 12% proximal and 42% distal, LCx about 15% and 35%, with higher proximal LCx taper in women **[secondary, abstract via search]** ([Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44200-026-00138-7)). Diameter scales with the length of the tree downstream: on CCTA, proximal diameter vs cumulative length has exponent 0.277 ± 0.075 in 43 patients ([Choi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)).

The useful checks are therefore **anatomical**. The LAD gives off diagonals and septals and should taper more per unit length than the LCx, which should taper at least as much as the RCA. If the tracer does not reproduce LAD > LCx ≥ RCA, the extraction is suspect. **No study tests taper against BSA, sex, age, hypertension or diabetes on CCTA.** The literature covers absolute diameter, which is larger in men and with body size. Women's diameters are 9% smaller even after BSA normalisation ([Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)). The proximal RCA is 3.9 ± 0.6 vs 2.8 ± 0.5 mm by dominance, while the LAD is unaffected ([Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)). LVH enlarges diameters by about 17% ([Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)). If diameter scales multiplicatively with body size along the whole vessel, then **fractional taper (Δlog D per cm) should be nearly independent of sex and BSA**, while absolute taper (mm/cm) should be larger in men. That contrast is a clean internal check. A strong BSA effect on fractional taper points to a resolution artefact.

The pitfalls are concrete. Diameter steps down at each side branch, so endpoint taper (Dprox − Ddist)/Dprox depends on where the segment ends. Partial volume makes 2–3 mm distal vessels, only 4–8 voxels across, look relatively larger and flattens distal taper. Calcium blooming and remodelling create local bulges or waists. Glagov remodelling preserves the lumen until plaque occupies about 40% of the vessel area ([Glagov 1987 via reference listing](https://insight.jci.org/references/scholar/6633/B1)). Diffuse CAD leaves lumen area 30–50% below normal for the myocardial bed it supplies ([Seiler 1992 via review](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomy-and-function-relation-in-the-coronary-tree-from-bifurcations-to-myocardial-flow-and-mass)). Nitroglycerin raises CCTA diameters by **8–30%, more distally than proximally** **[secondary, review via search]** ([Eur Radiol 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00330-015-3791-3)), which flattens taper. Without vs with nitrate, the LAD measures 3.0 vs 3.5 mm ([Thieme 2006](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-2006-926755.pdf)). BL nitro is not in the headers, and FU has `scandata_nitro`. **Any BL–FU taper comparison is confounded by nitro**, and a BL-only analysis assumes a uniform protocol that cannot be verified. Lumen area also varies 8–10% over the cardiac cycle ([Ge 1994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1025457/)).

**Richer extraction is needed here.** Replace endpoint taper with a robust (Theil–Sen or Huber) slope of log area-equivalent diameter, sqrt(4A/π) from each contour, against arc length. Fit it per segment, excluding steps within a few mm of side-branch ostia and the bifurcation flare. Report it as %/cm. Area-equivalent diameter is less noise-sensitive than minimum or maximum diameter.

## LM bifurcation angle: sex is the check, and the ramus is the main threat

On CCTA in people without stenosis, angle B (LAD–LCx) averages **about 72–80° with an SD of 20–23°**. Angle A (LM–LCx) is 126–143° and angle C (LM–LAD) 121–139° in the "proximal" convention, pooled across CCTA studies with more than 200 patients ([scoping review, PMC9385039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)). The 300-patient zero-calcium atlas used 3D centrelines at 75% R-R, averaging branch directions over 5–10 mm depth. It reports B = 78.9 ± 23.1°, A = 126.1 ± 21.1° and C = 138.6 ± 18.9° **[secondary, values tabulated by the scoping review]** ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). Some papers report LM–branch angles in the flow convention, 180° minus the proximal value. A CKD study gives LM–LAD as 35.9 ± 11.4° that way ([PLoS One 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0198566)). A tracer mean far outside 65–95° for B more likely reflects the convention or fitting depth than the population.

**Male sex widens angle B by about 10°.** In the atlas, men averaged 85° vs 74° for women (p<0.001), and the difference survived height adjustment (p=0.002) ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). Male sex gave a risk ratio of 2.07 for B >80° in 196 CCTA patients **[secondary, via scoping review]** ([Temov & Sun via PMC9385039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)). **An intermediate artery widens B even more: 89 ± 21° vs 75 ± 23°**, without changing the inflow angle or LM diameter. About a quarter of normal atlas patients had one ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). BMI >25 gave a risk ratio of 2.54 for B >80° in one univariable analysis **[secondary]** ([scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)). Age is essentially uncorrelated (r = 0.010) ([PMC9917625](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9917625/)). **No study reports the LM angle by dominance or heart size.** A CGPS null there can be neither confirmed nor refuted. The associations with hypertension, diabetes and LDL (OR 3.77 for B >80° with untreated LDL **[secondary]**, [scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9385039/)) come from disease-enriched groups and should not be used as checks. The plaque scans should show a 5–15° wider angle. Small cross-sectional studies find 10–18° differences ([Juan 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28473705/)), but a 2024 CAC study was null ([Eur J Anat 2024](https://eurjanat.com/articles/assessment-of-the-left-coronary-artery-bifurcation-angle-as-a-coronary-artery-disease-risk-factor-and-its-impact-on-coronary-artery-calcification/)), and part of the difference is the higher male share in the plaque group.

Phase moves the angle by a **mean absolute 6–7° per person**, with signed means of only ±1–3°: systole vs diastole in 44 dual-source CT scans ([Handran 2015](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/left-main-bifurcation-angle-and-changes-throughout-cardiac-cycle-quantitative-implications-left-main-bifurcation-stenting-and-stents)). Phase therefore adds noise, not bias. Semi-automatic 3D CT angles agree between observers to 1.8 ± 5.8° ([Casciaro 2009](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10558-009-9084-1)). In 3D QCA of LM disease, the proximal and distal angles correlate at r = −0.75 ([Girasis 2010](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/28713)). A tracer reporting A, B and C should reproduce a strong negative A–B correlation, which makes a cheap internal check, though the benchmark comes from a diseased cohort.

**Richer extraction is needed.** The tabular data cannot identify a ramus. `ct_cag_im_stenosis` is filled for all 1,801 patients and the plaque-related `im` columns only where there is plaque (35 BL). `ct_cag_im_eval` is also filled for all 1,801. Whether it encodes "segment present" should be checked on dtu-gpu-7 before relying on it. The tracer must detect a trifurcation and label the LCx correctly. **A ramus mislabelled as LCx distorts B by more than the sex effect**, so branch-labelling errors are the main way the angle could group people wrongly. Fix the convention (proximal, about 120–145° for A and C), the fitting depth (5–10 mm to match the atlas) and the bifurcation point. Then include ramus presence as a covariate or stratifier next to the angle.

## Diameter ratios: dominance moves them, sex and body size should not

On CTA in the normal atlas, area-equivalent diameters are **LM 3.5 ± 0.8, LAD 3.2 ± 0.7 and LCx 3.0 ± 0.7 mm**. Men are about 20% larger (LM 4.1 vs 3.3 mm). The Finet ratio Dm/(Dd1+Dd2) is **0.658 ± 0.083**, and the best-fitting diameter exponent is **α = 2.4**, close to Huo–Kassab's 7/3 ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). A meta-analysis pools the coronary flow–diameter exponent at 2.39 (95% CI 2.24–2.54), with I² = 99% **[secondary, abstract via search]** ([PubMed 38787386](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38787386/)). Finet's linear rule holds only for near-symmetric splits (Ds/Dl ≥ 0.75), while Huo–Kassab holds across all ratios ([Huo et al.](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/optimal-diameter-of-diseased-bifurcation-segment-a-practical-rule-for-percutaneous-coronary-intervention)). For the typical LAD/LCx pair, Murray, Finet and Huo–Kassab coefficients differ only between 0.634 and 0.678, so CGPS cannot discriminate between the laws and should use 7/3 as the expected value.

The expected ratios depend on where the diameters are sampled. From the atlas means (research arithmetic, ratio of means), **LM/LAD ≈ 1.09, LM/LCx ≈ 1.17 and LAD/LCx ≈ 1.07**, and these are nearly identical in men (1.11 / 1.14 / 1.03) and women (1.10 / 1.18 / 1.07). Applying the laws to near-carina diameters instead gives LM/LAD ≈ 1.22–1.31 and LM/LCx ≈ 1.30–1.40. Invasive QCA gives LM 4.5 ± 0.5 and proximal LAD 3.7 ± 0.4 mm, a ratio of about 1.22 ([Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)). The gap between 1.09 and 1.22–1.31 reflects measurement definition. The atlas LM figure averages the whole trunk, while the laws assume near-carina reference diameters. **Sampling location shifts the ratios more than the choice of law does.** The confluence region is eccentric (Dmax 5.2 vs Dmin 3.6 mm, eccentricity 1.4) ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)), so sampling inside the flare or mixing minimum with area-equivalent diameters biases the ratios. Finet used the first 5 mm of each daughter on IVUS ([Finet 2008](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/fractal-geometry-of-arterial-coronary-bifurcations-a-quantitative-coronary-angiography-and-intravascular-ultrasound-analysis)).

**Dominance is the one physiological correlate expected to move LCx-based ratios.** The LCx and RCA depend on dominance and the LAD does not ([Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)). So left dominance should lower LM/LCx and LAD/LCx. Left dominance occurs in about 8–10% of CT and angiography cohorts ([CCTA dominance, PMC13363422](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13363422/)). That is roughly 100–150 left-dominant people in CGPS, enough for a direction check. In the atlas, an intermediate artery made both LAD and LCx slightly smaller relative to the LM (women 2.7 / 2.6 vs 3.0 / 2.8 mm) ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)), as expected when flow splits three ways. The ratios should be roughly **sex- and size-invariant**. A strong sex or BSA effect on a ratio, as opposed to on a diameter, suggests partial-volume bias: small lumens lose relatively more to blur, which pushes ratios towards 1 in women and in the LCx. No source reports hypertension or diabetes effects on the ratios.

Plaque distorts the ratios in both directions. In Motreff's IVUS series, 10 of 38 angiographically normal LMs had diffuse disease, which shrank the LM lumen by about 1.2 mm without any visible stenosis ([Motreff 2010](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/diffuse-atherosclerotic-left-main-coronary-artery-disease-unmasked-by-fractal-geometric-law-applied-to-quantitative-coronary-angiography-an-angiographic-and-intravascular-ultrasound-study/pdf)). Deviation from Murray's law tracks dense calcium on IVUS ([Schoenenberger 2012](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/22261173)). The ~600 plaque scans should therefore show wider ratio scatter, which is a contrast, not a normal reference. Per-case prediction of LM diameter from the daughters has a precision of only about 0.8–1 mm for every law ([ABC Cardiol 2025](https://new.scielo.br/j/abc/a/x4td9RRjsfcNcF9kcHtjf9n/?lang=en)), so laws validate cohort distributions, not individual measurements.

Richer extraction is **modest**: fix the sampling windows (LM midshaft, proximal to the flare; 5–10 mm into each daughter, past the flare), use area-equivalent diameter from each contour, and take a median over each window rather than a single contour. Medis diameters exist only for diseased segments, so direct diameter agreement with Medis is limited to remodelled anatomy.

## Clustering: run it as a descriptive test of a continuum, not as the model

**Clustering geometry is defensible only as a secondary, descriptive analysis.** The coronary shape models built from plaque-free CCTA mostly capture size and LM angle, both of which differ by sex. In 446 bifurcations from 211 zero-calcium patients, shape was not clearly associated with hypertension, smoking or diabetes ([Medrano-Gracia 2017, abstract only](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12265-016-9720-2)). Your four scalar features therefore already span the main modes of variation. The only direct study of coronary geometry phenotypes clustered LM stem length, bifurcation angle, ostium angulation and branch thickness in **76 patients into four phenotypes**, and validated them only by CFD differences, with no outcome ([BioMed Eng OnLine 2024](https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-024-01205-3)). No coronary study has tested cluster existence against a continuous null. Even a well-defined anatomical grouping of internal carotid geometry was classified with **32.3% error** ([AneuRisk65 classifier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.08257)). In type 2 diabetes, simple continuous clinical features predicted progression and treatment response better than data-driven clusters built from them ([Dennis 2019](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/36310)). That outcome is the likely one here too. **Keep the continuous geometry block in the prespecified LR test**, and report any clusters next to it.

**Normalise before clustering, or the clusters will rediscover sex and body size.** Use dimensionless features wherever possible: diameter ratios, fractional taper (%/cm), angles, and curvature × local diameter or summed turning angle. Residualise any feature that stays in mm or 1/mm on `male` and `clin_bsa`. Sex differences in diameter and LM angle persist after BSA or height adjustment ([Dodge 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232); [Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)), so run the analysis both with and without sex residualisation and report both versions. Phase is the hardest problem. It moves LAD curvature by up to 0.18 mm⁻¹ ([Gross 1996](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/684552)) and the angle by about 7° ([Handran 2015](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/jic/articles/left-main-bifurcation-angle-and-changes-throughout-cardiac-cycle-quantitative-implications-left-main-bifurcation-stenting-and-stents)), and no phase variable exists in the tables. Check whether the NIfTI or DICOM metadata store the reconstruction phase. If they do not, include `preparation_heartrate` and `scan_qualitative_quality` as covariates, and confirm that the clusters do not split along them. Stratify or adjust LM-angle clusters for ramus presence and `ct_cag_dominance`. Build clusters on the clean, visually read cohort only. Plaque and remodelling distort diameters, so the plaque scans should be projected onto clusters fitted on clean scans, not pooled with them.

**Test whether the clusters exist, then whether they are stable.** First compare a one-component Gaussian mixture against k = 2…K by BIC. If k = 1 wins, report that there are no clusters. Then simulate same-sized datasets from a single fitted multivariate Gaussian (or Gaussian copula), rerun the clustering, and compare silhouette and stability. If the simulated continuum scores as well as the real data, the clusters only partition a continuum. The gap statistic, mclust BIC and silhouette are standard tools, but the research did not verify their primary sources. For stability, use Hennig's clusterwise bootstrap with B ≥ 100. Mean Jaccard ≥ 0.75 indicates a stable cluster, 0.6–0.75 a pattern with doubtful membership, and ≤ 0.5 a dissolved cluster ([fpc::clusterboot](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/fpc/html/clusterboot.html)). A secondary source attributes a >0.80 threshold to Hennig instead, and stability alone does not prove validity. Validate surviving clusters on variables not used to build them: sex, `scan_age`, `ct_cag_dominance`, ramus presence and `score2`. Only then, prespecified and descriptive, compare new-plaque rates and the cluster ΔC against the continuous block's ΔC within the same person-split CV. If you want richer shape descriptors than four scalars, the AneuRisk pipeline (landmark-register radius and curvature profiles, then functional PCA) runs directly on your per-step contours ([Sangalli 2009](https://sangalli.faculty.polimi.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2009_Sangalli-Secchi-Vantini-Veneziani-JASA_compresso.pdf)). Any fPCA components must be fitted within each fold, or fixed on the clean cohort beforehand, to keep the single LR test intact.

## Conclusion

The literature lets CGPS validate its extraction against anatomy rather than against disease, which matters for a thesis about predicting disease. Sex, dominance and the ramus make up a battery of known-groups checks that is independent of the outcome. Risk factors either have tiny effects (hypertension), no reliable direction, or are confounded by plaque. Two shortcomings in the extraction are more likely to group people wrongly than any choice of metric: an unlabelled ramus contaminating the LM angle, and endpoint taper picking up branch steps. Both have clear fixes. The literature is also thin in places, so the clean CGPS scans would supply the first general-population reference for per-segment curvature, per-segment taper and per-person LM ratio distributions. Report those distributions as a result in their own right.

The largest unresolved risk is unrecorded acquisition state. Phase and BL nitroglycerin each produce errors of the same order as the sex effects the validation relies on, and neither appears in the BL tables. Settle the scanner metadata (reconstruction phase, nitro protocol at BL) before the validation is run, because that determines whether a weak sex effect would indicate a bad tracer or just noisy scans. On clustering, the prior evidence suggests a continuum. A well-tested "no discrete types" result, with Jaccard and continuous-null checks, would be informative and fully compatible with the prespecified continuous design.
