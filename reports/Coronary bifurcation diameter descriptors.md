# Measure bifurcation calibre as ratios, outside the flare

**Measure calibre on each limb of the four named junctions (LM to LAD/LCx with a ramus flag, LAD-D1, LCx-OM1, RCA crux) as a median EDT radius over a 3 mm window that starts one junction-sphere radius (1·r_J) from the junction node. Summarise it as the Finet ratio at the left main, the daughter ratio at every junction, and a Huo-Kassab optimality ratio in place of the junction exponent. Treat the "during" zone as geometry to skip, not as a calibre to measure, until the repo has cross-sectional areas.** The case rests on three findings. First, the flow-diameter laws are not settled on CT: the pooled coronary exponent is 2.39, close to Huo-Kassab's 7/3 and well below Murray's 3, and normal-CTA studies disagree on which law fits. Second, the exponent is numerically ill-conditioned. Its error gain is n²/H, it is unsolvable in 28 % of ImageCAS-X LM junctions, and a 0.25 mm additive radius bias alone turns an HK-like 2.44 into a Murray-like 2.79. The Finet ratio and simple radius ratios have well-behaved first-order errors. Third, on 36 ImageCAS-X reference cases the EDT radius sits 23 % (LAD) and 43 % (LCx) above its downstream plateau at the junction and settles only after about 1.25·r_J and 2.25·r_J. Where the window starts therefore moves the ratios by up to two-thirds of a between-subject SD. Even so, the best descriptor, the LM Finet ratio, has a smallest detectable change of about one between-subject SD. It supports group-level inference only. The only coronary calibre study with five years of follow-up is a small symptomatic cohort, and no study has tested bifurcation calibre in a plaque-free tree against later events. The realistic prior therefore comes from retinal calibre: HR 1.05 to 1.20 per SD after adjustment. That is too small to detect with hard events in the plaque-free Herlev-Østerbro subset, so the outcome needs to be new plaque or a broader composite. Following the supervisor's split, ImageCAS-X fixes definitions, windows, estimator, error model and reference distributions. Outcome linkage happens only in Herlev-Østerbro, after those choices are committed and dated. Every ImageCAS-X number here comes from scripts that live only in a session scratchpad (`/tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/f80fe26a-7a3a-478e-9133-57e051f5b10c/scratchpad/`: `mc_propagation.py`, `phantom_radius.py`, `phantom_y.py`, `lm_profile.py`, with their `.txt` outputs). ## Four junctions, three zones, and a flare that reaches past 2 mm

Every coronary bifurcation standard divides the region into a parent segment, a core and two daughters. The European Bifurcation Club (EBC) model names a proximal main vessel (PMV), a distal main vessel (DMV) and a side branch (SB) around a central **polygon of confluence (POC)**. It adds **5 mm segments beyond the treated region and a 3 mm SB ostial segment**, and places the point of bifurcation at the centre of the largest circle touching all three contours ([EBC QCA consensus](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/quantitative-angiography-methods-for-bifurcation-lesions-a-consensus-statement-update-from-the-european-bifurcation-club)). Inside the POC, single-vessel diameter algorithms are replaced by fractal-law references, because raw diameters there are not representative ([EBC QCA consensus](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/quantitative-angiography-methods-for-bifurcation-lesions-a-consensus-statement-update-from-the-european-bifurcation-club)). CT and IVUS studies translate this into sections 5 mm proximal to the carina, at the carina and 5 mm distal, with the reference taken at the healthiest point within 10 mm ([Frontiers Cardiovasc Med 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2023.1292517/full)). Engineering practice scales distances by radius instead. VMTK takes daughter sections one maximum inscribed sphere beyond the bifurcation (`NumberOfDistanceSpheres` default 1) ([vmtkbifurcationsections source](https://github.com/vmtk/vmtk/blob/master/vmtkScripts/vmtkbifurcationsections.py)). The carotid standard measures the parent **three radii proximal** and the daughters **one sphere radius distal** to the bifurcation origin ([Bijari et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321819/)).

The repo's `topology/angles.py` already sits between these conventions. It skips a core of `core_scale = 1.0` times r_J, the EDT radius at the junction node, then takes the median radius over `window_mm = 3.0` on each limb, reading the parent backwards. The junction sphere r_J is the 3D analogue of the EBC's point-of-bifurcation circle, and the 3 mm window matches the EBC ostial segment. The phantom and real-data runs show this default is close to optimal, with one exception that matters. In a Y phantom built as a union of capsules, so with **no** anatomical flare, the EDT along a daughter axis still starts 24 to 53 % above the daughter's true radius. The inscribed sphere keeps touching the parent's far wall, and this geometric excess ends within 0.46 to 1.2·r_J [SIM] ([robustness note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Coronary%20bifurcation%20diameter%20descriptors/robustness_math_tests.md)). With a start at k ≥ 1·r_J, every tested geometry recovered the true Finet ratio to within 0.005 at a point-spread σ of 0.3 mm. Starting at the node with a 2 mm window was off by −0.065 to −0.093 [SIM].

The real LM junctions behave differently on the LCx. On 36 test-split reference masks (native resolution, delivered centerline, EDT exactly as `topology/radius.py`), the median r_J was 1.51 mm. The profile normalised to a 5 to 10 mm plateau looked like this [MEASURED] ([lm_profile.txt](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/f80fe26a-7a3a-478e-9133-57e051f5b10c/scratchpad/lm_profile.txt)).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LAD | 1.23 | 1.12 | 1.01 | 1.03 | 1.00 | 1.5 mm (0.9 to 3.0, 6.5) |

The LAD excess matches pure geometry, since the phantom gives 1.24 for a daughter of 0.8·r0. The LCx excess is larger and decays over about 2.25·r_J, against 0.5 to 1.2·r_J in the phantom. That is consistent with real ostial funnelling, an early OM or ramus take-off, or a taper-depressed plateau, and EDT alone cannot tell these apart. **The default window clears the median LAD flare but not the LCx flare in about half of cases**, which biases r_LCx/r_LM up and the Finet ratio down in that subset. Moving all three limbs to k = 2 shifted the Finet ratio by 0.023 and r_LCx/r_LM by 0.036 (median |Δ|) [MEASURED]. A daughter-only k = 2 with the parent at k = 1 is untested.

Three details need to be fixed in advance. Each daughter window must stop at the next branch's own junction sphere. If the segment is shorter than r_J + 3 mm plus the next branch's sphere, the case should be **flagged rather than borrowing from the next junction**. Finet himself measured "the artery segment adjacent to the bifurcation … with no other neighbouring bifurcation" ([Finet et al. 2008](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/fractal-geometry-of-arterial-coronary-bifurcations-a-quantitative-coronary-angiography-and-intravascular-ultrasound-analysis)). The LM parent window must be clipped at the ostium: only 25 of 36 cases had at least 5 mm of LM, and LM length ranged from 2.8 to 22.7 mm [MEASURED]. A symmetric ±5 mm LM window was incomplete in 12 of 98 training scans ([regional feasibility note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors/regional_feasibility.md)). Finally, a ramus changes the LM junction. The LM angle is 89 ± 21° with an intermediate artery against 75 ± 23° without ([Medrano-Gracia atlas](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)), and the Huo-Kassab law extends to a trifurcation as D_p^(7/3) = ΣD_i^(7/3) ([EuroIntervention review](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomy-and-function-relation-in-the-coronary-tree-from-bifurcations-to-myocardial-flow-and-mass)). No normal Finet value exists for trifurcations. The ramus stratum should therefore be reported separately rather than pooled.

## Ratios survive the error algebra; the junction exponent does not

Four diameter laws compete, and the evidence does not crown one. Murray's cube law (D_p³ = D_1³ + D_2³) implies constant wall shear across the junction. Morphometric coronary exponents nevertheless range from 2.06 to 3.20, and an invasive-flow study found 2.15 to 2.38 fitted best, "not 3.0" ([Taylor et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2022.871912)). A 2024 meta-analysis of 18 studies pooled the exponent at **2.39 (95 % CI 2.24 to 2.54)**, with I² = 99 % and no healthy-versus-diseased difference (2.38 against 2.29) ([Taylor et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11380967/)). Finet's linear rule D_p = 0.678 (D_1 + D_2) came from 173 bifurcations in 59 patients with normal angiograms ([Finet et al. 2008](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/fractal-geometry-of-arterial-coronary-bifurcations-a-quantitative-coronary-angiography-and-intravascular-ultrasound-analysis)). On 446 normal CTA bifurcations, Huo-Kassab (7/3), area preservation and Murray had nearly identical RMS error (0.165, 0.169, 0.169) and Finet the worst (0.456) ([Medrano-Gracia 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5323506)). The same group's atlas paper nonetheless says bifurcations obey Finet "much more" than HK or Murray ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). **The fit depends on where and how diameters are sampled, so the thesis should not try to decide "which law holds" from CT radii.**

The error analysis explains why [DERIVED] ([robustness note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Coronary%20bifurcation%20diameter%20descriptors/robustness_math_tests.md)). All the candidate descriptors are homogeneous of degree zero, so a common multiplicative error cancels exactly. An additive bias b does not cancel. It moves q = r1/r0 by b(1 − q)/r0 and the Finet ratio F = r0/(r1 + r2) by b(1 − 2F)/(r1 + r2). The second slope is negative for any F above 0.5, so an EDT-like positive bias lowers F. For the exponent n, implicit differentiation gives **dn = (n²/H)(p1 d ln r1 + p2 d ln r2 − d ln r0)**, where p_i = (r_i/r0)^n and H is the Shannon entropy of the flow split. The gain diverges as one daughter carries the whole split, and n has no solution once a noisy daughter radius reaches the parent's. The Monte Carlo puts numbers on this [SIM].

| Geometry (r0, r1, r2 mm) | SD(n) per 0.1 mm noise | Unsolvable at σ = 0.25 mm | Finet SD per 0.1 mm noise | Finet shift at b = +0.25 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LM-like (2.0, 1.6, 1.4) | 0.60 | 15 % | 0.046 | −0.024 |
| Side branch (1.5, 1.35, 0.8) | 1.00 | 35 % | not run | −0.037 |
| Distal (1.0, 0.85, 0.55) | 1.02 | 37 % | 0.10 | −0.056 |

A 1 SD exponent error spans the entire 2 to 3 literature range. Noise also **biases** n downward through selection: at a side branch with σ = 0.25 mm the median of the solvable draws falls from 2.39 to 1.73. On the real LM data, **10 of 36 cases (28 %) had a daughter window radius at or above the LM radius**, the exact unsolvability condition [MEASURED]. This matches the 12 to 37 % unsolvable rate on 100 training scans, where the exponent's method ICC was only 0.48 to 0.69 ([regional feasibility note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors/regional_feasibility.md)). The exponent should be reported descriptively and never modelled.

The retinal literature solved the same problem with the **optimality ratio** Γ = ((d1³ + d2³)/2)^(1/3)/d0. It equals 2^(−1/3) = 0.794 at a Murray optimum for any asymmetry. At 10 % noise its bias was under one sixth of the junction exponent's, and it rose significantly under NO-synthase inhibition, which shows sensitivity to endothelial function ([Witt et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2954284/)). Coronaries follow roughly 7/3, not 3, so the natural coronary version replaces the cube. Γ_HK = ((d1^(7/3) + d2^(7/3))/2)^(3/7)/d0 equals 2^(−3/7) = 0.743 exactly whenever the HK law holds, whatever the asymmetry [DERIVED here]. Its signed deviation, Γ_HK − 0.743, is always defined and behaves like a ratio under noise. It is the law-deviation descriptor the thesis should use in place of n. The sign matters: in 253 IVUS patients a high Murray ratio, meaning a parent large relative to its daughters, was associated with more dense calcium at bifurcations after adjustment for age, sex and risk factors [ABS] ([Schoenenberger et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22261173/)). No normal coronary value of Γ exists yet, so ImageCAS-X would supply the first reference distribution.

The daughter ratio has the best mechanistic support of any calibre descriptor. In 230 synthetic left-coronary CFD models, **the low-WSS area followed a quadratic in the LAD/LCx diameter ratio with R² 0.91 to 0.94**, forming in the smaller branch. Angle and tortuosity changed it by at most a few percent at a neutral ratio [FT] ([Garcha and Grande Gutiérrez 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11770140/)). In 39 human left trees, diameter was the only geometry-to-WSS correlate that survived multiple-comparison correction [FT, preprint] ([Shen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)). The ratio is also size-free on ImageCAS-X (|ρ| ≤ 0.29 with every size proxy), which absolute radii are not (ρ 0.56 to 0.75 with LM radius) ([regional feasibility note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors/regional_feasibility.md)).

The resulting descriptor set has one primary, a few secondaries and some items kept purely descriptive. The ICCs are method ICCs (EDT against surface distance) on 100 training scans.

The LM Finet value of 0.615 to 0.63 sits below Finet's angiographic 0.678, the CT atlas mean of 0.658 ± 0.083, and above the 0.57 of a CT study that used 10 mm medians ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy); [arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)). The spread across studies is larger than any sex effect, and the bias algebra predicts that EDT-based Finet reads low. **The gap to 0.678 is therefore a measurement convention, not anatomy**, and the thesis should say so.

## EDT stays the estimator; its radius-dependent bias is the real threat

The repo measures radius as the EDT at each centerline point, the distance to the nearest background voxel centre (`topology/radius.py`). That is an inscribed-sphere estimator, the same quantity VMTK uses to define its tubes and reference points ([VMTK branch splitting](http://www.vmtk.org/tutorials/BranchSplitting.html)). The phantom runs overturn the earlier worry that it over-reads by half a voxel. **On an ideal binary cylinder at 0.5 mm voxels, EDT over-reads by only +0.01 to +0.05 mm** [SIM]. The +0.135 mm EDT-minus-surface gap found earlier is mostly the surface estimator reading low, because a minimum distance to a staircase surface is an extreme-value statistic biased by −0.15 to −0.21 mm ([robustness note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Coronary%20bifurcation%20diameter%20descriptors/robustness_math_tests.md)).

The biases that matter depend on radius, and a radius-dependent bias does not cancel in a ratio. Blurring by a Gaussian point-spread function of σ = 0.5 mm (FWHM 1.2 mm) makes EDT under-read by −0.03 mm at 3 mm radius but **−0.17 mm at 0.75 mm**, which is −1 % to −22 %. In a Y phantom at that blur, the Finet ratio then read +0.023 to +0.046 high and r2/r0 read 13 % low. At σ = 0.3 mm the same ratios were within 0.005 of truth [SIM]. Typical CT in-plane FWHM is 0.5 to 0.7 mm, but that figure comes from an unverified snippet [SNIP] ([AJR artefacts review](https://ajronline.org/doi/10.2214/AJR.07.2138)), and the effective blur of the ImageCAS annotations and of CAS-Net output is unknown. **The segmentation's boundary behaviour, not the radius algorithm, decides whether D1, OM1 and crux ratios can be trusted.** Ellipticity adds a second, multiplicative bias. EDT returns roughly the minor semi-axis, so at b/a = 0.8 it reads 0.14 mm low at R = 2 mm and 0.27 mm low at R = 3 mm against the area-equivalent radius [SIM]. That cancels in a ratio only if parent and daughter share the same ellipticity, which oval daughter ostia do not.

The recommendation is to fix EDT for both cohorts, because Herlev-Østerbro will have only binary lumens from CAS-Net. Never mix EDT with surface distance, which shifted the side-branch Finet by 0.05 to 0.11 ([regional feasibility note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors/regional_feasibility.md)). Add an area-equivalent radius, √(A/π) from sub-voxel cross-sections perpendicular to the centerline, as a sensitivity analysis. It is unbiased under ellipticity and was nearly exact on binary phantoms [SIM]. It is also what the normal CTA atlas used, cutting the lumen mesh every 0.5 mm with a centreline-normal plane ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). The repo has no cross-section code yet, so this is the one new piece of measurement code the diameter work justifies.

The same finding reframes the "during" zone. At the junction node the EDT is about the parent radius (r_J/r0 = 0.93 to 0.99 in the phantom) whether or not there is any real widening [SIM], so an EDT flare index r_J/r0 carries almost no anatomical information. The carotid literature defines flare as the maximum cross-sectional area in the bifurcation over the parent reference area ([Bijari et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321819/)). The CAAS 3D method interpolates virtual contours through the POC for the same reason ([3D QCA methodology](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-novel-dedicated-3-dimensional-quantitative-coronary-analysis-methodology-for-bifurcation-lesions)). **Until area sections exist, the "at" zone contributes the angle and r_J as a nuisance variable, not a calibre descriptor.** An area-based flare is a later exploratory item, and no normal CTA value for it was found.

## Normal values give a sanity band, and covariates come mainly from Herlev-Østerbro

Published healthy CT lumen diameters are smaller than angiographic ones, and IVUS vessel diameters are larger than both. The modality must match before any comparison. Zero-calcium CT cohorts put the LM at 3.3 to 4.0 mm. In 127 plaque-free patients, men had LM 4.02 ± 0.52, LAD 3.62 ± 0.50 and LCx 3.51 ± 0.53 mm against 3.33 ± 0.61, 3.01 ± 0.50 and 2.88 ± 0.57 mm in women ([arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)). The 300-subject CT atlas gives LM 3.7 ± 0.8 mm and LCx 3.1 ± 0.7 mm ([Medrano-Gracia 2016](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). Dodge's angiographic LM of 4.5 ± 0.5 mm is an upper anchor, not a CT reference ([Dodge et al. 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)). The ImageCAS-X medians (r_LM 1.485, r_LAD 1.214, r_LCx 1.121 mm, i.e. diameters of 3.0, 2.4 and 2.2 mm) sit at the low end of this band [MEASURED]. That fits EDT's minor-axis behaviour and the blur bias, but it also means **absolute diameters from this pipeline are not comparable to published normals, while ratios are.** No CT source reports D1, OM1, PDA or PLB diameters in a healthy sex-stratified cohort. Indian angiographic data put the diagonal at 1.65 to 1.83 mm ([PMC6309284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6309284/)).

The determinants split cleanly between absolute calibres and ratios. **Sex** makes men's coronaries 9 to 21 % larger. The difference survives BSA indexing in Dodge (p < 0.01) and in the CT LM study (p = 0.001), though not in a normal Indian cohort ([Dodge et al. 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232); [arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489); [PMC5560873](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5560873)). The Finet ratio, however, was **0.57 in both sexes (p = 0.984)** ([arXiv 2311.18489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18489)). **Dominance** moves the LCx between 3.4 and 4.2 mm and the RCA between 2.8 and 3.9 mm, but leaves the LAD alone ([Dodge et al. 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)). The LM daughter ratio, LCx-OM1 and the crux therefore need dominance as a covariate. **Age** has little effect in most cohorts, except a rise in female LM with age in one Indian series ([PMC10811496](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10811496/)). **LV hypertrophy** adds 17 % in men, and endurance training widens mid-vessel diameters by about 10 % ([Dodge et al. 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232); [PMC8620029](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8620029/)). Between-person variability runs to a CV of 10 to 25 % for absolute diameters, against 3.5 to 13 % for the Finet ratio ([robustness and normal-range notes](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Coronary%20bifurcation%20diameter%20descriptors/normal_ranges.md)).

This divides the work between the two cohorts, as the supervisor asked. `Descriptors.xlsx` has only Scan ID, Image Quality, Dominance and Disease, and **no age or sex** ([fair diameter report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Fair%20diameter%20scalars%20for%20regression.md)). ImageCAS-X can therefore supply pooled reference distributions, dominance and image-quality strata, and all measurement-error statistics, but not age- or sex-conditioned norms. Those come from the Herlev-Østerbro baseline. They should be estimated on the plaque-free trees **before, and blind to, outcome linkage**, as age- and sex-conditioned z-scores. The body-size term should enter as a covariate, never as a divisor, because ratio variables in regression create spurious associations ([Kronmal 1993](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/2983064)). BSA from height and weight is the default, and LV mass where available. Size adjustment matters less than it seems: coronary diameter scales with myocardial mass with an exponent of only about 0.22 ([Choi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)). The dimensionless ratios need it least, which is one more reason to make them the primaries. The CT atlas found no association of hypertension, smoking or diabetes with bifurcation shape parameters in normal trees ([Medrano-Gracia 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5323506)). These risk factors belong in the outcome model as confounders of the outcome, not as determinants of the descriptor.

## A three-tier test protocol with pass thresholds set in advance

Robustness has to be shown at three levels, and each has a pre-registered pass criterion ([robustness note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Coronary%20bifurcation%20diameter%20descriptors/robustness_math_tests.md)). **Tier 1 is synthetic and runs in about a minute on one CPU.** Algebra tests check scale invariance to 1e-12, compare the bias slopes and the n²/H gain against finite differences, and check that n is undefined if and only if max(r1, r2) ≥ r0. A cylinder suite (R from 0.75 to 3 mm, twelve or more random orientations, point-spread σ of 0, 0.3 and 0.5 mm, ellipticity 1 and 0.8) must keep binary-mask EDT bias within ±0.05 mm, and it records the blur and ellipticity biases as the known error model. A Y-phantom suite covers LM-like to distal radii and 50 to 110° angles, and adds resampling from native to 0.5 mm, 0.1 and 0.25 mm centerline jitter, and a filleted junction with real flare. At the default window and σ ≤ 0.3 mm it must recover the Finet ratio to within 0.01 and daughter-to-parent ratios to within 0.02. A window scan must show the chosen (k, L) on a plateau, with |F̂(k ± 0.5) − F̂(k)| ≤ 0.01. The default k = 1, L = 3 mm passes.

**Tier 2 compares the reference with CAS-Net on the 160 test scans** and cannot run yet, because CAS-Net predictions do not exist. The correspondence-safe first step is to evaluate the predicted mask's EDT on the ground-truth centerline points. This isolates segmentation error from centerline and labelling error. The step is then repeated with predicted centerlines to get the total error. Per descriptor and junction, report ICC(2,1) absolute agreement with its 95 % CI, read against Koo and Li's bands ([Koo and Li 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/)). Add Bland-Altman bias and limits with a regression of difference on mean to catch radius-dependent bias ([Bland and Altman 1986](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/ba.pdf)), the window-level σ, and a stratification by image quality. The real data already bound what CAS-Net may cost. At cohort-median LM radii the Finet noise gain is 0.058 per 0.1 mm, and the observed between-subject SD is 0.094. The window-level radius noise in ground-truth EDT is therefore **at most about 0.16 mm** [DERIVED + MEASURED]. If CAS-Net's σ̂ exceeds that, network noise dominates the biological signal. The window-level σ is the right unit, because boundary errors are correlated over millimetres and a median does not reduce them by √n.

**Tier 3 converts reliability into what the outcome analysis can detect.** SEM = SD_between·√(1 − ICC), and the smallest detectable change (SDC) is 1.96·√2·SEM ([Weir 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15705040/)). For the LM Finet ratio (SD_between 0.094), ICC 0.85 to 0.90 gives an **SDC of 0.08 to 0.10, about one between-subject SD**. Individual classification is out of reach. A group difference of 0.3 SD costs only a 1.1- to 1.2-fold larger sample. The LM daughter ratio at ICC 0.63 has an SDC of 1.7 SD and a 1.6-fold sample inflation. The parent-to-daughter ratios moved by 0.10 to 0.12 between k = 0 and the default, two-thirds of their SD, so **fixing the window is not optional** [DERIVED + MEASURED]. No published CT reliability study of Finet or daughter ratios exists to benchmark against. Scan-rescan reliability, which the Herlev-Østerbro analysis actually needs, cannot be measured on ImageCAS-X's single scans. Tier 2 supplies only the method component.

These numbers need two provenance fixes before the thesis uses them. First, the four scratchpad scripts listed in the opening paragraph must be committed and rerun. The earlier regional scripts, whose 100-scan ICCs this report reuses, live in a different session's scratchpad and must be moved as well. Second, `lm_profile.py` read native-resolution ground-truth masks (in-plane 0.32 to 0.40 mm) from the first 40 ids of `test.txt`, not a random or quality-stratified sample. The rerun must state which resolution is the method and draw a documented sample. Test-split geometry without outcomes does not leak the Disease label. Even so, reference distributions should be confirmed on training scans, leaving the test split for Tier 2.

## Both arms must see the same windows, masks and normalisation

The earlier fair-diameter analysis set the principle, and bifurcation descriptors do not change it. **Both the regression and the per-point network must see the same domain, the same bifurcation masking and the same normalisation, differing only in representation** ([fair diameter report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Fair%20diameter%20scalars%20for%20regression.md)). The precedent sets the bar. A transformer reading per-point RCA curvature reached AUROC 0.67 against 0.60 for logistic regression on mean tortuosity ([Tello Ayala et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). Across 71 clinical studies, however, machine learning had no C-statistic advantage over logistic regression in comparisons with low risk of bias ([Christodoulou et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30763612/)).

Applied to junctions, this gives four rules. First, **the junction core is masked identically in both arms.** Points within the skipped core (k·r_J, the same k as the descriptor windows) are dropped from the network's per-point radius channel. The flare they carry is mostly inscribed-sphere geometry and would hand the network an artefact the regression never sees. The earlier report's "one to two local radii" mask should be set to this k, with k = 2 on daughters as the matched sensitivity analysis. Second, **bifurcation descriptors enter both arms as the same numbers**: as scalars in the regression and as junction tokens or late-fused junction features in the network. The network should not have to reconstruct them from masked points, and the regression should not get junction information the network lacks. Third, **normalisation is shared.** If the regression uses ratios, the network's radius channel is expressed relative to the same parent reference, for example r(s)/r_LM,window. Covariates (age, sex, BSA, dominance, ramus flag, LM length, image quality or scanner) form one vector with one encoding, late-fused in the network, and a covariate-only model is the floor for both. Fourth, **an information-matched rung sits between the two.** Functional logistic regression on principal components of the same masked profile tests whether a network win comes from reading the profile or from the network itself ([logitFD](https://rjournal.github.io/articles/RJ-2022-053/); [Goldsmith et al. 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1198/jcgs.2010.10007)).

In the regression the primary model is short. The LM Finet ratio is the declared primary calibre descriptor. The LM daughter ratio, Γ_HK deviation and the LAD-D1 and LCx-OM1 daughter ratios are secondaries under Holm or FDR control, with OM1 absence and the ramus stratum as their own variables. Continuous terms enter as restricted cubic splines with pre-specified knots. Any use of several diameter terms at once needs a penalty tuned in the inner loop, because choosing among candidates on the full cohort biases cross-validated estimates even on null data ([Varma and Simon 2006](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Bias-in-error-estimation-when-using-for-model-Varma-Simon/bf75f2555e8485d536ac217068b7963de3d6fa02)). Observed effects shrink with reliability: the observed log odds ratio is about λ times the true one ([Frost and Thompson 2000](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-985X.00164)). λ must come from short-interval repeats, not the ten-year pairs, and regression calibration should be reported alongside the naive estimate. The earlier geometry report declared the LM daughter-to-daughter angle as its overall primary ([plaque-prone report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors.md)). The LM Finet ratio is the calibre primary within the same pre-registration, and the two should be declared together, in one dated commit, before any Herlev-Østerbro outcome is opened.

## Expect HR 1.05 to 1.2 per SD, so choose an endpoint with events

There is no direct precedent to borrow an effect size from. The only coronary calibre measure with five or more years of follow-up is coronary volume over myocardial mass. In 325 symptomatic patients followed for 5.4 years, low volume carried HR 2.43 (1.02 to 5.75) after adjustment [ABS] ([Benetos et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31894527)). That estimate is inflated by an in-sample cut-point, revascularisation in the endpoint and a symptomatic population. The bifurcation-level evidence is cross-sectional: the IVUS Murray-ratio study ([Schoenenberger et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22261173/)), plaque concentrated on low-WSS outer walls in 94 % of partly affected bifurcation sections [ABS] ([van der Giessen et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19378688/)), and a 17-bifurcation serial study in which low shear computed with a Murray flow split predicted lumen loss [ABS] ([Sakellarios et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28606882/)). The closest analogue in healthy people is retinal. In an individual-participant analysis of 22,159 CHD-free adults, narrower arterioles carried HR 1.17 per 20 µm in women and 1.02 in men ([McGeechan et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19755365/)). ARIC over 16 years gave 1.06 to 1.14 per SD ([Seidelmann et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27682886/)), and deep-learning calibre in SEED gave 1.12 fully adjusted ([Cheung et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-020-00626-4)). The single retinal study of bifurcation diameter optimality found an association with ischaemic heart disease death over ten years that survived adjustment, but it was a small nested case-control study with no reported OR [ABS] ([Witt et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16585415/)). The retinal CHD signals were often female-only, so a **sex interaction should be pre-specified**, and the unadjusted analysis must be read with care, since small arteries partly proxy female sex and its lower risk.

The cohort arithmetic then decides the endpoint. The published Herlev-Østerbro CT analysis covered 9,533 asymptomatic adults, 54 % without atherosclerosis. It recorded 71 MIs over a median 3.5 years, mostly in diseased strata, where obstructive disease carried an adjusted RR of 9.19 ([Fuchs et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36972540/)). For a standardised continuous exposure in a Cox model, the events needed at 80 % power and two-sided α = 0.05 are about 7.85/(ln HR)². That is **about 236 events for HR 1.2 per SD and about 860 for HR 1.1**. With ICC 0.85 dilution, the 236 rises to about 330 [DERIVED here]. Ten-year MI counts in the plaque-free subset will plausibly be in the tens, not hundreds, an inference from the 3.5-year count. **A hard-event analysis restricted to healthy trees is therefore underpowered for any realistic effect.** Three designs remain, in order of preference. The first is new plaque on a repeat scan, with plaque-free regions as the unit. The earlier power chain (OR 1.3 per SD, 20 % regional incidence, λ = 0.7) needed about 345 patients ([plaque-prone report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors.md)), which depends on a serial subset whose size is not public. The second is a composite of MI, revascularisation and cardiovascular death in the whole cohort, adjusted for baseline plaque extent, with the plaque-free subset as the test of independence. If an association appears only in the whole cohort, the descriptor is probably proxying existing disease. The third reports per-SD HRs with CIs as an estimation study, stated as such in advance. Whichever is chosen, the incremental C-statistic will be small, since a retinal deep-learning score added only 0.013 to 0.023 over QRISK3 ([Reti-CVD, BMC Med 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36691041/)). A null result with its reliability correction and power reported is a publishable first, because no one has tested this question.

## Conclusion

The before, during and after framing turns out to be asymmetric. "Before" and "after" are measurable, provided the window starts outside a flare that is mostly inscribed-sphere geometry and, on the LCx, runs longer than geometry predicts. "During" is not a calibre at all with an EDT estimator, because the junction sphere reads the parent radius whether or not the lumen widens. The practical consequence is that the one new piece of code the diameter work justifies is a centreline-normal cross-section area. It would supply the ellipticity-free sensitivity radius, the only honest "at the junction" flare measure and a way to tell LCx funnelling from geometry, all at once. The second consequence is conceptual. The law-deviation question the field keeps asking ("Murray or Huo-Kassab?") is not answerable from CT radii, but a law-referenced ratio (Γ_HK) keeps the physiological meaning without the ill-conditioning. It is a descriptor no coronary study has used, and ImageCAS-X can provide its first normal distribution.

For the thesis, the binding constraint moves from geometry to design. The LM Finet ratio is reliable enough for group inference and not beyond. Realistic effects are retinal-sized. Hard events in plaque-free trees are too few. The contribution therefore rests on getting the order right: definitions, windows, masks and error model fixed and dated on ImageCAS-X, then age- and sex-conditioned norms in the Herlev-Østerbro baseline, then one pre-registered outcome test with its dilution correction. That order also keeps the regression and the network honest, because both inherit the same masked domain and the same junction numbers.
