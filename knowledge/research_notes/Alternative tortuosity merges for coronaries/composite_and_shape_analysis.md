# Composite tortuosity indices and shape analysis as alternatives to the SCC x Grisan merge

Evidence levels used below. **[FULL]** = read in full text this session. **[ABSTRACT]** = abstract, landing page, code page or search snippet only. **[FETCH-SUMMARY]** = full text passed through a summarising fetch tool, so numbers are probably right but wording and labels may be paraphrased; treat as one step below [FULL]. **Derivation** = reasoning by this researcher, stated by no source.

## Q1. What exactly are Bribiesca-Sánchez 2024 (3D-SCC) and Bribiesca 2013 (2D SCC tortuosity)?

### Takeaway
The 2024 paper is open access (CC-BY) and was read in full. It defines a signed slope chain α_j = ±arccos(T_j·T_{j+1})/π in (−1, 1) and a signed torsion chain β_j = arcsin(T_i·(N_i×N_j)/(|N_i||N_j|))/π in [−0.5, 0.5], with tortuosity τ = Σ|α_j| + Σ|β_j|. The "slope in [0,1], torsion in [0,0.5]" definitions that the earlier report flagged as unverified belong to the authors' 2023 LNCS paper, not to the 2024 one; for tortuosity the two give the same number because absolute values are taken. Validation is qualitative only: synthetic knots, 90 sperm flagella, no expert reference, no noise experiment, no statistics. The measure fixes Grisan's helix/C-shape zero, but it is scale-invariant by design and therefore radius-blind, and its torsion term is, by derivation, badly noise-sensitive on near-straight polylines.

### Cited Findings
**Bribiesca-Sánchez et al. 2024, Pattern Anal Applic 27:74 [FULL]** ([Springer, open access](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9); arXiv id: none according to [Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9?fields=title,abstract,openAccessPdf,externalIds,tldr,citationCount), which lists it as hybrid OA, CC-BY, 2 citations)
- Input is a 3D polygonal curve. Tangent T_j = unit vector X_j→X_{j+1} (Def. 2). Normal N_j = T_{j−1} × T_j (Def. 3), which is the binormal of the discrete osculating plane in standard terminology. Open curve with m segments has n = m − 1 inner vertices ([PAA 2024, §2, p. 3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Slope change (Eq. 2): α_j = sign_{α_j} · arccos(T_j·T_{j+1}/(|T_j||T_{j+1}|))/π, range (−1, 1); 0 = straight, 1 = reversal. sign_{α_1} = +1 arbitrarily. The sign is kept when the curve keeps curling in the same direction ("C-shape") and flipped when the segments form an "S-shape". The test uses the frame of the last non-collinear vertex i: v_i = N_i × T_i as provisional y-axis, and the sign is kept if (T_i·v_i)(T_{j+1}·v_i) < 0, flipped otherwise (the equation is partially garbled in the PDF text layer; the verbal description is unambiguous) ([PAA 2024, Eq. 2 and Algorithm 1, pp. 4 to 5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Torsion (Eq. 3): β_j = arcsin(T_i·(N_i × N_j)/(|N_i||N_j|))/π, range [−0.5, 0.5], signed by the right-hand rule, "the amount a segment s_i needs to roll along segment s_j to be co-planar with s_{j+1}"; i is the most recent non-collinear vertex. Remark 1: if α_j = 0 then β_j = 0. The first inner vertex of an open curve has no torsion, so n_β = n − 1 ([PAA 2024, Def. 5, Eq. 3, Algorithm 2, Remark 1, pp. 3, 5 to 6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Accumulated descriptors: α_a = Σα_j (Eq. 4), α_aa = Σ|α_j| (Eq. 5), Slope Direction Factor SDF = α_a/α_aa (Eq. 6; 1 = all same direction, i.e. helix-like), β_a (Eq. 7), β_aa = Σ|β_j| (Eq. 8). A curve is straight iff α_aa = 0; planar iff β_aa = 0; any closed curve has α_aa ≥ 2 ([PAA 2024, §3.1, p. 8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- **3D-SCC tortuosity (Eq. 9): τ = α_aa + β_aa.** Upper bound for equal-length segments: τ_max = n + (n−1)/2 = (3n−1)/2 for open curves. **Normalised tortuosity (Eq. 10): τ_N = τ/τ_max ∈ [0, 1)**. Worked example Fig. 1a: τ = 3.65 + 1.35 = 5, τ_N = 5/(9+4) = 0.38 ([PAA 2024, §4, pp. 8 to 9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Invariance claims: translation, rotation, uniform scaling (encoding unchanged); mirror image leaves slope chain unchanged and inverts torsion signs; reversing traversal reverses the chains with inverted signs; closed curves are start-point invariant up to shift. τ itself is invariant to all of these because only absolute values enter. Uniqueness of the encoding requires that "corresponding segments in each curve should match in length" ([PAA 2024, §3, §4.1, pp. 7 to 9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)). For planar curves the slope chain equals the 2D SCC (or its mirror) ([p. 7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Segments "do not need to be of the same length" for computing the chain (p. 4), but from §4.2 on the authors restrict to equal-size segments ([PAA 2024, pp. 4, 8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Validation, in full: (a) three synthetic closed curves (unknot, trefoil, complex) with τ = 5.18, 6.04, 31.28 (Table 2); resolution check was only 100 vs 1000 vertices, where accumulated values "maintained several digits of precision"; (b) a 3D Fary-Milnor analogue (non-trivial knot ⇒ α_aa ≥ 4); (c) 90 freely swimming human sperm, flagellum centerline traced as ~400 equal-length segments and smoothed with a least-squares B-spline, per-experiment mean τ 3.4 to 5.2 (τ_N 0.033 to 0.050), mean SDF 0.88 ("helix-like"); (d) head trajectories, τ 17.19 vs 48.6 for two examples; (e) a rotation demo (Table 3) and two comparisons with classical metrics on hand-picked curves: a parabola vs a spiral with equal arc/chord (τ 0.60 vs 5.53) and two flagella of 40 × 1 µm segments where all six curvature metrics are identical but τ = 2.74 vs 5.26 (Table 4) ([PAA 2024, §5, pp. 9 to 15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- No expert grading, no noise or jitter experiment, no inter-method statistics, no code release ("data available upon reasonable request"). The conclusion says the features "are statistically significant, in contrast to their 2D counterparts", but no test is reported in the paper ([PAA 2024, §6 and Data availability, pp. 15 to 16](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- The paper's own lineage: ref. [6] is Bribiesca-Sánchez, Guzmán, Darszon, Corkidi, Bribiesca 2023, "A measure of tortuosity for 3D curves: identifying 3D beating patterns of sperm flagella", LNCS 14062, pp. 363 to 374. There, "slope changes are scaled between 0 and 1, and the torsion between 0 and 0.5", and tortuosity was "the sum of all absolute slope changes and torsions". The 2024 paper adds signs so that the encoding is invertible ([PAA 2024, p. 2 to 3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)). **The previous report's "unverified" snippet definitions therefore describe the 2023 LNCS version.**
- The paper cites Johnson & Dougherty 2007 and Lisowska et al. 2014 among its tortuosity references, and its Table 1 lists κ_ta, κ_tr, κ_a, κ_r, κ_sd with application references ([PAA 2024, Table 1 and refs, pp. 3, 16](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).

**Internal inconsistencies found in the 2024 paper [FULL]**
- Table 2 gives τ_N = 0.0518, 0.0640, 0.313 for τ = 5.18, 6.04, 31.28 on curves "initially sampled with 100 vertices". These are τ/100, not τ/τ_max as Eq. 10 defines (for a closed curve with n = 100, τ_max = 150) ([PAA 2024, Table 2, Def. 12, pp. 9 to 10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- The closed-curve bound is written "n + n/2 = (3n−1)/2", which is arithmetically wrong (n + n/2 = 3n/2) ([PAA 2024, §4.2, p. 9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Table 4: two different flagella have identical values to 2 to 3 digits for all six curvature measures, including κ_ta = 3.25. The text attributes the different 3D-SCC value to torsion ([PAA 2024, Table 4, p. 14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).

**Bribiesca 2013, Pattern Recognit 46(3):716 to 724 [ABSTRACT]**
- SCC is obtained "by placing straight-line segments of constant length around the curve (the endpoints of the straight-line segments always touching the curve)", slope changes scaled to (−1, 1); "independent of translation, rotation, and optionally, of scaling" ([ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256822731_A_measure_of_tortuosity_based_on_chain_coding); [search-snippet summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256822731_A_measure_of_tortuosity_based_on_chain_coding)). The full text was not retrieved (Elsevier paywall; ResearchGate returns 403). The 2024 paper confirms that 2D SCC tortuosity is "the accumulation of absolute slope changes" and was applied to retinopathy of prematurity ([PAA 2024, p. 1 to 2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Lisowska et al. 2014 included SCC tortuosity among five indices on RET-TORT, "never before evaluated comparatively with retinal vessels" ([ResearchGate abstract, RET-TORT](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/270660781_An_experimental_assessment_of_five_indices_of_retinal_vessel_tortuosity_with_the_RET-TORT_public_dataset)) [ABSTRACT; its correlation values were not retrieved].
- A 2025 retinal paper proposes an SCC-based "scale-independent" tortuosity ([PubMed 39986365](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39986365/)) [TITLE ONLY].

### Inferences
- **Derivation: for a discrete polyline, Σθ_j equals κ_ta (total absolute curvature), so α_aa = κ_ta/π.** Table 4's identical κ_ta = 3.25 therefore implies α_aa ≈ 1.03 for both flagella, so the entire 2.74 vs 5.26 difference is torsion (β_aa ≈ 1.71 vs 4.23). The paper's headline advantage over curvature metrics is the torsion term alone.
- **Derivation: β is ill-conditioned on noisy, nearly straight polylines.** N_j = T_{j−1}×T_j has magnitude sin θ_j; when θ_j is tiny its direction is set by noise. Remark 1 only zeroes β when α is *exactly* 0, with no threshold. If the dihedral angle φ between consecutive planes is uniform on [0, π), then |β| = min(φ, π−φ)/π has mean 0.25, so a jittered straight line with n vertices would score β_aa ≈ 0.25n, i.e. τ_N ≈ 0.17 from torsion alone. For comparison, the smoothed sperm flagella scored τ_N 0.033 to 0.050. On 0.5 mm CCTA centerlines at small ℓ, torsion would likely dominate τ and be mostly noise. This matches the project rule "angle 1, curvature 2, torsion 3" derivatives (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`).
- **Derivation: arcsin folds the dihedral angle.** φ and π − φ give the same |β|; a pure binormal flip (φ = π, planar S-shape) gives β = 0. Inflections are therefore carried by the slope *sign*, not by torsion. The slope-sign rule (frame of last non-collinear vertex, "S-shape ⇒ flip") is a ready-made, published 3D inflection detector that could replace the binormal-flip rule proposed in the earlier merge; it has the same missing-threshold problem.
- **Derivation: τ_N is not resolution-free.** For a smooth curve Σ|α| → (total curvature)/π as n grows, while τ_max ≈ 1.5n = 1.5 L/ℓ, so τ_N ∝ ℓ × (mean curvature). τ_N is therefore a per-vertex average angle whose value scales with the chosen segment length; only τ (a sum) converges, and τ is length-extensive, not length-normalised. For per-vessel comparison the natural length-normalised quantity is τ/L (rad-equivalent per mm), which is the earlier report's τ_SCC/L plus a torsion term.
- **Against the two known weaknesses:** a helix gives constant nonzero α and β per vertex and a C-shape gives nonzero α, so 3D-SCC does not have Grisan's zero. But 3D-SCC is angle-only and explicitly scale invariant, so a 90° bend of radius 2 mm and one of radius 20 mm score the same Σ|α| (derivation). It does **not** fix radius blindness; it formalises it.

### Gaps
- Bribiesca 2013 full text (constant-length placement details, exact normalised tortuosity) was not retrieved; its Pattern Recognition PDF is paywalled.
- The 2023 LNCS paper (ref. [6]) was not retrieved.
- No independent validation of 3D-SCC (expert, noise, or vessel) was found. Semantic Scholar lists only 2 citing papers.

## Q2. Does any combination of tortuosity metrics outperform single metrics against expert grading or outcomes?

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin, 2D, small-sample and mostly retinal/corneal. Where the combination was compared head-to-head with single metrics (Hart 1999), the gain was 0 to 5 percentage points and not consistent across datasets. Supervised combinations (Annunziata 2016, Poletti 2012, Ramos 2019) match or beat individual observers, but either report no single-metric baseline or add non-tortuosity information (caliber, artery/vein, location). A 2025 systematic retinal test found that no global aggregation scheme exceeded the best single local index. No 3D or coronary study compares a combined index with single metrics against expert grading.

### Cited Findings
**Retina / cornea**
- **Hart et al. 1999 [FULL]**: seven measures t1 to t7 (arc/chord − 1; total curvature tc; total squared curvature tsc; each normalised by arc length or chord). Logit classifiers, cross-validation, segments tortuous vs not and 20 images (10 vs 10). Classification rate with **all measures combined: 85.6 % (manual segments) vs best single t3 82.9 %; 91.5 % (auto segments) vs t1 91.3 %; networks 95 % (manual) vs t3 90 %; networks 90 % (auto) vs t4 95 %**, i.e. the combination was worse than t4 there. Integrated ROC: all 0.935/0.970/0.95/0.86 vs best single 0.927/0.960/0.88/0.99 ([Hart 1999, Table 2, p. 248](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)).
- Hart 1999 also tried "measures that multiplied the length-normalized measures by the average vessel diameter. However, these measures performed slightly worse than their counterparts" ([Hart 1999, p. 248](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)). This is the only direct test found of a radius-weighted tortuosity, and it was negative (2D retina, small sample).
- Hart 1999 introduced *compositionality* (the tortuosity of a vessel made of segments with equal tortuosity equals that tortuosity), satisfied by tc/s and tsc/s (arc-length normalised), and argued these "a priori come closest to the ophthalmologist's notion" ([Hart 1999, §2, Table 1, pp. 243 to 244](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)).
- **Annunziata et al. 2016, IOVS [FETCH-SUMMARY]**: 18 measures (maximum curvature, inflection-point density, average curvature, each at six spatial scales) on 140 corneal nerve images, four tortuosity grades, three observers (11 images discarded for complete disagreement). A wrapper with a multinomial logistic ordinal regressor selected **mean curvature at scales 2 and 5** ("high- and low-frequency turns") as the best representation, the "tortuosity plane". Accuracy about 81 % vs consensus; Spearman of the system vs observers 0.7594 and 0.8880, reported as higher than inter-observer values (0.7225 to 0.8017), P < 0.001. **No performance of the best single measure was reported** ([Annunziata 2016, PMC4794090](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4794090/)).
- **Poletti, Grisan et al. 2012, EMBC [ABSTRACT]**: vessel-level measures (some including caliber) aggregated to image level and "combined in a supervised approach"; 20 ROP images ordered by three clinical graders and three imaging experts; the combined index reached **Spearman 0.95 with ground truth**, "comparable to the clinical graders' one and better than the retinal imaging experts' one". No single-measure baseline in the abstract ([PubMed 23367040 via Europe PMC](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23367040/)).
- **Ramos et al. 2019, Sci Rep [FETCH-SUMMARY]**: τ_c = τ_Grisan × f_c, with f_c a weighted sum of artery/vein, caliber, distance to optic disc and distance to fovea terms times vessel length; weights fitted by NSGA multi-objective optimisation; 200 images, 5 specialists, binary relevant/non-relevant tortuosity. The integrated metric "substantially outperformed" Grisan alone; artery/vein was "the most relevant anatomical factor" ([Ramos 2019, PMC6934469](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934469/)). This combines a tortuosity metric with context, not several tortuosity metrics.
- **ROPtool tortuosity + width (PMC12309758) [FETCH-SUMMARY]**: 92 RetCam images (368 quadrants), three-expert consensus; a "tortuosity-weighted plus" DI × ATI/12 + ATI reached AUC 0.941 and a regression 1.4491 × DI_max + 0.4391 × mean TI reached 0.934 on the validation half. Tortuosity alone was not reported ([PMC12309758](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12309758/)). Again tortuosity plus caliber, not tortuosity plus tortuosity.
- **Khansari et al. 2017 VTI [ABSTRACT/CODE]**: a multiplicative composite, VTI = 0.1 × (L_arc × SD of tangent angles × number of critical points × mean distance measure between inflections)/L_chord; "shown to perform better than available method in matching with visual perception of tortuosity" ([MATLAB File Exchange](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/72986-vessel-tortuosity-index-vti); [GitHub code](https://github.com/maz369/Vessel-Tortuosity-Index-VTI-); [PubMed 28856050](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28856050/)). Validation numbers not retrieved.
- **"Bridging local and global tortuosity", PLOS One 2025 [FETCH-SUMMARY]**: eight local indices (DF and T1 to T7) × five global aggregation schemes (MT, WAL, WAC, MTWA, TDG) on RET-TORT (30 arteries, one ophthalmologist). Best: WAL + T3 ρ = 0.893, WAC + T3 0.891, WAL + T4 0.887; local T2 0.866; MTWA and TDG ρ < 0.50. The global schemes preserved but "do not exceed the best local index's individual performance" ([PMC12331121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)). **Conflict**: an earlier fetch of the same paper summarised the best pairs as T4 and T6 with ρ 0.864 to 0.873 ([PMC12331121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)); the index labels need checking in the full text before citing.
- **Retest-stable Vascular Curvature Index (PMC11500049) [FETCH-SUMMARY]**: a single metric based on change of angular momentum, test-retest Spearman 0.87 to 0.92; better retest reliability than arc/chord, distance, angle, tortuosity density and squared-curvature tortuosity, not significantly better than inverse spherical radius tortuosity (p = 0.14) ([PMC11500049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500049/)). Relevant as evidence that a reliability criterion, not expert agreement, can rank metrics without labels.
- Ramos 2018: inter-rater κ 0.22 to 0.76 for retinal tortuosity ([PMC6245828](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6245828/)) (already in the earlier report).

**Cerebral**
- **Bullitt et al. 2003, IEEE TMI [ABSTRACT]**: three metrics in 3D: distance metric (DM, arc/chord), inflection count metric (ICM, arc/chord × (inflections + 1), a *product* combination) and sum of angles (SOAM). They define three clinical patterns of abnormal tortuosity; ICM "appears to be the most effective in detecting several types of abnormalities", while SOAM "may be more effective in recognizing tightly coiled, 'corkscrew' vessels" ([PubMed 12956271 via Europe PMC](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12956271/)). The formula for ICM is from the earlier report's Bullitt reading, not re-verified here. Conclusion: different metrics catch different patterns; Bullitt reports them side by side rather than blending them.

**Coronary**
- **Chiastra/Pedrigi-type automated CCTA pipeline (APL Bioeng 2024) [FETCH-SUMMARY]**: local tortuosity = arc/chord over a 1 cm reference arc; tortuosity angle TA = angle between least-squares lines fitted to the upstream and downstream half-arcs; **tortuosity score TS = number of branches with at least three bends with TA ≥ 45°** (0 to ≥ 4). 281 CCTA (238 train, 43 test); only the segmentation was validated against experts (Dice 0.896, MSD 1.027 mm), not the tortuosity score ([PMC10807932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10807932/)). This is the standard clinical "bend count × angle threshold" score, operationalised automatically.
- **Deep learning on invasive angiography (Sci Rep 2023) [ABSTRACT]**: CNN classifying 658 angiographies as tortuous vs not, accuracy 87 ± 6 %, AUC 0.96 ± 0.03, "comparable" to independent experts ([PubMed 37429940](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37429940/)). Labels are the binary clinical definition; 2D projections.
- A 2025 HMM approach to unsupervised coronary tortuosity assessment exists ([Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-95841-0_27)) [TITLE ONLY]; the earlier report's EHJ abstract gives precision 0.87 on 18 LADs.

### Inferences
- Across the studies with a head-to-head comparison, "combine everything" gives small, inconsistent gains on tiny samples (Hart: 20 images for networks) and can lose to a single well-chosen measure. The supervised wins (Annunziata, Poletti, Ramos) are selected and evaluated on 20 to 200 2D images, often without an external test set, so optimism bias is likely.
- The most reusable result is Annunziata's: **two scales of one measure (mean curvature) beat richer combinations**, and the chosen pair separates high-frequency from low-frequency turns. That is a "merge" by scale, not by metric family.
- Several "combined" indices already embed a product of count × amplitude (Bullitt ICM, Grisan's (n−1)/n × Σ arc/chord excess, Khansari VTI, the clinical TS). None has shown an advantage over a plain curvature integral in 3D.
- Without expert labels, weights for any learned combination cannot be fitted on ImageCAS-X. Any composite must be either unweighted/structural or evaluated by the reliability criteria (ranking stability, jitter ICC, extractor ICC, floor effect) in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`.

### Gaps
- Chandrinos 1998 and Kalitzeos tortuosity combinations: no source retrieved in this pass.
- Lisowska 2014 RET-TORT numbers (including SCC) not retrieved.
- Annunziata single-measure performance is not reported (or was not surfaced by the fetch); the claim "combination beats single" cannot be made from that paper.
- No 3D vessel study (cerebral, coronary, carotid) compares a composite index against single metrics on expert grading.

## Q3. Which approach addresses both known weaknesses (chain-code radius blindness, Grisan helix/C-shape zero)?

### Takeaway
No single published method addresses both. Grisan's zero comes from requiring an inflection; any curvature-integral measure (SCC density, κ_a, 3D-SCC) removes it. Radius blindness comes from measuring only turning angles; it is addressed by measuring curvature at a scale (radius of curvature, i.e. "tight vs wide"), which multi-scale curvature (Annunziata's two-scale plane) or a κ-weighted integral (squared curvature, Hart t5) does, since ∫κ² ds penalises tight bends more than wide ones of equal angle. Shape-analysis frameworks (SRVF, FDA, persistent homology) handle both implicitly but produce distances or profiles, not a tortuosity index, and none has been applied to coronary tortuosity.

### Cited Findings
**Curvature at scale / squared curvature**
- Hart's t3 (tsc) "places a greater emphasis on the parts of the curve that have high curvature" and "the curvature is greater for small vessels, t3 will emphasize the tortuosity of smaller vessels" ([Hart 1999, p. 244](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)); t3 was the best single measure on manual segments ([Table 2](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)).
- Bribiesca-Sánchez 2024 list κ_tr (total squared curvature), κ_r (RMS curvature) and κ_sd (squared curvature derivative) as existing measures and note "there is no upper bound for absolute curvature", which they present as a weakness their bounded angle measure fixes ([PAA 2024, Table 1, p. 3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)).
- Annunziata's tortuosity plane uses mean curvature at two spatial scales as its two axes ([PMC4794090](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4794090/)).
- Bullitt 2003: SOAM (sum of angles, a curvature integral) was the metric that picked out tightly coiled vessels ([PubMed 12956271](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12956271/)) [ABSTRACT]; an earlier search snippet states only SOAM "correctly assign[ed] the highest tortuosity to the tightest coil of a given total path length" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10582739_Measuring_Tortuosity_of_the_Intracerebral_Vasculature_From_MRA_Images)) [SNIPPET].
- Dimensionless groupings L/R (path length over radius of curvature) and L/λ are used in mathematical-modelling reviews to characterise vessel curvature ([Vascular tortuosity: a mathematical modeling perspective, PMC10717352](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10717352/)) [SNIPPET].

**Elastic shape analysis (SRVF)**
- SRVF q(t) = β̇(t)/√|β̇(t)|; the L² metric on q is an elastic Riemannian metric on curves, invariant to translation, rotation and reparameterisation ([Dimensionality Reduction using Elastic Measures, arXiv 2209.04933](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.04933)) [SNIPPET].
- Brain arterial networks (BAN) have been analysed as elastic shape graphs (3D curves joined at nodes): geodesics, means, covariance and PCA on 92 subjects; age had "a clear, quantifiable effect", with increasing shape variance with age; gender effects "require further investigation" ([Statistical Shape Analysis of Brain Arterial Networks, arXiv 2007.04793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04793)) [ABSTRACT]. An extended SRVF exists for tree-like 3D objects ([arXiv 2110.08693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08693)) [TITLE ONLY]. No tortuosity index is derived in either.
- No study applying SRVF to coronary centerlines was found in two searches.

**Functional data analysis (AneuRisk)**
- AneuRisk analyses the ICA's radius profile and centerline curvature as functions of arc length, with registration to remove "ancillary variability" and functional PCA; 65 patients ([MOX-Polimi AneuRisk page](https://mox.polimi.it/research-areas/statistics/research/high-dimensional-complex-data-functional-spatial-and-object-data/aneurisk/)) [SNIPPET]. Curvature is estimated with free-knot regression splines ([Sangalli, Secchi, Vantini, Veneziani 2009, JRSS-C 58(3):285 to 306](https://sangalli.faculty.polimi.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2009_Sangalli-Secchi-Vantini-Veneziani-JRSSC_compresso.pdf)) [TITLE ONLY].
- A related clinical study: in 130 ICAs, aneurysmal ICAs had higher mean curvature (p < 0.001), independent of age, hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia and smoking; non-aneurysmal ICAs were less curved in men than in women ([Lauric et al. 2014, J Neurointerv Surg, PMID 24335804](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24335804/)) [ABSTRACT]. Single metric (mean curvature), consistent with women-higher tortuosity.

**Statistical shape of coronary centerlines**
- Coronary atlases from CCTA with calcium score 0: 122 scans (angles, diameters, centerlines, PCA) ([PubMed 25485418](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25485418/)) [SNIPPET]; 211 patients / 446 bifurcations, PCA shape scores tested against risk factors; size and bifurcation angle differed by sex; hypertension, smoking and diabetes showed no association ([Medrano-Gracia et al. 2017, PMC5323506](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5323506/)) [ABSTRACT]. These model bifurcation shape, not tortuosity, and require registration/correspondence.

**Topological descriptors**
- Persistent homology of brain artery trees (Bendich et al. 2016, Ann Appl Stat) gave "heightened correlations with covariates such as age and sex, relative to earlier analyses", and the age correlation remained significant after controlling for earlier summaries ([arXiv 1411.6652](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6652)) [ABSTRACT]. A search snippet states the analysis showed age "correlated with certain measures of how brain arteries bend through space" and persisted after rescaling by total artery length ([search result, Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-applied-statistics/volume-10/issue-1/Persistent-homology-analysis-of-brain-artery-trees/10.1214/15-AOAS886.full)) [SNIPPET]. Filtration details not retrieved.

**Deep learning**
- The only coronary DL tortuosity classifier found works on 2D invasive angiograms with binary labels ([PubMed 37429940](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37429940/)) [ABSTRACT]; a DL CCTA pipeline computes a rule-based TS after segmentation, not a learned tortuosity ([PMC10807932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10807932/)).

### Inferences
- **Derivation: why curvature-integral measures remove Grisan's zero.** Σθ (SCC, SOAM, κ_ta), κ_a and 3D-SCC all accumulate turning regardless of sign changes, so a helix (constant κ, constant torsion) and a C-shape score > 0. Grisan's zero is a property of requiring n ≥ 2 turns, not of the arc/chord term.
- **Derivation: why squared curvature or two scales address radius blindness.** A bend of angle Θ at radius R has ∫κ ds = Θ (radius-free) but ∫κ² ds = Θ/R. So κ_tr (or κ_r = RMS curvature, which is length-normalised and has units mm⁻¹) separates one tight bend from one wide bend of the same angle, which Σθ cannot. The cost is a second derivative, i.e. more noise amplification, and no upper bound.
- **Derivation: a two-scale SCC is the 3D, label-free analogue of Annunziata's plane.** Compute SCC turning density Σθ/L at a short chord ℓ_s (tight, high-frequency bends) and a long chord ℓ_L (wide, low-frequency course). A tight bend contributes at ℓ_s but is chord-cut at ℓ_L; a wide C contributes at both. The ratio or the pair distinguishes "one wide arc" from "several tight bends" (criterion 1) without an inflection detector, and a helix scores at both scales. The ℓ values would need anchoring to CCTA noise (the earlier report's jitter bias ≈ 3σ/ℓ² argues ℓ_s ≥ ~3 mm) and possibly to lumen radius.
- Hart's negative result for diameter-multiplied measures is a warning that radius normalisation (κ·r) is not automatically better; it was tested only in 2D retina on a small set.
- SRVF, FDA and persistent homology are strongest when there is a population-level question (e.g. age/sex effects, group differences) and correspondence/registration is possible; they give distances or modes, not a scalar that satisfies "zero on a line, monotone in amplitude". For a thesis that must produce a per-vessel index keyed by segment label, they are analysis tools on top of an index, not replacements for it.
- The torsion term of 3D-SCC is the only published element aimed specifically at out-of-plane winding, but by the derivation in Q1 it is the least robust quantity to extract from 0.5 mm centerlines.

### Gaps
- No coronary application of SRVF, FDA curvature profiles or persistent homology for tortuosity was found.
- No study testing curvature-at-scale or κ·r normalisation in 3D coronaries against experts or reproducibility.
- Bendich 2016 quantitative correlations and filtration not retrieved.

## Q4. What is the minimal, interpretable merge a master's thesis could implement and validate on ~800 ImageCAS-X centerline trees without expert labels?

### Takeaway
The literature supports reporting two or three complementary, threshold-light quantities side by side rather than a learned blend: (1) a scale-anchored turning density (SCC Σθ/(πL) at a fixed chord ℓ, equivalent to κ_a); (2) the same density at a second, longer chord, or RMS curvature, to capture bend tightness; optionally (3) a signed-slope-based turn count or SDF from 3D-SCC as an interpretable "shape" tag (C/helix vs S). The 3D-SCC torsion term should be computed only as a stability check, because its noise behaviour is unknown and likely poor. Selection among candidates should use the pre-registered reliability criteria, which are label-free, with the few-rater blind ranking of ~30 vessels as the only expert check.

### Cited Findings
- Selection criteria are label-free except criterion 7 (Kendall τ against a blind expert ranking of ~30 vessels) (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`, local file).
- Reliability-first ranking of metrics without expert labels has precedent: the retinal VCI study ranked seven metrics by test-retest correlation ([PMC11500049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500049/)) [FETCH-SUMMARY]; Kjeldsberg 2021 ranked carotid bend detectors by coefficient of variation across settings ([PMC8626959](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626959/)) (from earlier report).
- Kashyap 2022 found average absolute curvature (κ_a) best associated with low WSS in 127 CCTA patients ([PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)) (from earlier report), which gives component (1) a coronary precedent.
- Annunziata's two-scale mean curvature is the best-supported "combination" in the retinal/corneal literature ([PMC4794090](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4794090/)) [FETCH-SUMMARY].
- 3D-SCC provides SDF = α_a/α_aa (1 = all turning in one direction, helix/C-like) and a signed slope chain whose sign runs mark S-shaped reversals ([PAA 2024, Eq. 6, Algorithm 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)) [FULL].
- Hart's compositionality property holds for arc-length-normalised curvature integrals (tc/s, tsc/s), which suits per-segment and per-vessel outputs that must be consistent ([Hart 1999, Table 1](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)) [FULL].
- The PLOS One 2025 study found length-weighted (WAL) or chord-weighted (WAC) aggregation of segment values preserved expert agreement, while mean-of-segments and density-style schemes (MTWA, TDG) did poorly ([PMC12331121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)) [FETCH-SUMMARY]. Relevant to going from segment to vessel values.

### Inferences
- **Proposed minimal merge (derivation, not from any source):** per named vessel and segment, resample at constant chord ℓ; compute
  - D_ℓ = Σθ_n/(πL) at two chords (e.g. ℓ_s ≈ 3 mm, ℓ_L ≈ 8 mm; to be fixed from the jitter experiment, not from data outcomes), reported as the pair (D_s, D_L) and optionally the ratio D_s/D_L as a "tightness" index. This is Annunziata's plane in 3D, SCC-discretised, with no inflection detector and no torsion.
  - SDF and the count of slope-sign runs per cm from the signed 3D-SCC slope chain at ℓ_L, as a descriptive C/helix vs S tag, replacing Grisan's (n−1)/n gate with an explicit, reported shape category rather than a multiplicative zero.
  - Compare against baselines: arc/chord, κ_a from a spline, RMS curvature, and the earlier SCC × Grisan merge.
- Why this addresses both weaknesses: D_s and D_L are positive for helices and C-shapes (Grisan's zero removed); their pair separates tight from wide bends of equal total angle (radius blindness reduced), using scale rather than lumen radius, so it does not depend on the unverified radius array.
- What it does not fix: absolute scale still has to be chosen; plaque-induced local deflection will raise D_s more than D_L, which could be used as a diagnostic (a stenosis-shaped spike at ℓ_s with no change at ℓ_L) but also violates "must not read high because of plaque" unless ℓ_s is large enough.
- Label-free validation plan (derivation): synthetic suite including helix, wrapped sinusoid, one wide arc vs several tight bends with equal total angle, each randomly rotated; Spearman ρ of rankings across ℓ-sweeps (criterion 2); ICC under 0.5 mm jitter (criterion 3); floor/ceiling fractions per vessel (criterion 5); correlation with length, radius and Image Quality (criterion 6); only then the ~30-vessel blind ranking. Do not fit weights to collapse (D_s, D_L) into one number unless the 30-vessel ranking is reserved for testing, since there are no labels to learn from.
- Torsion: compute β_aa/L at ℓ_L only as a sensitivity analysis; if its jitter ICC is low (expected by the Q1 derivation), document that the 3D-SCC torsion chain was tested and rejected on reliability grounds. That is a publishable negative result for a thesis because no one has tested 3D-SCC under noise.

### Gaps
- No source validates any of these combinations on 3D coronary centerlines; every element of the proposed merge is a derivation.
- The ℓ values that keep SCC density above the jitter floor on ImageCAS-X centerlines are unknown until the jitter experiment is run.
- Whether the radius array exists in the ImageCAS-X VTK files is still unchecked (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`), so κ·r normalisation cannot yet be planned.
