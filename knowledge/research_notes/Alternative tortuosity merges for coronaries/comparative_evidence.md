## Q1. Across beds, which metric families win against which reference, and does any combined/merged metric beat single ones?

### Takeaway
Against haemodynamic and outcome references, every head-to-head study with a clear winner favours an integral-of-curvature family (mean or total absolute curvature) over arc/chord (TI): low WSS in 127 coronaries, adjusted aortic-event OR in 65 carotids (though TI's raw AUC was marginally higher there). TI wins only on observer reproducibility of manual measurement (aorta, iliac) and sometimes on simple outcome AUC. Torsion on its own drives helical flow but is not a better single tortuosity index. No study found a merged geometric index (curvature + turn count, or the chain-code + Grisan merge) that beats a single curvature index in 3D. The only "combinations" that won were mixed geometry-plus-haemodynamics models (SCAD, AUC 0.95 to 0.97, preprint) or retinal metrics adding anatomical context (Ramos 2019). The two 3D wins for inflection or turn-count metrics are Bullitt 2003 (cerebral MRA, several abnormality types) and Grisan-type turn metrics against retinal expert grading (2D).

**Coronary-specific findings pulled from the table.**
- Against CFD low WSS in 127 no-CAD coronaries, curvature integrals beat TI, and κ_a beat both RMS curvature and the derivative-of-curvature metric in every region; the LCx is weak for all (best R² 0.061). — [Kashyap 2022 [F]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)
- Small-n CFD studies disagree on TI's direction by segment (Pinho: TI LMS-LCx r = +0.63, TI LMS-LAD r = −0.56) and Malvè found TI of LM-LAD relevant, so the Kashyap null for TI is not universal. — [Pinho 2019 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11517-018-1904-2); [Malvè 2015 [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7574594/)
- Torsion's documented role is on helical flow (r = 0.64, swine), and in idealised models its effect on WSS is an order of magnitude below curvature's (3 % vs 22 %). — [De Nisco 2019 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10439-018-02169-x); [Selvarasu 2012 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4006984)
- Tortuosity's haemodynamic sign is disputed: Vorobtsova argues higher WSS (protective) via helicity; Xie finds low oscillating WSS downstream of bends >120°; Shen finds high helicity also produces adversely high WSS in stenosed trees. — [Vorobtsova 2016 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10439-015-1492-3); [Xie 2013a [A]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2013.05.005); [Shen 2025 [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12324882/)
- Lumen diameter or diameter ratio outweighs curvature/tortuosity as a WSS determinant in three independent designs (RCA models, 230 synthetic trees, SCAD within-patient). — [Johnston 2007 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9155/52/9/013); [Garcha 2025 [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11770140/); [Zhang 2026 [P]](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.12.26360329)
- For SCAD, whole-tree curvature is higher in cases, but at the lesion segment the geometric discriminator is lower torsion (curvature only borderline). — [Zhang 2026 [P]](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.12.26360329)
- No study was found that relates any 3D CCTA tortuosity metric to invasive FFR or CT-FFR; the searches returned only FFR-CT method papers and 2D-angiography microvascular studies. (Search result, see Gaps.)

### Inferences
- Metric-family ranking by reference, as far as the evidence goes: **low WSS / haemodynamics**: mean absolute curvature > RMS ≈ derivative curvature > TI (coronary, one large study). **Clinical outcomes**: curvature integral ≈ TI on AUC, curvature better adjusted (carotid); at the right spatial scale both work (iliac). **Expert perception**: turn/convexity-change metrics (Grisan, chain-code sign change) > arc/chord (retina 2D), and ICM > SOAM except for corkscrews (cerebral 3D). **Helical flow**: torsion. **Observer reproducibility of manual measurement**: TI.
- This split is itself the best argument for the earlier report's advice to report a curvature density and a turn-based index side by side: the curvature family is what haemodynamic and outcome evidence supports; the turn family is what perception evidence supports. There is no evidence that a single blended number beats either.
- Combined indices that did beat single ones (Moon's d20·cosθ, Zhang's curvature + haemodynamics, Bullitt 2005's discriminant, Ramos 2019's context-aware metric) all combine tortuosity with *non-tortuosity* information. None merges two tortuosity formulas. A chain-code + Grisan merge would therefore be novel and unsupported by precedent either way.
- Since Kashyap's R² for κ_a is at most 0.23, and diameter dominates WSS in other studies, low WSS is a weak and partly confounded reference for choosing a metric. Any merged metric would need to beat κ_a by a margin larger than the sampling noise of an R² near 0.1 to 0.2 at n = 127, which is unlikely to be demonstrable.

### Gaps
- No 3D CCTA study relating tortuosity (any definition) to invasive FFR, CT-FFR or perfusion ischaemia was found. Coronary tortuosity–ischaemia links in the found literature are 2D angiographic (Zebić Mihić 2023, Li 2020 CFR/IMR, Jansen 2021), already in the earlier notes.
- No coronary 3D study was found that compares an inflection/bend-count metric with a curvature integral against any reference. Bullitt 2003 (cerebral) is the only 3D comparison including an inflection count.
- Vorobtsova 2016 full text (tortuosity definition, correlation values with helicity) was paywalled; only the abstract was read. Same for De Nisco 2019, Pinho 2019, Malvè 2015 numbers beyond the abstract.
- "Xie 2014" and "Wang/Liu curvature-torsion" papers named in the brief were not located as distinct metric-comparison studies; Xie's 2013 papers are single-family (bend angle) CFD. Searches for curvature-torsion vs plaque in coronaries (e.g., Tang group IVUS papers) returned nothing citable in this pass.
- Ramos 2018 per-metric AUCs were not extractable from the text fetched.
- Bullitt 2003 effect sizes per metric were not retrieved (abstract only).

## Q2. What reproducibility (ICC/CV) numbers exist for curvature, torsion, arc/chord and bend counts in 3D?

### Takeaway
Reproducibility numbers exist almost only for arc/chord TI on large vessels (aorta, iliac), where manual TI is highly reproducible (ICC 0.95 to 0.98; iliac r 0.79 to 0.94), and automated iliac TI agrees with manual only moderately (ICC 0.73). For curvature, the evidence is a fitting-circle method on the aorta (ICC 0.82 to 0.85) and long-term stability in the carotid (TAC fold change 0.03 ± 0.30 vs TI 0.30 ± 0.66). For bend counts, the only 3D stability numbers are CVs from the ICA (under 5 % for Bogunović, 47 to 58 % for torsion-based). **No ICC, CV or scan-rescan value was found for curvature, torsion, TI or bend count on coronary CCTA.** Cardiac phase changes inflections and Menger curvature (earlier report).

### Cited Findings
- Thoracic aorta CT, 66 TEVAR patients, 2 radiologists, repeated >28 days: interobserver ICC **TI 0.97 to 0.98**, **fitting-circle diameter (curvature) 0.82 to 0.84**, **centerline angle at branch orifices 0.39 to 0.75**; intraobserver 0.98 to 1.00, 0.82 to 0.85, 0.44 to 0.75. 95 % LoA: TI −5.5 to 5.6 %, angle −10.9 to 10.9 %, fitting circle −18.0 to 24.0 %. Variability of TI not related to calcification, atheroma or diameter. — [Thoracic aorta variability 2018 [A]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29705085/)
- Aorta MRA, 211 Marfan + 20 controls: aortic tortuosity index (arc/chord family) ICC intra 0.968, inter 0.955. ATI >1.95 gave 12.8× probability of the combined endpoint. — [Franken 2015 [A]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26005802/)
- Iliac CT, 376 iliacs: TI intra r = 0.94, inter r = 0.79 (Pearson, not ICC); subjective grade κ 0.71 / 0.65. — [Kristmundsson 2012 [A]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22545879/)
- Iliac CT, fully automated deep-learning TI vs manual: Pearson 0.95 (0.94 to 0.96) but **ICC 0.73 (0.69 to 0.77)**, bias −0.09. High correlation with a systematic offset: the relevant pattern for reference-vs-CAS-Net centerline agreement (criterion 4). — [Barb 2026 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrad.2025.112609)
- Carotid, Loeys-Dietz, 8 to 10 year serial scans: TAC fold change 0.026 ± 0.298 vs TI 0.302 ± 0.656; no ICC reported and serial scans confound real change with error. — [Lee 2024 [F]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11255721/)
- ICA siphon bend counts: parallel-transport curvature-vector detector CV <5 %; torsion-extrema detector CV 47 to 58 %; bend count 3 to 33 with centerline resolution. — [Kjeldsberg 2021 [prior]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626959/)
- ICA siphon automated vs manual landmarks: for all but one landmark, bias ns or variability within 50 % of inter-observer variability. — [Bogunović 2012 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2012.01.006)
- Coronary dynamics: curvature of a distal LAD site varied 0.25 to 1.8 cm⁻¹ over the cardiac cycle (one case, biplane angiography). In CCTA, inflections per segment 0.63 in end-systole vs 0.51 end-diastole, Menger curvature 0.085 vs 0.078 mm⁻¹. — [Gross & Friedman 1998 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0021-9290(98)00012-8); [van Zandwijk 2019 [prior]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)
- Retinal (2D) reference for expert agreement: inter-rater κ 0.22 to 0.76, intra-rater 0.35 to 0.83 (5 experts); new peripapillary method interobserver ICC 0.92. — [Ramos 2018 [F]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6245828/); [Zou 2025 [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12574746/)
- Kashyap 2022 reports no reproducibility data; its derivative-of-curvature metric had kurtosis 63.25 in LM, versus 2.38 for κ_a. — [Kashyap 2022 [F]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/); [prior notes]

### Inferences
- The reproducibility ordering expected from derivative order (TI > curvature > torsion ≈ bend counts from torsion) is supported wherever measured: aorta TI ICC 0.97 > curvature 0.82 > angle 0.39 to 0.75; ICA curvature-vector bends CV <5 % vs torsion bends CV ~50 %. This is consistent with `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`'s derivative-noise rule but is not coronary evidence.
- Most published ICCs are observer ICCs on a fixed centerline tool, i.e. landmark placement variance. They are not the jitter/extractor ICCs that criteria 3 and 4 require. ImageCAS-X would be producing the first coronary numbers of the relevant kind.
- A bend or turn detector built on binormal/torsion flips (the earlier report's 3D Grisan twist rule) belongs to the less stable family in Kjeldsberg; the parallel-transport curvature-vector rotation is the better-evidenced detector to use for turn splitting.

### Gaps
- No coronary CCTA ICC/CV for curvature, torsion, TI or bend count (inter-observer, scan-rescan or extractor) was found in this pass.
- No torsion reproducibility number in any bed was found, only the Kjeldsberg bend-count CVs for a torsion-based detector.
- Franken 2015, the 2018 aorta paper and Barb 2026 were read as abstracts only; details of centerline software were not checked.

## Q3. Which reference standard is realistically available on ImageCAS-X for validating a merged metric?

### Takeaway
None of the literature's physiological references (CFD WSS, FFR, plaque follow-up, SCAD, scan-rescan) exists on ImageCAS-X. What is realistic: (a) reference-free internal checks (synthetic curves, scale/jitter stability, extractor agreement between reference and CAS-Net centerlines, Ramírez-style compositional self-consistency); (b) a blind multi-rater expert ranking, which is the one reference the perception literature validates, with the caveat that experts disagree (κ as low as 0.22); (c) Descriptors.xlsx strata only as confound checks (Image Quality) and weak construct checks (Dominance, Disease), never as selection targets. Running CFD on a subset to reproduce Kashyap's low-WSS test is possible in principle but is a separate project.

### Cited Findings
- Descriptors.xlsx columns are Scan ID | Image Quality | Dominance | Disease (repo `ARCHITECTURE.md`, line 65); `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md` forbids opening `Disease` during metric selection and lists Image Quality correlation as criterion 6. — local repo files.
- Kashyap's CFD reference required per-patient CFD with low-TAWSS thresholds; its best R² was 0.232. — [Kashyap 2022 [F]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)
- In coronaries, RRT rather than TAWSS or OSI correlated best with fatty streak location (r = 0.71), so even a CFD reference has an unresolved choice of WSS metric. — [Kandangwa 2025 [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11782302/)
- The ASOCA group used TSVI as the only haemodynamic metric separating stenosed from non-stenosed trees (AUC 0.876), with curvature weaker (AUC > 0.711). — [Zhang 2024 [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11416812/)
- Expert references are noisy: best retinal metric AUC 0.80 vs individual experts 0.61 to 0.83 against consensus; Grisan and chain-code sign-change metrics tracked experts best. — [Ramos 2018 [F]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6245828/)
- A reference-free test exists: compare whole-segment tortuosity against the aggregated tortuosity of its sub-segments (Spearman) to pick local index + weighting without experts. — [Ramírez 2025 [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)
- Automated-vs-manual agreement can show r = 0.95 but ICC 0.73, so agreement must be reported as ICC/Bland-Altman, not correlation. — [Barb 2026 [A]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrad.2025.112609)
- Dominance: the earlier notes found no study linking coronary tortuosity to dominance; Zebić Mihić 2023 reports tortuous arteries and left dominance as separate predictors of non-obstructive CAD (OR 7.96 and significant, respectively). — [Zebić Mihić 2023b [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10534717/)
- Disease direction depends on endpoint: coronary tortuosity negatively correlated with CAD (OR 0.755) and positively with hypertension (OR 1.533) in 2D angiography. — [Li 2011 [A]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/)

### Inferences
- Realistic validation ladder on ImageCAS-X for a merged metric, in order of cost: (1) synthetic suite; (2) ℓ/smoothing rank stability (Spearman) on train split; (3) jitter ICC; (4) Ramírez-style compositional consistency, which specifically tests the Grisan-style aggregation step that the merge introduces; (5) extractor agreement (ICC + Bland-Altman) reference vs CAS-Net centerlines; (6) Image Quality stratum: values and ICCs should not shift with Image Quality; (7) blind ranking of ~30 vessels by ≥2 raters with their own κ reported, since single-rater validation (Grisan 2008) is weak and multi-rater κ can be 0.22.
- Dominance can serve as a weak construct check only for RCA/LCx length and course (a dominant vessel is longer and wraps further), which mostly tests length normalisation (criterion 6), not tortuosity validity. There is no literature-backed expected direction.
- Disease should stay closed until the definition is locked; afterwards the only literature-backed expectations are "inverse with obstructive CAD" and "positive with non-obstructive CAD" (2D angiographic sources), which is the planned analysis, not a validation.
- A small CFD subset (e.g., reproduce Kashyap's LM bifurcation protocol on ~30 no-CAD ImageCAS-X cases) is the only way to test the merged metric against the reference that currently favours κ_a. Given R² ≈ 0.1 to 0.2 at n = 127, a subset of 30 would have no power to separate metrics; it could only check the sign. Not recommended as a selection tool.
