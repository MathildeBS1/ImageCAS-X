# Vascular Tortuosity Quantification Outside Coronary CTA: Retinal, Cerebral/Intracranial, Carotid, and Renal Imaging

## What tortuosity indices and tree-level aggregation methods are used in retinal vascular imaging?

### Takeaway
Retinal imaging is the field with the most mature tree-level tortuosity mathematics: single-vessel curvature-integral indices (Hart, Grisan tortuosity density) are well defined, and at least one paper (the "bridging local and global tortuosity" study) gives explicit, tested formulas for turning many per-segment tortuosities into one whole-tree number, with five candidate weighting schemes ranked by how well they reproduce expert/geometric ground truth. Most clinical tools (VAMPIRE, RISA, ROPtool, CAIAR) instead report per-vessel or per-image aggregate statistics (mean/whole-tree composition) without publishing a single closed-form tree formula.

### Cited Findings
- Curvature-integral single-vessel indices: Hart et al. proposed the integral of vessel centerline curvature and the integral of curvature squared along the vessel path; the squared-curvature version most closely matched ophthalmologists' subjective concept of tortuosity — [A narrative review of retinal vascular parameters (Part I)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10679626/)
- Retinal vascular tortuosity is commonly defined as "the integral of the curvature square along the path of the vessel, normalized by the total path length" — [A New Retest-Stable Tortuosity Metric for Retinal Vessel Analyses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500049/)
- Grisan's tortuosity density (τ) decomposes a vessel into "turn curves" (sections of constant curvature sign) and is computed (one literature formulation) as Tg = [(n−1)/Lc] · Σᵢ(Lᶜᵢ/Lˣᵢ − 1), where n is the number of turn/sub-segments, Lc is the chord length of the whole segment, and Lᶜᵢ/Lˣᵢ are the arc/chord lengths of sub-segment i; Grisan's τ was reported as the measure most accurate at reproducing ophthalmologist perception, outperforming other measures tested — [Bridging local and global tortuosity of retinal vessels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)
- The same paper gives exact **local** indices built from: chord D = √[(x(t₁)−x(t₀))²+(y(t₁)−y(t₀))²]; curve length L(C) = ∫ₜ₀^t₁ √[x′(t)²+y′(t)²] dt; total curvature TK(C) = ∫ₜ₀^t₁ |κ(t)|√[x′(t)²+y′(t)²] dt; total squared curvature TSK(C) = ∫ₜ₀^t₁ κ²(t)√[x′(t)²+y′(t)²] dt; and eight derived local indices (DF = L/D; T1 = L/D − 1; T2 = TK; T3 = TSK; T4 = TK/L; T5 = TSK/L; T6 = TK/D; T7 = TSK/D) — [Bridging local and global tortuosity of retinal vessels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)
- **Tree-level aggregation formulas (the key generalizable result):** given N vessel sections with local tortuosity τ(Cᵢ), length L(Cᵢ), and chord D(Cᵢ), the paper defines and tests five global-combination schemes: Mean Tortuosity, τ_MT(C) = Σᵢτ(Cᵢ)/N; Weighted Additivity on Length, τ_WAL(C) = Σᵢ[L(Cᵢ)·τ(Cᵢ)] / Σᵢ L(Cᵢ); Mean Tortuosity with Weighted Additivity, τ_MTWA(C) = Σᵢ[L(Cᵢ)·τ(Cᵢ)] / [N·Σᵢ L(Cᵢ)]; Weighted Additivity on Chord, τ_WAC(C) = Σᵢ[D(Cᵢ)·τ(Cᵢ)] / Σᵢ D(Cᵢ); and Tortuosity Density Global, τ_TDG(C) = Σᵢτ(Cᵢ) / [N·Σᵢ L(Cᵢ)] — [Bridging local and global tortuosity of retinal vessels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)
- Validation of aggregation quality is done via two mathematical self-consistency tests rather than only correlation with humans: the **Hart inequality**, min(τ(C₁),τ(C₂)) ≤ τ(C) ≤ max(τ(C₁),τ(C₂)), and the **Grisan inequality**, max(τ(C₁),τ(C₂),τ(C₃)) ≤ τ(C), applied to an unbranched vessel split into sub-segments vs. measured whole ("vessel compositionality" testing) — [Bridging local and global tortuosity of retinal vessels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)
- Empirically, against Spearman-rank correlation with ophthalmologist rankings on the BioImLab RET-TORT dataset (30 artery segments), the WAL and WAC weighting schemes combined with local indices T4 (TK/L) or T6 (TK/D) achieved the highest correlations (ρ ≥ 0.860) — [Bridging local and global tortuosity of retinal vessels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)
- The global (whole-retina) tortuosity in one computational framework is explicitly described as "the weighted composition of the tortuosity values computed for each vessel composing the retinal vascular tree," with per-vessel tortuosity first computed and then integrated into one number for the whole retina; the paper reports that anatomical covariates (artery vs. vein, distance to optic disc, distance to fovea, vessel caliber) modulate this — [Computational assessment of the retinal vascular tortuosity integrating domain-related information](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934469/) — **note: the exact weighting formula could not be verified from the full text (login-walled); this claim is from the PMC abstract/summary only.**
- VAMPIRE (Vessel Assessment and Measurement Platform for Images of the REtina) is a semi-automatic platform that extracts vessel width, branching coefficients, and tortuosity at scale for large fundus-image cohorts, used across multiple international studies linking retinal vasculature to systemic disease — [VAMPIRE: Vessel assessment and measurement platform](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/VAMPIRE:-Vessel-assessment-and-measurement-platform-Perez-Rovira-MacGillivray/29d231be0c9afe0b7381bc1c3f7f904ee31f4830)
- RISA (Retinal Image multiScale Analysis), used for ROP plus-disease diagnosis, reports two per-vessel measures: **Tortuosity Index** (vessel length ÷ straight-line distance between endpoints) and **Integrated Curvature** (cumulative sum of angles between successive tangent vectors along the vessel, normalized by vessel length) — [Comparison of RISA parameters](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Comparison-of-Retinal-Image-MultiScale-Analysis-RISA-system-parameters-tortuosity_fig2_50265202) and search-derived summary of the Evaluation of a Computer-Based System for Plus Disease Diagnosis (Ophthalmology, AAO)
- ROPtool is a semi-automated program that objectively measures retinal vascular tortuosity and vessel width for plus-disease assessment in ROP — search-result summary citing ROPtool literature (title only verified; full formula not retrieved)
- A retest-stable alternative, the **Vascular Curvature Index (VCI)**, is described as based on "change in angular momentum" (dL/dt = m·v·r′(t), with L = I·ω, I = r²m) and is explicitly designed to resist segmentation/skeletonization noise; it is reported separately for arteries, veins, and all vessels, with retest reliability Pearson r = 0.89 and Spearman r = 0.87 for "overall vessels," and a skewness of −0.52 in n = 44 healthy retinas (mean age 41.3) — **no explicit tree-aggregation formula or absolute reference range is given** — [A New Retest-Stable Tortuosity Metric](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500049/)
- A four-level whole-image severity grading approach computed 14 per-vessel-segment tortuosity metrics, then aggregated them to **image-level** statistics (mean, min, max across all segments in the fundus image), and trained classifiers (J48 decision tree 92.66%, Rotation Forest 91.19%, Distributed Random Forest 99.42% accuracy) against two ophthalmologists' independent 4-level severity labels (normal/mild/moderate/severe) on 504 images — [Four Severity Levels for Grading the Tortuosity of a Retinal Fundus Image](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605460/)

### Inferences
- The retinal-imaging field has converged on a two-stage pattern directly transferable to coronary trees: (1) compute a per-segment/per-vessel tortuosity index (curvature-integral or arc/chord-based), then (2) combine across all segments/vessels in the tree using a length- or chord-weighted average rather than a simple mean, because length-weighted schemes (WAL/WAC) were empirically closer to expert judgment than an unweighted mean.
- The Hart/Grisan compositionality inequalities are a useful, purely mathematical sanity check that a tree-level aggregation is well-behaved (a whole vessel's tortuosity should bound sub-segment tortuosities appropriately) and could be applied to validate any coronary tree-level score even without new expert grading data.
- For a machine-learning end-goal, the image-level mean/min/max aggregation-then-classifier pattern (14 features → mean/min/max → RF) is a proven, simple recipe for turning heterogeneous per-vessel tortuosity into one usable feature vector.

### Gaps
- The exact tree-level weighting formula in the Nature Scientific Reports "domain-related information" paper could not be verified beyond its abstract summary; the full text sits behind a login wall and was not independently confirmed.
- CAIAR's (Computer-Assisted Image Analysis of the Retina) exact tortuosity formula and its validation statistics in the 10-year-old cohort could not be retrieved (source blocked the fetch); only its existence and use for pediatric normative validation is confirmed by title/search snippet.
- The BMC Medical Research Methodology inter/intra-expert correlation paper (distance metric DM vs. expert grading, correlation coefficients) could not be fetched in full (redirected through an authentication wall); its existence and general topic are confirmed but exact correlation numbers are not.

## What methods quantify tortuosity of the cerebral/intracranial vasculature or carotid siphon in relation to aneurysm or stroke risk, and are any of them whole-tree/whole-circulation composites?

### Takeaway
The cerebrovascular literature almost universally applies the same Bullitt-derived per-vessel-segment metrics (Distance Metric, Sum-of-Angles Metric, Inflection Count Metric, plus Relative Length, Triangular Index, Product-of-Angle-Distance, mean curvature) to **named individual arterial segments** (e.g., ICA C6/C7, MCA M1, ACA A1) — nearly all of these studies report a panel of per-segment/per-side values rather than fusing the whole anterior or posterior circulation into a single composite score; the "novel vessel tortuosity scale" is the closest thing to a tree-wide ordinal severity index but is a manual/visual scoring system, not a closed-form geometric aggregate.

### Cited Findings
- The foundational cross-domain paper defining the three classic centerline-tortuosity metrics used across nearly all subsequent cerebral studies is Bullitt, Gerig, Pizer & Aylward, "Measuring Tortuosity of the Intracerebral Vasculature From MRA Images," IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging 2003;22(9):1163–1171: it defines the **Distance Metric (DM)** = ratio of actual centerline path length to the straight-line distance between endpoints; the **Sum of Angles Metric (SOAM)** = sum of all angles of curvature along the centerline, normalized by vessel length (equivalently, total curvature integrated along the curve, normalized by path length); and the **Inflection Count Metric (ICM)** = (number of 3‑D inflection points, i.e. loci of locally minimal total curvature) × DM, i.e. DM multiplied by the count of sign changes in curvature — [Measuring Tortuosity of the Intracerebral Vasculature From MRA Images](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12956271/); SOAM was found most sensitive to high-frequency, low-amplitude coiling abnormalities — search-derived summary of the same paper
- A "novel vessel tortuosity scale" study defines a **Tortuosity Index (TI) = Distance_actual / Distance_straight** per named vessel (MCA, ACA, BA) and, in parallel, a manual ordinal visual scale (1–4) built by summing counts of "large vessel turns" (>45° deviation) and "small vessel turns" per vessel, then mapping the sum to an ordinal category; both TI and the visual scale significantly predicted cerebral aneurysm (CA) formation (p<0.001) and rupture (p<0.000) in aggregate, but the correlation between TI and CA formation/rupture was significant only for the MCA and not for the other major vessels tested — [Cerebral vascular tortuosity and aneurysm formation and rupture: a novel vessel tortuosity scale](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12919387/)
- A case-control computer-aided study of 54 unruptured MCA-aneurysm patients vs. 54 matched controls quantified MCA trunk tortuosity together with radii, cross-sectional areas, branch asymmetry ratios, branch angles, and hemodynamic parameters as candidate risk factors — [Morphological and Hemodynamic Risk Factors for Middle Cerebral Artery Aneurysm](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7005042/) (title/topic confirmed by search; exact tortuosity formula in the JNS 2018 companion paper could not be fetched — blocked)
- A companion computer-aided analysis found that increased anterior cerebral artery (ACA) tortuosity is associated with increased risk of anterior communicating artery (ACoA) aneurysm development, using Bullitt-style descriptors (figure captions reference "RL = relative length," "SOAM = sum of angle metric") but also with smaller aneurysm dome size once formed — [Increased tortuosity of ACA might be associated with increased risk of ACoA aneurysm development](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6795631/)
- A multi-parametric computer-aided study of bilateral ICA and MCA quantifies **Relative Length (RL)**, **Triangular Index (TI)**, and **mean curvature (κ_mean)** per artery/segment; on the aneurysm-bearing side, TI was higher at the ICA "C6" segment, and RL, TI, and κ_mean were all higher at the "C7" segment versus the non-aneurysm side; multivariate analysis found TI to be an independent risk factor specifically for **aneurysm instability** (not just presence) — [Computer-aided assessment of intracranial arterial tortuosity as a predictor of aneurysm occurrence and instability](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00234-026-03913-0) (retrieved via search synthesis; full-text formulas behind an access wall)
- A related ICA tortuosity/aneurysm study defines **RL** using l (straight-line distance) and lc (absolute path length of the analyzed segment), **Triangular Index (TI)** and **Product of Angle Distance (PAD)** from triangle geometry constructed on the segment's turning angle (sides a, b, c and angle α), and **ICM** as the count of inflection points (d_ni) along the ICA course; comparing ICA-aneurysm to no-aneurysm groups: RL was lower (0.46 vs 0.51, p=0.023), while SOAM (0.39 vs 0.32, p=0.003), PAD (0.38 vs 0.32, p=0.011), TI (0.30 vs 0.27, p=0.034) and ICM (0.30 vs 0.22, p<0.001) were all higher in the aneurysm group; the study explicitly did **not** establish clinical cutoff values, relying on group-comparison statistics instead — [Tortuosity of the Internal Carotid Artery and Its Clinical Significance in the Development of Aneurysms](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406528/)
- For the carotid siphon specifically, one paraclinoid-aneurysm study classifies siphon shape into four morphological types (V, U, C, S; cf. the separate Lang & Reiter A/B/C classification, reported as A 30%/B 40%/C 30% in the general population) and defines three angle parameters at the siphon's bends: posterior angle α, anterior angle β, and the "Clinoid@Ophthalmic" angle γ between the C5 and C6 ICA segments; ROC analysis found β the best discriminator between aneurysmal and non-aneurysmal siphons, with optimal cutoff 18.25° (AUC 0.861, sensitivity 78.6%, specificity 83.3%), versus α cutoff 98.55° (AUC 0.753) and γ cutoff 85.3° (AUC 0.816, specificity 90.5%); smaller angles (sharper bends) were associated with higher aneurysm risk — [Increased Carotid Siphon Tortuosity Is a Risk Factor for Paraclinoid Aneurysms](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9127410/)
- Increased internal carotid artery tortuosity (cervical segment) is reported as a risk factor for spontaneous cervicocerebral artery dissection — [Increased Internal Carotid Artery Tortuosity is a Risk Factor for Spontaneous Cervicocerebral Artery Dissection](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33402322/) (association-level claim; exact metric/threshold not independently verified in this pass)
- Cavernous-segment ICA tortuosity has been linked to technical/clinical outcomes of mechanical thrombectomy in acute ischemic stroke — [Impact of cavernous internal carotid artery tortuosity on outcomes in mechanical thrombectomy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13488311/) (association-level claim only; not independently verified beyond title/topic)

### Inferences
- Unlike retinal imaging, the intracranial/carotid literature has not, in the sources found, produced a validated method that fuses tortuosity across the *whole* cerebral arterial tree (e.g., combining both ICAs, both MCAs, both ACAs, the basilar artery, etc.) into one composite number; instead the near-universal design is a **per-named-segment panel** (often just left vs. right, or bilateral comparison of the same named vessel), analyzed with group-comparison statistics (t-test/Mann-Whitney, ROC, multivariate logistic regression) rather than a single tree-level scalar.
- This is a genuine methodological gap relative to retinal imaging's WAL/WAC aggregation formulas: a coronary tree-level score could not straightforwardly "borrow" a cerebral whole-tree formula because none appears to exist in the fetched sources; what can be borrowed is (a) the per-segment metric definitions themselves (DM/SOAM/ICM/RL/TI/PAD/κ_mean), and (b) the analytic strategy of comparing paired/bilateral segments and using ROC-derived cutoffs (as in the carotid-siphon β-angle example) as a template for defining a "normal" threshold once a tree-level score exists.
- The "novel vessel tortuosity scale" paper's finding that tortuosity-outcome correlation held for the MCA but not other named vessels is a caution: a single tortuosity index does not necessarily generalize across all branches of one tree, which argues for either vessel-role-specific normal ranges or an explicitly weighted composite (as retinal imaging does) rather than a naive average across branches of different anatomical roles.

### Gaps
- No source in this pass described a published whole-cerebral-circulation (e.g. whole Circle of Willis or bilateral full anterior circulation) composite tortuosity score; all found methods are per-segment or per-artery-pair.
- The JNS 2018 middle cerebral artery tortuosity paper and the MDPI/PMC ICA tortuosity papers were access-blocked (403) for their full mathematical appendices; formulas reported above for RL/TI/PAD/κ_mean are reconstructed from figure captions and abstract-level text picked up via search, not verified against the papers' full Methods sections.
- Stroke-risk (as opposed to aneurysm-risk) tortuosity quantification (e.g., for the cervical ICA/dissection or thrombectomy-outcome studies) was only confirmed at the level of topic/association; exact formulas and thresholds for those specific studies were not retrieved in this pass.

## Are there established normal-range / reference-distribution methodologies for tree-level (multi-vessel) tortuosity scores, and how do fields handle combining vessels of different length/role into one score?

### Takeaway
The only field found to have built a large, stratified, population-based normative reference specifically for whole-tree tortuosity metrics is retinal imaging (UK Biobank, N=10,151); it reports six distinct whole-tree tortuosity measures stratified by age decade and sex using mean(SD)/median(IQR), rather than fitting one unified reference distribution across all metrics or combining metrics into a single index. Other fields (cerebral, visceral/Marfan) that measure tortuosity across multiple vessels generally do **not** combine them into one score at all — they report a per-vessel panel and rely on paired/case-control comparison rather than a population reference range.

### Cited Findings
- The UK Biobank-derived normative study measured 10,151 healthy participants using the RMHAS (Retina-based Microvascular Health Assessment System) software, computing tortuosity as a **whole-tree/whole-image aggregate** (not per individual vessel) across six distinct sub-metrics: Tortuosity Density (mean 0.543 arteries / 0.590 veins), Curve Angle (mean 9.71° arteries / 10.1° veins), Fractal Tortuosity (mean 0.84 arteries / 0.838 veins), Inflection Count Tortuosity (mean 0.226 arteries / 0.245 veins), Distance-Based Tortuosity (mean 1.09 arteries / 1.08 veins), and an Angle-Based Tortuosity measure; ranges are stratified by sex and by age decade (40–50, 50–60, >60 years), reported as mean(SD) with median(IQR) shown in supplementary figures — [Population-Based Normative Reference for Retinal Microvascular Atlas](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11950772/) — **the paper's own mathematical formulas for these six metrics were not disclosed in the retrieved text (only described qualitatively; readers are referred to a supplementary table).**
- A separate population study (n > 2,900) reports mean/median retinal arteriolar tortuosity of 2.99×10⁴ / 2.73×10⁴ and venular tortuosity of 4.64×10⁴ / 4.19×10⁴, noting venules are significantly more tortuous than arterioles, and that tortuosity-index distributions from computer-assisted programs are typically **positively skewed**, which is why medians and IQRs (25th/75th percentile) rather than means/SDs are recommended for summarizing them — search-derived synthesis citing retinal tortuosity/blood-pressure literature; exact primary source for the skewness/reporting recommendation not independently re-verified beyond the aggregated search summary
- By contrast, a study of familial retinal arteriolar tortuosity (FRAT) reports a simple **per-vessel-segment mean** compared between FRAT eyes (mean tortuosity 1.1244, range 1.1044–1.1438) and control eyes (mean 1.0818, range 1.0746–1.0872), i.e., a case/control range rather than an age/sex-stratified population reference — [Familial retinal arteriolar tortuosity and quantification using OCTA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6425085/) (values reported per search synthesis of this PMC article)
- The retest-stable VCI metric paper explicitly states it has **no absolute reference range**: in n=44 healthy retinas it only reports the shape of the distribution (skewness −0.52) and retest reliability, not a normal-range cutoff — [A New Retest-Stable Tortuosity Metric for Retinal Vessel Analyses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500049/)
- The Marfan-syndrome visceral-artery tortuosity study is a clear example of a field measuring **multiple, anatomically distinct vessels** (splenic artery, right renal artery, left renal artery) but explicitly choosing **not** to fuse them into one composite score: each artery is analyzed and reported separately (e.g., median DM [IQR]: splenic 1.75 [1.57–2.18] controls vs. 2.44 [1.92–2.80] Marfan, p<0.001; left renal DM 1.13 [1.09–1.23] controls vs. 1.40 [1.29–1.70] Marfan, p<0.001; splenic ICM/SOAM ratio 50.91 [43.19–65.62] controls vs. 73.35 [62.26–93.63] Marfan, p<0.001), using them as a panel of independent diagnostic features rather than one score — [Increased visceral arterial tortuosity in Marfan syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13023-020-01369-w) (values retrieved via a successful authenticated redirect fetch)
- The renal-artery literature found in this pass uses only a **single-vessel** Tortuosity Index (path length ÷ straight-line distance) as a predictor of renal-branch occlusion after branched endovascular aortic aneurysm repair; no whole-kidney or bilateral-renal-tree composite tortuosity score was found — [Tortuosity is the Significant Predictive Factor for Renal Branch Occlusion after Branched EVAR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1078588415006887) (title/topic-level confirmation from search results; full formula not independently fetched)

### Inferences
- No field examined has published a single unified reference-distribution *methodology* explicitly designed for combining unequal-length, different-role vessels into one tree-level scalar with population percentiles; the UK Biobank study comes closest but treats "whole-tree tortuosity" as an already-aggregated deep-learning-derived measure per sub-metric (six separate normative ranges), not as a single fused score with combining-weights disclosed in the retrieved text.
- Where multiple named vessels are measured in the same study (Marfan visceral arteries; bilateral cerebral segments), the consistent practice is to keep them as a **panel of separate, paired features** analyzed with group statistics, rather than averaging or otherwise combining them — this is itself an actionable finding: it suggests that for a first-pass coronary tree-level score, reporting a small per-branch panel (e.g., LAD/LCx/RCA tortuosity computed identically) alongside one aggregate (using a length- or chord-weighted mean per the retinal WAL/WAC precedent) may be more defensible than committing to a single fused number from the outset, since no other field examined found it necessary to prove that fusion is superior to a panel.
- Age/sex stratification (as in the UK Biobank study) and skew-aware reporting (median/IQR, per the retinal literature's stated preference for positively-skewed tortuosity distributions) are the two normative-range design choices best supported across sources, and both should carry over directly to a coronary tree-level normal range.

### Gaps
- The exact formulas for the UK Biobank study's six tortuosity sub-metrics (Tortuosity Density, Curve Angle, Fractal Tortuosity, Inflection Count Tortuosity, Distance-Based Tortuosity, Angle-Based Tortuosity) were referenced as being in a supplementary table that was not retrieved in this pass — these are worth chasing down directly if the report writer wants exact math rather than descriptive statistics.
- No reference-distribution methodology paper specifically for cerebral/intracranial or renal tree-level tortuosity was found; this appears to be a genuine gap in the literature (or at least was not surfaced by the queries run), not just a retrieval failure — both fields' tortuosity papers found here are case-control or single-cohort comparisons, not population-normative studies.

## Have these fields published machine-learning risk-prediction models using tortuosity-derived features, and what exact features were engineered?

### Takeaway
Confirmed, verified ML applications of tortuosity features exist mainly in retinal image severity grading (14 tortuosity metrics aggregated to per-image mean/min/max, fed to tree-based classifiers) and, more tentatively, in intracranial aneurysm-rupture prediction and ROP plus-disease deep learning; however, most of the aneurysm/rupture-risk ML literature surfaced in this pass used tortuosity only as one variable among many morphological/hemodynamic/radiomic features rather than a family of engineered tortuosity-specific features (percentiles, PCA components, etc.), and the exact feature engineering could often not be verified beyond search-result summaries.

### Cited Findings
- The clearest, best-verified example: for retinal fundus tortuosity severity grading, 14 per-vessel-segment tortuosity metrics were computed and then aggregated to **image-level features via mean, min, and max across all vessel segments in the image** (a simple statistical-aggregation feature-engineering step), and these image-level feature vectors were classified by J48 Decision Tree (92.66% accuracy), Rotation Forest (91.19%), and Distributed Random Forest (99.42% accuracy, RMSE 0.00000194) against 4-level ophthalmologist ground truth on 504 images — [Four Severity Levels for Grading the Tortuosity of a Retinal Fundus Image](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605460/)
- For ROP plus-disease diagnosis, conventional pipelines use handcrafted features including vessel tortuosity, dilation, and density from various retinal regions as discriminative inputs to classifiers, while more recent pipelines instead use deep learning (e.g., GoogLeNet-based) directly on preprocessed/harmonized fundus images, reporting accuracy 99.3%, precision 98.8%, recall 99.1%, F1 99% for detecting plus disease — [Automated diagnosis of plus disease in retinopathy of prematurity using quantification of vessel characteristics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10944526/) (values as reported in search synthesis of this PMC article; exact tortuosity feature list within the handcrafted pipeline not independently re-verified)
- For intracranial aneurysm rupture-status prediction, a comparison of four ML algorithms (Random Forest, XGBoost, SVM, Multi-Layer Perceptron) on combined clinical and radiographic features found Random Forest achieved the highest accuracy (85%) — [Comparison of Ruptured Intracranial Aneurysms Identification Using Different ML Algorithms and Radiomics](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10453422/) — **whether tortuosity specifically was one of the input features, and how it was engineered, could not be confirmed from the retrieved summary; this is listed as a gap below.**
- Aneurysm-growth prediction models more broadly are reported to incorporate tortuosity alongside diameter, intraluminal-thrombus thickness, wall shear stress, and radiomic features, using SVM, CNN, and gradient-boosting model families — [Current state-of-the-art and utilities of machine learning for... AAA management](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666496822000152) — this source concerns **abdominal aortic aneurysms**, outside the requested scope (retinal/cerebral/carotid/renal), and is included only as a cross-field analogy; flagged explicitly as scope-adjacent, not a direct answer.
- A statistical shape model (SSM) built via PCA on ascending-aorta shape found that its **second principal component mode primarily describes the curvature/tortuosity of the ascending aorta**, i.e., tortuosity emerges as a natural PCA axis of shape variation rather than a hand-computed index, and this PCA-derived shape descriptor was then used to regress/predict aneurysm growth rate — [A Machine Learning Approach to Investigate Shape Features and Predicted Risk of Ascending Aortic Aneurysm](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5630492/) — again an **aortic**, not retinal/cerebral/carotid/renal, example; included as the one directly-confirmed instance of "PCA components of tortuosity" the key questions asked about, but scope-adjacent.
- An intracranial vessel age-prediction ML paper title (predicting age from intracranial vessel tortuosity and thickness information) was located but its full text could not be retrieved (access-blocked); its existence indicates that tortuosity + thickness feature vectors have been used as ML inputs for a biological (age) prediction target in the cerebral vasculature, but the exact engineered features and model performance are unverified — [Assessing Machine Learning Models for Predicting Age with Intracranial Vessel Tortuosity and Thickness Information](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci13111512) (title-only; content not independently confirmed)

### Inferences
- The one fully-verified ML pipeline (retinal 4-level severity grading) supports a specific, transferable feature-engineering recipe for a tree-level ML input: compute several tortuosity indices per vessel/segment, then take simple summary statistics (mean/min/max, and by extension percentiles) across all segments in the tree as the ML feature vector — this avoids having to solve the "one true tree-level formula" problem before doing any ML, since a random-forest-style model can implicitly learn the right combination.
- Outside retinal imaging, the evidence in this pass suggests tortuosity is usually just **one covariate among several** morphological/hemodynamic variables in cerebrovascular and aortic rupture/growth prediction, rather than the primary feature family with dedicated engineering (percentile binning, PCA) — with the singular exception of the ascending-aorta PCA shape model, where tortuosity emerges as a PCA mode rather than being separately engineered.
- Because several of the highest-value ML papers for this question (intracranial rupture-prediction feature lists, the intracranial age-prediction paper) were access-blocked, this catalogue should be treated as a partial rather than exhaustive account of tortuosity-based ML risk models outside retinal imaging; a follow-up pass with institutional access to Neurosurgery/Radiology and MDPI's brainsci journal would likely surface exact feature lists.

### Gaps
- The exact tortuosity-derived input features (raw indices vs. percentiles vs. PCA components) used in the intracranial aneurysm-rupture RF/XGBoost/SVM/MLP comparison (PMC10453422) could not be confirmed — only that "clinical and radiographic features" were used and RF reached 85% accuracy.
- The intracranial vessel age-prediction paper's exact tortuosity feature engineering and ML architecture could not be retrieved (403 on both the DOI redirect and the resolved MDPI URL).
- No stroke-risk (as opposed to aneurysm-risk) ML model using tortuosity-derived features was found for the carotid or cerebral circulation in this pass.
- No renal-artery ML risk-prediction model using tortuosity features (whole-kidney or otherwise) was found; the renal tortuosity literature located here is limited to a single geometric ratio used in surgical-planning risk assessment (branch occlusion after EVAR), not an ML model.
