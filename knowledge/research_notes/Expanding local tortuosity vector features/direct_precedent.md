# Direct precedent for single- vs multi-channel local geometric profiles fed to learned sequence models predicting vascular/cardiac outcomes

## Key Question 1: Beyond Tello Ayala et al., are there other studies using per-point/per-segment sequential geometric profiles as input to a learned sequence model (CNN, RNN, transformer, GNN) for a coronary or other vascular outcome? What input channels did they use, and did they justify the choice?

### Takeaway
One directly comparable precedent was found outside the coronary/CAD-prediction setting: a retinal cardiovascular-risk transformer (HVAAT module) that tokenizes vessel segments as a 4-channel vector (radius, bifurcation angle, segment length, tortuosity) and passes the ordered token sequence through a transformer encoder. No paper found combines curvature with torsion specifically as multiple channels in a learned sequence model for a clinical outcome; most true multi-channel profile work is in hemodynamic surrogate modeling (predicting WSS/pressure fields), not clinical outcome classification.

### Cited Findings
- Tello Ayala et al. (2026, JACC Adv, PMC13308244) confirmed in full text: the transformer is "a local-attention neural network transformer that operates on the ordered vector of local tortuosity along the artery... together with age and sex." The single geometric channel is discrete curvature (turning angle at each skeleton point); no other channel (radius, torsion, branch angle) is included, and no ablation or justification for the single-channel choice appears in the text — [PMC13308244 full text via Europe PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772963X26002504), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42312790/)
- The logistic-regression comparator in the same paper used only "mean tortuosity, age, and sex" — a global scalar reduction of the same single channel, not a different/richer channel set — [PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)
- An explainable retinal deep-learning framework for cardiovascular risk stratification (PMC13330239) contains an "HVAAT" (hierarchical vessel-attribute attention transformer) module that builds a per-segment attribute tensor A_i = [r_i, α_i, l_i, t_i] ∈ R⁴ — radius, bifurcation angle, segment length, and tortuosity — embeds each segment as a token, and passes the sequence of tokens through a 2-layer, 4-head transformer encoder (embedding size 64) to produce "interpretable risk components per vessel attribute" feeding a cardiovascular risk score — [PMC13330239](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13330239/)
- In that same retinal paper, tortuosity and diameter are described as computed via "a curvature-based algorithm and local width filters" respectively, but the exact curvature formula, and any ablation isolating single- vs multi-attribute performance, is not present in the retrievable full text — [PMC13330239](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13330239/)
- A deep-learning wall-shear-stress (WSS) surrogate for stenosed/bifurcating coronary arteries used vessel radius, bifurcation angles, and curvature as combined geometric input channels to a network predicting a hemodynamic field (TAWSS), not a clinical outcome — this is a learned model consuming multiple geometric channels together, but the "outcome" is a physics field, not disease status — [Frontiers in Bioeng. and Biotech.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2024.1360330/full), [PMC11345599](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11345599/)
- A related geometric-deep-learning benchmark ("GReAT") for artery WSS assessment reports an input-feature ablation identifying surface normals, geodesics, vessel radius, inflow, and inlet pressure as important channels, and states that adding point coordinates and surface-normal features "significantly increased evaluation accuracy" — again a hemodynamic-field prediction task, not CAD/clinical-outcome classification — [arXiv:2508.19030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19030)
- A 3D-centerline unsupervised Hidden Markov Model for coronary tortuosity segmentation used curvature plus Lempel-Ziv complexity as combined sequential features over sliding windows, achieving precision 0.87 / recall 0.86 against expert annotation — this is a sequential multi-channel feature approach, but the task is tortuosity-severity segmentation/annotation-matching, not disease prediction, and the model is an HMM (probabilistic, not a trained deep network in the CNN/RNN/transformer/GNN sense) — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-95841-0_27)

### Inferences
- The one true multi-channel profile-as-sequence model that shares Tello Ayala et al.'s architecture family (transformer over an ordered per-segment vector) and targets a cardiovascular clinical outcome is in the retinal domain (HVAAT/PMC13330239), not coronary CT/angiography. Its 4 channels (radius, angle, length, tortuosity) are exactly the kind of expansion the thesis is considering (diameter + branch angle + a curvature-derived quantity), suggesting that when practitioners do go multi-channel, radius/diameter and bifurcation angle are the first additions before tortuosity/curvature, not torsion.
- No paper was found that includes torsion as an explicit input channel to any learned sequence model for a coronary or other vascular clinical outcome. Torsion appears in the literature almost exclusively as a physical/engineering descriptor (e.g., microvessel fabrication) or a hand-computed Frenet quantity in classical geometric-analysis pipelines, not as a channel fed into a trained network for outcome prediction.
- Multi-channel geometric inputs are common when the learned model's target is a physical/hemodynamic field (WSS, pressure) rather than a clinical/disease label; for clinical-outcome prediction specifically, single-channel (curvature/tortuosity-only) profiles remain the norm in the papers retrieved.

### Gaps
- Could not obtain any ablation from the HVAAT/PMC13330239 paper isolating tortuosity-alone vs the full 4-attribute vector; the retrievable full text does not report per-attribute or subset performance, only that the four attributes are used together.
- No coronary-specific (as opposed to retinal) precedent for a multi-channel per-point sequence model predicting CAD or a comparable clinical endpoint was found in this pass.

## Key Question 2: Has anyone published an ablation study for this kind of profile-based model where the number/type of geometric channels is varied and performance is reported for each configuration (e.g., curvature-only vs curvature+torsion vs curvature+torsion+angle+diameter)?

### Takeaway
No ablation study of this exact kind (varying geometric channel count/type in a per-point sequence model for a coronary or vascular clinical outcome) was found. The closest analogues are channel-importance ablations for hemodynamic-field surrogate models (not outcome classifiers), and even those do not sweep curvature/torsion/angle/diameter combinations specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Tello Ayala et al. report no ablation over channel count; the full text (via Europe PMC) states only the single-channel curvature/tortuosity input and does not vary it — [PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)
- The GReAT geometric deep learning benchmark for artery WSS assessment reports an ablation over input features (surface normals, geodesics, vessel radius, inflow, inlet pressure), finding that adding point coordinates and surface-normal features "significantly increased evaluation accuracy" — this is a channel-count/type ablation, but for a WSS surrogate model, not a coronary/vascular disease-outcome classifier, and it does not include curvature, torsion, or bifurcation angle as swept channels — [arXiv:2508.19030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19030)
- Search for "ablation study" combined with coronary tortuosity/curvature feature vectors returned only ablations of network architecture components (e.g., progressively adding architectural modules in a segmentation network) rather than ablations of the geometric channel set itself — [PMC10333289 / Sci Rep 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37868-6), [arXiv:2305.04208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04208)

### Inferences
- The absence of a channel-count ablation in Tello Ayala et al. itself, despite it being the field's most directly relevant precedent, suggests the choice of curvature-only was not empirically justified within that paper; it may be a default/available-feature choice (their pipeline is built on an automated vessel-segmentation-derived curvature measure) rather than a deliberate result of testing alternatives.

### Gaps
- No direct precedent exists for a channel-count/type ablation in a profile-based sequence model predicting a coronary or vascular clinical outcome. This absence should be reported as a genuine literature gap: the thesis's planned curvature-only vs multi-channel comparison would be, as far as this search found, a novel ablation for this outcome-prediction setting, not a replication of an existing published sweep.

## Key Question 3: Outside cardiology, are there analogous "shape profile as sequence input to neural network" precedents in other geometric medical ML domains (retinal vasculature, cerebral aneurysm/aorta geometry, airway tree analysis) where multiple geometric descriptors were combined as channels for a learned model, with reported effect on performance?

### Takeaway
Yes — retinal vasculature (transformer, 4 channels: radius/angle/length/tortuosity) and airway-tree COPD classification (tree kernels over vector-valued branch measurements, e.g. branch radius/shape plus wall-thickness) both combine multiple geometric descriptors as inputs to a learned model and report a performance effect, though airway kernels are a kernel-method rather than a deep sequence network. Aortic-aneurysm and intracranial-aneurysm literature mostly report multi-feature deep learning on 3D meshes/images rather than an explicit ordered 1D per-point channel-vector profile.

### Cited Findings
- Retinal cardiovascular-risk transformer (HVAAT, PMC13330239): per-segment tokens [radius, bifurcation angle, length, tortuosity] fed through a transformer encoder to give per-attribute interpretable risk components — direct multi-channel sequence-model precedent, retinal domain — [PMC13330239](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13330239/)
- Airway-tree COPD classification via "geometric tree kernels" (Springer 2013 / arXiv:1303.7390): kernels operate on anatomical trees "endowed with vector valued measurements made along the tree," explicitly stated to be able to include "branch shape, branch radius or other vector valued properties," and were applied with airway-wall-thickness measurements along the tree, giving "a significant increase in the classification accuracy of COPD from geometric tree structure" versus prior methods — a multi-channel geometric-profile precedent, but the learned/statistical model is a kernel method with kernelized hypothesis testing, not a CNN/RNN/transformer/GNN — [arXiv:1303.7390](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.7390), [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-38868-2_15)
- Vessel centerline profile extraction for general vessel/vascular-tree feature learning (Deep-FExt / related work) uses "13 1D profiles calculated at each sampling point along a vessel branch," including vessel scale, image intensity, centerline curvature, tubularity measure, intensity statistics, gradient statistics, and distance along the branch — a clearly multi-channel per-point profile, but the downstream task is vessel segmentation/centerline extraction itself, not a clinical outcome — [arXiv:1704.03743](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.03743)
- Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) literature: geometric deep learning models (e.g., SE(3)-symmetric transformer) predict AAA growth "directly on the vascular model surface enriched with local, multi-physical features," and mean centerline curvature is reported as "the only significant predictor of peak wall stress and subsequent rupture risk" in a separate geometric-parameter study — these show multi-feature geometric deep learning for AAA, but as mesh/surface-based models rather than an explicit ordered per-point channel-vector profile analogous to the coronary curvature vector — [arXiv:2506.08729](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08729), aneurysm rupture-risk geometric parameter findings via search snippet (specific primary source not independently verified in full text this pass)
- Intracranial aneurysm rupture prediction (TransIAR net, PMC10140611/ScienceDirect S2666389923000466) uses a multiscale 3D CNN plus a transformer encoder over 3D CTA-derived structural/morphological features of the aneurysm and its neighborhood — combines multiple morphological descriptors through a learned (CNN+transformer) pipeline, but again on 3D image/mesh data rather than an explicit 1D ordered geometric-channel vector — [PMC10140611](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10140611/)

### Inferences
- Across domains, when a learned sequence/graph model does combine several geometric descriptors, the combination set most often chosen is (radius/diameter, angle, some length/position measure, tortuosity or curvature) — torsion is essentially absent as a chosen channel in every domain surveyed here, cardiac or otherwise.
- The exact structural analogue the thesis is proposing (ordered 1D per-point vector, multiple geometric channels, transformer, coronary/vascular clinical outcome) has its closest existing precedent in the retinal HVAAT model, not in any coronary-artery-specific paper.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from retrievable full text whether the airway tree-kernel COPD study's multi-channel result (wall thickness + branch shape) was compared against a single-channel-only version in a controlled ablation; the search snippet reports overall gain "in comparison with state-of-the-art methods," which may not isolate the channel-count effect specifically.
- No AAA or intracranial-aneurysm paper found in this pass reports an explicit 1D ordered-profile (per-point along centerline) transformer/CNN comparable in structure to Tello Ayala et al.; these domains work mostly at the mesh/surface or whole-image level.

## Key Question 4: Is there any indication of a consensus "recommended feature set" for coronary/vascular tortuosity or curvature-based CAD prediction models, distinguishing essential vs optional/exploratory features (with torsion flagged as mechanistically plausible but rarely measured)?

### Takeaway
There is no consensus standardized feature set; reviews explicitly say the field lacks agreement on a rigorous tortuosity metric. Torsion is specifically noted in the literature as a real Frenet-frame quantity distinct from curvature, but is described as rarely quantified for coronary arteries specifically (more often used for other vascular beds), consistent with the thesis's own framing of torsion as exploratory rather than established.

### Cited Findings
- "The field has not arrived at a consensus on a rigorous, standardized tortuosity metric," and multiple different tortuosity metrics (tortuosity index = arc length / chord length; absolute-curvature measure; RMS curvature; average squared-derivative-curvature) are used inconsistently across studies, which the review attributes as a likely contributor to "inconsistent and contradictory" reported clinical associations — [Scientific Reports, "Accuracy of vascular tortuosity measures using computational modelling"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-04796-w)
- Curvature-based measures (e.g., average-absolute-curvature) "showed stronger correlation with low wall shear stress" than non-curvature-based measures, while "the tortuosity index, the most widely used measure in the literature, showed no significant correlation to low TAWSS" — evidence that among existing scalar metrics, curvature-family measures are favored over pure arc-length-ratio tortuosity for mechanistic (WSS) relevance — [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-04796-w)
- "Quantification of tortuosity is widely used for different arteries but rarely for coronary arteries, which could be due to the complexity of coronary circulation affected by cyclic systolic and diastolic movements of the heart" — direct statement that coronary-specific tortuosity/torsion quantification lags behind other vascular beds — (source snippet from search aggregation of coronary tortuosity index/angle-measurement literature; exact originating paper not independently re-verified via full-text fetch in this pass — treat as moderate-confidence)
- Centerline geometric feature sets described in coronary machine-learning pipelines explicitly separate "curvature (bending), calculated using Frenet curvature" from "tortuosity (non-planarity), calculated using Frenet torsion" — i.e., in classical differential-geometry-based coronary feature engineering, torsion is a named, distinct, computable quantity (not merely "mechanistically plausible but unmeasured"), but it appears in feature-engineering/registration contexts rather than as an input to a learned outcome-prediction sequence model — [search aggregation citing Frenet curvature/torsion coronary centerline feature methodology; primary source not independently fetched this pass]
- Bifurcation-lesion classical/interpretable ML models for CAD (PMC12184847) use scalar bifurcation features (e.g., "exponent of vessel diameter" and "area expansion ratio") identified as key discriminative features — this is indirect precedent (classical multivariable/interpretable ML on hand-engineered scalar features per lesion, not a per-point sequence/profile model) — [PMC12184847](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12184847/)

### Inferences
- No published "recommended feature set" exists for coronary tortuosity/curvature CAD prediction, whether for classical regression or learned sequence models; the field is still fragmented across at least four distinct scalar tortuosity definitions, before even reaching the question of multi-channel profiles.
- Given that (a) curvature-based measures already outperform tortuosity-index in mechanistic (WSS) correlation, and (b) torsion is essentially unused in coronary-specific ML or clinical-outcome prediction contexts, adding torsion as a channel would be a genuinely exploratory contribution with no existing outcome-prediction result to benchmark against, positive or negative, in either direction. Diameter and bifurcation angle have more precedent as "next channels to add" (via the retinal HVAAT and coronary bifurcation-lesion literature) than torsion does.

### Gaps
- Two findings above (coronary-specific-rarity-of-torsion statement; Frenet curvature/torsion coronary feature-engineering statement) were only confirmed via search-engine synthesis snippets, not independently verified against the primary full text, because those specific source articles were not directly fetched in this research pass (time/tool-call budget). These should be treated as moderate- rather than high-confidence and re-verified with a direct full-text read if they are to be cited in the thesis at "essential vs optional" level of specificity.
- No systematic review or benchmarking paper specifically comparing feature-set choices across multiple coronary/vascular tortuosity-based ML studies (i.e., a review that itself catalogs "study X used channels {a,b}, study Y used {c,d}...") was located; the "consensus" answer here is inferred by this researcher from triangulating several individual papers, not read directly from a review that makes this comparison explicit.
