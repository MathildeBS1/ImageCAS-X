# LAD Feasibility: Torsion, Bifurcation Angle, Diameter, and Other Topological Descriptors

## Anatomical branch density: how many named branch points does the LAD have vs the RCA?

### Takeaway
The LAD anatomically gives off more named side branches along its course than the RCA (multiple diagonals plus multiple septal perforators, vs. the RCA's handful of named branches), so bifurcation points are geometrically more numerous along the LAD. However, the CAD-*outcome* literature does not track angle at each of these bifurcations — it concentrates almost entirely on one or two proximal bifurcations (LAD-LCx and, less often, LM-LAD), so the density of *evidence-backed* angle features is much narrower than the density of anatomical branch points.

### Cited Findings
- The LAD gives off two branch types along its course: diagonal branches (running epicardially, supplying the lateral LV wall and anterolateral papillary muscle) and septal perforators (running perpendicular into the septum, supplying the anterior two-thirds of the interventricular septum); in most people the first septal perforator precedes the first diagonal branch as the LAD's first branch — [Wikipedia: Left anterior descending artery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_anterior_descending_artery)
- Aggregated web search summary (not independently verified against a single primary source) reports diagonal branches are usually 1-5 and septal perforators usually 1-6 per LAD — this is an AI-generated synthesis of multiple anatomy pages (IMAIOS, STS database) rather than a directly quoted figure from one paper, so treat the exact range with caution — [IMAIOS e-Anatomy: Proximal LAD artery](https://www.imaios.com/en/e-anatomy/anatomical-structures/proximal-lad-artery-121133752), [IMAIOS e-Anatomy: LAD septal perforators](https://www.imaios.com/en/e-anatomy/anatomical-structures/lad-septal-perforators-121133476), [STS Adult Cardiac Surgery Database Training Manual](https://www.sts.org/sites/default/files/documents/CoronaryAnatomy.pdf)
- The AATS TSRA coronary anatomy primer describes RCA branches only qualitatively ("several branches"), naming conus branch, SA nodal artery, then right ventricular branches, without giving a count comparable to the LAD's diagonal/septal enumeration — [AATS TSRA Primer: Coronary Artery Anatomy](https://www.aats.org/tsra-primer-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- Despite this anatomical richness, the CAD-association literature located for this review measures bifurcation angle almost exclusively at the LAD-LCx bifurcation (and, in a smaller body of work, the LM-LAD angle) — a systematic review of 13 eligible articles on left coronary bifurcation angle and CAD studied the LAD-LCx angle specifically and did not treat diagonal-branch takeoff angles as a separate studied endpoint — [Evaluation of the Relationship between Left Coronary Artery Bifurcation Angle and Coronary Artery Disease: A Systematic Review, PMC9457427](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9457427/)
- One IVUS comparison study explicitly frames the LAD/first-diagonal bifurcation as a common surrogate lesion model, but for procedural (bifurcation stenting) comparison rather than as a geometric CAD-risk biomarker — [An intravascular ultrasound comparison of LAD/first diagonal branch versus distal left main coronary artery bifurcation lesions, EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/an-intravascular-ultrasound-comparison-of-left-anterior-descending-artery-first-diagonal-branch-versus-distal-left-main-coronary-artery-bifurcation-lesions)

### Inferences
- Anatomically, bifurcation angle *could* be sampled at several points along the LAD (each diagonal and septal takeoff), unlike the RCA where named branch points are genuinely sparse. This is a real structural difference favoring LAD over RCA for a per-point bifurcation-angle channel, purely from a "how many branch points exist to sample" standpoint.
- But the outcome evidence does not follow this anatomical density: essentially all CAD-association angle literature for the LAD locks onto the single LAD-LCx bifurcation (sometimes the LM-LAD angle upstream of it), not a running series of diagonal-takeoff angles. So while the *feature could be computed* at many points along the LAD, only the angle values near the LAD ostium/LAD-LCx bifurcation currently have any CAD-outcome literature behind them. A per-point bifurcation-angle channel along the whole LAD would therefore be anatomically well-motivated but only evidence-backed near the proximal segment, not along the full centerline.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found giving a precise, directly-quoted range (e.g., "on average N diagonal branches") from a single anatomic study; the 1-5 diagonal / 1-6 septal figures come from an aggregated search summary, not a verified citation. A dedicated anatomy/imaging paper (e.g., cadaveric or CCTA branch-counting study) would be needed to firm this up.
- No study was found that computes or evaluates bifurcation angle at each diagonal takeoff along the LAD's full length (as opposed to just the single most-proximal LAD-LCx bifurcation) in relation to CAD, stenosis, or plaque.

## LAD-specific tortuosity/curvature/torsion and CAD/stenosis/plaque burden: is LAD better- or worse-studied than RCA?

### Takeaway
The LAD is confirmed as more heavily studied for geometric (curvature/torsion/tortuosity) CAD biomarkers than the RCA, including at least one head-to-head geometry comparison paper and a recent LAD-specific nomogram using curvature/torsion/tortuosity index as predictors of stenosis etiology; it is plausible to call the LAD the best-studied vessel for coronary geometry among the three major arteries, though this review did not find an explicit LCx or RCA equivalent nomogram to confirm the LAD's outright primacy.

### Cited Findings
- A comparative angiographic geometry study of 67 angiographically normal coronary arteries (32 LAD, 35 RCA; no LCx) from 52 patients used cubic polynomial fitting on 3-D reconstructions from biplane cineangiography to compute curvature, torsion, and tortuosity (tortuosity = 1 − chord length/arc length) at each centerline point; it found LAD mean torsion 3.73 cm⁻¹ vs RCA 2.84 cm⁻¹ (P<0.0001), LAD maximum torsion 31.16 cm⁻¹ vs RCA 20.44 cm⁻¹ (P<0.005), i.e. the LAD has significantly *higher* torsion than the RCA, while overall tortuosity index was higher for RCA than LAD in this cohort — [Cataloguing the geometry of the human coronary arteries: a potential tool for predicting risk of coronary artery disease, PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- The same study found that within the LAD, curvature, torsion, and tortuosity are all highest in the distal segment (torsion approx. 3.31 cm⁻¹ proximal, 3.36 cm⁻¹ mid, 3.82 cm⁻¹ distal in the extraction obtained), i.e. a systematic proximal-to-distal increase along the LAD's own length — [Cataloguing the geometry of the human coronary arteries, PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- A CT-based study specifically on the LAD found tortuosity (vessel-length-to-straight-line-distance ratio) is associated with hypertension, but — per the abstract-level search summary — was *not* independently related to physical performance; this paper's title and abstract focus on hypertension/physical performance rather than CAD/stenosis outcomes directly, and full text could not be retrieved (blocked by a bot-check page) — [Tortuosity of the left anterior descending artery is associated with hypertension and is not independently related to physical performance: A cardiac computed tomography study, PubMed 39210716](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39210716/)
- A retrospective cross-sectional CT study (n=133, 26 with >50% proximal LAD stenosis) found a combined tortuosity-and-angle metric predicted severe proximal LAD stenosis: measuring d20 (chord length across a 20 mm curved LAD segment via central-luminal-line software) and cosθ (cosine of the LM-LAD angle), the product d20×cosθ <15.5 had OR 11.36 (P<0.001) for severe proximal LAD stenosis, AUC 0.767, sensitivity 80.8%/specificity 71.0%; hypertension (OR 4.54) and pack-years (OR 1.04) were co-predictors. This study covered proximal LAD only, was single-center/retrospective with a small stenosis subgroup (n=26), and explicitly did not study RCA or LCx — [Predicting severe proximal LAD coronary artery stenosis using proximal LAD tortuosity and the LM-LAD angle, Quantitative Imaging in Medicine and Surgery](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/117032/html)
- A recent (2026, per PubMed ID metadata) nomogram paper reportedly derives curvature, torsion, and tortuosity index (plus other geometric morphological parameters and accessory-structure features) from CCTA to predict "heterogeneous etiology of atherosclerosis" specifically in the LAD; full text could not be retrieved (bot-check blocked access), so sample size, effect sizes, and exact predictor list are unconfirmed beyond the abstract-level search summary — [A nomogram for predicting heterogeneous etiology of atherosclerosis in the left anterior descending coronary artery using geometric morphological parameters, PubMed 40043631](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40043631/)
- A search-engine synthesis (not independently verified against one paper) characterizes the LAD as "the most significant coronary vessel... distinctively longer, more tortuous, and more frequently affected by atherosclerotic disease than other coronary arteries," consistent with, but not proof beyond, the PMC2759354 torsion/tortuosity comparison above.
- Curvature-plaque relationships have direct IVUS/angiography evidence in the LAD specifically: a classic study (referenced in search results, not independently fetched in full) examined plaque development, vessel curvature, and wall shear stress in coronary arteries via X-ray angiography plus IVUS, and other IVUS work specifically maps atherosclerotic plaque distribution within the LAD by location — [Plaque development, vessel curvature, and wall shear stress in coronary arteries assessed by X-ray angiography and intravascular ultrasound, PMC2590653](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2590653/); [Atherosclerotic plaque distribution in the left anterior descending coronary artery as assessed by intravascular ultrasound, American Journal of Cardiology](https://www.ajconline.org/article/S0002-9149(02)03242-3/abstract)
- Older physiologic work specifically located plaque preferentially at the LAD wall opposite the circumflex origin near the LM bifurcation, attributed to local low/oscillatory wall shear stress from the bifurcation geometry — [Relation of atherosclerosis to arterial wall shear in the left anterior descending coronary artery of man, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0002870386905053)

### Inferences
- Taken together, the LAD does appear to be the most geometrically-characterized coronary artery in the literature surveyed: it has (a) a head-to-head curvature/torsion/tortuosity comparison against the RCA, (b) at least one dedicated tortuosity+angle stenosis-prediction study, (c) an apparently recent dedicated nomogram using curvature/torsion/tortuosity index together, and (d) LAD-specific plaque-location/wall-shear-stress work going back decades. No equivalent breadth (nomogram, dedicated nonspecific stenosis-prediction paper) was found for the RCA in this pass, which supports treating LAD as better-studied for geometric CAD biomarkers than RCA, though this is an absence-of-evidence rather than a proof of non-existence for RCA.
- The LAD-specific finding that curvature/torsion/tortuosity are highest distally (not proximally, where most stenosis-prediction work has concentrated) suggests that if the CGPS pipeline wants a per-point torsion or curvature channel, the distal LAD is where these signals will show maximum dynamic range, but that is also the segment likely to have thinner/noisier centerline reconstructions (small caliber vessel, more susceptible to registration/segmentation noise) — a tension worth flagging given the RCA torsion noise-sensitivity conclusion in the parent research.

### Gaps
- Two of the more directly relevant recent papers (the LAD hypertension/physical-performance tortuosity study, and the LAD nomogram with curvature/torsion/tortuosity index) could not be fully retrieved past bot-check pages; only abstract-level or search-summary content is reported here for those two. Their exact effect sizes, whether torsion specifically (not just tortuosity index) was independently predictive, and sample sizes remain unconfirmed.
- No equivalent geometry-cataloguing study (curvature+torsion+tortuosity, segment-by-segment) was found for the LCx for comparison; only LAD vs RCA was directly compared in PMC2759354.
- Whether the LAD's geometry papers use "torsion" in the strict Frenet-Serret sense (third derivative) versus a looser "tortuosity index" (arc/chord ratio) varies by paper; readers should not conflate the two purely by keyword. Only PMC2759354 among the sources found here explicitly computes Frenet-Serret torsion, in cm⁻¹ units, for the LAD.

## LAD luminal diameter along its course: tapering, typical range, diagonal-takeoff effects, and use as predictor vs. normalizer

### Takeaway
The LAD tapers substantially and roughly linearly from proximal to distal segments (approx. 3.7±0.4 mm proximal to 1.9±0.4 mm distal, per one normal-anatomy reference), and diameter is used both as a direct outcome-relevant predictor (absolute lumen area/diameter outperforming %stenosis for predicting functional significance) and implicitly as a normalizing factor at branch takeoffs in quantitative coronary angiography (QCA) bifurcation software; direct LAD-specific outcome evidence parallel to the RCA's FFR-CT diameter study was not clearly located in this pass, and diagonal-takeoff-specific diameter-change data (a "run-off" effect analogous to Murray's law branching) was not directly evidenced either, only inferred from QCA methodology descriptions.

### Cited Findings
- Normal-anatomy reference values: proximal LAD 3.7±0.4 mm, distal LAD 1.9±0.4 mm, i.e., roughly a 49% diameter reduction from proximal to distal, consistent with an approximately linear taper — [Lumen Diameter of Normal Human Coronary Arteries, Circulation](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232) (as characterized in search synthesis; the primary Circulation "232" reference could not be independently re-verified past the search summary in this pass)
- A tapered luminal stenosis is formally defined (in CCTA reporting/QCA literature) as a >50% diameter reduction occurring over approximately 5 mm of vessel length, distinguished from an abrupt stenosis over a much shorter segment — this is a general CCTA definitional convention, not LAD-specific, but directly applicable to interpreting local diameter-derivative features along the LAD centerline — [Quantitative coronary CT angiography: absolute lumen sizing rather than %stenosis predicts hemodynamically relevant stenosis, PMC5052288](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5052288/)
- The same quantitative CCTA study found minimal lumen area (MLA) ≤1.8 mm² (sensitivity 90.9%, specificity 89.3%) and minimal lumen diameter (MLD) ≤1.2 mm (sensitivity 90.9%, specificity 85.2%) were optimal cutoffs for predicting functionally-relevant stenosis, and that absolute lumen area/diameter outperformed %diameter stenosis for this prediction — this is a general coronary (not exclusively LAD) finding but is the type of direct diameter-outcome evidence sought for the LAD specifically — [Quantitative coronary CT angiography, PMC5052288](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5052288/)
- QCA bifurcation-lesion methodology (used in LAD-diagonal bifurcation lesion studies) measures diameter in three separate segments around a bifurcation: proximal main branch, distal main branch, and side branch, using dedicated bifurcation QCA software (e.g., CAAS), which implies diameter is explicitly expected to change/step down across a diagonal takeoff and is measured on both sides of it rather than assumed continuous — [Assessment of Clinical, Electrocardiographic, and Physiological Relevance of Diagonal Branch in LAD Bifurcation Lesions, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1936879812007947)
- A binary size threshold of ≥2.5 mm vs <2.5 mm is used to classify diagonal branches as angiographically significant, and dominance between two diagonal branches is defined when one diagonal's diameter exceeds the other's by more than 2x — indicating diameter is used as a categorical/normalizing descriptor for branch importance in bifurcation-lesion classification, rather than as a continuous per-point CAD-risk feature in that literature — [Anatomical attributes of clinically relevant diagonal branches in patients with LAD bifurcation lesions, EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomical-attributes-of-clinically-relevant-diagonal-branches-in-patients-with-left-anterior-descending-coronary-artery-bifurcation-lesions)

### Inferences
- The LAD's taper is well-characterized in normative anatomy terms (proximal-to-distal ranges), which is useful for defining an expected local-diameter baseline/normalization curve for a per-point diameter channel; deviations from this expected taper (e.g., focal narrowing beyond what proximal-to-distal position predicts) could be a meaningful derived feature distinct from raw diameter.
- The general (non-LAD-specific) finding that absolute lumen diameter/area outperforms %stenosis for predicting functional significance supports including raw local diameter as a channel, not only a stenosis-ratio-derived feature, echoing the RCA situation where diameter already has direct outcome evidence (per the parent research summary). This general evidence appears to apply across vessels including LAD, but a dedicated LAD-only outcome study parallel to the RCA-specific FFR-CT study referenced in the parent research was not found in this pass.
- Diagonal-branch takeoffs are documented to coincide with an expected physiological diameter step-down (parent vessel narrows after giving off a branch, per basic conservation-of-flow / Murray's-law reasoning and the QCA convention of measuring proximal/distal main-branch diameter separately around a bifurcation), so a raw per-point diameter channel along the LAD will show real, physiological "sawtooth" drops at each diagonal takeoff that should not be mistaken for stenosis without accounting for branch locations.

### Gaps
- No LAD-specific outcome study directly analogous to the RCA's FFR-CT ostial diameter study (as referenced in the parent research) was identified in this pass; the diameter-outcome evidence found here (PMC5052288) is general/pooled across coronary segments, not LAD-isolated.
- No study was found that explicitly quantifies the magnitude of the diameter step-down at LAD diagonal takeoffs (e.g., percent diameter reduction per diagonal, or how many diagonals before the LAD reaches a given caliber) as opposed to general bifurcation QCA methodology descriptions.
- The Circulation "Lumen Diameter of Normal Human Coronary Arteries" reference (proximal/distal LAD values) could not be directly re-verified with full text in this pass; the numbers are reported from a search-engine synthesis rather than a confirmed direct quote.

## LAD-specific torsion reliability and CAD/hemodynamic association, vs. RCA situation

### Takeaway
No LAD-specific study was found that evaluates torsion's numerical reliability/noise-sensitivity or its independent association with CAD outcomes; the one LAD-vs-RCA geometry paper found (PMC2759354) computes and reports torsion values for the LAD but does not perform a reliability/noise analysis for torsion nor test its association with clinical CAD endpoints (stenosis, MACE, plaque burden) — it is a normal-anatomy geometry cataloguing study, not an outcome study. General (non-coronary-specific, non-LAD-specific) literature on vascular centerline geometry confirms torsion (third-derivative-based) is inherently noise-amplifying, which applies equally to LAD and RCA centerlines and does not differentiate the two vessels.

### Cited Findings
- Torsion at a centerline point is computed from the first, second, and third derivatives of the parametrized centerline curve via the Frenet-Serret formulas; because it depends on the third derivative, its computation is noted in the vascular-geometry literature to be prone to large errors and noise amplification — [Automated landmarking of bends in vascular structures: a comparative study with application to the internal carotid artery, PMC8626959](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8626959/); [Accuracy of vascular tortuosity measures using computational modelling, PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)
- Mitigations described in this general vascular-geometry literature include discrete-geometry derivative estimation with adjustable window size for noise robustness, cubic polynomial fitting for derivative computation, and excluding points near the ends of the vessel axis (too few neighboring points for reliable curve fitting) — these mitigations are generic to any vessel (carotid, coronary) and are not LAD- or RCA-specific — [Hybrid Framework for Blood Vessel Morphology Classification, arXiv:2607.14195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14195); [Cataloguing the geometry of the human coronary arteries, PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- PMC2759354 (the LAD-vs-RCA comparison study) does report torsion by vessel and by segment (see prior section) using cubic polynomial fitting, but the paper's stated purpose is cataloguing normal geometry as "a potential tool for predicting risk," not a completed CAD-outcome association study; no CAD/stenosis/MACE endpoint or torsion-vs-outcome statistic was reported in the extracted content — [Cataloguing the geometry of the human coronary arteries, PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- The LAD's torsion was found to be significantly higher than the RCA's (mean 3.73 vs 2.84 cm⁻¹, P<0.0001; max 31.16 vs 20.44 cm⁻¹, P<0.005) in that same normal-anatomy cohort, meaning that whatever noise floor exists in torsion estimation, the LAD's true (or apparent) torsion signal sits on a higher baseline than the RCA's — potentially a more favorable signal-to-noise situation for the LAD if the noise floor is comparable across vessels, though this is speculative since noise was not separately quantified per vessel — [Cataloguing the geometry of the human coronary arteries, PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- Torsion is theorized (not established as an outcome-correlate) to be more relevant than curvature specifically for generating helical flow patterns, per the same paper's discussion — this is a hemodynamic-mechanism hypothesis, not a demonstrated CAD-association finding — [Cataloguing the geometry of the human coronary arteries, PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- A separate arXiv preprint on "High-Intensity Helical Flow" in coronary hemodynamics exists in the search results but was not fetched in this pass; its relevance to LAD-specific torsion-flow association is unconfirmed — [High-Intensity Helical Flow: A Double-Edged Sword in Coronary Artery Haemodynamics, arXiv:2502.06161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06161)

### Inferences
- The LAD is not meaningfully better-evidenced than the RCA on the specific question of "does torsion predict CAD outcomes": no direct outcome study was found for either vessel. The one relevant coronary-specific torsion paper found is a normal-geometry cataloguing study (not diseased cohorts, not stenosis/outcome-linked), so the parent research's RCA conclusion ("torsion is noise-sensitive with no coronary outcome evidence") transfers essentially unchanged to the LAD: torsion computation is equally noise-sensitive by the general vascular-geometry math (this is vessel-agnostic, a property of third-derivative estimation on any discretized centerline), and no coronary outcome evidence (LAD or RCA) was located.
- The one point of LAD-specific nuance is that the LAD's baseline torsion magnitude is measurably higher than the RCA's in normal anatomy, which is a mildly favorable feasibility signal (a higher true-signal baseline to detect against a presumably similar absolute noise floor) but is not itself evidence that torsion is clinically informative for CAD in the LAD.

### Gaps
- No study was found that reports test-retest reliability, inter-scan/inter-reader repeatability, or a explicit noise-quantification (e.g., an SNR or coefficient-of-variation) for torsion computed specifically on LAD (or RCA) centerlines extracted from CT/CTA data (as opposed to biplane cineangiography, used in PMC2759354).
- No CAD-outcome, stenosis-severity, or plaque-burden association study for LAD torsion (as distinct from tortuosity index or curvature) was found in this research pass. This mirrors the RCA gap identified in the parent research and should be reported as unresolved for both vessels, not resolved in favor of either.

## LAD-specific bifurcation-angle-and-CAD association studies (diagonal branch angle, LAD/LCx angle) vs. the RCA ostial takeoff angle study

### Takeaway
Unlike the RCA (where the parent research found one ostial/aortic-takeoff-angle FFR-CT study as essentially the sole angle-outcome evidence), the LAD has a comparatively larger body of angle-outcome literature, but it is concentrated on the LAD-LCx bifurcation angle (and secondarily the LM-LAD angle), not on diagonal-branch takeoff angles; a dedicated systematic review of 13 studies found consistent qualitative association (wider LAD-LCx angle to higher CAD presence/severity) but explicitly states the evidence is "insufficient" to firmly establish an association with stenosis degree, and proposed angle cutoffs varied widely (60 degrees to 80.5 degrees) across studies.

### Cited Findings
- A cross-sectional study found a significant correlation between LAD-LCx bifurcation angle and Gensini score (a CAD severity index), described as a "linear correlation," per the search-engine summary of the paper; full text could not be retrieved (bot-check blocked access) so the exact r/p values, sample size, and methodology are unconfirmed beyond this summary — [LAD-LCx bifurcation angle and severity of coronary artery disease; is there any correlation? A cross-sectional study, PMC11168269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11168269/)
- A systematic review (13 eligible studies, from Australia, China, Taiwan, Turkey, South Korea, Argentina, and a joint Saudi Arabia/India/Oman effort) on left coronary bifurcation angle and CAD found that wider LAD-LCx angles are consistently associated with increased CAD presence, and that individuals with >50% stenosis had significantly wider LAD-LCx angles than those with <50% stenosis in multiple studies, attributed to altered hemodynamics/wall shear stress at wider bifurcation angles; however, the review explicitly concludes "insufficient valid evidence exists to support associations between LAD-LCx angle and degree of coronary stenosis," and proposed predictive angle cutoffs ranged from 60 degrees to 80.5 degrees across studies (most clustering near 80 degrees), indicating a lack of methodological convergence — [Evaluation of the Relationship between Left Coronary Artery Bifurcation Angle and Coronary Artery Disease: A Systematic Review, PMC9457427](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9457427/)
- A separate CT study on LM-LAD angle (not LAD-LCx) found that combining the LM-LAD angle (as cosθ) with a proximal-LAD tortuosity measure (d20) gave OR 11.36 (P<0.001) for severe proximal LAD stenosis (n=133, 26 events), AUC 0.767; this is angle-outcome evidence but for the upstream LM-LAD angle, not a diagonal-branch angle, and is proximal-LAD-specific — [Predicting severe proximal LAD stenosis using LAD tortuosity and LM-LAD angle, QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/117032/html)
- A related paper (title only, from search results, not fetched) reports on the "clinical usefulness of the angle between left main coronary artery and left anterior descending coronary artery for the evaluation of obstructive coronary artery disease," suggesting the LM-LAD angle specifically (distinct from LAD-LCx) is an independently pursued line of angle-outcome research — [Clinical usefulness of the angle between left main coronary artery and left anterior descending coronary artery for the evaluation of obstructive coronary artery disease, PMC6136703](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6136703/)
- A separate CKD-focused 3-D CT analysis studied the relationship between the LM-LAD bifurcation angle and coronary artery calcium score (title only, from search results, not fetched in full) — [Relationship between left main and left anterior descending arteries bifurcation angle and coronary artery calcium score in chronic kidney disease, PMC5997324](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5997324/)
- No study located in this research pass measures a diagonal-branch takeoff angle (LAD-to-first-diagonal, or LAD-to-second-diagonal) as a continuous per-point or per-branch CAD-outcome predictor; the diagonal-branch angle literature found is procedural/interventional (bifurcation-stenting technique, e.g. the EuroIntervention diagonal-branch anatomical-attributes paper), not a CAD-risk or stenosis-severity association study — [Anatomical attributes of clinically relevant diagonal branches in patients with LAD bifurcation lesions, EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/anatomical-attributes-of-clinically-relevant-diagonal-branches-in-patients-with-left-anterior-descending-coronary-artery-bifurcation-lesions)

### Inferences
- The LAD is meaningfully better-evidenced than the RCA for bifurcation-angle-and-CAD association in absolute terms (a systematic review with 13 studies, plus a separate LM-LAD-angle line of work, vs. the RCA's single ostial-takeoff-angle FFR-CT study per the parent research), but the LAD evidence base has its own weakness: the systematic review's own conclusion is that evidence for a stenosis-severity association is "insufficient," and cutoff values are inconsistent across studies (60-80.5 degrees). So "more studies exist" should not be read as "more settled evidence" — the LAD-LCx angle literature is larger but not clearly more conclusive than the RCA's single study.
- Critically, essentially all LAD angle-outcome evidence found here concerns bifurcation/trifurcation angles at or near the LAD's *origin* (LAD-LCx, LM-LAD), not diagonal-branch takeoffs distributed along the LAD's length. This means that, contrary to the anatomical branch-density argument (LAD has many named branches, so bifurcation angle could be a densely-sampled feature), the *evidence-backed* angle feature for the LAD is actually just as proximally concentrated as it is for the RCA: one location (or two, counting LM-LAD and LAD-LCx as distinct) near the vessel origin, not a running series of per-diagonal angles with demonstrated CAD relevance.
- This substantially changes the framing suggested by the task's premise: bifurcation angle is not "densely sampled and well-populated" along most of the LAD's length in the outcome literature, even though it *could be* geometrically. If a per-point bifurcation-angle channel is added for the LAD, it would need a design decision on how to handle the many diagonal takeoffs (which have anatomical geometry but no outcome literature) versus the one or two well-studied proximal bifurcations (which have outcome literature but represent only 1-2 points along the whole centerline). A feature that is nonzero only near LAD-LCx/LM-LAD and zero/undefined elsewhere would most directly reflect the current evidence base; a feature computed at every diagonal takeoff would be anatomically motivated but currently evidence-free beyond the most proximal one or two bifurcations.

### Gaps
- The two most directly relevant primary papers for LAD-LCx angle and CAD severity (PMC11168269 and the LM-LAD/CKD calcium-score paper PMC5997324) could not be fully retrieved (bot-check pages blocked full text); reported statistics for PMC11168269 are limited to a search-engine summary (correlation "linear," no r/p values obtained) rather than a verified direct quote.
- No study was found that tests a diagonal-branch takeoff angle (as opposed to LAD-LCx or LM-LAD) against any CAD, stenosis, or plaque outcome. This is a genuine evidence gap for the LAD, parallel in kind (though not necessarily in degree) to the RCA's branch-angle sparsity noted in the parent research.
- Whether the proposed LAD-LCx angle cutoffs (60-80.5 degrees) are directionally consistent with the RCA's ostial-takeoff-angle findings (higher or lower angle indicating risk) was not compared in this pass, since the RCA study details were not re-fetched here (out of scope per the parent research already covering the RCA).
