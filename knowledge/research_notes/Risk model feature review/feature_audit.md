# Audit of thesis/risk_models/01_model_features.tex (feature definitions, derivations, cited claims)

Line numbers refer to 01_model_features.tex. Status tags: [CONFIRMED] = checked against the source or by calculation; [SUSPECTED] = plausible problem I could not fully verify. Sources fetched 2026-09-28: Witt (PMC2954284), Garcha (PMC11770140), Taylor (PMC11380967), Ogundimu (PMC5045274), Schoenenberger abstract (Europe PMC), Han/ICONIC (search summary only). Other study numbers were checked only against the repo's refs.bib notes (thesis/refs.bib), not re-fetched. Bib read levels are quoted where they limit what the text may claim.

## 1. What is wrong, overstated, or misunderstood in the feature definitions and justifications?

### Takeaway
The algebra is sound (Gamma_HK optimum, Eq. risk-dependence, the centering identity, VIF arithmetic, torsion arithmetic). The scientific problems sit in the mechanistic story around Delta_HK: (a) the sign of the expected effect is supported by a Murray n=3 finding and a shear argument that does not survive the switch to n=7/3, (b) the only endothelial evidence points to the opposite sign, (c) EDT radii carry additive bias that ratios do not cancel, and (d) the LM Delta_HK mechanism predicts plaque in the LM, which is excluded from the outcome.

### Cited Findings (checks of maths and definitions)

- Gamma_HK optimum 2^(-3/7) is correct [CONFIRMED by algebra, l.106-110]. If r1^(7/3)+r2^(7/3)=r0^(7/3) then Gamma = (1/r0)(r0^(7/3)/2)^(3/7) = 2^(-3/7) = 0.7430. "Whatever the asymmetry" is true because the daughters enter only through the power sum. Witt's own ratio is the n=3 analogue, ((d1^3+d2^3)/(2 d0^3))^(1/3), optimum 2^(-1/3) = 0.794, same orientation as the text's, so the generalisation is faithful — [Witt 2010, PMC2954284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2954284/)
- "Shear scales with r^-3 at fixed flow" is correct Poiseuille, tau = 4 mu Q/(pi R^3) [CONFIRMED; the thesis's own eq:wss at clinical_background/05_hemodynamics.tex:50]. Only the wording "falls with the cube" (l.117) is loose; it falls as the inverse cube, i.e. rises 8x when radius halves.
- Eq. risk-dependence (l.160-164) is algebraically right [CONFIRMED]: with r2 = rho r1 and r0 = F r1 (1+rho), Gamma = (1/(F(1+rho)))((1+rho^(7/3))/2)^(3/7). Sanity check: rho=1 gives F = 0.673, matching Finet's 0.678, so the two laws agree for a symmetric split.
- Centering identity Eq. risk-center (l.402-410) is right [CONFIRMED]: Cov(I,z)=E[z](1-p)=0 because E[z]=p*0. The VIF example (VIF 5, R^2 0.8, SE x2.2, l.379) is right (sqrt 5 = 2.236). Torsion arithmetic (0.5 rad/mm gives one 2pi turn per 12.6 mm, l.174) is right.
- Weight count in tables (12 and 5) adds up [CONFIRMED].

### Errors and overstatements, in order of severity

1. **The shear argument for Delta_HK rests on n=3, not on the exponent the thesis uses.** [CONFIRMED]
   - Schoenenberger's abstract opens "If Murray's law is obeyed, shear stress is constant over the bifurcation" and defines the ratio by Murray's law, with a higher ratio meaning lower shear — [Europe PMC 22261173](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:22261173%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json). Constant shear holds only for Q proportional to r^3.
   - Under HK, Q ~ r^(7/3), so tau ~ Q/r^3 ~ r^(-2/3). A bifurcation exactly on the HK law has larger parents at lower shear than the daughters. Delta_HK = 0 therefore does not mean "shear-neutral". The l.117 chain (negative Delta_HK, wide parent, low shear) is the fixed-flow argument, which is valid for comparing two parents that carry the same flow, but the reference point 2^(-3/7) is not the shear-neutral point.
   - Numerical consequence: a typical healthy bifurcation on the HK line has Murray-n=3 ratio r0^3/(r1^3+r2^3) above 1 (for a symmetric split r0 = 1.346 r1 against Murray's 1.26 r1). Schoenenberger's "high ratio" group was defined against n=3, so a bifurcation with Delta_HK = 0 would be classed "deviant, low shear" in their scheme. The calcium finding does not transfer to the sign of Delta_HK without re-deriving the cut. l.35 and l.166 ("carries a disease finding with a predicted direction") and l.116-118 overstate this.
   - Also, that finding is cross-sectional dense calcium in 253 diseased IVUS patients ([bib note](thesis/refs.bib): F 5.93 p=0.016 proximal), not new plaque, not low-shear-driven early lesions. Dense calcium is a late, stable component; the text says "plaque" without qualification.

2. **The two lines of evidence for Delta_HK point in opposite directions.** [CONFIRMED]
   - Witt: the optimality ratio ROSE under L-NMMA, six healthy male volunteers, p = 0.03 — [PMC2954284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2954284/). A rise is above the optimum, so positive Delta.
   - Schoenenberger/shear argument: a wide parent, so a ratio BELOW the optimum, so negative Delta (l.117).
   - l.113 says departure "in either direction" is linked to endothelial dysfunction and disease, then the Tables (l.241, 262) pre-specify a one-sided negative sign. If both stories were right, |Delta| or a quadratic would be the feature, and a linear signed Delta_HK would have opposing contributions cancelling. The pre-specified sign has one weak source (shear inference, wrong exponent) and one contradicting source.
   - The endothelial inference is also strongly overstated: l.115 "The ratio therefore tracks endothelial function" comes from n = 6 volunteers, retinal arterioles under pharmacological NO blockade (a vasomotor change in vessels of tens of micrometres), and the authors themselves say only that it "may be a useful indicator". It is not evidence for epicardial bifurcations or for plaque.

3. **Scale invariance is claimed for a multiplicative change only; segmentation and EDT bias is additive.** [CONFIRMED logic, illustrative numbers mine]
   - l.42-45: ratios cancel a common factor "whether through a larger heart or a different scanner calibration". True for a pure multiplicative rescale. CCTA blur, threshold choice, partial volume and the EDT discretisation (distance to nearest background voxel centre is off by up to about half a voxel, 0.25 mm at the 0.5 mm cache) are additive offsets on the radius. An additive delta does not cancel in (r+delta)/(r'+delta) and matters most for the thin daughters (D1, OM1, ~0.8-1.0 mm radius).
   - Illustration (my invented but plausible radii): LM (2.2, 1.7, 1.5) mm gives Delta_HK = -0.014; adding +0.25 mm to all three gives +0.014, -0.25 mm gives -0.048. LAD-D1 (1.6, 1.3, 0.9): Delta -0.041, moves to -0.003 or -0.091. The bias-induced swing is the same size as the whole signal. The need is for a noise/bias study (perturb the segmentation, dilate/erode by one voxel, re-measure) before any weight is interpreted.
   - Witt's robustness claim (bias under one sixth of the junction exponent at 10% noise) was for simulated random noise, not systematic offset — [PMC2954284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2954284/).
   - Finet's quote (l.44) supports biological size-independence of the ratio, not immunity to measurement offsets; l.43-44 conflates the two.

4. **Mechanism/outcome mismatch for the LM features.** [CONFIRMED from the text's own definitions]
   - Delta_HK at the LM (Tables, l.241, 262) is argued through low shear on the wide parent wall, i.e. the LM. But l.209 excludes the LM from the outcome ("new plaque confined to them is not an outcome"). The LAD and LCx outcomes are whole-domain, the features are at one bifurcation, and l.202 argues "shear acts locally". A reviewer will ask where in the LAD/LCx domain the LM-level low shear is supposed to act. Same for theta and lambda: low shear from separation at the daughter's lateral wall is local to the first centimetre of the daughter, whereas the outcome is "any new plaque anywhere in LAD with all diagonals".
   - Outcome definition per artery also mixes "geometry at the first scan" with 10 years of unmodelled exposure. Not a feature error, but it undercuts the "stand-in for local shear" framing (l.22).

5. **The shear logic for lambda (smaller daughter gets low shear) uses a different mechanism than the one used for Delta_HK.** [CONFIRMED]
   - Poiseuille at fixed flow makes the SMALLER vessel the HIGHER-shear one. Garcha's low-WSS region in the smaller branch arises from the flow distribution: outlet resistances were scaled by diameter with Murray exponent 2.6 and, in the authors' account, the larger branch draws flow while the smaller sees low-velocity regions, TAWSS < 0.5 Pa — [Garcha 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11770140/). The text cites that result (l.74-75, 86) but l.117 uses the fixed-flow r^-3 rule for the parent. Both can be true, but the text never says so, and a lopsided daughter under n=7/3 splitting has higher, not lower, nominal shear (tau ~ r^(-2/3)).
   - The Garcha fit is quadratic in LAD/LCx ratio with a "neutral" ratio near 0.94 (range 0.65 to 1.40, ALWSS 0 to ~11.5%), so total low-WSS area is U-shaped, minimum near 0.94, not 1. A monotone sign per artery follows only if you use "risk goes to the smaller branch" and lambda = ln(rLAD/rLCx) is centred at 0. The logistic weight (a linear term in lambda) does not encode a neutral point at 0.94 or at the dominance-specific value; the tables say dominance is a separate additive term so the lambda-dominance interaction is unmodelled (Garcha fitted separate curves per dominance group).
   - Garcha is 230 synthetic CFD geometries with fixed BCs, not patients (bib note), and the ALWSS magnitudes are small (0 to ~11%). "Strongest mechanistic support of the caliber features" (l.77) is a statement about the only simulation available.

6. **Diameter evidence in Shen 2025 supports absolute diameter, the thing the design removes.** [CONFIRMED against bib note]
   - l.76-77: "vessel diameter was the only geometric quantity whose correlation with shear survived correction" is used to support the daughter ratio. The bib note says only diameter correlations survived; it does not say ratios. The thesis, by design (l.42-46), uses ratios only and no absolute radius. So the best-supported determinant of shear in that cohort is excluded and the inference to rho is a leap. Participants' absolute size will reappear via age/sex/dominance only.

7. **"Huo-Kassab law" as a bifurcation law and its provenance.** [CONFIRMED from bib note; partly SUSPECTED]
   - The bib note (huo2012scaling) states the paper does not write the bifurcation form; the text (l.97) says HK derived 7/3 from minimum energy "which the coronary version of Eq. risk-murray therefore uses". Derivation of the r^(7/3) junction relation from the flow exponent plus flow conservation is the thesis's step, which is legitimate, but "the Huo-Kassab law" as a name for it should be flagged as a reading, and n=7/3 is a tree-level flow-diameter exponent fitted largely on ex vivo casts including small vessels [SUSPECTED, not re-read here], not a validated per-bifurcation equality for 1-2 mm epicardial vessels.
   - Taylor 2024 supports 2.39 (2.24-2.54) but (i) I^2 = 99%, (ii) it pools health and disease, IVUS, QCA, CTA and post-mortem, (iii) it is a flow-diameter exponent for Strahler order >= 5 epicardial vessels, (iv) the CI covers 7/3 but also 2.5, and excludes only 3 — [Taylor 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11380967/). Authors call 2.39 "very close agreement" with 7/3. The text (l.96) omits heterogeneity and the fact that a population-mean exponent says little about individual-bifurcation deviations, most of which will be biological scatter plus measurement noise.

8. **Finet ratio as a "sensible radii" check is inconsistent with the text's own law.** [CONFIRMED by calculation]
   - l.167: compare F with 0.678. Under the HK relation F depends on rho: F = 0.673 (rho 1), 0.682 (0.75), 0.720 (0.5), 0.789 (0.3). D1 and OM1 splits with rho ~ 0.5-0.7 are expected to give F 0.69-0.72 under HK, and Finet's constant 0.678 is a different (fractal) law with a different asymmetry dependence. A deviation from 0.678 at an asymmetric bifurcation is not evidence of bad radii.
   - l.165 "could never change one while holding the other two fixed" is true as a functional statement, but F is a nonlinear function of (rho, Gamma), so the three features are not linearly singular; the accurate phrase is functional dependence, with strong but not perfect correlation.

9. **LM is not always a bifurcation.** [SUSPECTED: prevalence not checked here]
   - A ramus intermedius (trifurcation) is present in a sizable minority. l.209 notes it is excluded from outcomes but the feature definition (l.37-40) has no rule for it. Flow to the ramus makes r1^(7/3)+r2^(7/3) < r0^(7/3) by construction and biases Delta_HK negative (the exact sign that is predicted to raise risk) and lambda-based flow splitting is also affected. Needs an explicit exclusion or a three-daughter form.

10. **EDT offset rule (l.38-40) is heuristic.** [SUSPECTED]
    - "One junction radius r_J" assumes the skeleton branch point sits at the carina and that the flare ends r_J away. The skeleton junction location depends on the centerline algorithm and on the angle (wide angles shift it), and the flare length scales with parent/daughter ratio, not with r_J alone. No sensitivity of the features to the offset (0.5, 1, 2 r_J) or window (2, 3, 5 mm) is shown. Also EDT gives the inscribed radius, which is smaller than the area-equivalent radius off centre or in non-circular (elliptical) lumens; Poiseuille scaling depends on hydraulic/area radius. "Median EDT in a 3 mm window" is reasonable and robust to spikes, but 3 mm is 3 to 4 diameters for D1, so tapering enters.

11. **Curvature scale and cardiac-phase dependence contradict the scale-invariance rationale.** [CONFIRMED conceptual]
    - The ratio section (l.42-46) praises scale-invariant features, but mean absolute curvature (mm^-1) scales inversely with size, so the "larger heart" effect the ratios were designed to remove is fully present in kappa. Age/sex only partially handle this. Curvature also depends on cardiac phase, smoothing scale (l.131 says smoothed at "the same scale" without the value), and the choice of L; none stated. For a 3D unsigned curvature |kappa| is already non-negative; the absolute value in Eq. risk-kappa is redundant (harmless).
    - Kashyap's best R^2 is 0.112 for area of LOW wall shear at the left-main bifurcation in disease-free patients (bib note: percent area below 0.4 Pa), so "best-tested link" is a low ceiling, and no RCA was studied, but the RCA models rely entirely on curvature (l.214).
    - Li 2011 (OR 0.755, 0.574-0.994, p = 0.045; bib note) is borderline and defines tortuosity as a binary count of 45-degree bends; MACE did not differ. The text cites it accurately but never states the direction it implies for curvature, and the tables leave the curvature sign blank, which is honest.

12. **Torsion argument is heuristic.** [SUSPECTED]
    - l.173-174: small out-of-plane RMS (2.1% of length) plus a constant 0.50-0.57 mm^-1 mean |tau| is read as noise. Plausible, but planarity RMS does not bound torsion: torsion is the rate of rotation of the osculating plane and blows up where kappa is near 0, and a mean of |tau| is dominated by near-straight segments. A cleaner demonstration would perturb a known analytic curve (planar or helical) with the centerline's error level and show tau saturates near the measured value; or show mean |tau| is insensitive to true shape across vessels. The conclusion (drop torsion) is probably right; the evidence as written does not prove it.

### Inferences

- The one-sided sign for Delta_HK is the most exposed pre-registration in the document. The safest fix is to state that the sign is not established, use Delta_HK with an unsigned or quadratic term as a secondary test, or re-derive the reference at n=3 to line up with Schoenenberger and explain the 7/3 versus 3 choice as a deviation.
- The features that survive scrutiny best mechanistically are angle and lambda (both through flow separation/flow split), with the caveat of Garcha's synthetic setting.
- An additive-bias sensitivity analysis on the ImageCAS-X segmentations (dilate/erode by one voxel, vary r_J and window) is the natural way to defend the caliber family before touching Herlev-Osterbro.

### Gaps

- I did not re-read Huo-Kassab 2012 itself; details of what tree data (ex vivo, vessel range) support 7/3 are from memory and the bib note only.
- Han/ICONIC HR values (2.90, 6.84) and CIs not re-verified; only the search summary and 35.1/44.5 mm, 73.3/38.9% figures were confirmed. Sakellarios, van der Giessen, Kashyap, Dodge, Finet, Stone numbers rely on refs.bib notes (abstract level), not re-fetched.
- Ramus intermedius prevalence, junction-position accuracy of the centerline builder, and the actual smoothing scale in the code were not checked.

## 2. Which cited claims do not support what the text says?

### Takeaway
Most numbers are reported accurately (per the bib notes), but several studies are used for a claim stronger than, or different from, what they showed: Stone (progression vs initiation), ICONIC (bifurcation location vs bifurcation geometry), Schoenenberger (n=3, calcium), Witt (n=6, opposite direction), Shen 2025 (absolute diameter), Ogundimu (EPV rule), Sakellarios (n=17).

### Cited Findings

- **Stone/PREDICTION (l.19-20).** Text: "Plaque starts where the vessel wall feels low shear... low shear at baseline predicted where the lumen narrowed." The bib note says low ESS predicted the SECONDARY endpoint (decrease in lumen area), NOT the primary endpoint (plaque area increase), which baseline plaque burden predicted; population was 506 post-ACS PCI patients, 374 reimaged; analysed in 3 mm segments. The text's "lumen narrowed" is literally right but "plaque starts" is unsupported: it is progression in already-diseased arteries, and the primary endpoint was not met. The thesis outcome is de novo plaque in plaque-free trees — [PREDICTION, Circulation 2012 (bib note)](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.112.096438). [CONFIRMED against bib note; paper full text not read by me]
- **Sakellarios (l.21).** n = 17 bifurcations in 15 patients (bib note). p = 0.0006 is for plaque-burden increase from models that included the daughter branch with Murray flow split; the text sentence ("low shear predicted an increase in plaque burden (p = 0.0006), and the prediction improved when the daughter was modeled") attaches the best p-value to the general claim. Also the bib note says the daughter-included models were better than main-vessel-only, not that they proved the Murray split. The l.328 sentence that the caliber features "carry" the flow-splitting diameters "without a flow simulation" is an assumption: the features omit total flow, angle interaction and the carinal geometry.
- **van der Giessen (l.27).** 72% versus 31% is quoted correctly per the bib note. The unit is CT cross-sections "quarters", n=65 sections, patient count not given, and it studied plaque already present. "Never at the flow divider alone" matches the note ("never exclusively at the flow divider").
- **ICONIC/Han (l.28-30).** The three "adverse geometric characteristics" are distance from ostium, bifurcation LOCATION, and tortuosity (binary); the 35.1 vs 44.5 mm, 73.3% vs 38.9% and 4.3 vs 1.4% tortuous figures were confirmed by search summary — [ACC journal scan](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Journal-Scans/2022/02/04/18/53/Association-of-Plaque-Location); [JAMA Cardiol](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2788006). The text's "one adverse geometric feature" (l.29) is never defined and l.30 concludes "the geometry of a bifurcation is therefore worth measuring", but ICONIC shows that lesions sit at bifurcations, not that angle, rho, lambda or Delta_HK predict anything. It is a nested case-control of existing precursor lesions (116 vs 116 controls), not de novo plaque. [CONFIRMED that features tested are location, not geometry descriptors; HR values not re-verified]
- **Angle studies (l.57-62).** The text says "all five studies measured patients referred for suspected disease". Temov 2016 (l.57) reports only the LM angle distribution (n=196, 79.4 +/- 23.0, 35.5 to 178 degrees) and is not a disease association study; Wang 2024 (l.61) is a 76-artery shape clustering with simulation, not a patient outcome study. So the count of five "studies of angle and disease" is four, and only three are clinical association studies. Juan 2017 (l.60) found no association after adjustment (p=0.064), which is a null result reported honestly but sits in a list that ends with "expected to raise risk". Cui's OR 1.42 per unspecified unit (l.58) is unusable as effect size; the bib should be checked for unit. The angle definition also differs between studies (LM to LAD vs LCx distal angle, different measurement segments), so the thesis's initial-3-mm straight-line angle (l.50) is not the quantity measured in those cited papers. [SUSPECTED for definitional mismatch; not checked in each paper]
- **Garcha (l.73-77).** Numbers match the bib note and fetched text (R^2 0.91/0.94, quadratic). Points not stated in the text: U-shape with neutral ratio ~0.94, separate curves per dominance group, exponent 2.6 in the flow split, synthetic. See section 1 item 5.
- **Witt (l.98, 114-115).** "Simpler to compute and more robust to noise than solving for n": correct for simulated random noise. "Rose when NO synthesis was blocked (p = 0.03)": correct, but n = 6 healthy males, retina, and the direction is upward — [PMC2954284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2954284/). The generalisation "departure from an optimal ratio has been linked both to endothelial dysfunction and to plaque" (l.113) has one retinal pharmacology study for the first and one n=3-based IVUS calcium study for the second; the link to plaque in the retina is absent.
- **Schoenenberger (l.116-118, 166).** See section 1 items 1-2. Text says "253 patients... bifurcations whose parent was wide relative to its daughters, and so under low shear, held more dense calcium on both sides of the branch point after adjustment": consistent with the bib note (proximal p=0.016, distal p=0.024). But the Murray ratio uses n=3 and the outcome is dense calcium.
- **Shen 2025 / Zhang 2024 (l.76, 136, 175).** Correctly flagged as the same 39 ASOCA trees (l.136 "in the same 39 ASOCA trees as above"). Zhang's "AUC above 0.71" stenosed vs non-stenosed uses trees that already have stenosis, so it cannot separate cause from effect of curvature; it also is within-sample, no external validation. Fine as motivation only.
- **Kashyap (l.134-135, 146-149).** Numbers per bib note; the "explained none of the low-shear area, p=0.86" matches. The thesis's later step "so a vessel can be long relative to that line either by winding or sweeping one arc" is an explanation the thesis supplies, not one stated by the note. Also the low-WSS outcome is an area at the LM bifurcation and its branches, not plaque.
- **Ogundimu (l.323-324).** Misattributed emphasis. The paper concludes EPV >= 20 is needed WHEN LOW-PREVALENCE BINARY predictors are present, and that continuous or common predictors were adequate at EPV = 10; it also recommends penalised likelihood if 20 cannot be guaranteed, and its title states adequate sample size "is not simply related to EPV" — [Ogundimu 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5045274/). It studied Cox mostly. The thesis applies a flat "20 per weight" to a logistic model with mostly continuous features, and it says nothing about Model 2 (an encoder, far more parameters). Modern sample-size criteria for prediction models (Riley et al.) target shrinkage and overall risk precision and should be cited or computed; I did not verify them here. [Riley not searched]
- **Taylor (l.96).** See section 1 item 7. Also the cited paper's CI excludes n=3 but is also compatible with 2.5, so "close to 7/3" is fair but does not favour 7/3 over other exponents.
- **Dodge (l.290-296).** Consistent with the bib note (83 angiograms out of 9160; women 9% smaller, persists after BSA normalisation; RCA 3.9 vs 2.8, LCx 3.4 vs 4.2, LAD unaffected; age no effect). Limitations: angiography, ~1992 QCA, only 83 normal cases selected for smooth borders (selection toward straight, larger-lumen arteries), so it fixes direction, not a CCTA-scale normal range. Line 296 then says "Age did not change normal diameter [Dodge], but tortuosity increases with age [Kahe]" and infers curvature rises with age; fine, but Kahe's vessel bed (coronary vs retinal) was not checked here [gap].
- **Dominance (l.299-302).** Veltman HR 3.20 (n=1425), Gebhard (6382, no overall effect, 6.45 with LM disease), Khan (255,718 ACS, OR 1.27) are quoted from bib-level info I did not re-fetch. Khan's population is ACS, an unrelated setting to a healthy-cohort incidence model; worth stating. Left dominance also confounds Delta_HK/lambda, as the text notes for lambda.
- **Tello Ayala (l.309-313).** Text is careful. Additional points from the bib note: the CAD label was called by the same cardiologist on the same LAO frames (circularity), segmentation succeeded in only 78.7% of eligible loops (selection), and the bib note says it "does not license claims about 3D CTA-derived centerlines". The text acknowledges 2D and no test, but "motivates Model 2 directly" (l.313) is strong for one study with an AUC gap of 0.07 and non-overlapping CIs but no test, on a differently defined outcome (CAD on angiography, not incident plaque). Patient-level train/test split not stated in the bib note; if image-level, leakage would inflate the transformer.

### Inferences

- The text's calibration ("promising signal rather than proof") on Tello Ayala is a model for how the other motivating studies should be handled; Stone, Schoenenberger, Witt and ICONIC currently carry more weight than they can bear in the same section.
- Section 1 (l.18-22) says the plaque-starts-at-low-shear claim as established fact; the evidence base in the document is progression or cross-sectional.

### Gaps

- Did not obtain full texts for Han (HR, CIs, definition of "one adverse feature"), Sakellarios, van der Giessen, Kashyap, Dodge, Veltman, Gebhard, Khan, Cui, Juan, Mansouri, Wang, Temov, Kahe. Verdicts on these rely on bib notes.
- Whether the Cui odds ratio unit is available from the full text.

## 3. What is ambiguous or unstated that a reviewer would challenge?

### Takeaway
The biggest gaps are the outcome definition (whole-domain, 10-year, selection on survival and rescan), the missing noise/bias validation of the radius pipeline, undefined or inconsistent handling of anatomical variants (ramus, codominance, missing D1/OM1), and the absence of any sample-size or validation plan for Model 2.

### Cited Findings (each is a reviewer question rather than a sourced fact; sources given where applicable)

- **What exactly is "new plaque" and how is it graded?** (l.188, 210) Presence on CCTA (any plaque? non-calcified? CAC-scored?), reading threshold, inter-reader variability, and whether progression in artery domains includes the branches assigned in l.207-209. CGPS baseline paper defines obstructive plaque by stenosis, which is not the same as any plaque. Fuchs 2023 as cited describes the baseline cohort, not the follow-up scan protocol (bib note not checked).
- **Selection/attrition.** l.188: "Part of the cohort was scanned a second time about ten years later." Participants who had an MI or died in between, or who declined, are missing; rescan probability plausibly depends on baseline risk. The l.210 restriction to whole-tree plaque-free trees further selects a low-risk subgroup. The event count (l.324 needs ~240/240/100) is not estimated from any known incidence. The header comment (l.11-13) admits the size and interval are unconfirmed, but the body (l.188) states them as fact.
- **Cardiac phase, scanner and reconstruction.** Curvature and radii depend on ECG-gating phase (prospective diastolic acquisition typical), reconstruction kernel, contrast level and heart rate. Not mentioned; the ImageCAS-X trained model is applied to a different cohort; see Fuchs and cohort description for scanner (not checked).
- **Are the radii actually valid?** No validation of EDT radii against ground truth (phantom, IVUS, or QCA), and no reproducibility (repeat scan or repeat segmentation) numbers. The header comment says bifurcation reliability numbers "are not yet in the repo, so none of them is used here", which leaves l.37-45 unsupported by any measurement-error figure. ImageCAS-X labels themselves were curated, not necessarily lumen-boundary-accurate at sub-voxel scale. See section 1 items 3 and 10.
- **Junction definition.** How is the "branch point" defined when a centerline builder places it, and how are D1 and OM1 identified ("first") when branches are close, or when D1 arises proximally or as a doubled origin? A missing D1 is coded absent (l.215), but a wrongly labelled second diagonal would silently enter. Ramus intermedius (section 1 item 9).
- **Dominance categories.** l.292 defines dominance by which artery "supplies the back of the heart's lower wall" while the model uses three categories; the standard definition is the origin of the PDA (and posterolateral branches), with a balanced/codominant category. l.207-208 assigns PDA and PLA to RCA or LCx by dominance and does not say what happens when the RCA gives the PDA and the LCx gives the PLA (codominant). How dominance is labelled in the ImageCAS-X and Herlev-Osterbro pipelines is not stated.
- **Missing covariates that set flow.** Shear ~ Q/r^3, and Q is set by myocardial mass (LV mass, BMI, heart rate, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, LDL, CAC, statins). The models get age, sex and dominance only (l.212-214), so the "stand-in for low shear" argument has no flow term and the classical risk factors that dominate 10-year plaque incidence are omitted. Was that intended as a "geometry incremental over risk factors" test? Not stated. Compare Dodge's LV hypertrophy +17% (bib note).
- **Linear-logit assumptions.** Signed Delta_HK and lambda are entered linearly, but Garcha shows a U-shaped low-shear area in the diameter ratio and Witt suggests deviation in both directions could matter. Nonlinearity is then only available in Model 2, which confounds the intended "mean versus profile" comparison (the control network partly deals with this, l.196).
- **Multiplicity and pre-specification.** Three domains, two models, several feature families; sign predictions are pre-specified but no primary endpoint, alpha allocation, or performance metric (AUC, calibration, incremental over clinical model, decision-curve) is stated in this section. Internal validation "by cross-validation" is mentioned at l.365 only.
- **Model 2 details.** Encoder architecture, input length L, resampling to fixed grid, treatment of the removed branch-point segments in the profile (l.131), missing-segment masking and training set size vs parameters are absent. "Late fusion" comparison assumes both models see identical data, which is stated (l.222). The only sample-size argument is for Model 1.
- **Curvature definition.** Smoothing scale, L, whether L is fixed in mm or fraction of vessel length (the vessel may be shorter than L), and how the excised branch-point neighbourhoods are handled in the mean. Also curvature is computed on centerlines from a segmentation of a trained network, so segmentation noise near calcified or stented segments matter (plaque-free selection mitigates).
- **Angle window.** The initial 3 mm window after r_J captures the flow-entry direction, whereas most literature angles (e.g., LM distal angle) are measured over longer segments or by different reference lines; larger angles found in the literature are not directly comparable to the thesis quantity, and angle noise for a 3 mm straight-line fit at 0.5 mm voxels is not quantified.
