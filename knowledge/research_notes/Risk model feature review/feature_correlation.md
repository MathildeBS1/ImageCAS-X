## Q1. Which specific features are near-duplicates, and which one to keep from each group?

### Takeaway
The only exact duplicate is the Finet ratio F, which the tex already drops; it correlates about -0.95 with Delta_HK by construction, so F and the area ratio stay reported-only. The larger problems are not the pairs the tex lists: (a) Delta_HK is mostly a noisy re-measurement of the parent radius r0 (reliability about 0.14 to 0.40 in simulation), (b) the LM and LAD-D1 (and LM and LCx-OM1) Delta_HK values share a radius reading, (c) mean curvature is the hub of a large family of curvature and tortuosity summaries (Spearman 0.7 to 0.86 with them), and (d) lambda and rho are one quantity (|lambda| = -ln rho, r = -0.99), which the design avoids by using lambda only at the LM and rho only at side branches.

### Cited Findings
- Algebra (from the tex itself, Eq. `risk-dependence`): with F = r0/(r1+r2) and rho = min/max, Gamma_HK = h(rho)/F, where h(rho) = ((1+rho^(7/3))/2)^(3/7)/(1+rho). h runs from 0.743 at rho=0 to 0.5 at rho=1. So Delta_HK is a deterministic function of (F, rho): F is the "size of the parent" axis and rho the "asymmetry" axis. This is derived, not cited.
- Under the Huo-Kassab law itself, F is not free: F_expected(rho) = h(rho)/0.743, which gives F = 0.673 at rho=1, close to Finet's observed 0.678 (value quoted in the tex, citing Finet 2008). Delta_HK is therefore "F relative to the value the law predicts for this rho", i.e. F residualised on rho. That is why Delta_HK and rho are nearly uncorrelated (SIM below) while Delta_HK and F are almost perfectly anti-correlated.
- Redundancy analysis and variable clustering are the standard unsupervised tools for finding such groups: [Harrell, Hmisc varclus](https://rdrr.io/cran/Hmisc/man/varclus.html) is described as assessing collinearity and redundancy and separating variables into clusters that can be scored as a single variable; [Harrell's blog](https://www.fharrell.com/post/score-data-reduction/) lists score consolidation and dimensionality reduction as the two aims of data reduction.
- Curvature family (DATA, `simple_measures_sigma05.csv`, 560 train cases per vessel, Spearman with kappa_a5, the mean absolute curvature at the 5 mm scale): LAD/LCx/RCA: nb45 0.74/0.73/0.76; nb90 0.83/0.74/0.70; max_bend 0.79/0.77/0.76; soam -0.80/-0.67/-0.56. R^2 of rank(kappa_a5) on the other nine summaries jointly: 0.86 (LAD), 0.78 (LCx), 0.77 (RCA), i.e. an implied VIF of about 7, 4.5, 4.4 if all of them were entered.
- Curvature family, LAD regional table (DATA, `regional_lad_train560.csv`, 2800 rows across five regions, so rows are not independent across regions): kappa_a5 with ti10_mean 0.86, kB 0.84, max_bend_deg 0.73, total_turning_deg 0.63, arc_chord 0.53; ti10_mean with kB 0.97. length_mm with total_turning_deg 0.90 and arc_chord 0.87, so any "total" bend count is mostly vessel length.
- The tex's LM-caliber claim that "lambda or rho with Delta_HK" are one family is correct in the algebraic sense but overstated numerically. SIM: with r0 generated from the Murray-type law plus a 5% to 10% biological deviation, corr(rho, Delta_HK) = 0.00 to 0.02 and corr(lambda, Delta_HK) = -0.02 to 0.03, while corr(F, Delta_HK) = -0.94 to -0.97 and corr(area ratio, Delta_HK) = 0.94 to 1.00. Real trees may deviate systematically with rho; that is exactly the quantity the ImageCAS-X table should measure.
- Noise: the tex says Delta_HK and F are "computed from the same three radii". SIM (`sim_reliability.py`, EDT noise sd 0.1 / 0.2 mm on each radius, 5% true deviation): squared correlation of observed with true feature: lambda 0.89 / 0.67; rho 0.75 / 0.40; Delta_HK 0.40 / 0.14; F 0.42 / 0.15. Delta_HK is the noisiest feature because dGamma/Gamma = -dr0/r0 + (daughter terms), and at the LM r0 is the largest radius but the biological deviation being sought is small. The noise sd values are assumed, not measured; the tex notes that the reliability scripts in `reports/` are not in the repo.
- An additive radius bias (EDT over-reads by up to half a voxel, per `topology/radius.py` docstring) did not change reliability in the simulation and moved corr(feature, overall size) only to about -0.06, so the "ratios cancel scale" argument survives a constant bias in this simple setting. Scanner-dependent bias was not simulated.
- Shared-measurement noise across bifurcations (SIM): the LAD radius just past the LM is one reading used as a daughter of the LM and as the parent of LAD-D1. With sd 0.2 mm, corr(Delta_HK at LM, Delta_HK at D1) = -0.34 when the reading is shared versus 0.01 if measured independently. Lambda at LM versus rho at D1 stayed about 0 (they take different ends of the ratio). Same mechanism applies to LCx and OM1. This is the strongest concrete case that "features shared across LM and side-branch blocks" are correlated purely by measurement.
- Anatomical (not numerical) redundancies already in the tex: dominance with lambda (Dodge: LCx larger in left dominance, cited in the tex); age with mean curvature (Kahe, cited in the tex). Additional candidates I could not verify with a source or data: LM angle with LAD-D1 angle (shared LAD trunk direction), and LM length or LM radius with lambda. These need the feature table.

### Inferences
Proposed keep/merge/drop list for a first-pass pre-specification (to be confirmed by the correlation matrix on the ImageCAS-X training trees):
1. F and area ratio: drop from models, keep as QC (already the tex position). Delta_HK is the one to keep from the F/area/Delta_HK trio, on the grounds that it is the only one of the three that removes the rho dependence.
2. Delta_HK versus rho/lambda: keep both. They are nearly orthogonal in simulation, so no pruning is licensed; but Delta_HK is the least reliable feature and the one most likely to have a wide interval. Its pre-specified sign (negative) should be checked against the ImageCAS-X distribution, and its reliability (repeat extraction with perturbed junction offset `core_scale`, or the EDT noise sensitivity already in `angles.extract_case(core_scale=...)`) reported before any outcome is opened.
3. Do not enter both lambda and rho for the same bifurcation, and never add |lambda| or lambda^2 at the LM. The tex already avoids this; state it as a rule.
4. Mean curvature: keep exactly one curvature scalar in Model 1 (kappa_a). Do not add nb45, max_bend, kB, ti10, total turning or arc_chord as extra scalars; they are 0.6 to 0.9 rank-correlated with it and, for length-scaled ones, mostly encode vessel length. For Model 2, the profile encoder output is by construction highly dependent on kappa_a; this is intended and handled by the control network.
5. Angle: no source says it is collinear with caliber; keep, but measure.
6. Across LAD and LCx models the LM block (theta, lambda, Delta_HK) is the same three numbers. That is not within-model collinearity, but the two models' outcomes are correlated within a patient and the LM Delta_HK of the two models carries a single noisy reading; interpret the "opposite sign of lambda" prediction as one contrast, not two independent findings.
7. If the LM-to-side-branch shared-radius correlation (-0.34 in simulation) is confirmed on real data, options in order of preference: measure the shared LAD/LCx radius on a window used by only one bifurcation (parent of D1 taken proximal to D1 and distal to a fixed offset from LM), or center the D1 Delta_HK on the LM value, or accept and report the generalised VIF of the block.

### Gaps
- No real feature table; all bifurcation correlations are simulated. The single most valuable next step is the 150-case extraction (script provided).
- EDT noise magnitude on real ImageCAS-X centerlines is assumed (0.1 to 0.2 mm), not measured.
- I did not find published correlations between coronary bifurcation angle and daughter-diameter ratio or Delta_HK-type optimality ratios; the tex also states none exist for coronary Gamma_HK.
- Correlation between kappa_a and age or dominance cannot be checked; `Descriptors.xlsx` has only Scan ID, Image Quality, Dominance, Disease (no age or sex).

## Q2. What does correlation do to prediction versus interpretation, per model type?

### Takeaway
For prediction, correlation is largely harmless when training and deployment share the same correlation structure, for penalised logistic regression as well as networks; for interpretation, individual weights (Model 1) and permutation-based importances (Model 2) are unreliable, and selection is unstable. The tex's stance (prediction fine, weights not independent effects, judge by family) is consistent with the literature, with three qualifications below.

### Cited Findings
- [Leeuwenberg et al. 2021, arXiv:2101.01603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01603) compare shrinkage, dimensionality reduction and constrained methods in simulation: no effect of collinearity was observed on predictive performance, but collinearity harmed the stability of predictor selection across all methods, most for Lasso.
- Dormann et al. 2013 (already cited in the tex; not re-fetched by me) report severe distortion of estimation beyond |r| about 0.7 and worse prediction when the new data's correlation structure differs; the tex quotes this.
- [Heinze, Wallisch and Dunkler 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/bimj.201700067): variable selection, especially in explanatory modelling where effect estimates matter, can compromise model stability, unbiasedness of coefficients and validity of p-values and confidence intervals.
- [Pavlou et al. 2016, Stat Med](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/sim.6782) (few-events penalised regression review, via search summary only): ridge performed well except with many noise predictors; Firth's correction was inferior for prediction to the other shrinkage methods considered. Lasso, elastic net, adaptive lasso and SCAD were also compared.
- [Zou and Hastie 2005](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/67/2/301/7109482): the elastic net's grouping effect makes strongly correlated predictors enter or leave together, unlike lasso.
- [Hooker et al. 2021](tex cites; not re-fetched): permutation importance overstates correlated features; refit instead (tex position).

### Inferences
- Model 1 (unpenalised or ridge logistic): prediction unaffected; individual weights of Delta_HK, F-type or curvature-summary sets unreliable. Ridge splits weight within a group, so ranks are stable but not effect sizes. Because the caliber block (lambda, rho, Delta_HK) is nearly orthogonal in simulation, its VIFs may turn out near 1 to 2, in which case the tex's pessimism about weight interpretation is a hedge, not a finding. The exception is dominance with lambda and the presence indicator terms.
- Model 2 (late-fusion network): prediction unaffected; importance by permutation is unsafe; refit-by-family (tex) is right. A second-order issue is that the profile encoder can rediscover kappa_a, so "profile versus mean" compares nonlinear function classes unless the control network is matched (tex has this).
- Qualification 1: the "harmless for prediction" claim depends on measurement-error structure being shared between training (ImageCAS-X) and deployment (Herlev-Osterbro segmentations by a different pipeline). The shared-radius noise (-0.34 between LM and D1 Delta_HK in simulation) is a pipeline property that would change if the segmenter changes. That is a stronger reason to pre-specify pruning than collinearity itself.
- Qualification 2: low reliability (Delta_HK 0.14 to 0.40) attenuates its weight and lowers its power; this is a bias toward zero, not a collinearity problem, and it is not fixed by dropping or penalising.
- Qualification 3: the VIF rule in the tex (report, do not threshold) is consistent with O'Brien 2007 (tex citation, not re-fetched by me); the |r| > 0.7 within-family rule is the only threshold.

### Gaps
- Could not read Dormann 2013, Hooker 2021, O'Brien 2007 or the Leeuwenberg full text here (PDF unparsed); statements attributed to them come from the tex's own citations or the abstract.
- No source found on collinearity effects specific to late-fusion networks with a convolutional profile encoder.

## Q3. What is a defensible, pre-specified pruning procedure (not outcome-peeking)?

### Takeaway
Use an unsupervised, outcome-blind pipeline on ImageCAS-X only (no Herlev-Osterbro outcomes): (1) theory-driven candidate list frozen; (2) pairwise/cluster analysis with a fixed rule; (3) keep-one-per-cluster by pre-written tie-breakers based on reliability and mechanism; (4) sample-size check by Riley criteria; (5) ridge as the fixed fallback. Everything from step 2 on is computed on ImageCAS-X trees, before outcomes are opened, exactly as the tex already promises; the additions below make the rule fully deterministic.

### Cited Findings
- [Riley et al. 2019, Stat Med (part II)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30357870/) and [BMJ 2020;368:m441](https://www.bmj.com/content/368/bmj.m441) (via [search summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/sim.7992)): minimum n and events E are set by three criteria: global shrinkage factor at least 0.9, absolute difference at most 0.05 between apparent and adjusted Nagelkerke R^2, and precise estimation of overall risk; the sample size depends on the number of candidate predictor parameters p, so pruning before modelling directly lowers the required events. I could not retrieve the formula text (403 / cookie wall); the shrinkage formula S = 1 + p/(n ln(1 - R2_CS/S)) is from my memory and must be checked before it goes into the thesis.
- [Riley/Martin 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09622802211046388) (via search snippet): even models meeting the minimum sample size show substantial bootstrap variability in tuning parameters and performance, so ridge penalties should be tuned with reported variability. Another snippet noted that penalisation can be unreliable in small samples.
- [Harrell 2015 / blog](https://www.fharrell.com/post/score-data-reduction/) and [RMS notes](https://hbiostat.org/rmsc/multivar): variable clustering and redundancy analysis are unsupervised and used for data reduction without the outcome; penalised ML "typically" performs best with many predictors but data reduction is "competitive and sometimes more interpretable"; PCA is advocated when variables are correlated.
- [Kuhn and Johnson, Feature Selection chapter](https://aml4td.org/chapters/feature-selection.html): a technique that ignores the outcome when removing predictors is unsupervised, e.g. correlation-based redundancy filters. (Bias from outcome-informed screening outside cross-validation was not established by a fetched source.)
- Heinze 2018 above: automated selection harms unbiasedness and inference, arguing for background-knowledge-based, pre-specified reduction.
- Leeuwenberg 2021 above: excluding correlated variables arbitrarily lacks scientific justification; they compare alternatives such as shrinkage and dimension reduction.

### Inferences
Proposed protocol (write into the analysis plan before any Herlev-Osterbro outcome is unblinded):
1. Freeze the candidate list from theory: the tex's table plus the curvature scalar. Anything added later is a labelled deviation.
2. On ImageCAS-X training trees only, compute (a) Spearman matrix, (b) Hmisc-style `varclus` dendrogram (1 - rho^2), (c) VIF/GVIF per model, and (d) bootstrapped reliability under a perturbed junction offset (`core_scale` 0.5, 1, 1.5) for each feature.
3. Rule: within any cluster with |Spearman| > 0.7 (the Dormann level already in the tex), keep the member with (i) highest reliability from step 2d, then (ii) a stated mechanistic route, then (iii) simplest definition; record ties broken alphabetically to avoid discretion. Do not use association with any outcome, including ImageCAS-X disease labels.
4. Do not prune to a VIF cutoff; report VIF/GVIF as diagnostics (tex/O'Brien). Do not prune pairs below 0.7.
5. Reliability floor: if Delta_HK reliability is below a pre-set value (e.g. 0.5), demote it to exploratory rather than dropping silently; the value is a judgement I am suggesting, not a cited threshold.
6. Sample size: count parameters after pruning and re-run the Riley criteria with the expected prevalence of new plaque. The tex's 20 events per weight (Ogundimu) is a rule of thumb and is conservative or liberal depending on prevalence and R^2; Riley's criteria replace it and should be shown alongside it.
7. If events fall short, the fallback is fixed in advance: LM block primary; side-branch block ridge-penalised in both models (tex). Consider elastic net only as a sensitivity analysis: under collinearity it groups predictors but is unstable in selection (Leeuwenberg), so it should not be used to pick features.
8. Optional pre-specified merge instead of drop: for the curvature family, a first principal component or a fixed average of z-scored kappa_a and one bend-count measure. I do not recommend it: it adds a definition that the tex has argued against (RMS curvature was dropped for measuring the same thing), and PCA components are harder to tie to a mechanism.
9. Interpret at the family level: likelihood-ratio test of each family (caliber, angle, curvature, participant), following the tex.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a defensible numeric reliability floor or an agreed unsupervised correlation cutoff for prediction models specifically; 0.7 is Dormann's, and I did not re-verify it.
- Riley formulae not retrieved as text; verify before quoting.
- Real-data VIFs and cluster structure are pending the feature table.
