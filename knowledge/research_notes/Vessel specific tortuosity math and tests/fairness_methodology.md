# Fairness and selection methodology for per-vessel coronary tortuosity

Scope note: the earlier reports in `reports/` already cover metric formulas (arc/chord TI, kappa_a, K, Q, Grisan turns), outcome links and the robustness evidence (carotid, aorta, van Zandwijk phase shift, Kjeldsberg bend detectors, Frenet-Serret sampling study). None of that is repeated here. These notes cover only (a) what the measure should be invariant to, (b) how to choose settings or metrics per vessel without cherry-picking, (c) validation without ground truth, (d) combining per-vessel values at patient level, (e) how retinal benchmarks handle selection, and (f) a critique of the 8 criteria in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`.

## 1. Normalisation and confounds: what should tortuosity be invariant to?

### Takeaway
A fair measure must be exactly invariant to rigid motion and, as a property of the estimator, insensitive to sampling density, truncation and resolution; those are nuisance. Invariance to uniform scaling (heart or body size) is a modelling choice, not a requirement: dimensionless shape indices (arc/chord, total turning K) are scale-free by construction, whereas curvature per mm scales as 1/size, and retinal evidence shows exactly that behaviour. The literature on cardiac size indexing says: do not divide by a size variable by default; keep the raw measure and either adjust for size as a covariate or use an empirically derived allometric exponent, verified by a residual correlation near zero.

### Cited Findings
- In a 2025 retinal standardisation study, every tested tortuosity index changed with image magnification: "τ hart displayed an inverse square relationship with the scaling factor" and "τ grisan" an "inverse proportionality response"; the paper states τ grisan "lacks scale invariance, meaning that the resulting value depends on the chosen unit of measurement". **[full text]** ([PMC12265663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12265663/))
- The same study reports resolution/resolving-power effects of "as much as 40% depending on the actual tortuosity index and the FOV", that the same vessel imaged in different parts of the field of view can give a different tortuosity, and that the centerline extraction algorithm mattered even between methods of similar segmentation accuracy: "correctness is not a reliable indicator of the quality of tortuosity assessment". **[full text]** ([PMC12265663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12265663/))
- Lisowska et al. 2014 compared five retinal indices (distance measure, tortuosity density, two curvature measures, slope chain code) across centerline sampling rates on RET-TORT: curvature measures were best at high resampling but "the most sensitive to variations of the number of samples"; slope chain coding was best at low sampling rates; they conclude that choosing an index "requires attention to numerical details and ideally standardization thereof". **[abstract-only]** ([EMBC 2014, doi:10.1109/embc.2014.6944850](https://doi.org/10.1109/embc.2014.6944850); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/An-experimental-assessment-of-five-indices-of-with-Lisowska-Annunziata/2156588688e5100e2c810bf9a538929a624f28df))
- Ratio indexing is statistically hazardous: Kronmal 1993 shows that ratios in regression can produce incorrect or misleading inferences, recommends avoiding them, and says a ratio should enter a regression only as part of a full linear model that also includes its components as main effects (examples BMI, FEV). **[abstract-only]** ([JRSS A 156:379-392](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/2983064))
- In echocardiography, simple isometric indices (e.g. dimension/BSA) "rather than removing the correlation, actually increased the correlation with all body size variables", while dividing by body size raised to an empirically fitted allometric exponent (e.g. BSA^0.449, height^0.428) left residual correlations of -0.01 to 0.01. **[abstract-only, via search snippet]** ([Derivation of a size-independent variable, adult population, JASE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S089473170700884X); paediatric companion [EHJ-CVI](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/10/1/50/2464711))
- Counterpoint that indexing can still carry outcome information: LV mass indexed as BSA^1.5 or height^2.7 detected hypertrophy more often and was associated with more incident events than plain BSA indexing; a separate paper argues prognostic association supports BSA indexing. **[abstract-only, via search snippet]** ([PMC10632399](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10632399/); [isometric vs allometric review, JCVI](https://e-jcvi.org/DOIx.php?id=10.4250%2Fjcvi.2019.0114))

### Inferences
- **Invariance ledger to state in the thesis** (derivation, not literature): (i) rigid motion: exact invariance required for every metric, testable on synthetic curves; (ii) uniform scale s: arc/chord TI and total turning K are exponent 0; kappa_a (rad/mm) and Grisan-type sums per length are exponent -1; squared-curvature energies are exponent -3 when integrated or -2 per length. The retinal finding (Hart inverse square, Grisan inverse) matches this bookkeeping, so the thesis should list the expected scale exponent of each metric and check it on scaled synthetic curves, rather than claim "scale invariance" generically. (iii) sampling density, resampling step, smoothing scale and truncation: these are estimator artefacts and should be driven to insensitivity (criteria 2 and 3). (iv) scanner/resolution: cannot be tested on ImageCAS (one scanner protocol per scan) and must be stated as a limitation.
- **When scale invariance throws information away.** For haemodynamics, what matters is bend radius relative to lumen radius (the Dean-type curvature ratio, a standard fluid mechanics quantity; no source fetched here). A dimensionless TI of a small and a large heart with the same shape are equal, but the smaller heart has tighter bends in mm. If the plaque hypothesis is haemodynamic, curvature in mm^-1 (or kappa times local radius) carries information that TI discards. If the hypothesis is about a developmental or ageing "shape" phenotype, scale-free TI is the right object. The thesis can keep both, since the panel already pairs TI (scale-free) with kappa_a (scale-bearing).
- **Recommended handling of size:** do not divide kappa_a by a heart-size proxy. Keep it in mm^-1, and add a size covariate (for example total centerline length of the tree, or a heart-size proxy if available in CGPS) to the outcome model, per Kronmal. If an indexed value is wanted for descriptive plots, fit the allometric exponent on the healthy reference set and show the residual correlation is near zero, as the echo papers do.
- **Vessel length is both nuisance and biology.** A truncated centerline lowers arc/chord (the most tortuous distal parts are lost) and changes kappa_a if tortuosity varies along the vessel. That estimator dependence should be tested by truncating the same curve (synthetic and real) to fixed fractions, which isolates estimator bias from the real length-tortuosity association. A real correlation between length and tortuosity across patients should not be removed by construction.

### Gaps
- No coronary study was found that normalises tortuosity by heart size or tests allometric scaling of coronary geometry. The scaling argument above is derivation only.
- Sankey, Dean-number and coronary allometry sources were not fetched; the Dean-number point is standard physics but uncited here.
- Which echo paper gave which exponent could not be confirmed from snippets; verify before citing.

## 2. Preventing cherry-picking when metrics or settings differ per vessel

### Takeaway
The defensible pattern, borrowed from radiomics, is: one selection rule applied identically to every vessel, written down before any outcome is looked at, using only label-free criteria (reproducibility ICC with a pre-set lower-CI threshold, synthetic behaviour), evaluated on the training split, then frozen and confirmed on held-out data, followed by a fixed reported panel with multiplicity control. Per-vessel differences in settings are then outputs of a pre-registered rule, not choices.

### Cited Findings
- The IBSI validation phase assessed feature reproducibility with a "two-way, random-effects, single-rater, absolute agreement" ICC and classified each feature by the **lower bound of the 95% CI** of the ICC following Koo and Li: poor < 0.50, moderate 0.50 to 0.75, good 0.75 to 0.90, excellent > 0.90. **[full text]** ([IBSI, Radiology 2020;295:328-338, RUG repository PDF](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/119920642/The_Image_Biomarker_Standardization_Initiative_Standardized_Quantitative_Radiomics_for_High_Throughput_Image_based_Phenotyping.pdf); [RSNA](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/abs/10.1148/radiol.2020191145))
- Koo and Li 2016 give the same bands (poor < 0.5, moderate 0.5 to 0.75, good 0.75 to 0.9, excellent > 0.9), note there are 10 ICC forms, and require that the form be stated because each form carries different assumptions and interpretation. **[abstract-only]** ([PMC4913118](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/); [PubMed 27330520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27330520/))
- IBSI showed that "seemingly well-defined mathematic formulas can vary greatly in the numeric results they produce": initial consensus between 25 teams was weak for 232 of 302 features (76.8%) in phase I; causes included differences in interpolation and morphological representation. After iteration, 169 of 174 features were standardised, and in phase III 166 (CT), 164 (PET) and 164 (MRI) were excellently reproducible. IBSI defines compliance as computing features on a digital phantom and comparing against reference values, and it defers modelling guidance to TRIPOD. **[full text]** ([IBSI PDF](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/119920642/The_Image_Biomarker_Standardization_Initiative_Standardized_Quantitative_Radiomics_for_High_Throughput_Image_based_Phenotyping.pdf))
- Zwanenburg et al. 2019 classified features as robust when the ICC 95% CI lower bound was at least 0.90, computed across 18 image-perturbation chains (noise, translation, rotation, volume growth/shrinkage, supervoxel contour randomisation) and test-retest scans in 31 NSCLC and 19 HNSCC patients. Test-retest robust fractions were 73.5% vs 34.0% between cohorts. The chain noise + translation + volume change + contour randomisation had the fewest false-positive robust features (3.3% NSCLC, 10.0% HNSCC) and "may be used to assess feature robustness" when test-retest imaging is unavailable. **[abstract-only]** ([arXiv 1806.06719](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06719); [Sci Rep 9:614](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-36938-4))
- Traverso et al. 2018 (41 studies) found no consensus on which shape or texture features are reproducible, and flagged poor reporting of extraction software, preprocessing and "the cutoff value used to distinguish stable features". **[abstract-only]** ([IJROBP 102:1143-1158](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360301618309052); [Maastricht record](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/repeatability-and-reproducibility-of-radiomic-features-a-systemat/))
- The Radiomics Quality Score has 16 items including phantom study, imaging at multiple time points, "feature reduction or adjustment for multiple testing", multivariable analysis with non-radiomic features, prospective study registered in a trial database, validation, and open science; total range -8 to 36, and registered prospective studies carry the heaviest weight (7 points). **[secondary]** ([cholangiocarcinoma RQS review, Insights Imaging 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13244-023-01365-1); [RQS meta-analysis, PMC11835903](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11835903/)). The original Lambin 2017 page could not be fetched (paywall redirect).
- Bridgeford et al. 2021 selected the best of 192 fMRI preprocessing pipelines on 28 CoRR datasets by maximising discriminability alone, "without accessing outcome labels", and give a permutation two-sample test for whether one pipeline is significantly more discriminable than another. **[full text]** ([PLoS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009279](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009279))

### Inferences
- **Selection protocol for the thesis** (synthesis, derived from the sources above):
  1. Write the rule before computing any values: candidate metrics, the parameter grid (smoothing scale, chord length, resampling step), the label-free criteria, the ICC form (ICC(2,1) absolute agreement, as IBSI), and the thresholds on the ICC **lower 95% CI bound**, not the point estimate. Commit it to git with a date; that is the thesis-scale equivalent of registration.
  2. Apply the same rule to each named vessel (LM, LAD, LCx, RCA). If the rule yields different chord lengths for LCx and RCA, that is permitted because the rule was fixed and the outcome was not used.
  3. Tie-breaking must also be pre-specified (for example "smallest smoothing scale whose ICC lower bound passes"), otherwise choosing among several passing settings is a hidden degree of freedom.
  4. Select on the 560-case training split only; confirm the chosen settings on val (80) and report test (160) numbers once.
  5. Report every candidate and every vessel in a table, pass or fail, so readers can see what was discarded (Traverso's reporting complaint).
- **Prefer "same metric, vessel-specific scale" over "different metric per vessel".** A different formula per vessel makes cross-vessel comparison impossible; a different smoothing scale expressed in mm is a calibration of one estimator. The thesis should state that preference explicitly.
- **Multiplicity:** with 2 primary metrics x 4 vessels x one outcome, there are 8 tests. Either pre-specify one primary (for example a patient-level composite, section 4) and treat the rest as secondary, or control family-wise error (Holm) or FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg) over the fixed panel. RQS explicitly rewards adjustment for multiple testing.
- **Threshold choice:** 0.75 (Koo-Li "good") on the lower CI bound is a defensible gate for inclusion; 0.90 (Zwanenburg, IBSI "excellent") is a defensible gate for a primary metric. Using the lower bound rather than the point estimate makes the gate depend on sample size, which is desirable because it penalises settings estimated on few vessels (LM is short and often too short).

## 3. Validation without ground truth

### Takeaway
Three label-free tools are well established: synthetic phantoms with known answers (IBSI's digital phantom is the radiomics analogue), perturbation-based ICC when no test-retest scans exist (Zwanenburg 2019), and discriminability, which is a rank-based, multivariate generalisation of the between/within variance ratio that provably lower-bounds downstream predictive accuracy (Bridgeford 2021). Agreement with expert grading is the only external anchor and in retinal work rests on 30 vessels and one rater.

### Cited Findings
- Discriminability is the probability that two measurements of the same item are closer to each other than to a measurement of a different item; it works on distance ranks, is "robust to monotonic transformations", handles multivariate and non-Euclidean data, and under a multivariate mixture model with additive noise "provides a lower bound on the predictive accuracy of a subsequent classification task". In their simulations ICC could not distinguish some scenarios and could become negative with outliers, and the fingerprinting index had almost no power when nearest neighbours were other individuals. **[full text]** ([Bridgeford 2021, PLoS Comput Biol](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009279))
- The pipeline chosen by discriminability alone (FNNNCP) was significantly more discriminable than about 80% of the 192 strategies at alpha 0.05. **[full text]** ([Bridgeford 2021](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009279))
- IBSI used a small 80-voxel digital phantom with a 74-voxel ROI so that exact values were known, then a real CT image, then a multimodality validation set: phantom, then real-data consensus, then reproducibility. **[full text]** ([IBSI PDF](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/119920642/The_Image_Biomarker_Standardization_Initiative_Standardized_Quantitative_Radiomics_for_High_Throughput_Image_based_Phenotyping.pdf))
- Perturbation can stand in for test-retest; noise + translation + volume + contour randomisation minimised false-positive robustness. **[abstract-only]** ([Zwanenburg 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06719))
- Expert agreement on RET-TORT: a 2025 study ranked eight local indices against a single ophthalmologist's ordering of 30 artery segments with Spearman's rho; best local index T6 rho = 0.895, T4 0.863, T7 0.790, distance factor 0.770; best global weighting [T6]WAL 0.873. **[full text]** ([PLOS One 2025, PMC12331121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/))
- The 2025 retinal standardisation study found that agreement with the expert ranking (SRCC) fell as magnification and upsampling increased, so expert agreement is itself acquisition-dependent. **[full text]** ([PMC12265663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12265663/))

### Inferences
- **ICC is population-dependent, and pooling vessels inflates it.** ICC = between-subject variance / total. If LAD, LCx and RCA are pooled, systematic between-vessel-type differences count as "signal" and make a noisy metric look reliable. Compute jitter ICC **within each named vessel**. This is the most important refinement to criterion 3.
- **Add discriminability alongside ICC.** For a scalar metric, discriminability on jitter replicates is close in spirit to ICC but rank-based, so it is robust to the skewed, near-floor distributions typical of arc/chord (TI clusters near 0.1 to 0.2 in healthy coronaries). For a multi-metric panel (TI, kappa_a together) it gives one replicability number for the whole vector, and its permutation two-sample test is a principled way to say "setting A is more replicable than setting B" rather than eyeballing ICC differences. Its bound on predictive accuracy is the formal argument that selecting on replicability cannot hurt, and does not peek at the outcome.
- **Perturbation design for centerlines:** mirror Zwanenburg's chain at the geometry level: spatially correlated point jitter (about 0.5 mm), small rigid transforms (tests invariance), endpoint truncation of a few mm at the distal end and ostium (analogue of volume growth/shrinkage), and resampling phase shifts. For the segmentation route, a perturbed-mask re-extraction is the analogue of contour randomisation.
- **Synthetic phantoms are the IBSI digital phantom of this thesis:** a small set of curves with analytically known TI, kappa_a and K (line, arc, helix, sinusoid, rotated copies, scaled copies) against which the implementation is checked to a tolerance, before any real data.
- **Expert agreement gives a ceiling, not a target:** a metric cannot credibly agree with one rater better than two raters agree with each other. With about 30 vessels, a Spearman rho of 0.8 has a wide confidence interval; the thesis should report the CI and use expert agreement as a pass/fail gate rather than as the criterion that picks between passing candidates, otherwise the 30 ranked vessels become a small, overfitted outcome.

## 4. Statistical treatment when metrics differ per vessel

### Takeaway
No source found addresses this for tortuosity, so the recommendations are transferred statistical practice: standardise within vessel against a reference distribution fixed on the training split (or healthy cohort), then either average standardised values into a pre-specified patient composite, or, preferably, fit a mixed model with vessel as a fixed factor and patient as a random effect, testing the vessel-by-tortuosity interaction explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- Kronmal's warning against ratios applies equally to ad hoc per-vessel divisions: include components as main effects in a full model rather than dividing. **[abstract-only]** ([Kronmal 1993](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/2983064))
- The RQS rewards multivariable analysis with non-radiomic features and adjustment for multiple testing. **[secondary]** ([Insights Imaging 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13244-023-01365-1))

### Inferences
- **Option A, within-vessel z-scores:** z = (x - mu_v) / sd_v with mu_v, sd_v from training-split (or healthy CGPS) vessels of type v, frozen and applied unchanged to val/test and CGPS. Because TI and kappa_a are right-skewed with a floor at 0, use a log or rank-based inverse-normal transform before standardising, and fix that transform on the training split too. A patient composite is then the mean of available vessel z-scores; missing vessels (NaN rows) should be handled by averaging available ones and reporting how many were available, not by imputation.
- **Option B, mixed model (preferred for the plaque analysis):** outcome at vessel level ~ tortuosity_z + vessel + tortuosity_z:vessel + age + sex + hypertension + size covariate + (1 | patient). This respects within-patient correlation, lets the effect differ by vessel, and avoids the "no segment vs segment within patient" issue in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md` because vessels are compared to the same vessel in other patients via the fixed vessel effect. The global test of tortuosity (main effect plus interactions) is one pre-specified primary test, which also addresses multiplicity.
- **If different metrics are used per vessel,** only Option A composites or per-vessel models are legitimate; a pooled model requires a common metric. This is another reason to prefer same-metric, vessel-specific scale.
- Standardising against the healthy reference distribution also makes CGPS values interpretable as percentiles of a normal population, which is the natural fairness claim across patients.

### Gaps
- No tortuosity paper was found that reports per-vessel z-scoring or mixed models for coronary geometry; this whole section is inference.

## 5. How retinal tortuosity benchmarks handle selection and agreement

### Takeaway
Retinal benchmarks rank candidate indices by Spearman correlation with an expert ordering on RET-TORT (30 arteries, 30 veins in the original dataset, one expert), and more recent ones add sensitivity to sampling and magnification. None pre-registers a selection rule, and all rest on small single-rater gold standards, which the thesis should not imitate uncritically.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 PLOS One: 8 local indices and several global weightings, 30 RET-TORT artery segments ranked by one ophthalmologist, Spearman rho as the sole agreement statistic (values in section 3). **[full text]** ([PMC12331121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/))
- Lisowska 2014: five indices on RET-TORT, performance tracked as a function of sampling rate; conclusion that the best index depends on sampling and that standardisation is needed. **[abstract-only]** ([doi:10.1109/embc.2014.6944850](https://doi.org/10.1109/embc.2014.6944850))
- Poletti, Grisan, Ruggeri 2012 estimated image-level tortuosity in wide-field ROP images; the Poletti measure is described as the sum of squared angle changes divided by vessel length. **[abstract-only, via search snippet]** ([EMBC 2012, doi:10.1109/embc.2012.6347105](https://doi.org/10.1109/embc.2012.6347105))
- 2025 standardisation study: SRCC against the expert ranking as the performance measure, recomputed under magnification, resolution and extractor changes. **[full text]** ([PMC12265663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12265663/))
- Kalitzeos 2013 is a narrative review of formulas, clinical applications, pitfalls; it notes retinal tortuosity is a stable marker unaffected by pulse and highly heritable. **[abstract-only]** ([Exp Eye Res 106:40-46](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S001448351200320X))

### Inferences
- The retinal practice supports two elements of the thesis design: Spearman or Kendall rank agreement with experts, and explicit sweeps over sampling. It does not supply a model for outcome-blind selection: indices are picked because they correlate best with the same expert ranking used to report them. The thesis's criterion 7 should therefore be a threshold gate, with the rho confidence interval reported.
- Grisan 2008 and Lotmar details are already in the earlier reports and were not re-fetched.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch Grisan 2008 or Poletti full text here to confirm their agreement statistics; the earlier reports cite Grisan 2008 directly.
- RET-TORT composition (30 arteries + 30 veins) is from memory and prior reports, not confirmed in this pass.

## 6. Critique and refinement of the 8 criteria in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`

### Takeaway
The criteria are well aligned with radiomics practice (label-free, thresholds set first, plaque never used). Four refinements would make the fairness claim auditable: specify the ICC form and gate on its lower CI bound per named vessel; add scaling and truncation tests to criterion 1; reframe criterion 6 so that real biology is not removed; and demote criterion 8 from a selection gate to a post-lock sanity check, because sex enters the outcome model.

### Cited Findings
- ICC form must be stated and the lower 95% CI bound used against Koo-Li bands. **[full text]** ([IBSI](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/119920642/The_Image_Biomarker_Standardization_Initiative_Standardized_Quantitative_Radiomics_for_High_Throughput_Image_based_Phenotyping.pdf)); **[abstract-only]** ([Koo & Li](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/))
- Indices differ in scale behaviour (inverse, inverse-square). **[full text]** ([PMC12265663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12265663/))
- Discriminability selects pipelines without labels and bounds downstream accuracy. **[full text]** ([Bridgeford 2021](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009279))
- Ratios and "remove by construction" adjustments can create spurious associations. **[abstract-only]** ([Kronmal 1993](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/2983064))

### Inferences (criterion by criterion)
1. **Synthetic curves.** Keep. Add: (a) a uniform-scaling test with each metric's expected exponent declared (0 for TI and K, -1 for kappa_a), so "scale behaviour" is verified rather than asserted; (b) a truncation test (same curve cut to 100/80/60%) separating estimator bias from biology; (c) a resampling test on analytic curves where the true value is known, which is the IBSI digital-phantom step. Numeric tolerance should be written down.
2. **Rank stability (Spearman rho over smoothing/resampling).** Keep, but compute within each named vessel, and pre-state the grid and the rho threshold. Add the pre-specified tie-break rule for choosing among passing settings.
3. **Jitter ICC.** Specify ICC(2,1) absolute agreement; gate on the lower 95% CI bound (0.75 to pass, 0.90 for a primary metric); compute per named vessel, never pooled (pooling inflates ICC). Use a Zwanenburg-style perturbation chain: correlated jitter, rigid transform, endpoint truncation, resampling phase. Report discriminability for the panel as a complementary, rank-based statistic.
4. **Reference vs CAS-Net-derived centerlines.** Keep; ICC plus Bland-Altman, per vessel. This is the closest thing to a reproducibility study across "measurement systems" that the data allow; state clearly it is not scanner or phase reproducibility.
5. **No floor or ceiling effect.** Pre-state the threshold (a common convention is more than 15% of values at the extreme; source not fetched here). Evaluate per vessel; LM will likely fail on length alone, which should be reported, not tuned away.
6. **Low correlation with length, radius, Image Quality.** Split in two. Image Quality is a pure measurement nuisance: require no shift across strata in values and in ICC. Length and radius may carry true biology (larger or longer vessels may truly be more tortuous), so "low correlation" should be replaced by "estimator bias from truncation and resampling below a pre-set tolerance on synthetic and truncated real curves"; the observed length correlation is then reported and handled as a covariate in the outcome model (Kronmal), not used to reject a metric.
7. **Blind expert ranking (Kendall tau).** Keep, but use at least two raters, report inter-rater tau as the ceiling, report the tau CI (n about 30 is small), and use it as a gate only. Stratify the 30 by vessel type or restrict to one vessel type, otherwise the rater may be ranking vessel identity.
8. **Healthy CGPS distribution, women higher.** This is a known-groups validity check, but sex is a covariate in the planned outcome model, so selecting on it is a mild form of outcome use and the Groves 2009 evidence is 2D angiography. Recommend: lock the definition using criteria 1 to 7 on ImageCAS, then report criterion 8 once as a confirmatory known-groups check, stating the expected direction in advance.
- **Missing criterion to add:** a written, dated selection rule (metric grid, thresholds, tie-break, split usage) committed before criteria 2 to 7 are computed, plus a fixed reporting panel listing every candidate and vessel with pass/fail. This is what converts "different settings per vessel" from cherry-picking into a pre-registered calibration.

### Gaps
- No vascular-geometry precedent for per-vessel pre-registered selection; the framework is a transfer from radiomics and neuroimaging.
- The 15% floor/ceiling convention (commonly attributed to Terwee et al. 2007 for questionnaires) was not fetched and should be sourced before use.
- Whether ImageCAS-X has enough LM length per case for any criterion is an empirical question for the training split.
