# Per-vessel coronary anatomy and what it implies for vessel-specific tortuosity

Scope note. Outcome evidence (plaque, CAD association of metrics) and robustness evidence (smoothing, noise, CV across segmentations, Kashyap 2022 per-region R²) are already in `reports/` and `research_notes/Choosing a coronary tortuosity descriptor/`, `.../Alternative tortuosity merges for coronaries/`, `.../Merged tortuosity methods for coronaries/`. They are not repeated here. This note covers only how the vessels differ from one another. Every cited finding is tagged **[full text]** (the article body was read) or **[abstract only]** (only the abstract or a search/secondary summary was read). Units: 1 cm⁻¹ = 0.1 mm⁻¹; radius of curvature = 1/κ.

## Q1. Typical course and gross shape per vessel, and what that means for "baseline" curvature

### Takeaway
The vessels split into two anatomical classes: atrioventricular (AV) groove vessels (RCA, LCx) that wrap around the base of the heart, and interventricular or free-wall vessels (LAD, diagonals, OMs, PDA, PLA) that run over contracting myocardium toward or around the apex. The RCA's C-shape inflates any global chord-based index by construction (angiographic arc/chord median about 2.0 against about 1.3 for LAD and LCx), so raw whole-vessel L/D is not comparable across vessels. The only 3D per-vessel reference values found are from biplane angiography at end-diastole in normal LAD and RCA (Zhu 2008): mean curvature about 0.5 to 0.66 cm⁻¹ (radius about 15 to 20 mm), highest distally in the LAD, lowest mid-RCA. No comparable 3D reference exists for the LCx or side branches.

### Cited Findings

Segment definitions and landmarks (these fix where "proximal/mid/distal" comparisons can be made):
- SCCT 2014 18-segment model: pRCA = "Ostium of the RCA to one-half the distance to the acute margin of heart"; mRCA = "End of proximal RCA to the acute margin of heart"; dRCA = "End of mid RCA to origin of the PDA"; LM = "Ostium of LM to bifurcation of LAD and LCx"; pLAD = "End of LM to the first large septal or D1 (>1.5 mm in size) whichever is most proximal"; mLAD = "End of proximal LAD to one-half the distance to the apex"; dLAD = "End of mid LAD to end of LAD"; pCx = "End of LM to the origin of the OM1"; OM1 = "First OM1 traversing the lateral wall of the left ventricle"; mid/distal LCx = "Traveling in the atrioventricular groove, distal to the OM1 branch to the end of the vessel or origin of the L-PDA"; plus D1, D2, OM2, R-PDA, L-PDA, R-PLB, L-PLB, ramus (segment 17), L-PLB (segment 18). [full text] [SCCT guidelines, Leipsic et al., J Cardiovasc Comput Tomogr 2014;8:342-358, Appendix 1](https://cdn.ymaws.com/scct.org/resource/resmgr/Docs/Fellows&Residents_of_SCCT/Interpretation_guidelines.pdf)
- The same guideline notes that on transaxial images "tortuous arteries will move in and out of plane", and lists tortuosity only as a qualitative descriptor of stenosis extent in the report template; it gives no quantitative tortuosity definition. [full text] [SCCT 2014](https://cdn.ymaws.com/scct.org/resource/resmgr/Docs/Fellows&Residents_of_SCCT/Interpretation_guidelines.pdf)
- The segment landmarks are therefore mixed in kind: branch origins (D1, OM1, PDA), fractions of distance (half-way to acute margin, half-way to apex) and surface landmarks (acute margin, AV groove, apex). Only the branch-origin landmarks are directly available from a labelled centerline tree. (Inference from the SCCT definitions above.)

Reference geometry, LAD and RCA (biplane angiography, 3D axes):
- Zhu et al. 2008: 32 LAD and 35 RCA, angiographically normal, 52 patients (mean age 53.4, 61.5% female), end-diastolic frames only. Vessels split into thirds by landmarks (LAD: origin to first septal, to D2, to apex). [full text] [Zhu 2008, PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- Zhu 2008 mean curvature, proximal/mid/distal: LAD 0.52 / 0.50 / 0.57 cm⁻¹; RCA 0.66 / 0.58 / 0.62 cm⁻¹. Max curvature: LAD 0.87 / 1.10 / 1.59; RCA 1.27 / 1.09 / 1.51 cm⁻¹. Tortuosity (1 − chord/arc): LAD 0.06 / 0.05 / 0.15; RCA 0.16 / 0.06 / 0.17. Mean torsion: LAD 3.31 / 3.36 / 3.82; RCA 2.46 / 2.03 / 2.91 cm⁻¹. [full text] [Zhu 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- Zhu 2008: "in the LAD, curvature, torsion and tortuosity are generally highest in the distal portion; in the RCA, these parameters are smallest in the middle segment"; "the LAD exhibits significantly higher torsion measures, but lower tortuosity, than the RCA" (torsion P<0.005, tortuosity P<0.0001; mean torsion 3.73 vs 2.84 cm⁻¹). Two factors (curvature+tortuosity; torsion) explain >80% of geometric variability. [full text] [Zhu 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)

Whole-vessel arc/chord differs by vessel because of gross shape:
- Zebić Mihić et al. 2023, 2D invasive angiography, TI = vessel length / straight-line length from ostium to the smallest visible branch, in diastole, with a vessel-specific projection (LAD: AP or RAO cranial; LCx: AP or LAO caudal; RCA: LAO and AP). Median TI (IQR), NOCAD vs OCAD: LCx 1.30 (1.13-1.47) vs 1.15 (1.10-1.30); LAD 1.26 (1.18-1.45) vs 1.18 (1.14-1.26); RCA 2.00 (1.79-2.27) vs 1.87 (1.65-2.17). The authors attribute the high RCA value to its naturally curved C-shaped course. [full text] [Zebić Mihić 2023, PMC10795752](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/)
- Van Zandwijk et al. (CCTA, 71 patients, 137 arteries: RCA 38.7%, LAD 32.8%, LCx 28.5%) report pooled arc/chord of 1.33 at artery level and 1.10 at segment level at end-diastole, and pooled Menger curvature (5 mm spacing) of 0.081 mm⁻¹ at artery level; no per-vessel breakdown was reported. [full text] [van Zandwijk 2019/2020, PMC7165136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)
- Note the gap between Zhu's end-diastolic mean curvature (0.05 to 0.066 mm⁻¹) and van Zandwijk's 0.081 mm⁻¹: different modality, smoothing and point spacing, so absolute curvature reference values do not transfer between studies. [full text for both] [Zhu 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/); [van Zandwijk](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)
- A 2024 CCTA study of 39 left coronary trees (RCA excluded) found whole-tree average absolute curvature negatively correlated with diameter (r = −0.474, p = 0.002), "suggesting that smaller arteries tend to be more curved". [full text] [Royal Soc Open Sci 2024, PMC11416812](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11416812/)

Lengths, sizes and gross variants:
- Main-trunk length from origin to about 1 mm diameter: LCx 8.4 to 10.7 cm, LAD 10 to 13.8 cm, RCA 11.2 to 18.7 cm; LM 5 to 20 mm. [abstract only, search summary of AJR review] [AJR 2007, Normal and variant coronary anatomy on high-resolution CT angiography](https://www.ajronline.org/doi/full/10.2214/AJR.06.1295)
- Coronary atlas, 300 zero-calcium normal CTAs at 75% R-R: LM length 10.5 ± 5.3 mm, LM ostium diameter 3.5 ± 0.8 mm; LM bifurcation angle about 80° against about 50° at other major bifurcations; LM angle wider in males (85° vs 74°, p<0.001) and wider with a ramus (89° vs 75°); LAD diameter falls about 0.25 mm per 10 mm; "female hearts showed more pronounced spherical geometry requiring higher inflow curvature". [full text, journal HTML] [Medrano-Gracia et al., EuroIntervention, computational atlas](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy); earlier 122-scan version [abstract only] [Medrano-Gracia 2014, PubMed 25485418](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25485418/)
- Shepherd's crook RCA (superiorly oriented ostium, proximal RCA ascends then turns back into the groove) has an estimated prevalence of about 5% and is classified as a non-haemodynamically significant variant. [abstract only, search summary] [Saglam 2014, Kardiol Pol, PubMed 25371304](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25371304/); [case report PMC9803697](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9803697/)
- Wrap-around LAD (distal LAD continues past the apex onto the inferior wall): 26 to 35% when defined as perfusing at least 25% of the inferior wall, 73 to 78% with any apical branch, and 47% (right dominance) to 87% (left dominance). [abstract only, figures from a case report's literature summary] [PMC12056774](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12056774/)

Repo-local measurements (from the earlier note, not literature): in 20 ImageCAS-X cases, chained vessel lengths median LAD 128 mm, RCA 107 mm, LCx 81 mm, LM 10 mm; side branches D1 42, OM1 45, R-PDA 48, R-PLA 45 mm, with 1 to 4 of 6 to 22 side branches under 20 mm. [`research_notes/Choosing a coronary tortuosity descriptor/repo_data_constraints.md`, Q3]

### Inferences
- The RCA's global arc/chord near 2.0 is mostly the C-shape around the acute margin, not pathology; the LCx is also a groove vessel but shorter and wraps less, so its arc/chord sits near the LAD's. Any whole-vessel chord-based index must therefore be compared within vessel (z-scored per vessel or per SCCT segment), never pooled across vessels.
- Segment-level arc/chord (van Zandwijk 1.10 vs 1.33 artery level) removes most of the gross-shape component because a 3-segment split cuts the C-shape into gentler arcs. This is an argument for SCCT-segment-level reporting of chord-based indices, particularly on the RCA.
- Local curvature is less shape-contaminated than arc/chord but still carries a vessel-specific offset (RCA mean curvature exceeds LAD in all three thirds in Zhu 2008) and a diameter effect (smaller vessels more curved), so side branches will read higher than trunks on raw curvature.
- The LAD's distal third (apical wrap, and in 26 to 78% of people the wrap onto the inferior wall) is the high-curvature, high-torsion part; a per-vessel method for the LAD should either stop at the apex landmark or report the apical portion separately, since whether the wrap is present is an anatomical variant, not tortuosity.
- The proximal RCA is where the shepherd's crook variant lives (about 5%), and Zhu found pRCA tortuosity 0.16 against 0.06 mid. A proximal-RCA outlier is more likely a variant than a disease marker.
- LM (median 10 mm here, 10.5 mm in the atlas) is too short for any shape index; the atlas reports it by bifurcation angle instead, which is the appropriate descriptor.

### Gaps
- No 3D per-vessel reference values for curvature, torsion or arc/chord were found for the LCx, D1/D2, OM, PDA or PLA, from either CCTA or angiography. Zhu 2008 covers only LAD and RCA.
- No per-SCCT-segment reference table from CCTA was found; van Zandwijk pooled all vessels.
- Per-segment typical lengths (e.g. pLAD, mLAD) were not found in a primary source; only whole-trunk ranges.

## Q2. Which vessel shows which tortuosity type, and variation with age, sex, hypertension, dominance, heart size and bridging

### Takeaway
Visually graded tortuosity concentrates in the left system: in angiography the LCx (26.9%) and LAD (21.1%) are tortuous far more often than the RCA (1.4%) under a "≥3 bends of ≥45°" rule, which suggests the bend-count rule is effectively calibrated on left-sided free-wall vessels. Female sex and hypertension are consistent associations; age is inconsistent after adjustment; larger hearts and higher BMI associate with less tortuosity. No study found reports tortuosity type (corkscrew, S-bend, loop, kink) per named vessel with numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Li et al. 2011 (angiography): CT (coronary tortuosity) = "≥3 bends (defined as ≥45° change in vessel direction) along main trunk of at least one artery, present both in systole and in diastole". Prevalence by vessel: LCx 26.9%, LAD 21.1%, RCA 1.4%, LAD and LCx together 9.9% of patients. Female OR 2.603 (p<0.001), hypertension OR 1.533 (p=0.006), CAD OR 0.755 (p=0.045); no age difference. [full text] [Li 2011, PLoS One, PMC3164184](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/)
- Eleid 2014 (SCAD): tortuosity in 78% of SCAD vs 17% of controls; recurrent SCAD occurred within tortuous segments in 80%; the prespecified markers "corkscrew appearance" and "multivessel symmetrical tortuosity" associated with extracoronary vasculopathy including FMD (p<0.05). [abstract only] [Eleid 2014, PubMed 25138034](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25138034/)
- Grading scheme used by Eleid and later Tweet 2024: mild = ≥3 consecutive curvatures of 45 to 90°, or ≥3 of 90 to 180° in an artery <2 mm; moderate = ≥3 consecutive 90 to 180° in vessels ≥2 mm; severe = ≥2 consecutive curvatures ≥180° in vessels ≥2 mm; measured at end-diastole. Tweet 2024 (116 SCAD patients): 82.8% tortuous; about 0.89-unit higher score per decade of age (p<0.0001); FMD 59.4% vs 25%; no hypertension association (35% in both); no per-vessel breakdown. [full text] [Tweet 2024, PMC11605948](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11605948/)
- The grading scheme explicitly makes the threshold diameter-dependent (<2 mm vessels need larger bends to score), which is a de facto per-vessel adjustment for side branches and distal segments. [full text] [Tweet 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11605948/)
- Groves 2009 (1221 angiograms): severe tortuosity (two consecutive 180° turns, visual) in 12.45%; female sex higher (p=0.039); lower significant CAD (p=0.003); hypertension and age ≥65 not predictors. [abstract only] [Groves 2009, PubMed 19585899](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19585899/)
- Chiha 2017 (870 angiograms): severe tortuosity in 45.2% of women vs 19.7% of men (p<0.001); age associated (p<0.001). [abstract only] [Chiha 2017, PubMed 28616559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28616559/)
- CCTA, 1280 patients (320-row CT), CAT = "at least three consecutive curvatures of more than 45° measured during systole or diastole": 35% prevalence; age OR 1.02 (p=0.001), female 59.1% vs 40.9% (OR 1.68), hypertension OR 1.35 (p=0.024), overweight OR 0.76 and obese OR 0.46; no per-vessel breakdown. [full text] [PMC8867736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8867736/)
- Sharfo 2024 (CCTA, 194 of 250 CATCH participants, LAD only, arc/chord from vessel tracking): LAD tortuosity associated with hypertension (p<0.001), female sex (p=0.01), age (p=0.045); not with calcium score. [abstract only] [Sharfo 2024, PubMed 39210716](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39210716/)
- Tello Ayala 2026 (38,691 RCA angiograms, LAO view, turning-angle score): female β = 0.17 SD, hypertension β = 0.06 SD, diabetes β = −0.11 SD; age not significant after adjustment. RCA chosen because "its course and branching are more predictable and typically easier to segment". [full text] [Tello Ayala 2026, PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)
- Hutchins 1977 (autopsy, 145 hearts with normal coronaries): tortuosity predicted by age (positive) and heart weight (negative) with about equal weight, and by arterial calibre (positive); tortuosity "increases with age and with cardiac shrinkage" and "decreases with cardiac enlargement". [abstract only, via search summary; Europe PMC has no abstract text] [Hutchins 1977, PubMed 141878](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/141878/)
- Myocardial bridging (LAD): among CCTA patients with an LAD bridge, 278 with LAD tortuosity vs 278 without; stenosis in 71.9% vs 57.6% (p<0.001). No geometry-level description of how bridging changes the shape of the bridged segment. [abstract only] [Cai 2020, PubMed 33423723](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33423723/)
- Review: age, hypertension, LV hypertrophy and coronary flow are proposed determinants of the "geodesic pattern" (curvature, torsion, tortuosity); coronaries differ from all other arterial sites by repetitive flexion with each contraction. [abstract only] [Turgut 2010, PubMed 19108911](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19108911/)
- Dominance: the only dominance-linked geometric finding located was the wrap-around LAD prevalence (47% right dominance vs 87% left dominance). [abstract only] [PMC12056774](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12056774/)

### Inferences
- The Li 2011 split (LCx 26.9%, LAD 21.1%, RCA 1.4%) cannot mean the RCA is geometrically straight, since its arc/chord is the highest of the three (Zebić Mihić 2023). A plausible reading is that the human grader discounts the RCA's large, expected C-curve and counts only superimposed small bends, whereas LAD and LCx small-scale bends are counted directly. That is, raters already apply a per-vessel baseline implicitly; an automatic method should do so explicitly.
- Hutchins' "shrinkage" mechanism (artery length fixed, heart smaller, so excess length must fold) predicts that a free vessel with slack forms S-bends and loops in the plane of the heart surface, and that the effect scales with how much length the vessel has relative to its territory. The long LAD and the LCx/OM on the lateral wall would be the natural places for this; the RCA in its fat-filled groove is more constrained. This is mechanism reasoning from abstract-level evidence.
- Because heart size and BMI move tortuosity in the opposite direction to age, a per-vessel index normalised by a scale of the vessel's own territory (e.g. chord or a heart-size proxy) is a candidate covariate or normaliser. ImageCAS-X has no heart mask, but the spread of the full coronary tree is a usable proxy.
- Type-by-vessel (corkscrew vs S-bend vs loop vs kink): corkscrew and symmetric multivessel tortuosity are reported in SCAD/FMD, i.e. a systemic arteriopathy phenotype, not tied to a named vessel. No numeric per-vessel type distribution exists in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No study found reports prevalence of corkscrew, S-bend, loop or kink per named vessel.
- No quantitative study of how bridging alters local curvature of the bridged LAD segment (straightened intramyocardial segment vs S-shaped entry/exit) was found; the Cai 2020 abstract only relates visual tortuosity to stenosis.
- No study of per-vessel tortuosity against dominance was found, beyond the wrap-around LAD prevalence.
- Hutchins 1977 full text was not accessed; the size of the heart-weight effect is unknown here.

## Q3. Should tortuosity be measured relative to the heart surface? Are there decomposition papers?

### Takeaway
No coronary paper was found that decomposes centerline curvature into an epicardial-surface component and a residual. The mathematics exists (Darboux frame: κ² = κ_g² + κ_n²), and it gives a clean argument: a curve confined to a surface of normal curvature k_n has at least that much curvature even if it runs "straight" (along a geodesic) on the surface. The literature closest to this idea is indirect: raters and the SCCT scheme treat groove courses as expected; epicardial geodesic distance fields are used for perfusion territories; and the atlas notes female hearts are more spherical and force more inflow curvature.

### Cited Findings
- The Darboux frame {T, g, n} for a curve on a surface is the surface analogue of the Frenet frame; curvature splits into a normal component (bending imposed by the surface) and a geodesic component (bending within the surface), κ² = κ_n² + κ_g², and torsion relates to the geodesic torsion. [full text] [Wikipedia, Darboux frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darboux_frame)
- Patient-specific AHA territory work projects each coronary centerline point to its closest point on an epicardial quadrilateral mesh and computes geodesic distance fields on that mesh, because extracted centerlines "do not lie exactly on the epicardial surface". This is the only coronary example found of an epicardial surface parameterisation of centerlines; it is used for territories, not curvature. [abstract/snippet only] [Patient-specific coronary artery supply territory AHA diagrams, PMC7860735](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7860735/)
- Atlas: "female hearts showed more pronounced spherical geometry requiring higher inflow curvature" at bifurcations. [full text, journal HTML] [Medrano-Gracia, EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)
- Zhu 2008 groups geometry into a curvature/tortuosity factor and a torsion factor explaining >80% of variance; this is a statistical, not anatomical, decomposition. [full text] [Zhu 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- CFD literature names the inner and outer bend walls of a coronary the "myocardial" and "epicardial" surfaces, showing that the main curvature plane is taken to be normal to the heart surface. [abstract/snippet only] [Royal Soc Open Sci 2024 search summary](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsos.241267)

### Inferences
- A useful vessel-specific decomposition for this thesis is: total curvature κ(s) = baseline (the curvature the vessel would have if it ran along the heart surface without meandering) + residual. Three ways to get the baseline, in increasing anatomical fidelity and implementation cost:
  1. Scale detrending: smooth the centerline heavily (e.g. Gaussian σ of order 10 mm) and take the residual curve relative to the smoothed one. The smooth curve captures the C-shape of the RCA and the apical wrap of the LAD; residual curvature or residual arc/chord captures meanders. This needs no heart surface and is the cheapest vessel-agnostic implementation, but the detrending scale should differ per vessel (larger for the long, strongly curved RCA; smaller for side branches of 40 to 50 mm).
  2. Surface proxy: fit a sphere or ellipsoid to all centerline points of a scan (both sides) as a proxy for the epicardium, then split each point's curvature vector into the component along the proxy's normal (κ_n, imposed) and the tangential component (κ_g, meander on the surface). For a curve on a sphere of radius R, κ ≥ 1/R. If the effective epicardial radius were around 40 to 50 mm, the floor would be about 0.02 to 0.025 mm⁻¹, a substantial fraction of the 0.05 to 0.066 mm⁻¹ end-diastolic means in Zhu 2008. The radius value is an assumption, not a sourced number; the fraction should be measured on ImageCAS-X.
  3. True epicardium: needs a heart or LV mask, which ImageCAS-X does not ship.
- The geodesic curvature κ_g is exactly the quantity a human grader sees as "bends" on the angiogram of an LAD or OM running over the ventricle; κ_n is dominated by the heart's global shape and size, which Hutchins links to tortuosity in the opposite direction to age. Separating them should reduce the confounding of heart size in any tortuosity association.
- Groove vessels (RCA, LCx) are the ones where κ_n is large and structured (they wrap around the base), so a surface or detrending correction matters most there. Free-wall vessels (LAD, D, OM) are less curved by the surface along their length except at the apex.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that decomposes coronary centerline curvature into a heart-surface component and a residual, or that uses geodesic curvature on the epicardium as a tortuosity index. This appears to be an open gap in the literature (a searched-for absence, not proof of absence).
- No sourced value for an effective epicardial radius of curvature per region (base, lateral wall, apex) was found.

## Q4. Cardiac motion per vessel and how curvature changes between systole and diastole

### Takeaway
Motion and curvature change differ systematically by anatomical class. Liao 2002 found AV-groove vessels (LCx, RCA) did not change curvature between end-diastole and end-systole, while vessels over contracting myocardium (LAD, diagonals, OMs, PDA, PLA, RV marginals) increased mean curvature by 38% and had about six times more systolic flexion points. The RCA moves the most in space (about 26 mm at its mid-point) but largely as a rigid translation. This argues for fixing the cardiac phase and for per-vessel (at least per-class) baselines, and it warns that curvature on free-wall vessels is phase-sensitive in a way that groove vessels are not.

### Cited Findings
- Liao et al. 2002: 21 arteries from 3D angiographic reconstruction at ED and ES, Frenet-Serret analysis. Type 1 = AV groove (LCx, RCA); type 2 = over contracting myocardium (LAD, diagonal, OM, RV marginal, PDA, posterolateral). Curvature change ED→ES: type 1 −0.02 ± 0.03 cm⁻¹ (unchanged); type 2 +0.33 ± 0.04 cm⁻¹, a 38% increase (P<0.001). Flexion points (areas of systolic bending): 0.38 ± 0.18 vs 2.40 ± 0.23 per vessel (P<0.001), more common in distal segments and branch vessels. [abstract only] [Liao 2002, Catheter Cardiovasc Interv, Europe PMC abstract of PMID 11870940](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ccd.10106)
- Van Zandwijk (CCTA, retrospectively gated): pooled curvature 0.090 mm⁻¹ at ES vs 0.081 mm⁻¹ at ED, 11.1% higher at artery level (p=0.002) and 8.9% at segment level (p<0.001); arc/chord 1.36 vs 1.33 (p=0.09, n.s.) at artery level and 1.12 vs 1.10 (p=0.005) at segment level; inflection points 2.20 vs 1.96 (artery) and 0.63 vs 0.51 (segment), both p<0.001. No per-vessel split reported. [full text] [van Zandwijk, PMC7165136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)
- Shechter et al. 2006 (biplane angiography, 10 patients): 3D displacement over the cardiac cycle RCA ostium 14.4 ± 1.9 mm, RCA mid 26.3 ± 3.1 mm, RCA distal 23.2 ± 1.6 mm; LM ostium 7.9 ± 2.2 mm; LAD at 5 cm 9.6 ± 1.3 mm; LCx at 5 cm 12.1 ± 2.4 mm. Peak systolic velocity RCA mid 130.7 ± 19.4 mm/s vs RCA ostium 69.8 ± 13.8; LCx 56.4 vs LM 34.5 mm/s. Mid-diastolic quiescence at about 0.6 to 0.8 of the cycle. [full text] [Shechter 2006, PMC2396264](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2396264/)
- LAD and LCx motion is predominantly cranio-caudal; RCA motion predominantly right-left. [abstract/snippet only] [Shechter 2006, ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7254408_Displacement_and_Velocity_of_the_Coronary_Arteries_Cardiac_and_Respiratory_Motion)
- Gross and Friedman 1998: in one distal LAD (beyond D3) over two cycles, curvature change during the cycle ranged from 0.25 to 1.8 cm⁻¹ depending on site. [abstract only] [Gross 1998, J Biomech, PubMed 9727346](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021929098000128)
- Ding, Zhu and Friedman 2002: "considerable variability in dynamic geometric parameters from site to site along a vessel, between the right and left anterior descending arteries, and among individuals". [abstract only] [Ding 2002, PubMed 12085995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12085995/)
- Clinical grading rules are phase-fixed: Eleid/Tweet measure at end-diastole; Li 2011 requires bends present in both systole and diastole; the 1280-patient CCTA study accepts either phase. [full text] [Tweet 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11605948/); [Li 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/); [PMC8867736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8867736/)
- After stenting, mean curvature at ED and ES decreased by 22% and 21% (straightening of the treated vessel). [abstract/snippet only, search summary] [Three-dimensional analysis of in vivo coronary stent–coronary artery interactions, Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:CAIM.0000041950.84736.e6)

### Inferences
- The groove/free-wall split is the strongest anatomical argument for vessel-specific handling in the literature found: the same metric has a different phase sensitivity on RCA and LCx (≈0%) than on LAD and side branches (≈+38% ED→ES). If ImageCAS-X reconstructions come from mixed or unknown phases, free-wall vessel curvature will carry extra variance that groove vessel curvature will not. The phase of ImageCAS-X reconstructions should be checked (typically a single diastolic or best-phase reconstruction, but this was not verified here).
- The Li 2011 "present in both systole and diastole" rule is a phase-robust bend filter. On single-phase CCTA it cannot be applied, which argues for metrics that are less flexion-sensitive on free-wall vessels (e.g. arc/chord or low-order curvature moments at a scale above the flexion-point scale) and for keeping inflection or bend counts only on groove vessels, where they are phase-stable.
- The RCA's large rigid displacement matters for motion artefact (blur, step artefacts at mid-RCA where velocity is highest) rather than for true shape change. A per-vessel quality or artefact filter should be strictest at mid-RCA.
- Van Zandwijk's segment-level arc/chord was phase-sensitive (p=0.005) while artery-level was not (p=0.09); the segment scale is closer to the flexion-point scale. Scale choice and phase sensitivity interact, and that interaction likely differs per vessel.

### Gaps
- No per-vessel ED vs ES curvature change from CCTA was found (van Zandwijk pooled vessels); Liao 2002 is angiographic, 21 vessels, abstract only.
- The Pagiatakis work named in the brief was not located by author search; results returned only Zhu, Liao and van Zandwijk.
- No per-vessel torsion change over the cycle was found in accessible text.

## Q5. Segment definitions and how studies report tortuosity per segment

### Takeaway
The SCCT 18-segment model gives landmark definitions that map directly onto the ImageCAS-X label set for branches (D1, D2, OM1, OM2, R-PDA, R-PLA, L-PDA, L-PLA, ramus) and on proximal/mid splits defined by the first branch (pLAD ends at the first septal or D1; pCx ends at OM1; dRCA ends at the PDA). The remaining splits (half-way to the acute margin, half-way to the apex) need either surface landmarks or an arc-length fraction. Published per-segment tortuosity is sparse: Zhu 2008 (LAD, RCA thirds), van Zandwijk (segment vs artery level, pooled), and Kashyap 2022's 10 mm LM-adjacent pieces (covered in the earlier report).

### Cited Findings
- SCCT 2014 segment definitions are listed under Q1 above. [full text] [SCCT 2014](https://cdn.ymaws.com/scct.org/resource/resmgr/Docs/Fellows&Residents_of_SCCT/Interpretation_guidelines.pdf)
- Zhu 2008 used anatomical thirds (LAD: origin to first septal, first septal to D2, D2 to apex) and found the between-segment pattern differs by vessel (LAD: distal highest; RCA: middle lowest). [full text] [Zhu 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)
- Van Zandwijk measured both "artery level" and "segment level" (456 segments, 137 arteries) and found segment-level arc/chord much closer to 1 (1.10 vs 1.33) and fewer inflections (0.51 vs 1.96 at ED). [full text] [van Zandwijk](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)
- Angiographic TI studies measure the whole vessel from ostium to the smallest visible branch, each in a vessel-specific projection. [full text] [Zebić Mihić 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/)
- ImageCAS-X label map: 1 LM, 2 LAD, 3 LCX, 4 D1, 5 D2, 6 OM1, 7 OM2, 8 IM, 9 RCA, 10 R-PDA, 11 R-PLA, 12 L-PDA, 13 L-PLA, 14 Other; the RCA, LAD and LCx are not split into proximal/mid/distal in the labels. [repo] `git show tree-extraction:docs_thesis/label_map.json`, quoted in `research_notes/Choosing a coronary tortuosity descriptor/repo_data_constraints.md`

### Inferences
- Per-segment splitting that can be done from ImageCAS-X labels alone, faithful to SCCT: pLAD = LAD from LM junction to the D1 junction (first septal is not labelled, so D1 is the available proxy; SCCT uses whichever is more proximal); pCx = LCx to the OM1 junction; RCA ends at the R-PDA junction (dRCA end). The pRCA/mRCA split (half-way to the acute margin) and the mLAD/dLAD split (half-way to the apex) have no labelled landmark; an arc-length half-split of the remaining trunk is the closest reproducible substitute and should be declared as a deviation from SCCT.
- Given the vessel-specific segment patterns in Zhu 2008, reporting only whole-vessel means would average away the distal-LAD and proximal/distal-RCA peaks that carry most of the between-vessel difference.

### Gaps
- No CCTA table of per-SCCT-segment tortuosity or curvature reference values in normal subjects was found.
- Typical per-segment lengths (pLAD, mLAD, pRCA and so on) were not found in a primary source.

### Inferences: cross-question synthesis for the thesis argument

- Two anatomical classes, not nine separate methods, are what the evidence supports: groove vessels (RCA, LCx) with a large, predictable surface-imposed curvature and near-zero ED→ES curvature change; free-wall vessels (LAD, D1/D2, OM, PDA, PLA) with smaller baseline but 38% systolic curvature increase and more flexion points (Liao 2002, abstract only). LM is a bifurcation, described by angle, not tortuosity.
- Vessel-specific reference frame: raw chord-based indices must be per-vessel normalised (RCA arc/chord ≈2 vs ≈1.3 for LAD/LCx). A baseline-plus-residual decomposition (scale detrending or surface-proxy κ_g/κ_n split) is anatomically motivated but has no coronary precedent; that absence is itself a thesis contribution point if implemented.
- Vessel-specific scale: detrending or window scale should scale with vessel length and diameter (trunks 80 to 130 mm vs side branches 40 to 50 mm; smaller vessels more curved).
- Vessel-specific segment: LAD distal third (apical wrap) and proximal RCA (shepherd's crook, about 5%) are where anatomical variants will masquerade as tortuosity, so they should be reported separately or flagged.
- Covariates for any per-vessel analysis: sex and hypertension (consistent), age (inconsistent), heart size or BMI (inverse), dominance (affects LAD wrap and which vessel supplies PDA/PLA).
