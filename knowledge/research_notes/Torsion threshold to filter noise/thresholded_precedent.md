# Precedent for thresholded/binarized tortuosity and curvature descriptors, and whether thresholding has ever rescued a null shape-descriptor finding

## How was the 737-patient "significant coronary tortuosity" (SCT) threshold in the LCx study constructed?

### Takeaway
The 737-patient study (the Iranian angiography cohort reporting LCx-SCT segments had *lower* stenosis and Gensini scores than non-SCT LCx segments) did not invent its own threshold from its own outcome data. It adopted a pre-existing categorical bend-angle-and-count classification (moderate CT = "≥3 consecutive curvatures of 90-180°"; severe CT = "≥2 consecutive curvatures of ≥180°" in an artery ≥2 mm diameter at end-diastole; SCT = moderate or severe), citing this scheme back to earlier angiographic tortuosity literature, and the two cardiologists who applied it were blinded to each other's assessments (one localized tortuosity, the other scored stenosis), which prevents in-study outcome leakage into the tortuosity call itself. However, the ultimate origin of the specific degree cutoffs (90°, 180°, and the 2-vs-3-consecutive-bend counts) traces to a small number of single-center studies whose own cutoff-selection process is not fully documented in the accessible record, so "pre-existing clinical convention" is the right characterization only in the sense of "borrowed from prior papers," not "validated against an independent reference population's normal range."

### Cited Findings
- Study identity, definition, and citation structure: SCT = moderate or severe CT, where moderate CT is "≥3 consecutive curvatures of 90° to 180°" and severe CT is "≥2 consecutive curvatures of ≥180°," in major epicardial arteries ≥2 mm diameter, measured at end-diastole; the paper cites three prior sources for this classification (mapped in the fetch summary to Eleid et al. 2014, Jakob et al. 1996, and Groves et al. 2009, though this specific reference-number-to-citation mapping came from an automated summary of the PDF and was not independently checked against the numbered reference list) — [The significant coronary tortuosity and atherosclerotic CAD; what is the relation?, PMC6335987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6335987/)
- Sample and assessment: n=737 (from 1,043 screened), 57% male; two blinded expert cardiologists, one assessing tortuosity location/severity and the other assessing stenosis severity independently, which is the specific design feature that rules out the tortuosity assessor being influenced by that patient's known stenosis status — [PMC6335987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6335987/)
- LCx finding: SCT LCx segments had lower ≥50% stenosis prevalence than non-SCT LCx (17.7% vs 31.1%, P=0.001) and lower Gensini scores (2.1±3.4 vs 5.2±9.5, P=0.01), alongside slower flow (higher TIMI frame count, 17.1±4.4 vs 12.7±4.4, P<0.001); findings held after age and sex adjustment — [PMC6335987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6335987/); confirmed independently in this project's own prior notes at [research_notes/Choosing a coronary tortuosity descriptor/outcome_evidence.md](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Choosing%20a%20coronary%20tortuosity%20descriptor/outcome_evidence.md)
- Candidate origin 1, Groves et al. 2009 (West Virginia Medical Journal 105(4):14-17, retrospective angiography cohort, one hospital, 8-month period): defined **severe** coronary tortuosity (SCT) as "two consecutive 180 degree turns by visual estimation" in a major epicardial artery — a purely visual/qualitative convention, not a cutpoint statistically optimized against that study's own disease outcome — [Groves 2009 abstract summary via search; PubMed 19585899](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19585899/)
- Candidate origin 2, Eleid et al. 2014 (spontaneous coronary artery dissection, SCAD, angiography cohort): graded LAD tortuosity 0-3, with moderate = "≥3 consecutive curvatures of 90-180°" in the LAD and severe = "≥2 consecutive curvatures of ≥180°" in the LAD (mild = "≥3 consecutive curvatures of 45-90°" in the LAD or "≥3 consecutive curvatures of 90-180°" in a diagonal branch) — this is the exact wording later re-used, unmodified, by the 737-patient SCT study, strongly suggesting Eleid 2014 (or a source it itself drew on) is the proximate origin of the specific degree/count cutoffs, applied originally to a different disease (SCAD) and later re-purposed for atherosclerotic CAD — [Eleid 2014 definition as summarized via search of Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2014; PubMed 25138034](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25138034/); corroborated independently by this project's own full-text-verified note in [research_notes/Choosing a coronary tortuosity descriptor/outcome_evidence.md](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Choosing%20a%20coronary%20tortuosity%20descriptor/outcome_evidence.md), line: "SCAD (Eleid 2014, n=246 SCAD vs 313 controls...); severe tortuosity (>=2 consecutive curvatures >=180 deg) HR 3.29...for recurrent SCAD"
- Candidate origin 3, Jakob et al. 1996 (Cathet Cardiovasc Diagn 38:25-31, LAD/LCx tracing in 62 patients: 16 controls, 14 hypertension, 32 aortic regurgitation): this paper's own tortuosity metric is a **continuous** Tortuosity Index (percent ratio of shortest distance to total artery length, i.e. an inverse arc/chord ratio), not a bend-angle-and-count categorical score. This is a material mismatch: if Jakob 1996 is genuinely one of the three sources the 737-patient SCT paper cites for its categorical bend-count definition, it can only be cited for the general clinical premise (tortuosity relates to chronic pressure/volume overload), not for the specific 90°/180°/count-of-bends thresholds, since Jakob's own instrument was a different, continuous measure entirely — [Jakob 1996 methodology via search summary; PubMed 8722854](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8722854/)

### Inferences
- The most defensible reading of the evidence is: the specific SCT degree-and-count cutoffs used in the 737-patient LCx study were **not** derived from that study's own outcome data (the blinded, split-rater design and the fact that the identical wording appears verbatim in the earlier Eleid 2014 SCAD paper both point away from in-study leakage), but they also were **not** derived from a reference population's measured normal range in the sense a clinician-scientist might hope (no percentile analysis of a healthy cohort's bend angles was found underlying these numbers). They most plausibly originate as a **qualitative, visually-anchored clinical convention** (Groves 2009's "two consecutive 180° turns by visual estimation" is explicit about this), which Eleid 2014 then extended into a 4-tier ordinal score and applied to SCAD, and which the 737-patient study then re-applied unchanged to atherosclerotic CAD outcomes in a different population.
- This means the threshold's legitimacy rests on face validity and repeated re-use across studies (a form of de facto standardization by citation), not on an independent normal-range derivation or a leakage-free statistical optimization against outcomes. It is a "pre-existing convention," but a visually-anchored one whose original justification (why 90°, why 180°, why 2 vs. 3 consecutive bends) is not traceable to a published normative or psychometric derivation in the sources located.

### Gaps
- Full text of Groves 2009 (West Virginia Medical Journal, a low-visibility regional journal) could not be retrieved beyond search-summary level; whether the "two consecutive 180° turns" cutoff was chosen a priori or tuned against that paper's own CAD association was not confirmed either way from what is accessible.
- Full text of Eleid 2014 was not retrieved directly in this session (paywalled at 403); the exact wording of its four-tier grading and any stated justification for the specific angle cutoffs come from search-summary level only and should be verified with a full-text read (via the paper-review skill) before being cited as load-bearing in the thesis.
- The 737-patient paper's own reference list (to confirm which numbered references 6, 9, 10 actually are) was not independently opened; the Eleid/Jakob/Groves attribution rests on one automated fetch summary and could be mis-mapped.
- No source was found documenting a formal reliability study (inter-rater kappa) for the specific 90-180-vs-≥180-degree distinction as used in the 737-patient study itself, separate from the general angiographic tortuosity kappa literature already in this project's notes (~0.5-0.9 range depending on study).

## What other coronary, retinal, or general vascular tortuosity studies define "tortuous" via an explicit threshold, and how were those thresholds set?

### Takeaway
Explicit-threshold definitions are the norm, not the exception, in angiographic coronary tortuosity research, and they cluster into three provenance types: (1) visually-anchored bend-angle-and-count conventions descended from Groves 2009 / Eleid 2014 and used essentially unchanged across many later cohorts; (2) a same-study "expert consensus" threshold created for one paper with no independent validation (the Tortuosity Severity Index); and (3) statistically-derived percentile cutoffs computed directly from a large cohort's own distribution (the MGH registry's 90th-percentile "high tortuosity" cutoff). Retinal tortuosity's clinical threshold ("plus disease" in retinopathy of prematurity) is qualitative and photograph-anchored from its 1949 origin, not a rescued continuous score. Carotid tortuosity has ROC-derived, outcome-optimized cutoffs of exactly the kind general biostatistics guidance warns against.

### Cited Findings
- **Bend-angle-and-count family (coronary, visually anchored, reused across cohorts):**
  - Li et al. 2011 (n=1,010 angiography, chest pain): CT = "≥3 bends (≥45° change of direction)" in the main trunk, assessed in both systole and diastole; prevalence 39.1% — [Li 2011, PMC3164184](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/), verified full-text in this project's prior note
  - The 1280-patient CCTA cohort: CT = "at least three consecutive curvatures of more than 45°"; prevalence 35% — [PMC8867736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8867736/), verified full-text in this project's prior note
  - Groves 2009 and Eleid 2014, as detailed above, are the apparent proximate origins of the 90°/180°-based moderate/severe grading reused by the 737-patient study and by Tweet et al. 2024's SCAD follow-up cohort (n=116) — [PMC11605948](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11605948/), verified full-text in this project's prior note
- **Same-study, expert-consensus threshold with no independent prior validation:** the Tortuosity Severity Index (TSI) study (n=200 chest-pain patients with abnormal stress test and normal angiography): "significant TSI" = mild/moderate CT with more than 4 total curvatures, OR severe/extreme CT with any number of curvatures; "not significant" = mild/moderate CT with ≤4 curvatures. The 4-curvature cutoff is not justified in the paper and was confirmed, via direct fetch, to have been "developed at Assiut University Hospital by consensus among experts in interventional cardiology" — i.e., an ad hoc, single-center consensus threshold, not adapted from prior literature and not derived from that study's own outcome data (the two graders were blinded to stress-test results when scoring tortuosity), but also never independently replicated elsewhere. Significant TSI predicted ischemia with OR 10.2 (95% CI 4.6-22.5) and was "the only significant predictor" in the multivariable model — [Validity of tortuosity severity index in chest pain patients, PMC6303525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6303525/), confirmed by direct fetch in this session and cross-checked against this project's prior note at [research_notes/Centerline tortuosity normal ranges/normality_outcome_readiness.md](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Centerline%20tortuosity%20normal%20ranges/normality_outcome_readiness.md)
- **Cohort-distribution percentile threshold (data-driven, not outcome-derived):** the MGH angiography registry (n=22,334 patients, 38,691 RCA angiograms) computed a continuous turning-angle-sum score and separately reported a 90th-percentile cutoff for "high tortuosity" (0.152 overall, 0.151-0.153 across race strata, 0.158 women vs 0.148 men), with extreme values defined as >99th percentile (>0.199); the paper's primary analyses, however, used the continuous score (OR per SD for CAD 1.05, for severe CAD 1.09), and a local turning-angle profile outperformed the single global score (AUC 0.67 vs 0.60) — [Tello Ayala 2026 / A Machine Learning Driven Approach to Quantifying Coronary Artery Tortuosity, PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/), verified full-text in this project's prior note. This is a threshold set from the study's own exposure distribution (a percentile cut), not from its own outcome data, and it was used descriptively alongside, not instead of, the continuous score.
- **A 2020 review explicitly flags the field's lack of a settled definition:** "Significant coronary tortuosity (SCT) is a term of confliction and the proper definition is not globally accepted" — [search-summary characterization; consistent with the multiplicity of competing definitions catalogued above and in this project's prior notes]
- **Carotid tortuosity, ROC-optimized, outcome-derived cutoffs (the leakage-risk pattern):**
  - Extracranial carotid artery (ECA) tortuosity: "optimal cutoff of ECA tortuosity is 12.5" to predict unfavorable outcome in anterior-circulation acute ischemic stroke, derived via ROC analysis in that same outcome cohort — [search-summary of Association between Extracranial Carotid Artery Tortuosity and Clinical Outcomes, PubMed 31786044](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31786044/)
  - Carotid Tortuosity Index (CTI) ≥1.2 as a "pathological" cutoff — [search-summary, Determinants of the Carotid Tortuosity Index](https://www.scipublications.com/journal/index.php/WJCMR/article/view/6216)
  - Total absolute curvature (TAC) ≥16.5 in Loeys-Dietz syndrome patients under 60, an "optimal threshold for predicting neurovascular events" also derived via ROC in the same outcome cohort — [search-summary of Three-Dimensional Curvature of the Cervical Carotid Artery Predicts Long-Term Neurovascular Risk, PubMed 39925271](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39925271)
  - These ROC-optimal-cutpoint designs are exactly the pattern that the general dichotomization literature (see next section) identifies as biased and prone to overstating an association, because the cutpoint is chosen from the same data used to test it.
- **Retinal tortuosity, qualitative/photograph-anchored threshold from the outset (not a rescued continuous score):** "plus disease" in retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is defined categorically as "dilation and tortuosity of retinal vessels" at the posterior pole, judged against reference standard photographs since the original International Classification of ROP; "pre-plus disease" is explicitly defined as "abnormal vascular dilation and/or tortuosity insufficient for plus disease," i.e. the field already frames this as a graded/thresholded continuum by design, not as an upgrade from a previously-tested continuous score. Tortuosity in this context (vessel length / straight-line distance) is documented to be higher peripherally than centrally in images both with and without plus disease. Vessel tortuosity's association with ROP was noted qualitatively as early as 1949 — [Classification of retinopathy of prematurity: from then till now, PMC9112211](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9112211/); [Plus disease diagnosis in ROP: vascular tortuosity as a function of distance from optic disk, PubMed 23538582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23538582/)

### Inferences
- There is no single accepted "significant coronary tortuosity" definition; the field has at least three live threshold families (Groves/Eleid bend-count, Li 2011's simpler ≥3-bends-of-≥45°, and the TSI's severity-plus-count composite), and they are not shown to be equivalent or mutually convertible. A thesis decision to add "a built-in minimum magnitude threshold" for torsion would be adding a fourth, novel convention to an already-fragmented field, not adopting an established standard.
- Every coronary threshold with a traceable origin was set either by visual/qualitative convention (Groves, Eleid) or by single-center expert consensus (TSI), not by a normal-range derivation from a healthy reference population. The one genuinely data-driven (percentile-based) threshold found (MGH registry) was descriptive, alongside a continuous score used for the actual outcome modeling, not a replacement for it.
- The carotid literature shows the clearest examples of outcome-optimized ("optimal cutoff") cutpoints, which is the specific leakage pattern the general dichotomization literature (below) singles out as biased; none of the coronary bend-count definitions reviewed here appear to have been constructed this way, but the carotid ROC-cutoff studies were.

### Gaps
- No source was found giving a formal derivation, sensitivity analysis, or validation study for why 90°, 180°, or the specific 2-vs-3 consecutive-bend counts were chosen over any nearby alternative, in either Groves 2009 or Eleid 2014.
- Whether "plus disease" thresholds in ROP were ever preceded by a documented null/noisy continuous vascular tortuosity score that the categorical definition then "rescued" was not found; the categorical framing appears to predate any continuous quantification attempt in this vascular bed (continuous ROP tortuosity indices such as ROPtool and i-ROP came later, as computational aids to the pre-existing qualitative diagnosis, not as a fix for a failed continuous predictor).
- Full texts of the ROC-cutoff carotid studies were not retrieved; whether those specific cutoffs were later independently replicated in a second cohort (as opposed to reported once) was not established.

## Is there any documented case where a continuous shape metric showed weak/null/noisy performance and a thresholded/binarized version of the SAME metric later showed a real, replicated predictive association?

### Takeaway
No such case was found, in coronary tortuosity, other vascular tortuosity/curvature literature, or general biomarker literature located in this search. Every threshold-based tortuosity/curvature definition found in this research was constructed as a threshold from the start (a categorical clinical convention, or a same-study consensus/ROC cutpoint), not as a rescue of a metric previously shown to be null when measured continuously. Where the literature actually compares a continuous version of a metric against a dichotomized version of the same underlying quantity, the documented direction of the effect runs the opposite way: dichotomizing obscured or destroyed a real, non-linear association that the continuous score had detected, rather than revealing one the continuous score had missed.

### Cited Findings
- Direct counter-example located (obscuring, not rescuing): in a biomarker evaluation methods discussion, when a non-linear (quadratic) relationship was found between a continuous biomarker (neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, NLR) and risk of death, "this association dissipated when NLR was dichotomized at its sample median" — i.e. dichotomizing a real, already-detected non-linear signal made it disappear, not appear — [Statistical Considerations in the Evaluation of Continuous Biomarkers, Journal of Nuclear Medicine 62(5):605, 2021](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/62/5/605)
- Within the tortuosity literature itself, the only place a same-study comparison of a graded/thresholded score against continuous alternatives appears is the LCx ischemia study, where a continuous tortuosity index (TI, arc/chord) and a categorical angle-based method were compared head-to-head and the *continuous* TI was judged more reliable, not the categorical one: "TI highest in lateral ischemia for LCx tortuosity...RCA no association; angle method only LCx-lateral (OR 4.9); authors judged TI more reliable" — [Zebić Mihić 2023, PMC10795752](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/), verified full-text in this project's prior note. This is the reverse of the hypothesized rescue: here a continuous metric outperformed a categorical one.
- Kashyap et al. 2022 (n=127 CTCA, left-main bifurcation, no CAD) found the classical (continuous) tortuosity index had *no* significant relationship with low wall shear stress at the LMCA (p=0.86), while a different continuous metric (average absolute curvature) did (R²=0.196-0.232, p<0.001); the paper's fix for the null tortuosity-index result was to swap in a better-behaved *continuous* metric, not to threshold the same metric — [Kashyap 2022, PMC8764056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/), verified full-text in this project's prior notes
- No source was found reporting that a coronary or other vascular torsion, tortuosity, or curvature metric was first published as a continuous score with null/noisy outcome results, and later re-published in thresholded/binarized form by the same or a different group with a positive, replicated result on independent data.

### Inferences
- The absence itself is the finding this key question asked for: within the literature accessible here, "thresholding rescues a null continuous shape descriptor" is not a documented phenomenon in coronary or general vascular geometry. Every coronary threshold found was thresholded by design from its first publication (Groves 2009, Eleid 2014, the TSI consensus panel), and the one place a continuous-vs-categorical comparison was run head-to-head on the same clinical question, the continuous version won.
- Applying a "built-in minimum magnitude" threshold to torsion in the thesis's own CAD model would therefore not be reproducing a known successful precedent from this literature; it would be attempting a novel maneuver (threshold-as-noise-filter for a specific, previously-excluded shape descriptor) with no supporting case in the tortuosity/curvature literature and one directly analogous counter-example (NLR) showing the maneuver can just as easily erase a real signal as create a spurious one.
- Given that torsion's specific documented problem is *estimator instability* (systematic, pipeline-dependent bias from third-derivative estimation, not simply zero-mean measurement noise around a true value — see this project's own note at `research_notes/Scalar tortuosity metrics for CAD models/scalar_torsion.md`), a magnitude threshold is a particularly weak fix: thresholding removes small values indiscriminately, but an estimator with a pipeline-dependent systematic bias does not necessarily produce small erroneous values near zero; it can produce a shifted distribution where the "noise" and the "signal" occupy the same magnitude range, so a floor would not cleanly separate them the way it would for exchangeable/symmetric measurement noise.

### Gaps
- Search coverage here is not exhaustive; a true rescue precedent could exist in a domain further from vascular geometry (e.g. some genomics or imaging-biomarker literature) that was not surfaced by the search terms used. Nothing found supports it, but "no reliable evidence located" should not be read as "definitively does not exist anywhere in all of biomedical literature."
- No paper explicitly testing "torsion, thresholded, vs. torsion, continuous" against any outcome (coronary or otherwise) was found in either direction; this specific comparison appears never to have been attempted for torsion specifically, so there is no evidence a threshold would help *or* hurt torsion in particular, only the general pattern (above) for other metrics.

## Does biostatistics/biomarker literature show dichotomizing a continuous predictor to be more often a genuine improvement or more often a source of lost power and researcher-degree-of-freedom risk?

### Takeaway
The general biostatistics literature is unambiguous and one-sided: dichotomizing a continuous predictor is treated as a well-documented net loss (of statistical power and precision) with a serious additional bias risk when the cutpoint is chosen from the same data used to test the association ("optimal cutpoint" methods). No general methodological source found argues dichotomization is typically the more sound choice; the standard advice is to keep predictors continuous (or use a flexible non-linear transformation) and only dichotomize when there is a strong, independently justified clinical or mechanistic reason.

### Cited Findings
- Royston, Altman, and Sauerbrei, "Dichotomizing continuous predictors in multiple regression: a bad idea," Statistics in Medicine 25:127-141, 2006: dichotomization is "equivalent to discarding approximately one-third of the data" in information terms and increases Type II error risk; "the use of a data-derived 'optimal' cutpoint leads to serious bias"; the paper's overall conclusion is that "dichotomization of continuous data is unnecessary for statistical analysis and in particular should not be applied to explanatory variables in regression models," illustrated with a randomized primary biliary cirrhosis trial as a case study — [Royston, Altman, Sauerbrei 2006, PubMed 16217841](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16217841/); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sim.2331)
- "Statistical Considerations in the Evaluation of Continuous Biomarkers," Journal of Nuclear Medicine 62(5):605, 2021: "Categorizing a continuous biomarker causes considerable loss of valuable information, which may in turn increase the chance of missing a real association"; dichotomization "assumes that a discontinuity in risk occurs at some biomarker value" and "assumes the relationship is flat within [each] interval," which "clearly does not adequately reflect the true...relationship between the biomarker and the outcome" when that relationship is actually continuous or non-linear — [JNM 2021](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/62/5/605)
- The same source's own worked example (NLR, quadratic vs. median-split dichotomization) shows the concrete mechanism: a real non-linear signal detected continuously "dissipated" once dichotomized — [JNM 2021](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/62/5/605)

### Inferences
- Applied to this thesis's specific decision: introducing a "built-in minimum magnitude" threshold for torsion converts a continuous, already-noisy measurement into a binary one, which the literature above predicts will *cost* statistical power on top of torsion's pre-existing estimator instability, not recover signal, unless the threshold is set from a source fully external to the CAD outcome being modeled (e.g., a pre-registered noise-floor derived from a synthetic/phantom or test-retest study of the torsion estimator itself, not from where torsion happens to correlate best with CAD status in the thesis's own data).
- If a threshold is used at all, the literature's core prescription is: set it *before* looking at outcome data, from a source independent of the outcome (e.g., an estimator noise-floor established by the project's own reliability/jitter analysis, analogous to the ICC-based length-floor and jitter gates this project already uses for tortuosity in `research_notes/Centerline tortuosity normal ranges/normality_outcome_readiness.md`), and to report it as a pre-registered sensitivity analysis alongside the continuous version, not as a replacement chosen because it "worked."
- Combined with the absence of any documented rescue precedent (previous section) and the specific double failure of the torsion mechanism (Shen et al. 2025's Bonferroni-Holm-corrected null torsion-helicity correlation, and the mechanistic pathway to WSS being bidirectional/"double-edged" per this project's own existing note at `research_notes/Scalar tortuosity metrics for CAD models/scalar_torsion.md`), the weight of evidence assembled here does not support that a magnitude threshold is likely to convert torsion into a useful CAD-model covariate; it is more consistent with the general biostatistical expectation that such a threshold would either do nothing, cost power, or manufacture an appearance of signal that reflects the specific cutpoint chosen rather than a real biological discontinuity.

### Gaps
- No coronary- or vascular-geometry-specific dichotomization methods paper (as opposed to general biomarker/regression methodology) was found; the general guidance above is not tortuosity- or torsion-specific and its transfer to a 3D centerline-derived scalar is an extrapolation, not a direct citation match.
- No formal power-loss calculation specific to a torsion-magnitude threshold (as opposed to the general "~1/3 of information" figure from Royston et al. for an arbitrary continuous-to-binary split) was performed or found; if this decision proceeds, a project-specific simulation (using the project's own jitter/ICC machinery already built for tortuosity) would be needed to quantify the actual power cost for torsion's particular noise profile.
