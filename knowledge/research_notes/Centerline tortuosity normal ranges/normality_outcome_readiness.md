## 1. Determinants of coronary tortuosity in people without CAD, their effect sizes, and which can be proxied from centerlines

### Takeaway
The consistent determinants are female sex, hypertension and small heart size (inverse with LV mass, LV length and BMI); age is positive in most studies but not all after adjustment. Almost all effect sizes come from invasive angiography with categorical bend-count definitions; on CTA the only continuous-metric determinant data are one LAD arc/chord study (Sharfo 2024, p-values only) and one 1280-patient categorical CCTA study. Of the determinants, only heart size and dominance can be proxied from centerlines; age, sex and hypertension cannot, and ImageCAS-X does not supply them per scan.

### Cited Findings
Sex, age, hypertension (summary of prior notes plus new sources)
- Categorical CCTA, 1280 patients: "at least three consecutive curvatures of more than 45°", prevalence 35%; age OR 1.02 per year, female OR 1.68, hypertension OR 1.35, overweight OR 0.76, obese OR 0.46 [FT, via prior note] ([PMC8867736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8867736/); summarised in [vessel_anatomy.md](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests/vessel_anatomy.md)).
- Angiography, 1010 chest-pain patients, ≥3 bends of ≥45°: female OR 2.603, hypertension OR 1.533, no age difference [FT, prior note] ([Li 2011, PMC3164184](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/)).
- Groves 2009 (1221 angiograms, visual severe tortuosity): female higher (p = 0.039); hypertension and age ≥65 not predictors [ABS] ([PubMed 19585899](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19585899/)). Chiha 2017 (870 angiograms): severe tortuosity 45.2% of women vs 19.7% of men; age associated (p < 0.001) [ABS] ([PubMed 28616559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28616559/)).
- Largest continuous study, RCA turning-angle score on 38,691 angiograms (22,334 patients): female β 0.17 SD, hypertension β 0.06 SD, diabetes β −0.11 SD, age not significant after adjustment [FT, prior note] ([Tello Ayala 2026, JACC Adv, PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/); the same paper is the PDF in the repo root).
- CCTA continuous, LAD arc/chord, 194 CATCH participants: associated with hypertension (p < 0.001), female sex (p = 0.01), age (p = 0.045) after adjustment; effect sizes not obtained [ABS] ([Sharfo 2024, PubMed 39210716](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39210716/)).
- Tortuosity severity index study (200 chest-pain patients, abnormal stress test, normal angiography; ≥3 curvatures >45° in systole and diastole, vessels >2.5 mm): tortuous vs not, hypertension 76.3% vs 37.4% (p < 0.001), female 77.4% vs 49.5% (p < 0.001), age 55.9 ± 9 vs 52.9 ± 10.2 y (p = 0.029), severe LVH 43% vs 2.8% (p < 0.001) [FT, fetch summary] ([PMC6303525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6303525/)).

Heart size, LV mass and body size (new)
- Oehler 2017 (75 angiography plus echo patients; ≥3 turns >45° in a major artery): tortuous vs non-tortuous, LV mass index 77.6 ± 18.5 vs 121.0 ± 41.3 g/m² (p < 0.01), LV diastolic length 8.3 ± 1.0 vs 9.2 ± 1.1 cm (p < 0.01), BMI 26.1 ± 4.1 vs 32.1 ± 7.2 kg/m² (p < 0.05); age, sex and hypertension not different in this sample; greater normalised mitral annular plane systolic excursion (lateral MAPSE: 13-fold odds per 0.1 of normalised MAPSE). Authors frame tortuosity as an adaptation to systolic longitudinal shortening, not to LV hypertrophy [FT, fetch summary] ([Oehler 2017, J Am Soc Echocardiogr, PMC5912164](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5912164/)).
- Hutchins 1977 autopsy (145 hearts with normal coronaries): tortuosity rises with age and falls with heart weight, with about equal weight, and rises with arterial calibre [ABS, prior note] ([PubMed 141878](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/141878/)).
- Zegers 2007 (3-case report and review): tortuosity "may be less clear in large hearts"; circumflex "most often affected, especially when associated with hypertension"; aetiology attributed to age-dependent or pathological change in elastic material [FT] ([Zegers 2007, Neth Heart J, PMC1877966](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1877966/)).
- Turgut 2007 (104 subjects): tortuosity associated with impaired LV relaxation (lower E, higher A, lower E/A, longer DT and IVRT) [ABS via search snippet; Springer page paywalled] ([Int J Cardiovasc Imaging 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10554-006-9186-4)).
- Kahe review: tortuosity "commonly associated with aging, hypertension, atherosclerosis"; elastin degradation proposed as mechanism; called "a benign entity that does not require specific treatment" [ABS] ([Kahe 2020, Coron Artery Dis, PubMed 31211725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31211725/)).
- Conflict to record: the LVH association (43% vs 2.8% in PMC6303525; Jakob 1996 pressure/volume overload per search snippet, not retrieved) points the opposite way to Oehler 2017 (lower LV mass index) and Hutchins 1977 (smaller heart). A plausible reconciliation (inference, not sourced) is that hypertension raises tortuosity through vessel wall remodelling while LV mass or heart size relative to vessel length lowers it, so hypertension and heart size must both be in a model and not used as proxies for each other.

Dominance
- No study relating per-vessel tortuosity to dominance was found beyond the wrap-around LAD prevalence (47% in right vs 87% in left dominance) [ABS, prior note] ([PMC12056774](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12056774/)). The ImageCAS-X data show that RCA whole-vessel arc/chord is a dominance variable: median TI 0.29 in left-dominant vs 0.81 in right-dominant (8 left, 3 co-dominant cases, P150), while κ_a at 5 mm does not differ (0.055 vs 0.046, p = 0.31) [REPO, scratch-only numbers, not yet reproducible] ([Vessel specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)).

What ImageCAS-X supplies for conditioning
- ImageCAS-X cohort: 1000 patients from Guangdong Provincial People's Hospital, 2012 to 2018, with "a documented medical history of ischaemic stroke, transient ischaemic attack, or peripheral artery disease"; 586 male and 414 female, mean ages 57.7 and 60.0 y; Siemens 128-slice dual-source; "either the 30–40% or 60–70% cardiac phase was selected based on optimal coronary image quality" [FT, repo PDF] ([ImageCAS-X paper, repo root](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/ImageCAS-X_%20a%20dataset%20and%20benchmark%20for%20coronary%20artery%20segmentation%20and%20centerline%20extraction%20in%20coronary%20CT%20angiography.pdf)).
- Of the 800 retained scans: 729 (91.1%) right, 41 (5.1%) left, 30 (3.8%) co-dominant; Image Quality 55 poor, 140 adequate, 168 good, 437 excellent; 388 (48.5%) labelled diseased (any calcified, non-calcified or mixed plaque) [FT, same PDF].
- `Descriptors.xlsx` carries only `Scan ID`, `Image Quality`, `Dominance`, `Disease` [REPO, column names checked 2026-09-26]. Age, sex, hypertension, BMI, LV mass and cardiac phase are therefore not available per scan.
- Cardiac phase matters: in CCTA, vessel curvature was 11.1% higher at end-systole than at diastole and inflection points were more frequent (p < 0.001) (n = 71) [FT, prior note] ([van Zandwijk 2019, PMC7165136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)); free-wall vessels increased mean curvature by 38% in systole while groove vessels did not change (21 arteries) [ABS] ([Liao 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ccd.10106)).

### Inferences
- Covariates to condition on, in order of evidence: sex, hypertension, heart size (or its inverse proxy BMI), age, dominance (RCA and LCx only, and mainly for arc/chord), cardiac phase, image quality.
- Centerline-computable proxies:
  - Dominance: computable from the tree (which side supplies the PDA/PLA, or RCA reaching the crux); on ImageCAS-X it is also given in Descriptors.
  - Heart size: candidate proxies are total tree length, the convex-hull volume or bounding-box diagonal of all centerline points, LAD ostium-to-apex chord, and the LM-to-crux distance. None has been validated against LV mass or LV volume in any source found; the LAD chord is the closest analogue of Oehler's LV long-axis length.
  - Vessel length and extent: computable, but they carry both biology (dominance, heart size) and segmentation reach (truncation), so they enter as covariates, never as normalisers (Kronmal 1993, cited in the Vessel specific report).
  - Age, sex, hypertension, BMI, phase: not computable from centerlines. On ImageCAS-X they are simply unavailable, so any ImageCAS-X "normal range" is marginal over an unknown mix of these.
- The cardiac phase mix in ImageCAS-X (systolic 30 to 40% or diastolic 60 to 70%, chosen per scan and not recorded in Descriptors) is an unmeasured source of variance of order 10% in curvature. It inflates between-scan spread in any ImageCAS-X distribution and must be named as a limitation. Whether the NIfTI or original DICOM headers record the phase was not checked.

### Gaps
- No CTA study reporting continuous per-vessel tortuosity reference values (mean, SD, percentiles by age and sex) in a population without CAD was found. Kashyap 2022 (below) gives means for 127 no-CAD left main bifurcations, but only for the short LM bifurcation region, not whole LAD, LCx or RCA; its Table 2 values were not extracted because the PDF layout garbled them.
- No effect size per year of age or per unit of LV mass for a continuous CTA metric exists in the retrieved literature; the Sharfo 2024 coefficients were not obtained.
- Jakob 1996 (tortuosity in pressure and volume overload) was not retrieved; the LVH direction therefore rests on one angiographic study.
- No validation of any centerline-derived heart-size proxy against CT LV mass or volume was found. This could be tested on CGPS, where the image and potentially LV segmentation exist, but not on ImageCAS-X from centerlines alone.

## 2. Outcome associations, effect sizes, measure definitions, and whether global or local measures carry the signal

### Takeaway
Outcome evidence is dominated by categorical angiographic bend counts and clusters in three places: SCAD (large effect), ischemia with non-obstructive coronaries (moderate to large, small single-centre studies, one null), and obstructive CAD (small and sign-inconsistent). MACE evidence is one null angiographic cohort and one abstract-level CCTA model. The only head-to-head comparisons of measure definitions, Kashyap 2022 against low wall shear stress in 127 no-CAD CTCA and Tello Ayala 2026 against CAD, both favour local curvature-type measures over whole-vessel arc/chord, but with small explained variance.

### Cited Findings
Already in prior notes (effect sizes only restated)
- SCAD: tortuosity 78% vs 17% in controls; severe tortuosity HR 3.29 (95% CI 0.99 to 8.29) for recurrence; 80% of recurrences in tortuous segments [ABS] ([Eleid 2014, PubMed 25138034](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25138034/)). Tweet 2024: 82.8% tortuous among 116 SCAD patients; 0.89 score units per decade of age [FT] ([PMC11605948](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11605948/)).
- Obstructive CAD: OR 0.755 (Li 2011); lower Gensini in significant tortuosity (Iranian n = 737); OR 1.05 per SD for CAD and 1.09 for severe CAD with the continuous RCA turning-angle score; local turning-angle profile AUC 0.67 vs global score 0.60 [FT] ([PMC3164184](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/); [PMC6335987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6335987/); [PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)).
- MACE: no difference over 2 to 4 y in 1010 angiography patients (Li 2011); CArTI CCTA model HR 2.69 high vs low risk in 992 patients, multi-feature, definitions not retrieved [ABS] ([Circulation 2025 abstract](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4365988)).
- Ischemia territory: arc/chord tortuosity index matched lateral ischemia for LCx and anterior for LAD (p < 0.001), not RCA; angle method only for LCx (OR 4.9) [FT] ([Zebić Mihić 2023, PMC10795752](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/)).

New
- Tortuosity severity index (200 patients, abnormal stress test, normal angiography): tortuosity in 68.1% with ischemic changes vs 18.3% without; tortuosity OR 6.2 (95% CI 2.5 to 15.3) and significant TSI OR 10.2 (4.6 to 22.5) for ischemia, "the only significant predictor" in the multivariable model [FT, fetch summary] ([PMC6303525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6303525/)). Note the heavy hypertension and LVH imbalance between groups reported above, which makes residual confounding likely.
- ANOCA vasomotor study (228 patients, 86% female, Eleid LAD grading; 32% none, 50% mild, 18% moderate, 0 severe): no association with epicardial spasm (p = 0.98), microvascular spasm, CFR < 2.0 (17%, 16%, 16%; p = 0.99), IMR ≥ 25 (23%, 35%, 32%; p = 0.25) or overall microvascular dysfunction (p = 0.70) [FT, fetch summary] ([PMC8792852](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8792852/)).
- CFR/IMR in tortuous arteries without atherosclerosis: n = 8, no control group; FFR 0.98 ± 0.007, CFR 1.5 ± 0.3, IMR 26.7 ± 2.3 [FT, fetch summary] ([PMC7509742](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7509742/)). FFR is essentially normal, so tortuosity alone does not produce an epicardial pressure drop detectable by FFR in this series.
- Patient-specific CFD (2 tortuous LADs from CT, each against a straightened model): rest perfusion pressure fell 3.0 to 4.0 mmHg; at maximal dilation blood supply fell 12.9% to 14.9% and perfusion pressure 8.1 to 11.8 mmHg; "the bend section effect played a more important role in determining the flow resistance" than added length [FT, fetch summary] ([Li 2013, PLoS One, PMC via journals.plos.org](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0064564)).
- Zegers 2007 hypothesis: tortuosity reduces perfusion pressure distal to the tortuous segment, and energy loss in a bend is set by the bend radius relative to diameter (at R/D = 2, separation loss equals friction loss, so total loss is twice a straight tube) [FT] ([PMC1877966](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1877966/)).
- Kashyap 2022 (127 CTCA patients with symptoms, no obstruction, zero calcium score; LM bifurcation with LAD and LCx branches; transient CFD): adjusted R² against the fraction of wall with TAWSS < 0.4 Pa was, for the tortuosity index, −0.011 (LM), 0.026 (LAD), 0.007 (LCx), −0.018 (whole bifurcation, p = 0.865); for average absolute curvature 0.196, 0.232, 0.061 (p = 0.013) and 0.112 (p = 0.001); RMS curvature 0.113, 0.164, 0.010, 0.093. Average absolute curvature was also closest to normally distributed (skew 1.02 whole bifurcation vs 2.71 for the tortuosity index; kurtosis 1.49 vs 9.46). Authors recommend average absolute curvature; they note total curvature is "not scale-invariant and would be affected by the length of the vessels", and that results may not apply beyond the LM region [FT, tables read from the PDF] ([Kashyap 2022, Sci Rep 12:865](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-04796-w); [open PDF](https://eprints.victorchang.edu.au/1198/1/1198-Accuracy-of-vascular-tortuosity-measures-using-computational-modellingDownload-PDFView-Complete-IssueScientific-Reports.pdf)).
- PCI outcomes (6951 patients pooled from 6 stent trials; 729, 10.5%, with moderate or severe tortuosity): 30-day target vessel failure 3.8% vs 2.4% (HR 1.64, 95% CI 1.09 to 2.46), driven by target-vessel MI [ABS via search snippet] ([JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2021](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcin.2020.12.027)). This is a procedural outcome, irrelevant to a population cohort, but it shows tortuosity is clinically meaningful where it matters mechanically.
- Wall shear stress geometry in 39 ASOCA trees: diameter was the only geometric feature whose correlation with WSS survived multiple-comparison correction [REPO citation] ([Shen 2025, arXiv 2502.06161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)), which tempers Kashyap's curvature result.

### Inferences
- Global vs local: the two definition comparisons found (Kashyap: curvature R² 0.11 to 0.23 vs arc/chord about 0; Tello Ayala: local profile AUC 0.67 vs global 0.60) and the CFD mechanism (bend radius drives loss, not length) all favour a local bending measure, averaged over the vessel, over whole-vessel arc/chord. The clinical literature that does use arc/chord (Zebić Mihić, Sharfo) worked on 2D angiograms or LAD only and did not compare. This is consistent with the repo's choice of κ_a as primary and TI as a secondary course variable.
- Plausible outcome channels for a general-population cohort such as CGPS: (a) MACE, where the evidence is null or abstract-level and the per-SD effect is likely small; (b) new plaque at follow-up (objective 10), where the evidence is mechanistic (low WSS on the inner and outer wall of curves) plus sign-inconsistent cross-sectional CAD associations; (c) extreme-tail flags (top 5%) as markers of a systemic arteriopathy (SCAD, FMD), which is the only setting with large effects but is rare, and SCAD itself will have very few events. INOCA and FFR endpoints will not exist in CGPS.
- Because the sign of the CAD association flips with definition and cohort, a pre-registered primary hypothesis should not claim a direction for plaque; a two-sided test with the direction reported is the defensible design.
- Measure-level requirement derived from the above: the measure must be (i) a per-vessel local-bending average (so it could carry a WSS-mediated signal), (ii) length-insensitive (so extent and truncation do not masquerade as tortuosity), (iii) near-normally distributed or log-transformable (for per-SD effects and z-score outlier flags), and (iv) defined at a declared scale. κ_a at 5 mm meets all four on the ImageCAS-X evidence; whole-vessel TI fails (ii) and (iii).

### Gaps
- No CCTA study with a continuous per-vessel tortuosity measure and hard outcomes (MACE, MI, death) with a reported per-SD effect, adjustment set and CI was found; CArTI is abstract only.
- No study relating tortuosity at baseline to new plaque at follow-up in serial CCTA was found.
- No CCTA study relating tortuosity to FFR or CT-FFR with a continuous metric was found (also a gap in the prior note).
- 5-year results of the PCI pooled analysis and the Esenboga 2020 angiographic perfusion study were not retrieved.
- Li 2012 (reversible SPECT perfusion defects without CAD, Chin Med J) was identified but its numbers were not retrieved ([PubMed 23044329](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23044329/)).

## 3. Is ImageCAS-X usable for "normal"? What must be stated about Herlev-Østerbro / CGPS, and what a centerline-only pipeline needs there

### Takeaway
ImageCAS-X cannot define normal. It is a hospital cohort with prior stroke, TIA or PAD, 48.5% with plaque, a mixed cardiac phase, no age, sex or risk factor columns, and 91% right dominance. It is usable for method development: reliability, scale choice, length-floor and truncation behaviour, per-vessel distribution shape, dominance and image-quality sensitivity, and prototyping outlier methods. Normal ranges belong to CGPS, and even there "normal" must be defined by exclusion or conditioning, because 46% of the asymptomatic CGPS CCTA participants have some CAD. CGPS needs vessel identification from an unlabelled tree before any per-artery value exists.

### Cited Findings
- ImageCAS-X population, phase, dominance, disease fraction and descriptor columns as listed in section 1 [FT, repo PDF; REPO].
- The repo's own thesis concepts document states ImageCAS-X "cannot license a normative description of healthy coronary topology" [REPO] ([Investigation priorities report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports.md)).
- CGPS CCTA: 9533 asymptomatic persons aged 40 or older without known ischaemic heart disease; 46% had some CAD, 10% obstructive, 10% extensive; extensive non-obstructive disease adjusted RR 2.70 for death or MI [ABS, prior note] ([Fuchs 2023, Ann Intern Med](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M22-3027)).
- CGPS used two different MDCT scanners, compared by calibrated calcium mass [snippet, prior note] ([Eur J Radiol](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0720048X16304272)).
- Automated coronary labelling from CCTA exists: a GCN ensemble on 104 patients from two hospitals labelled 10 AHA segment classes after automatic tree extraction [ABS] ([PMC11095121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/)); CPR-GCN conditions on image and geometry ([arXiv 2003.08560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08560)); an earlier atlas-free approach identified tree anatomy in CCTA ([PMC5677991](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5677991/)). Per-class accuracies were not extracted here; the prior longitudinal note recommends a coarse region scheme because side branches label poorly [REPO] ([longitudinal_design.md](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors/longitudinal_design.md)).
- ImageCAS-X centerlines are split into left (LM, LAD, LCx) and right (RCA) VTK files with per-point `segment_label` [REPO] (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`). The repo holds two LAD definitions (longest geodesic path in `tortuosity/vessels.py` vs chained same-named segments in `topology.graph`) [REPO] ([Investigation priorities report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports.md)).
- LM median length is about 10 mm on ImageCAS-X, too short for tortuosity; it is described by bifurcation angle instead [REPO] ([Vessel specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)).

### Inferences
- What ImageCAS-X numbers may be called: "per-vessel distributions in a clinically referred CCTA cohort (development sample)", never "normal ranges" or "reference values". Report them stratified by dominance and Image Quality only; do not stratify by Disease before the definition is locked.
- LM should be dropped from the tortuosity target and reported as NaN with reason. The target is LAD, LCx, RCA; the question's "LM, LAD, LCx, RCA" becomes three tortuosity vessels plus an LM angle.
- What the thesis must state about CGPS before it has data: (a) asymptomatic general population aged 40 or older, but not disease-free (46% any CAD), so "normal" is either the whole population (population reference distribution) or a plaque-free, risk-factor-free subgroup (healthy reference), and the choice must be pre-declared; (b) no delivered centerlines and no artery names, so every value depends on the CAS-Net segmentation, a centerline extractor and a labeller, each adding error that ImageCAS-X reference centerlines do not have; (c) scanner and protocol differ from ImageCAS-X (two MDCT scanners in CGPS, one Siemens dual-source in ImageCAS-X), and phase selection is unknown; (d) reproducibility cannot come from ten-year repeat pairs.
- Minimum centerline-only pipeline for CGPS: segmentation to binary lumen, skeleton to graph, left and right tree separation by ostium, main-path identification (LAD, LCx, RCA) and dominance call. A label-free fallback that needs no segment classifier: define LAD, LCx and RCA as the longest geodesic paths with rules (RCA from the right ostium; on the left, split the LM bifurcation by direction toward apex vs AV groove). The tree coordinates alone can support this because the apex and AV groove directions are recoverable from the tree's principal axes; this is an inference and needs validation against ImageCAS-X labels on training scans.
- The measure chosen now should be the one whose value least depends on where the extractor stops and on which side branch the labeller picks at a bifurcation. That again favours an average local measure (κ_a) with a length floor over whole-vessel arc/chord.

### Gaps
- CGPS access status, repeat-scan subset size, cardiac phase and reconstruction protocol, and available covariates (BMI, blood pressure, LV mass) are unknown from public sources.
- No published labeller has been validated on ImageCAS-X or on CAS-Net output, and no accuracy for main-vessel-only identification (LAD, LCx, RCA) was extracted.
- Whether the ImageCAS NIfTI or original DICOM headers carry the reconstructed phase was not checked.

## 4. A one-week work package and go/no-go criteria

### Takeaway
A week can deliver a thesis-ready "measurement and development distribution" section, not a normative one: committed code, a dated pre-registration, reliability numbers against pre-set gates, per-vessel distributions on a clean training confirmation set, and a decision on whether any harder method beats κ_a plus TI plus length. The decision rule should require a harder method to beat the simple ones on reliability and on unique information; interpretability breaks ties in favour of the simple ones.

### Cited Findings
- Reliability convention: ICC below 0.5 poor, 0.5 to 0.75 moderate, 0.75 to 0.9 good, above 0.9 excellent, judged on the 95% CI [FT via search summary] ([Koo & Li 2016, PMC4913118](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/)). IBSI grades feature reproducibility by the lower 95% bound of ICC(2,1) [REPO citation] ([IBSI 2020](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/119920642/The_Image_Biomarker_Standardization_Initiative_Standardized_Quantitative_Radiomics_for_High_Throughput_Image_based_Phenotyping.pdf)).
- Current state (scratch, not reproducible): κ_a at σ 1 mm, ℓ 5 mm has ICC(2,1) 0.97/0.95/0.94 (LAD/LCx/RCA) under 0.25 mm correlated jitter and 0.87/0.76/0.71 under 0.5 mm correlated over 1 mm; 0.95/0.88/0.93 after a 20 mm distal cut; TI 0.60/0.81/0.83 after the cut; TI rank-correlates 0.70 to 0.83 with length, κ_a 0.15 to 0.30; sliding TI(20 mm) is 0.94 to 0.97 rank-correlated with κ_a; Image Quality |ρ| ≤ 0.16 for all metrics [REPO] ([Vessel specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)).

## 5. Gaps that need more research rather than implementation

### Takeaway
Five questions cannot be settled by coding: what "normal" coronary tortuosity is in a general population as a function of age, sex and heart size; which cardiac phase and scanner effects move continuous CTA tortuosity; whether any continuous CTA tortuosity predicts MACE or new plaque after adjustment; how well main vessels can be identified in unlabelled trees; and the direction of the heart-size and LVH effects.

### Cited Findings
- No continuous CTA per-vessel reference values stratified by age and sex were found (search returned aortic and subclavian tortuosity reference studies instead, e.g. descriptive thoracic aorta TI 1.14 vs 1.05 at ≥65 vs <65 y) [ABS] ([PMC6478292](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6478292/)).
- Phase effect on CCTA curvature (11.1%) comes from one 71-patient study [FT, prior note] ([PMC7165136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)).
- Test-retest ICC of continuous CCTA curvature or tortuosity: not found in any prior note or this search.
- Heart size direction: inverse (Oehler 2017, Hutchins 1977) vs LVH positive (PMC6303525) as above.
- The prior notes flag Sharfo 2024 effect sizes, CArTI definitions and ICONIC tortuosity effect sizes as unobtained ([outcome_evidence.md](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Choosing%20a%20coronary%20tortuosity%20descriptor/outcome_evidence.md)).

### Inferences
- Research gaps (need literature or new data, not code):
  1. Normative continuous CTA tortuosity by age, sex, body and heart size in a general population. This is itself a thesis contribution that CGPS could provide, with the caveat of the 46% CAD prevalence.
  2. Phase and scanner sensitivity of κ_a; only CGPS or a dataset with multi-phase reconstructions can answer it.
  3. Whether heart-size proxies computed from the tree track LV mass or volume; needs CGPS images or a dataset with both.
  4. Outcome association of any continuous CTA measure with MACE or new plaque, adjusted; CArTI and ICONIC full texts should be read with `paper-review` before being cited as support.
  5. The sign of the plaque association (negative for bend counts in angiography cohorts, weakly positive for turning-angle sum in a registry). A population cohort with no selection on symptoms is the setting that could resolve it.
- Conflict inside the repo to resolve: `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md` criterion 8 says Groves found age and hypertension were not predictors "so no expected direction for them without a source", but hypertension is positive in Li 2011 (OR 1.53), the 1280-patient CCTA study (OR 1.35), Sharfo 2024, Tello Ayala 2026 and PMC6303525. The expected direction for hypertension is positive on the balance of evidence; age is mostly positive but not after adjustment in the largest study.

### Gaps
- Full texts not read in this session: Turgut 2007, Jakob 1996, Li 2012 (SPECT), Sharfo 2024, CArTI, ICONIC, the PCI pooled analysis (5-year results), and the GCN labelling paper's per-class accuracy. None of the new [ABS] items should carry a thesis claim before a `paper-review` pass.
- The Hsieh 1998 and Hutcheon 2010 references were cited from knowledge, not fetched in this session; verify the PubMed ID and BMJ URL before citing.
