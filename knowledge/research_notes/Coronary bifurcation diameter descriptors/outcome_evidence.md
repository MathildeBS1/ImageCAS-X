# Prognostic evidence for baseline vessel calibre at and around branch points (healthy or low-disease baseline)

Scope note: searches run 2026-09-28 (PubMed/Europe PMC abstracts, PMC full text, publisher pages). Evidence level is flagged per item: [FULL TEXT] = full text read; [ABSTRACT] = abstract only (via Europe PMC/PubMed); [SNIPPET] = only a search-engine summary or secondary page, treat as unverified. Several publisher pages (AHA, ACP, Springer) returned 403, so most coronary and retinal numbers below are abstract-level.

## Q1. Do baseline coronary bifurcation geometry or calibre descriptors predict incident plaque, plaque progression or events?

### Takeaway
No study was found in which lumen diameter, Murray/Finet ratio deviation or daughter ratios measured in a disease-free coronary tree predict hard events over 5 years or more. The closest calibre-type evidence is whole-tree lumen volume normalised to myocardial mass (V/M, CAVi): one small symptomatic cohort followed for 5.4 years, plus short-horizon or case-control work. Bifurcation-specific evidence covers association with plaque composition (cross-sectional IVUS), bifurcation plaque as a progression predictor in already-diseased patients (PARADIGM), and small serial CFD studies. For the thesis, CGPS itself (Fuchs/Kofoed 2023) is the key cohort, but its published prognostic analysis uses stenosis and extent, not diameter.

### Cited Findings
**Lumen volume / myocardial mass (whole-tree calibre surrogate)**
- Benetos et al. 2020, Int J Cardiovasc Imaging: 325 consecutive patients with *suspected* CAD (symptomatic referral, not disease-free), mean follow-up 5.4 ± 1.7 years, 36 MACE (11.1%; cardiac death, non-fatal MI or revascularisation). Coronary artery volume index (CAVi = coronary volume / myocardial mass) below 27.9 mm³/g: MACE 17.2% vs 4.5%. Univariable HR for low CAVi 3.79 (95% CI 1.66–8.65); adjusted for segment severity score HR 2.43 (1.02–5.75); adjusted for obstructive CAD HR 2.4 (1.002–5.75). Also stratified risk among patients *without* obstructive CAD (KM p = 0.049). [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 31894527](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31894527) / [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10554-019-01750-2)
  - This is the only study found with ≥5-year follow-up using a baseline coronary calibre measure; note the tiny sample, wide CIs, dichotomised cut-point derived in-sample, and MACE including revascularisation.
- Ding et al. 2025, Front Cardiovasc Med: single-centre retrospective, 168 ACS, 160 stable angina, 122 controls. V/M 21.7 ± 6.96 (ACS), 31.0 ± 9.90 (SAP), 43.3 ± 11.50 mm³/g (controls); AMI 17.8 ± 5.30 < UA 24.3 ± 6.70. AUC of V/M for ACS 0.78 (0.74–0.83); V/M + FFR-CT + %DS AUC 0.80 (0.76–0.85). CCTA performed 0.5–6 months (median 3.3 months) before the event. [FULL TEXT, via fetch summary] — [PMC11865212](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11865212)
  - Case-control design with a months-long horizon; lumen volume at this point is almost certainly lowered by the culprit disease itself, so it is not a healthy-baseline prognostic study. (The fetch summary claimed patients were "disease-free at CCTA"; this is inconsistent with the SAP arm and should be treated as unreliable.)
- ADVANCE registry (Fairbairn et al.), 3110 symptomatic patients with ≥30% stenosis: mean lumen volume 3003 mm³, LV mass 122 g, mean V/M 25.2 mm³/g; women had higher V/M and lower revascularisation rates independent of stenosis. [SNIPPET; outcome effect size not retrieved] — [van Assen & Onnis 2024 commentary, PMC11056761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11056761/)
- Age ≥65 and female sex predict *higher* V/M (because of lower myocardial mass), i.e. V/M is strongly confounded by the demographics the thesis will adjust for. [FULL TEXT commentary] — [PMC11056761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11056761/)
- TAVR cohort: low V/M (≤33.31 mm³/g) HR for MACE 6.14 (1.37–27.54) — a non-coronary-disease-selected but elderly valve population; wide CI. [SNIPPET] — [PMC12020294](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12020294/)

**Murray ratio / bifurcation diameter relations**
- Schoenenberger et al. 2012, Atherosclerosis: 253 patients, IVUS-VH, cross-sectional (no follow-up). Higher Murray ratio (mother vs daughters per Murray's law; high ratio = lower predicted shear) associated with more dense calcium and less fibrous/fibro-fatty tissue; after adjustment for age, sex, risk factors and medication: fibrous volume distal F = 4.90 (p = 0.028), fibro-fatty distal F = 4.76 (p = 0.030), dense calcium proximal F = 5.93 (p = 0.016) and distal F = 5.16 (p = 0.024). [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 22261173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22261173/)
- No study was found testing Finet ratio deviation or daughter-to-daughter diameter ratio as a *prospective* predictor of plaque onset or events.

**Bifurcation angle (related geometry, same branch points)**
- LM trifurcation angle 108° (SD 33) in diseased LCA vs 91° (28) in normal LCA (p = 0.04), n = 52; wider angle correlated with earlier estimated calcification onset (r = −0.34 to −0.43). Cross-sectional, with an assumed 25%/yr CAC progression used to back-extrapolate "onset age". [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 35775448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35775448/)

**Bifurcation plaque and progression (PARADIGM)**
- PARADIGM risk score (Eur Radiol 2024): 370 derivation + 219 validation patients with non-obstructive CAD and no high-risk plaque at baseline, median inter-scan interval 3.3 years (2.7–4.8). Progression (new ≥50% stenosis and/or HRP) in 28%. Multivariable OR 1.47 (1.17–1.84) per bifurcation plaque; spotty calcification OR 4.59; low-attenuation plaque OR 3.73; 25–49% stenosis OR 2.71; C-statistic 0.732 derivation, 0.668 validation. Baseline was *not* disease-free (plaque required); descriptor is plaque location, not diameter. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 37750979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37750979/)
- A PARADIGM review ("Plaque progression: where, why, and how fast?") exists in JCCT but was not accessed. [not read] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1934592521004640)

**CGPS (the thesis cohort) — published prognostic analysis**
- Fuchs/Kofoed et al. 2023, Ann Intern Med: CGPS, 9533 asymptomatic persons aged ≥40 without known IHD; 5114 (54%) no atherosclerosis, 3483 (36%) non-obstructive, 936 (10%) obstructive. Median follow-up 3.5 years (range 0.1–8.9); 71 MI, 193 deaths. Adjusted RR for MI: obstructive 9.19 (4.49–18.11), extensive 7.65 (3.53–16.57), obstructive-extensive 12.48 (5.50–28.12). Death/MI: non-obstructive-extensive 2.70 (1.72–4.25), obstructive-extensive 3.15 (2.05–4.83). No diameter or lumen-volume descriptor reported. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 36972540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36972540/) / [Ann Intern Med](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M22-3027)

**Large CCTA cohorts named in the brief (CONFIRM, SCOT-HEART, ICONIC, MESA, DanRisk, DANCAVAS)**
- CONFIRM2 (2025) AI-QCT: 24 patient/vessel/plaque variables; non-calcified plaque burden and diameter *stenosis* were the strongest predictors. No healthy-segment reference diameter predictor reported. [SNIPPET] — [JACC Imaging](https://www.jacc.org/doi/abs/10.1016/j.jcmg.2025.09.021)
- A 2025 JACC Imaging meta-analysis of advanced CCTA analyses for events exists (not read). [not read] — [ScienceDirect S1936878X25003079](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1936878X25003079)

### Inferences
- Realistic prior for the thesis: no published coronary-calibre-at-bifurcation prognostic effect size exists in a healthy baseline; the thesis would be first-of-kind rather than replicating a known effect. The nearest coronary number (CAVi HR ~2.4 for a dichotomised low-volume group, symptomatic cohort) is likely inflated by small sample, in-sample cut-point and inclusion of revascularisation.
- In CGPS, the dominant predictor is presence/extent of plaque (RR 8–12 for MI). In a *healthy-tree* subset (the 54% with no atherosclerosis), event counts will be very low: 71 MI in the whole cohort over median 3.5 years, most in diseased strata. Even with ~10-year linkage, the healthy subset may only yield tens of MIs, which limits power for per-SD HRs in the 1.1–1.3 range seen in the retinal literature.
- MESA's CT is non-contrast CAC scanning, so it cannot provide lumen diameters (inference from study design; not verified in this search). DanRisk and DANCAVAS: no diameter-outcome publications found.
- V/M and absolute lumen diameters are strongly sex- and body-size-dependent; any prognostic claim must adjust for sex, age and body size (BSA or myocardial mass), otherwise "small arteries" will largely proxy female sex, which carries lower CAD risk and would push HRs in the opposite direction.

### Gaps
- No prospective study found testing Finet/Murray/HK ratio deviation, daughter ratio, or LM/LAD/LCx reference diameter in a disease-free baseline as predictors of incident plaque or events. Specific searches of PARADIGM, SCOT-HEART, ICONIC, DanRisk, DANCAVAS for diameter-based predictors returned nothing; this is an absence of evidence in the searched sources, not a proven absence.
- Outcome effect sizes from ADVANCE (V/M vs revascularisation/events) were not retrieved.
- CT-FFR prognostic studies (e.g. ADVANCE 1-year outcomes) were not examined in detail for healthy-baseline subgroups.

## Q2. Mechanism: WSS at bifurcations as a function of diameter ratios; does ratio abnormality create low-WSS zones where plaque later forms?

### Takeaway
CFD work shows the size of the low-WSS area at the LAD/LCx bifurcation is governed mainly by the daughter diameter ratio (quadratic fit, R² 0.91–0.94), much more than by angle or tortuosity, and low WSS forms in the smaller daughter. Imaging studies show plaque concentrates on the low-WSS lateral (outer) wall and spares the flow divider, and one small serial CCTA study (3 years) shows low ESS predicts lumen loss when Murray's law is used for flow split. No study directly links a baseline diameter-ratio abnormality to later plaque in a healthy tree.

### Cited Findings
- Garcha & Grande Gutiérrez 2025, Sci Rep: 230 synthetic left coronary models. At a neutral LAD/LCx diameter ratio (0.94), angle and position had weak effects on low-WSS area (ALWSS 0.2–5.7%). With skewed ratios (0.65–1.40), ALWSS showed a strong quadratic relation with diameter ratio (R² = 0.91–0.94), rising to 0–11.5% (male models) and 0–9.8% (female), forming in the smaller branch. Tortuosity at neutral ratio changed ALWSS only 0.4–2.0%. Angle correlations flipped sign depending on dominance (R = −0.73 to 0.86). Mild 30% plaque did not alter trends. Low/high TAWSS thresholds 0.5 and 7 Pa. [FULL TEXT, via fetch summary] — [PMC11770140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11770140/)
- Van der Giessen et al. 2009, EuroIntervention (64-slice CT, 65 cross-sections near bifurcations): 88% had plaque; plaque in 72% of outer-wall (lowest WSS) quadrants, 62% inner bend, 38% outer bend, 31% flow divider. When only 1–2 quadrants affected, plaque was in the low-WSS quadrants in 94%; plaque was never found exclusively at the flow divider. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 19378688](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19378688/) / [EuroIntervention](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/plaque-and-shear-stress-distribution-in-human-coronary-bifurcations-a-multislice-computed-tomography-study)
- Sakellarios et al. 2017, EuroIntervention (PROSPECT MSCT substudy): 15 patients, 17 bifurcations, baseline and 3-year CCTA; patients were post-ACS (not disease-free). Murray's-law flow split gave the most accurate ESS; low ESS predicted significant lumen reduction (p = 0.007), plaque burden increase (p = 0.0006) and necrotic core change (p = 0.025). Omitting the side branch degraded prediction, with the largest ESS differences in the main vessel distal to the bifurcation. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 28606882](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28606882/)
- Murray's law implies constant WSS across a bifurcation when obeyed; a high Murray ratio implies lower WSS, and was associated with more calcified bifurcation plaque (IVUS, n = 253). [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 22261173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22261173/)
- PARADIGM plaque-location data (secondary summary): most frequent Medina pattern 1.1.0 (40%); 55% myocardial side, 17.3% lateral, 27.7% epicardial. [SNIPPET; original source not identified, treat as unverified] — [search result context, EuroIntervention/Houston Methodist listing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00330-023-09880-x)

### Inferences
- The mechanistic chain "abnormal daughter ratio → larger low-WSS area in smaller daughter / lateral wall → plaque there" is plausible and each link has support, but the links come from different study types (synthetic CFD, cross-sectional CT, 17-bifurcation serial study). A diameter-ratio descriptor is the most CFD-justified of the candidate bifurcation descriptors, more than angle.
- Because the diameter ratio drives WSS through the flow split, measurement placement (before/at/after carina) matters: post-bifurcation segments of the smaller daughter are where the effect should be largest.

### Gaps
- No serial study in initially plaque-free bifurcations quantifying whether baseline Murray/Finet deviation predicts where plaque later appears.
- Finet-ratio-specific CFD sensitivity (as opposed to Murray-based flow split) not found.

## Q3. Cross-domain precedent: healthy-baseline retinal calibre and branching geometry predicting ~10-year CV outcomes

### Takeaway
Retinal calibre is the best-established precedent for "baseline vessel size in healthy people predicts events a decade later", with consistent but modest effects: roughly HR 1.06–1.2 per SD (or per 20 µm) after risk-factor adjustment, often significant only in women for CHD. Retinal bifurcation diameter optimality has been linked to IHD death in one nested case-control study. Deep-learning retinal scores give larger per-unit HRs (about 1.4) and add only ~0.01–0.02 to C-statistics over QRISK3.

### Cited Findings
**Individual-participant meta-analysis**
- McGeechan et al. 2009, Ann Intern Med: 6 population cohorts, 22,159 CHD-free participants, 5–14 years follow-up, 2219 incident CHD (10.0%). Women: HR 1.16 (1.06–1.26) per 20 µm wider venules; 1.17 (1.07–1.28) per 20 µm narrower arterioles. Men: 1.02 (0.94–1.10) and 1.02 (0.95–1.10), non-significant. Adjusted for traditional risk factors; stronger in women without hypertension/diabetes. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 19755365](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19755365/)

**ARIC**
- Wong et al. 2002, JAMA: 9648 CHD-free adults aged 51–72, mean 3.5 years; per SD decrease in AVR, women RR 1.37 (1.08–1.72) for CHD and 1.50 (1.10–2.04) for MI; men RR 1.00 (0.84–1.18). [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 11879113](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11879113/)
- Seidelmann et al. 2016, Circulation: 10,470 participants without prior ASCVD or HF, mean 16 years follow-up, 1779 CHD, 548 ischaemic strokes, 2793 deaths. Per SD wider venules: HR 1.13 (1.08–1.18) death, 1.18 (1.07–1.31) stroke, 1.10 (1.00–1.20) CHD in women; per SD narrower arterioles: 1.06 (1.01–1.11), 1.14 (1.03–1.26), 1.13 (1.03–1.24); CHD association in women only (interaction p = 0.02); adjusted for Pooled Cohort Equation variables. In low-risk women (<5% PCE risk), 10-year event rate 5.6% in narrowest vs 2.8% in widest arteriolar quartile; calibre reclassified 21% of low-risk women to intermediate risk. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 27682886](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27682886/)

**Beaver Dam + Blue Mountains**
- Wang et al. 2007, Eur Heart J: pooled 7494 participants, 10–12 years follow-up, 653 CHD deaths, 299 stroke deaths. Per-SD continuous associations non-significant; threshold effects: narrowest arteriolar quintile HR 1.34 (1.11–1.62), widest venular quintile HR 1.24 (1.02–1.52) for CHD mortality; at age 43–69, 1.70 (1.27–2.28) and 1.41 (1.06–1.89). Baseline not restricted to CVD-free (adjusted instead). [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 17626032](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17626032/)

**Retinal branching geometry (closest analogue to bifurcation diameter ratios)**
- Witt et al. 2006, Hypertension: nested case-control in Beaver Dam, age 43–74, 126 IHD deaths and 28 stroke deaths over 10 years vs 528 matched controls. Suboptimal arteriolar diameter relationship at bifurcations (junction exponent deviation) associated with IHD death (p = 0.02 unadjusted), remaining significant after adjustment for age, sex, prior CVD and risk factors; decreased arteriolar tortuosity also (p = 0.011). Bifurcation *angles* were not associated. Effect sizes (OR) not in the abstract. Baseline included people with prior CVD (adjusted for). [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 16585415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16585415/) / [Hypertension](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.HYP.0000216717.72048.6c)
- Witt et al. 2010 proposed the "optimality ratio" as a noise-robust alternative to the junction exponent for bifurcation diameter optimality. [SNIPPET] — [PMC2954284](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2954284/)

**Deep-learning retinal calibre and scores**
- SIVA-DLS, Cheung et al. 2021, Nat Biomed Eng (the PDF in the repo): trained/validated on >70,000 images from 15 cohorts; ICC vs human graders 0.82–0.95. In SEED (Singapore, age 40–80; n = 8980 at risk, median 4.54 years, range 0.02–11.8), 851 incident CVD events (174 stroke, 340 MI, 181 CVD death, 156 mixed). Per SD narrower CRAE_B: HR 1.18 (1.08–1.30) adjusted for age/sex/ethnicity/fellow calibre; 1.12 (1.02–1.24) after also adjusting for BMI, MABP, HbA1c, cholesterol, smoking. CRAE_C fully adjusted 1.13 (1.02–1.26). Per SD wider CRVE_B: 1.14 (1.04–1.25) → 1.08 (0.98–1.18) fully adjusted; CRVE_C 1.21 (1.09–1.33) → 1.09 (0.99–1.21). Top vs bottom CRVE_B quartile HR 1.74 (1.37–2.21) → 1.30 (1.01–1.67). In the Beijing Eye Study, per SD narrower CRAE_B crude OR 1.27 (1.13–1.41) for 10-year all-cause mortality. The Methods section does not state explicit exclusion of prior CVD in SEED. [FULL TEXT, local PDF] — [Cheung et al., doi:10.1038/s41551-020-00626-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-020-00626-4); local file `/zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/A deep-learning system for the assessment of cardiovascular disease risk via the measurement of retinal-vessel calibre.pdf`
- Reti-CVD (UK Biobank validation, BMC Med 2023): 48,260 participants without CVD history, 11-year follow-up, 6.3% events; adjusted HR 1.41 (1.30–1.52); high-risk group 10-year CVD risk 13.1%; C-statistic increase over QRISK3 +0.014 (non-statin), +0.013 (stage 1 hypertension), +0.023 (middle-aged). Reti-CVD is a deep-learning score (trained to predict CAC from photos), not a calibre measurement. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 36691041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36691041/); original development: [Rim et al. 2021, Lancet Digit Health](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(21)00043-1/fulltext) [not read]

### Inferences
- Realistic per-SD effect sizes for a healthy-baseline calibre metric after full adjustment: HR 1.05–1.20. Age alone typically carries HR ~2 per decade for CHD and male sex HR ~2 (general epidemiology; not sourced in this search), so calibre is a weak marker relative to age/sex and will add little to C-statistic (retinal: +0.01–0.02 for a DL score; calibre alone reclassified low-risk women in ARIC).
- Retinal CHD associations appear sex-specific (women only in ARIC and McGeechan); a coronary analysis should pre-specify sex interaction.
- Retinal arteriolar narrowing reflects hypertension-driven microvascular remodelling; coronary epicardial diameter is a different vessel class (conduit, flow-adaptive), so the retinal effect sizes are a precedent for study design and power, not a direct prior for coronary effect direction.
- The retinal bifurcation-optimality finding (Witt 2006) is the only cross-domain evidence that deviation from optimal diameter relations at branch points, measured at baseline, predicts cardiac death ~10 years later; it is one small nested case-control study.

### Gaps
- Rotterdam Study retinal calibre and CHD/stroke (Ikram et al.) not retrieved; Rotterdam is included in McGeechan's pooled analysis.
- Effect sizes (ORs) from Witt 2006 not available in the abstract; full text blocked (403).
- Other DL retinal studies (e.g. Poplin 2018, RetiCAC, Framingham/UK Biobank retinal vasculometry) not reviewed.

## Q4. Studies with ≥5-year follow-up using coronary diameter at baseline; realistic effect sizes vs age/sex/risk factors

### Takeaway
Only one study found: Benetos 2020 (CAVi, whole-tree lumen volume / myocardial mass, n = 325 symptomatic, mean 5.4 years), with adjusted HR ~2.4 for low vs high CAVi and very wide CIs. No study with ≥5-year follow-up used bifurcation-level or segment-level reference diameters in a disease-free baseline. By analogy with retinal calibre, a realistic adjusted effect for a healthy-baseline calibre descriptor would be about HR 1.05–1.2 per SD, far smaller than CCTA plaque burden (CGPS RR 8–12 for obstructive disease) and smaller than age or sex.

### Cited Findings
- Benetos 2020: mean follow-up 5.4 ± 1.7 years; low CAVi HR 3.79 (1.66–8.65) univariable, 2.43 (1.02–5.75) adjusted for SSS; n = 325 suspected-CAD patients. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 31894527](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31894527)
- CGPS obstructive disease adjusted RR for MI 9.19 (4.49–18.11), median 3.5 years: the scale of effect plaque carries in the thesis cohort. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 36972540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36972540/)
- Retinal calibre adjusted per-SD HRs 1.06–1.18 over 16 years (ARIC) and 1.12–1.13 over median 4.5 years (SEED, SIVA-DLS). [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 27682886](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27682886/); [FULL TEXT] — [doi:10.1038/s41551-020-00626-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-020-00626-4)
- Retinal DL score adds ΔC 0.013–0.023 over QRISK3 at 11 years. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 36691041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36691041/)

### Inferences
- The thesis should frame bifurcation diameter descriptors as exploratory, report per-SD HRs adjusted for age, sex and a body-size term, and power-check against HR ~1.2 per SD with the event count available in the plaque-free subset.
- If the descriptor predicts events only in the whole cohort but not in the plaque-free subset, it is likely acting as a proxy for existing plaque (lumen narrowing/remodelling), not as an independent geometric risk factor. Restricting to CAD-RADS 0 / no-plaque trees is what makes the analysis a genuine healthy-baseline test.

### Gaps
- No coronary diameter study (segmental, LM, LAD/LCx, or Murray/Finet deviation) with ≥5-year follow-up and disease-free baseline was found.
- CT-FFR-derived prognosis in plaque-free patients not investigated.
- Age/sex HRs for CHD in CGPS specifically were not retrieved; the "HR ~2 per decade" comparison above is general knowledge, not sourced here.
