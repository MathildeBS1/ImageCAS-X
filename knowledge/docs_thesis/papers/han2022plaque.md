**Read:** 2026-10-04, full text on PMC. The supplement (eTables 3 to 5, eFigures) was not read.
**Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8792800/
**Verdict:** The closest prior design: CCTA geometry related to later events. It is lesion-level, in
referred patients who already had plaque, and with events mostly within a month of the scan, so it
does not address plaque onset.

## What they did

A nested case-control substudy of ICONIC, which sits inside the CONFIRM registry: 23,251 consecutive
patients with CCTA for **suspected CAD**. 234 ACS cases were propensity-matched 1:1 with event-free
controls. **118 of the 234 cases were excluded** because the culprit could not be adjudicated. This
leaves 116 cases (mean age 62.7, 69.0% men) plus 116 controls, with 548 lesions in the cases.

Three "adverse geometric characteristics" (AGCs) were scored per lesion on ≥64-row CCTA (Autoplaque)
by level-III readers:
- short distance from the ostium (below the median: <39 mm left, <60 mm right);
- bifurcation location (side-branch origin within the lesion);
- tortuosity (one bend >90° or three curves of 45 to 90°, 3-point angle, within the lesion; ICC 0.91
  over 30 lesions).

Plaque volumes were scored with QAngio CT.

## What they found

- **Culprit precursors vs non-culprit lesions in the same patients:**
  - distance from the ostium, median 35.1 vs 44.5 mm;
  - bifurcation 73.3% vs 38.9%;
  - tortuous 4.3% vs 1.4% (5 vs 6 lesions); all p < .05.
- **Multivariable Cox, adjusted for stenosis, adverse plaque features and quantitative plaque:**
  - short distance HR 2.71 (1.73 to 4.24);
  - bifurcation HR 2.14 (1.39 to 3.29);
  - tortuosity HR 2.31 (1.01 to 5.27), p = .04;
  - number of AGCs: 1 AGC HR 2.90 (1.38 to 6.08), ≥2 AGCs HR 6.84 (3.33 to 14.04).
- **Discrimination:** AUC 0.766 with AGCs vs 0.733 without.
- **Culprits vs the highest-grade stenosis in controls:** short distance HR 1.55 (1.02 to 2.33) and
  bifurcation HR 2.63 (1.77 to 3.92). **Tortuosity did not differ.**

## What the abstract does not tell you

- **"Future" ACS was mostly imminent: median 29 days from CCTA to event (IQR 5 to 386).** This
  describes lesions present at the scan, not risk years ahead.
- **Patients whose ACS culprit sat in a segment that was normal on CCTA (n = 21) were excluded.**
  Events arising from plaque-free segments, the onset case, are outside the analysis by design.
- The included cases had more plaque and stenosis than the excluded ones (conceded; eTable 5). The
  findings "might be limited to patients with high cardiovascular risk and extensive coronary
  atherosclerosis".
- Tortuosity rests on 11 lesions (5 + 6). The authors concede it is underpowered, and it was null
  against controls.
- Geometry is lesion-local and categorical (presence of a side branch, bend count), not tree-wide.
- No WSS was measured (conceded). Non-fatal ACS only.
- The caption of Figure 2 swaps the AGC and APC definitions (the paper's own typo); the Methods text
  is correct.

## What it licenses

- Literature review §3: "Han et al. (2022) related lesion position and vessel shape on CCTA to later
  acute coronary syndromes in patients referred for suspected disease, finding that culprit lesions
  sat nearer the ostium and more often at bifurcations."
- Gap 1 must not say that no study related geometry to later events. Correct framing: event studies
  exist, but at lesion level, in referred patients with existing plaque, and with events close to the
  scan.
- Table row: referred (CONFIRM), CCTA, ACS culprit lesions, lesion-level geometry, 116 cases + 116
  controls.

## What it does NOT license

- Plaque onset or "predicts who develops plaque": every lesion was already plaque, and culprits in
  normal segments were excluded.
- Long-term prediction: the median time to event was 29 days.
- Tortuosity as a robust risk factor: 11 lesions, borderline, null against controls.
- General population: referred for suspected CAD.
