# bergstrom2021scapis — SCAPIS: prevalence of subclinical coronary atherosclerosis

**Citation:** Bergström G, Persson M, Adiels M, Björnson E, Bonander C, Ahlström H, Alfredsson J,
Angerås O, et al. *Circulation* 2021;144(12):916-929. doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.055340.
PMID 34543072. PMCID PMC8448414. Open access.
**Read:** 2026-10-04, full text (Europe PMC full-text XML). The data supplement was not read.
**Source:** https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8448414/fullTextXML
**Verdict:** The other general-population CCTA resource next to CGPS. It establishes that
plaque is common in an unselected middle-aged population. It is cross-sectional and reports no
geometry.

## What they did

SCAPIS, a population-based prospective study at 6 Swedish sites, 2013 to 2018. Men and women aged
50 to 64 years were randomly invited from the census register; 30,154 were recruited (participation
50.3%). 27,385 had CCTA. After excluding 1,805 participants whose proximal-segment image quality
was poor and 398 with prior MI or coronary intervention, **25,182 were analysed (50.6% women)**.

Dedicated dual-source CT (Siemens Somatom Definition Flash), identical at every site. CCTA was
visually read by 36 readers using the 18-segment SCCT model. The 11 most relevant segments were
compulsory; the rest were reported only if diseased. Stenosis was graded as none, 1 to 49% or ≥50%.
CAC was scored on non-contrast scans. External validity was checked by inverse probability weighting
against register data.

## What they found

- Any CCTA-detected atherosclerosis: **42.1%** (95% CI 41.3 to 42.7). Stenosis ≥50%: 5.2%. Left
  main, proximal LAD or 3-vessel disease: 1.9%. Any non-calcified plaque: 8.3%.
- Onset was delayed by about 10 years in women.
- Plaque was most frequent in the proximal and mid LAD. The same held in the 3,867 participants
  with a single affected segment.
- CAC 0: 5.5% had CCTA plaque. CAC 1 to 10: 81.9%.

## What the abstract does not tell you

- **Cross-sectional only.** The authors concede it "prevents us from showing how atherosclerosis
  develops over time". This paper reports no repeat CCTA.
- **Selection:** the participation rate was 50.3%. CCTA was not done in about 2,200 participants
  (mostly safety reasons), and 1,805 were excluded for image quality, so the analysed sample is
  healthier-selected than the invited population. The authors call the exclusion rate "relatively
  high".
- Visual reading with non-independent CAC/CCTA reads (conceded). Non-compulsory distal segments
  were reported only when diseased.
- The authors attribute the proximal-LAD predilection speculatively to "branching sites perhaps with
  turbulent flow patterns". They measured no geometry.

## What it licenses

- Literature review §3: "Bergström et al. (2021) scanned a random sample of the general population
  with CCTA and found coronary plaque in a large share of participants without known disease."
- The gap: general-population CCTA cohorts exist (SCAPIS, CGPS), but they have reported plaque
  prevalence rather than geometry.
- Table row: general population, CCTA, present plaque (cross-sectional), n = 25,182.

## What it does NOT license

- Any longitudinal or onset claim: none in this paper.
- Any geometric explanation of the proximal-LAD pattern: authors' speculation, not measured.
- "Representative of the population": the participation rate was half, and there were further
  exclusions.

## Open questions

- Has SCAPIS published repeat CCTA or any centreline geometry? None found in this pass; worth a
  check before claiming that no general-population cohort has geometry.
