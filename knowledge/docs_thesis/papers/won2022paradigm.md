# won2022paradigm — PARADIGM: rapid progression in patients plaque-free at baseline

**Citation:** Won KB, Lee BK, Lin FY, Hadamitzky M, Kim YJ, Sung JM, Conte E, Andreini D, Pontone G,
Budoff MJ, Gottlieb I, Chun EJ, Cademartiri F, Maffei E, Marques H, de Araújo Gonçalves P, Leipsic
JA, Lee SE, Shin S, Choi JH, Virmani R, Samady H, Chinnaiyan K, Berman DS, Narula J, Shaw LJ, Bax JJ,
Min JK, Chang HJ. *Cardiovascular Diabetology* 2022;21(1):239. doi:10.1186/s12933-022-01656-9.
PMID 36371222. PMCID PMC9655903. Open access.
**Read:** 2026-10-04, full text (Europe PMC full-text XML). Supplementary tables S1 to S3 not read.
**Source:** https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9655903/fullTextXML
**Verdict:** A serial-CCTA cohort that started plaque-free and measured new plaque, so it partly
answers "no longitudinal study starts plaque-free". It measured no geometry, and it never modelled
new-plaque onset, only rapid progression.

## What they did

Retrospective case-control analysis inside the PARADIGM registry: international, prospective,
observational, 13 centres in seven countries, 2003 to 2015. Of 2,252 consecutive participants with
serial CCTA, 1,760 had interpretable images. **402 had no plaque on baseline CCTA** and form the
cohort: mean age 57.6 ± 10.0 years, 49.3% men, hypertension 43.0%, diabetes 12.9%. The paper does
not state why patients had CCTA or why they were rescanned. The registry is clinical and the patients
were not drawn from the general population.

≥64-row CT at both scans. Semi-automated plaque quantification (QAngioCT, Medis) with manual
correction by level-III readers blinded to clinical data. Segments ≥2 mm, modified 17-segment model,
registered between scans by branch takeoffs and distance from the ostia. Rapid plaque progression
(RPP) is defined as an annual change in percentage atheroma volume (PAV) of ≥1.0%/year. Median
inter-scan interval 3.6 years (IQR 2.7 to 5.0).

## What they found

- **New plaque at follow-up: 143 of 402 (35.6%).** RPP: 17 of 402 (4.2%).
- Baseline traditional risk factors (age ≥60, male sex, hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia,
  obesity, smoking) were **not significantly associated** with annual PAV change or with RPP (Table
  2; e.g. age ≥60 OR 2.551, 0.924 to 7.039, p = 0.071).
- HbA1c **measured at follow-up** was independently associated with annual PAV change (β 0.098 per
  1%, 95% CI 0.048 to 0.149, p < 0.001) and with RPP (OR 2.069 to 2.654 across six two-covariate
  models). ROC cut-off 7.05%, AUC 0.816 (95% CI 0.574 to 0.999).

The primary analysis was predictors of RPP and annual PAV change. New plaque is reported only
descriptively.

## What the abstract does not tell you

- **New-plaque onset was never modelled.** The 35.6% figure is descriptive. No predictor, risk
  factor or otherwise, was tested against new plaque. "Risk factors did not predict" applies only to
  RPP, an outcome with 17 events.
- **The "predictor" is measured at follow-up**, not baseline (Methods: all variables except age, sex
  and baseline risk factors are from the follow-up scan). It is an association, not a prediction.
- With 17 RPP events, the risk-factor ORs are underpowered (wide intervals; age ≥60 OR 2.55, p =
  0.07). The non-significance is not evidence of no effect.
- The AUC interval runs from 0.574 to 0.999.
- Conceded limitations: patients without plaque are not representative of PARADIGM; no serial
  clinical data between scans; medications uncontrolled; small arteries not assessed.
- The indication for the baseline and repeat scans is not given. A repeat CCTA in a plaque-free
  patient is itself a selection event (the Discussion says serial CCTA "has not yet been justified"
  in this population).

## What it licenses

- Literature review §3: "Won et al. (2022) followed patients without plaque on a first CCTA to a
  repeat scan and found new plaque in about a third of them, but measured no geometry."
- Literature review §3 gap: plaque-free cohorts with repeat CCTA exist, but they are clinical
  registries rather than the general population.
- In the table: clinical registry, serial CCTA, outcome new plaque (descriptive) and rapid
  progression; n = 402.

## What it does NOT license

- "Traditional risk factors do not predict new plaque": never tested; RPP only, underpowered.
- "General population": not stated; a clinical registry with unstated scan indications.
- Any geometry claim: none measured.
- HbA1c as a baseline predictor: it was measured at follow-up.

## Open questions

- What were the scan indications in PARADIGM (registry design paper, Lee SE et al. 2016, Am Heart J)?
  This matters for how strongly "not general population" can be stated.
- Has any PARADIGM substudy related geometry to progression? None found in this pass.
