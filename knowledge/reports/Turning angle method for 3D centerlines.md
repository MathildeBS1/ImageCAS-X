# Keep the chord turning angle, fix its bookkeeping

The thesis four-step method (chord respacing at fixed Euclidean step ℓ, 3D chord vectors, unsigned angle from the dot product, T_ℓ = Σθ_j / L_c) is a sound and well-founded way to compute mean absolute curvature at scale ℓ on 3D coronary centerlines, and no alternative estimator tested clearly beats it for a per-vessel scalar. It is Milnor's total curvature of a polygon inscribed in the centerline divided by its length ([Sullivan, arXiv math/0606007](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0606007)), it is exactly the in-plane part of Bullitt's SOAM evaluated on a respaced polyline ([Bullitt et al. 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603/)), and it shares its discretisation with Bribiesca's slope chain code ([Mateos et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11149256/)). The problems are in the bookkeeping, not the idea. The code divides by π and the plotting script multiplies it back, while the thesis says no division happens; the normaliser counts one more chord than there are angles, biasing every score low by a length-dependent factor (n−1)/n (about 5% for a median RCA, up to about 8% for short ones); the walk starts at whichever end the graph search returns, not at the ostium the thesis describes, which moves 3 of 56 high-decile and 11 of 56 low-decile RCAs between groups; and the worked example rounds a point that is not exactly 5 mm away. Two further items need disclosure rather than code: T_ℓ is a sampling operation that can alias, not a low-pass filter, and the "3σ/ℓ² always added" noise rule is an upper bound that overstates the bias on curved vessels. Every simulation number below comes from scratch scripts (`synth.py`, `real.py`, `dirn.py`, `exp.py`, `exp2.py`) in an ephemeral session scratchpad, not from the repository, and must be reproduced by committed code before any of it enters the thesis.

## The method is a textbook curvature estimator with a coronary pedigree

The numerator is on firm theoretical ground. For a polygon, total curvature is the sum of the turning angles at interior vertices, and the total curvature of a space curve is the supremum over all inscribed polygons; deleting a vertex never increases it, so an inscribed polygon's Σθ is a **lower bound** on the curve's ∫|κ|ds ([Sullivan §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0606007); [Wikipedia, Total curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_curvature)). Chord respacing puts every new vertex on the centerline, so the thesis polygon is exactly the object in Milnor's definition. In discrete differential geometry the turning angle at a vertex is the standard integrated curvature, and the pointwise discrete curvature is k_i = 2ψ_i / (|e_{i−1/2}| + |e_{i+1/2}|), which reduces to θ_i/ℓ when all edges are equal ([Vouga, DDG notes 1](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~evouga/uploads/4/5/6/8/45689883/notes1.pdf)). Equal chords are therefore not a cosmetic choice: they are the condition under which T_ℓ is the plain mean of a standard discrete curvature. On a circle of radius R, each angle equals the central angle 2 arcsin(ℓ/2R), so θ/ℓ overestimates 1/R by only about (ℓ/R)²/24, **under 1.1% for R ≥ 10 mm at ℓ = 5 mm** (derivation in the scratch notes).

The alternative discrete formulas are interchangeable at coronary angles. The sine (Menger), tangent and plain-angle variants differ by O((κℓ)²) ([arXiv 2502.09353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09353)); VMTK's finite-difference curvature on a uniform polyline works out algebraically to 2 sin(θ/2)/(ℓ cos²(θ/2)), within **2% of θ/ℓ for turns up to about 17°** ([VMTK source](https://github.com/vmtk/vmtk/blob/master/vtkVmtk/ComputationalGeometry/vtkvmtkCenterlineGeometry.cxx)). On 120 training RCAs the Menger variant changed the score by −0.6% with Spearman 0.9998 against the code (scratch `real.py`). The median RCA turns about 14° per 5 mm chord, well inside the regime where the formula choice is immaterial.

The vascular literature splits into angle-sum metrics on a polyline (Bullitt, Grisan's mean angle change, Tello, slope chain code) and pointwise curvature integrated along a smoothed, finely resampled curve (Hart, Kashyap, VMTK). Bullitt's SOAM uses the identical arccos of consecutive unit difference vectors, adds a torsional angle between successive osculating-plane normals, and divides by total polyline length in rad/cm, at one-voxel spacing ([Bullitt 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603/)). Kashyap et al., the one systematic coronary CTA comparison, instead smooth in VMTK, resample at 0.01 mm and compute average absolute curvature (1/L)∫|κ|ds ([Kashyap et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)). T_ℓ has the first family's mechanics and the second family's units, so the thesis can honestly present it as SOAM without its torsion term, evaluated at a stated chord scale. Dropping torsion is arguably an improvement, since the osculating plane is undefined on nearly straight segments and Bullitt gives no guard for that.

## Smoother estimators swap ℓ for a less interpretable knob

Every derivative-based alternative is "smooth, then differentiate twice", and its accuracy depends on the smoothing scale. VMTK's own documentation warns that its finite-difference derivatives are "very likely" affected by noise ([VMTK tutorial](https://github.com/vmtk/vmtk.github.com/blob/master/tutorials/GeometricAnalysis.md)), and a carotid landmarking study found bend counts ranging from **3 to 33 depending on resampling length alone** ([PMC8626959](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626959/)). In the scratch simulation (150 random smooth 100 mm curves, 0.25 mm spatially correlated jitter), every method at a well-chosen scale ranked vessels about equally well:

| Estimator | Spearman vs truth (noisy) | Median bias (noisy) | Test-retest Spearman |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chord ℓ = 2 mm | 0.903 | +66% | 0.847 |
| Chord ℓ = 5 mm (thesis) | 0.985 | −4.0% | 0.991 |
| Chord ℓ = 5 mm, (n−1) normaliser | 0.982 | −0.9% | 0.988 |
| Chord ℓ = 8 mm | 0.857 | −25% | 0.984 |
| Menger 5 mm | 0.978 | −1.3% | 0.989 |
| Gaussian σ = 2 mm | 0.987 | +5.5% | 0.994 |
| Smoothing spline λ = 50 | 0.980 | +1.8% | 0.995 |
| VMTK-like Laplacian + finite differences | 0.985 | +16% | 0.987 |

(Source: scratch `exp.py`/`exp2.py`; not yet reproducible from the repository.) The conclusion is that ℓ = 5 mm sits near the sweet spot and the alternatives gain at most a few thousandths of test-retest correlation while introducing a smoothing parameter that is harder to state in millimetres, plus implementation traps (the scratch runs found that `gaussian_filter1d(order=2)` on absolute coordinates leaks a +26% bias even on clean helices). Raw finite differences at 0.5 mm spacing are unusable (+900% or more bias at σ = 0.1 mm). Digital-geometry estimators with multigrid convergence proofs (λ-MST, integral invariants) are proven for staircase boundaries on a grid ([Lachaud et al., IVC 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0262885606003040); [Coeurjolly et al., DGCI 2013](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-37067-0_34)), not for sub-voxel VMTK polylines, so they are not an upgrade here. Chord respacing also beat arc-length resampling under white jitter (+14% vs +91% bias on one helix), because arc length itself is inflated by noise. If a second method is wanted as a robustness check, Gaussian scale-space smoothing along arc length (σ ≈ 2 to 3 mm) is the most defensible, since it is monotone in scale.

The angle formula itself is not a concern. arccos is ill-conditioned near zero and the arctangent form atan2(|a×b|, a·b) is the recommended habit ([Walker, Computing angle between vectors](https://www.jwwalker.com/pages/angle-between-vectors.html); [Possibly Wrong](https://possiblywrong.wordpress.com/2020/07/17/computing-the-angle-between-two-vectors/)), but in float64 on the real RCAs the two differ by at most **4.3e-14 rad** and T_5 is unchanged (scratch `real.py`). Switch to atan2 as a one-line hygiene change; it moves no result.

## Four defects in the code and text, resolved against the code

Where the notes disagree, the code reading settles it. `tortuosity/merged.py:45` returns `th.sum() / (np.pi * l * (len(V) - 1))`, the module docstring calls it "sum(theta) / (pi L)", and `scripts/tortuosity_deciles.py:94` plots `df.scc_5 * np.pi` "without the /pi". So **the code does divide by π**, and the thesis sentence at `thesis/week5/rca_tortuosity.tex:122-123` ("the division by π is left out") describes the plotted number, not the stored one. The reported median 0.048 mm⁻¹ matches `scc_5 × π` on the 560 training RCAs (0.0476, 10th/90th percentiles 0.036/0.074). The numbers are right; the code and prose disagree. Remove the π from `scc_density` and the `* np.pi` from the plotting script, or state in the code that the stored column is T_ℓ/π.

The **(n−1)/n normaliser bias** is the one real, length-dependent error. `len(V) − 1` is the number of chords n, but there are only n − 1 interior angles, so T_ℓ = ((n−1)/n) × mean(θ_j/ℓ). On clean circles and helices this produces about −5% at ℓ = 5 mm regardless of curvature, and it dominates the chord-versus-arc effect for any gentle bend. Across the cohort (9 to 29 chords per RCA, median 21) the factor runs from about 0.92 for a 69 mm RCA to 0.96 for a 137 mm one, and dividing by (n−1)ℓ instead raised scores by a median 5.0% (max 12.5%) and lowered the Spearman correlation with length from 0.361 to 0.330 in a 120-vessel subset (scratch `real.py`). That explains a small part, not most, of the reported length correlation. Fix it by dividing by `(len(V) - 2) * l`, the dual length of the n − 1 interior vertices, which is the discrete-geometry convention ([Vouga](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~evouga/uploads/4/5/6/8/45689883/notes1.pdf)); Bullitt's SOAM has the same mismatch (n−3 angles over n−1 edges), so the alternative is to keep it and define T_ℓ explicitly as total turning per polyline length. For left-tree branches as short as 30 mm the bias reaches about 17%, so the fix matters more once the analysis leaves the RCA.

The **walk direction is arbitrary**. `vessels.longest_path` returns the path from whichever double-sweep Dijkstra endpoint is farther, and `run_merged_tests.cohort` passes it straight to `scc_density`; only the plotting function reorients to ostium-first. The path started at the ostium in 61 of 120 checked RCAs. Because the chord walk's vertex phase and the dropped tail depend on the start, reversing the walk changed T_5 by a median 3.1% (90th percentile 10.3%, max 33.8%) over all 560 RCAs, and fixing the start at the ostium moved **3 of 56 high-decile and 11 of 56 low-decile RCAs** into the other group (scratch `dirn.py`). The thesis at line 93 says the RCA runs "from the ostium", and step 1 says "starting at one end", which is literally true but hides the inconsistency. Orient every path ostium-first before scoring, using the orientation `tortuosity_deciles.py:rca` already computes. Shifting the start by 0 to 4 mm also moved scores by a median 6.5% peak to peak, so averaging over a few start offsets or over both directions is a cheap further stabiliser, particularly for the fragile low decile.

The **worked example** is correct to its stated precision but not exact. With b = (4.70, 1.71, 0), |b| = 5.0014 mm, so the cosine is 23.5/25.007 = 0.9397 and θ = 19.99°, not 23.5/25 = 0.94 (which gives 19.95°). Both round to 20°, but step 1 promises chords of exactly ℓ, so write "about 0.94" or use the exact point (9.698, 1.710, 0).

The sphere-polyline intersection itself is correct. It takes the first input vertex outside the ball of radius ℓ, and by convexity the crossing on the segment before it is the curve's first exit; the larger quadratic root is the only root in [0, 1], and no division by zero can occur. The dropped tail was a median 2.55 mm (max 4.92 mm) on 120 RCAs.

## What the thesis should disclose about scale and noise

Three statements need qualifying. First, the noise rule. The note derivations disagree on the constant: one applies "3σ/ℓ² always added" directly (0.03 mm⁻¹ at σ = 0.25 mm, ℓ = 5 mm), the other measures it. Resolved against the measurements: for i.i.d. isotropic vertex noise on a straight line the exact constant is 3.07σ/ℓ per angle, and chord interpolation lowers it by about 20% at ℓ ≥ 5 mm (measured 0.0234 against the predicted 0.030 at σ = 0.25 mm). On a curved vessel the added bias is far smaller, because |θ + noise| is nearly unbiased when θ is large: on a 15 mm circle at σ = 0.25 mm, ℓ = 5 mm the excess was 0.0025, not 0.030 (scratch `synth.py`). The thesis should call 3σ/ℓ² an upper bound that applies where noise angles exceed true angles, and add that noise therefore inflates straight vessels most and compresses the low end of the distribution. The true residual jitter and its correlation length on ImageCAS-X centerlines are unknown, so any σ is an assumption.

Second, scale. Chord respacing is a sampling operation, not a low-pass filter. In the scratch aliasing test (a 40 mm arc with an 8 mm-wavelength wiggle), T_ℓ at ℓ = 3, 4, 5, 6 mm was 0.215, 0.025, 0.100, 0.046, non-monotone, whereas Gaussian smoothing decreased monotonically. Loops smaller than ℓ are skipped outright: one 5 mm chord stood in for up to 11.4 mm of arc in the real RCAs, so T_ℓ is a lower bound on turning in tightly looped RCAs such as the ostial loops the thesis highlights. The wording "mean absolute curvature measured at scale ℓ" is defensible only with "at scale ℓ" kept, plus a sensitivity curve over ℓ ∈ {3, 4, 5, 6, 8} mm, which the thesis partly has (0.085, 0.048, 0.040 at 2, 5, 8 mm).

Third, citations for the lineage and the discrepancy. Citing Milnor/Sullivan, Vouga and Bullitt would let the thesis state that T_ℓ is a recognised curvature estimator, which the current text asserts without support. The unexplained factor of two against van Zandwijk et al.'s 0.081 to 0.090 mm⁻¹ has a plausible, unchecked candidate: pointwise curvature of a fitted spline is not bounded by Milnor's inequality and reads high on noisy curves, whereas an inscribed polygon reads low. That should be checked in their methods before it is offered as an explanation. The thesis also calls the unsigned 3D angle a match for Tello's equation, which a project full-text note records as using absolute values normalised by π ([Tello Ayala et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)); that reading should be confirmed against the published equation before the "discards anyway" clause stays.

## Conclusion

The question "is this the best way" has a sharper answer than expected: at matched scale, the estimator formula barely matters (Spearman 0.98 to 0.99 across chord, Menger, spline and Gaussian methods), and what actually moves vessels between deciles is procedural, namely where the walk starts and how many chords the denominator counts. That shifts the thesis burden from defending the choice of turning angle, which theory and precedent already cover, to making the pipeline deterministic and length-neutral. After the ostium-first and (n−1)ℓ fixes, the decile groups and the length correlation should be recomputed, since the low group is the one that moved.

The more consequential open question is the noise floor. The whole case for ℓ = 5 mm rests on an assumed jitter level for centerlines whose actual residual noise has never been measured. Estimating it, for example from repeated or perturbed extractions of the same vessels, would turn the scale choice from a borrowed convention into a measured one and would also bound how much of the low-decile compression is noise rather than anatomy.
