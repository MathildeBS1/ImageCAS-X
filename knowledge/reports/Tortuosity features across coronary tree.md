# Diameter travels, torsion stalls, left main shrinks

Expanding Model 2 from RCA-only to the whole coronary tree does not change the RCA-level verdict of add diameter, scalarize angle, defer torsion, but it does force three vessel-specific amendments and one architectural rebuild. Diameter remains worth adding as a per-point channel on the RCA, LAD, and LCx, though on the LAD it will show a physiological sawtooth at every diagonal takeoff that must not be read as stenosis, and on the LCx its outcome evidence is thinner and gets undercut by two directly conflicting tortuosity-outcome studies. Bifurcation angle stays a scalar covariate rather than a dense per-point channel on all three vessels, not because branch points are anatomically sparse everywhere (the LAD and LCx have several) but because the CAD-outcome literature only backs the angle value at one or two proximal landmarks regardless of vessel. Torsion stays out of the primary design on every vessel: no coronary artery, RCA, LAD, or LCx, has a single outcome study linking torsion to disease, and the noise-amplification problem is a property of the third-derivative math, not of any one vessel's geometry. The left main should not get a per-point profile at all; at ~10.5 mm average length and as short as 2 mm in some patients, it yields too few centerline samples for a stable profile, and the entire LM literature has independently converged on scalar bifurcation features (angle, Finet's ratio, ostial diameter) rather than arclength sequences. Combining RCA, LAD, LCx, and LM into one model should use a shared-weight encoder with a vessel-identity signal and attention-based fusion that tolerates a missing or truncated LM, evaluated with patient-level, not vessel-level, cross-validation, because treating three or four correlated vessels from one patient as independent training instances is a documented way to manufacture a roughly 80% false-positive rate against a nominal 5% alpha. Most of what follows is either genuinely new literature (LAD, LCx, LM feasibility) or reasoned extrapolation from adjacent fields (clustered-data statistics, multi-lead ECG, other vascular trees), since no published study has run this exact multi-vessel, multi-channel, per-point design; every place that line is crossed is flagged explicitly below.

## LAD's branch anatomy is denser, but its evidence base is just as proximally concentrated

The LAD is the anatomically richest of the three peripheral vessels, giving off both diagonal branches and septal perforators along its length, in contrast to the RCA's sparse, mostly terminal branch pattern ([Wikipedia: LAD anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_anterior_descending_artery)). That richness does not translate into a dense evidence base for bifurcation angle, however: a systematic review of 13 studies on left coronary bifurcation angle and CAD, and the LAD-specific stenosis literature, measure angle almost exclusively at the LAD-LCx bifurcation and, secondarily, the LM-LAD bifurcation, never at individual diagonal takeoffs ([PMC9457427](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9457427/)). One CT study did combine a proximal-LAD tortuosity measure (d20, the chord length across a 20 mm curved segment) with the cosine of the LM-LAD angle into a single product term, reaching OR 11.36 and AUC 0.767 for severe proximal LAD stenosis in a 133-patient cohort with 26 events, but this is again a single proximal composite, not a running series of per-diagonal angles ([QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/117032/html)). The practical implication is that the LAD's verdict on bifurcation angle matches the RCA's, encode it as one or two scalar covariates near the vessel origin, even though the underlying anatomical argument for why (branch sparsity along most of the vessel) differs from vessel to vessel. Diameter fares better: normal-anatomy references put the LAD at roughly 3.7 ± 0.4 mm proximally tapering to 1.9 ± 0.4 mm distally, a taper of about 49% ([Circulation, lumen diameter reference](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)), and general (not LAD-exclusive) CT evidence shows absolute lumen diameter and area outperform percent-stenosis for predicting functionally significant disease ([PMC5052288](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5052288/)). The one LAD-specific wrinkle a per-point diameter channel must handle is a physiological step-down in caliber at each diagonal takeoff, documented indirectly through quantitative coronary angiography convention, which measures diameter separately on the proximal and distal side of every bifurcation because a real narrowing is expected there ([bifurcation QCA methodology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1936879812007947)). Without branch-location context, a raw per-point diameter feature would read these normal step-downs as focal stenosis; this confound is specific to the LAD's higher branch density and was not flagged for the RCA. On torsion, the LAD is measurably different in magnitude from the RCA, a comparative angiographic study of 67 normal arteries found LAD mean torsion of 3.73 cm⁻¹ against the RCA's 2.84 cm⁻¹ (P<0.0001), rising further in the distal LAD segment ([PMC2759354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)), but this is a normal-anatomy cataloguing study, not a disease-outcome study, and no CAD-association evidence for LAD torsion specifically was found anywhere in this research pass. The higher baseline signal is a mild feasibility point in torsion's favor (more true signal against a presumably similar noise floor) but it does not answer the question the RCA report already asked and could not answer for either vessel: whether torsion carries independent CAD information at all. The verdict for the LAD therefore mirrors the RCA's on all three channels, add diameter with a branch-location safeguard, scalarize angle at the LAD-LCx and LM-LAD landmarks, defer torsion, with the diameter-confound caveat as the one genuinely LAD-specific addition.

## The LCx carries the most tortuosity signal and the most contradictory outcome evidence

The LCx sits anatomically between the RCA and LAD in branch density, typically one to three obtuse marginal branches plus variable atrial and posterolateral branches ([LCx anatomy summary](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/circumflex-artery)), but its outcome literature diverges from both other vessels in a way that matters more than branch count. Two independent invasive-angiography cohorts report the LCx as the single most tortuous major coronary vessel, yet they disagree directly on what that tortuosity means: a 737-patient cohort found LCx segments meeting a "significant coronary tortuosity" definition had *lower* rates of ≥50% stenosis than non-tortuous LCx segments (17.7% vs 31.1%, P=0.001) and lower Gensini scores ([PMC6335987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6335987/)), while a separate 1,010-patient cohort found no significant association between LCx tortuosity and LCx atherosclerosis at all, in contrast to the LAD, where tortuosity was negatively associated with disease in the same study ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024232)). This is a materially different evidentiary situation than the RCA's clean absence of evidence: for the LCx there is present, but contradictory, data, which argues against using raw curvature or tortuosity magnitude as a stand-alone predictive channel even though it is anatomically the most information-dense vessel for that signal. Compounding this, because the LCx is consistently the most curved of the three vessels across all cohorts examined, a shared curvature channel across the whole tree risks functioning partly as an implicit vessel-identity indicator (curvature magnitude alone can distinguish LCx from RCA) rather than a disease marker, a risk this research specifically flags as more acute for the LCx than for the RCA. Diameter evidence for the LCx is present but more dispersed than the RCA's dedicated FFR-CT vessel-morphology study: normal proximal LCx diameter runs 3.4 to 4.2 mm ([Circulation reference](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.CIR.86.1.232)), and multi-vessel CT-FFR cohorts report LCx-specific correlations between vessel morphology and functional flow, though the specific correlation coefficients could not be traced to a single primary source with full confidence and should be re-verified before being treated as load-bearing. A bicentric outcome study on 432 patients also found that most anatomically intermediate LCx/obtuse-marginal lesions are physiologically insignificant, arguing for caution in reading anatomic diameter alone as a severity proxy on this vessel without an accompanying flow signal. Bifurcation angle on the LCx is genuinely more anatomically available than on the RCA, given the LCx's own branch points, but no dedicated LCx angle-outcome study parallel to the LAD-LCx or LM-LAD literature was found; angle should therefore stay a scalar feature here too, by default rather than by direct evidence. A distinct, LCx-specific confound also needs recording: coronary dominance affects LCx profile length itself, not just terminal connectivity as with the RCA, since left-dominant and codominant hearts extend the LCx to and past the crux, adding an entire distal segment (and its branches) that right-dominant LCx profiles never have ([dominance/crux morphology study](https://healthcare-bulletin.co.uk/article/morphological-study-of-the-coronary-artery-dominance-pattern-in-adult-human-hearts-4758/)). Because right dominance is the majority pattern (roughly 70% in most estimates), most LCx profiles will be systematically shorter than a left-dominant minority's, and dominance needs to enter the model as a covariate for the LCx specifically, not only for the RCA. Taken together, the LCx's channel verdict differs from the RCA and LAD only in degree, diameter is weaker evidence, angle stays scalar, torsion stays deferred, but the contradictory tortuosity-outcome literature and the vessel-identity-confound risk are LCx-specific cautions that do not appear on the other two vessels.

## Left main is too short for a profile; represent it as five scalars

The left main resolves the "still undecided" question decisively against a per-point profile. Population data put mean LM length at 10.5 ± 5.3 mm, with a clinically established short-trunk category at 5 mm or less and documented cases as short as 2 mm ([EuroIntervention computational atlas](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy); [LM length reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/920579/)). At this thesis's own 0.5 mm isotropic resampling resolution, a typical LM yields on the order of 15 to 25 centerline samples end to end, and a short-trunk case collapses to roughly 10 or fewer, which is this report's own arithmetic from the cited length statistics and the pipeline's known resolution, not a value reported in any source. That estimate alone would argue against a profile, but the stronger argument is that the clinical literature studying the LM has already made this same choice: no source located in this research computes curvature or torsion confined to the isolated LM trunk. Every geometric measurement that touches the LM either spans a combined LM-LAD or LM-LCx path, as in a CFD study that found LM-LAD tortuosity strongly predicted low wall shear stress downstream in the LAD (R² = 0.719, p = 0.008) using a whole-segment path-length ratio rather than an LM-only curvature series ([PMC7574594](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7574594/)), or measures curvature on the daughter branches just past the bifurcation rather than on the LM itself ([LM haemodynamics study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-73490-w)). This is strong indirect evidence, independent of the pure length argument, that the biomedical field itself treats isolated LM tortuosity as ill-posed. What the LM literature has instead converged on is a small set of well-characterized scalar and bifurcation-level features. The LAD-LCx angle at the LM bifurcation is one of the more studied coronary geometric biomarkers, with a 106-patient dual-source CT study reporting an optimal 78-degree cutoff for significant left coronary stenosis (AUC 0.719, or 0.805 restricted to non-calcified plaque) and independence on multivariate analysis (OR 1.423, P=0.002) ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0174352)), though a separate 313-patient cohort found the same angle's association weakened to non-significance (P=0.064) after adjusting for confounders like sex and obesity ([PMC5431433](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5431433/)), so this angle should be reported as a real but not clearly independent predictor, not a settled one. Finet's ratio, the daughter-to-parent diameter ratio from fractal branching laws, is the best-supported non-angle scalar: it significantly correlates with low endothelial shear stress in a 127-patient CFD study and is reported as the lowest-variance of the panel of morphology parameters tested there, meaning small deviations from the expected ratio may carry outsized hemodynamic meaning ([Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-73490-w); [fractal bifurcation laws](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12145935)). The concrete recommendation is therefore to represent the LM, if included at all, as roughly five scalar features fused alongside age and sex at the top of the network rather than through the sequence encoder: the LAD-LCx bifurcation angle, Finet's ratio (or the raw daughter-diameter sum against the Finet-predicted value), ostial LM diameter (normal reference 3.7 mm, IQR 3.2 to 4.1 mm), a distal LM diameter just before the bifurcation, and a binary or categorical flag for ramus intermedius presence, since a trifurcating LM has a measurably different baseline angle (89° ± 21° with a ramus vs 75° ± 23° without, P<0.001) and should not be silently averaged in with true bifurcations. This scalar-feature design is well evidenced by the LM literature itself; the specific choice of five particular features and their fusion point in the network is this report's own synthesis, not a cited architectural precedent.

## One shared trunk with vessel identity, fused by attention, split by patient

No published study combines an ordered per-point local geometric profile, multiple named coronary vessels, and a single per-patient clinical label the way this thesis's expanded Model 2 would; the closest analogues only cover one or two of those three ingredients. Segment-level CCTA stenosis and plaque classifiers apply one shared model architecture identically to LAD, LCx, and RCA and report comparable but not identical per-vessel accuracy (0.81, 0.80, 0.79), which is direct evidence that shared weights across vessels are a workable default rather than a compromise, but none of these studies fuses the three vessel scores into one patient-level output ([BMC Medicine](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-025-04478-0)). The one confirmed coronary study that does fuse multiple vessel-level units into a single patient-level prediction, AngioGraphCAD, builds a graph embedding per lesion and combines a variable number of these embeddings via masked attention into one patient-level representation, reaching patient-level AUC 0.70 against per-lesion AUC 0.71 to 0.73 on two validation cohorts ([AngioGraphCAD](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841526001489)). That masked-attention pooling over a variable-length, variable-composition set is the more directly supported precedent for this thesis's architecture than a fixed four-slot concatenation of RCA, LAD, LCx, and LM, because it naturally tolerates a patient whose LM segment is too short to score, or whose LCx or RCA extraction failed, without breaking the input shape. The concrete recommendation is a shared-weight sequence encoder (the same RNN or transformer trunk Model 2 already uses) applied to each vessel's per-point profile, with a vessel-identity embedding concatenated or added at the input so the shared weights can still specialize their behavior by which vessel they are reading, echoing the pattern seen across every other vascular tree examined, airway-tree labeling networks and hepatic/portal-vein segmentation networks both favor a shared backbone with a branch- or position-identity signal over fully independent per-branch networks, though this is convention by analogy from segmentation tasks, not a demonstrated accuracy comparison for a small classifier ([airway structure-position GNN](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841524002111); [hepatic/portal dual-decoder network](https://aapm.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/mp.17090)). The resulting per-vessel embeddings, plus the LM's five scalar features, plus age and sex, then feed the same attention-pooling fusion AngioGraphCAD uses, so a patient missing one vessel's clean extraction simply contributes fewer terms to the pooled sum rather than requiring imputation or a broken fixed-width vector. The sample-size consequence of moving from one vessel to three or four per patient is the single most important statistical correction this expansion requires, and it cuts against the intuitive framing that more vessels means more training data. General clustered-data and repeated-measures statistics are unambiguous that treating multiple correlated observations from one subject as independent instances inflates effective sample size and understates uncertainty; in nested designs structurally identical to this one (multiple correlated measurements from a single subject), ignoring the non-independence can push the true false-positive rate to roughly 80% against a nominal 5% alpha ([Aarts et al., nested-design effective sample size](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~sswang/literature_general_unsorted/aarts_effective_sample_size_NN.pdf)). The closest deep-learning analogue, multi-lead ECG modeling, treats a patient's multiple correlated leads as channels of one instance rather than as separate training examples for exactly this reason, and explicitly flags that letting recordings from the same patient land in both train and test splits lets a model learn patient identity rather than disease signal, inflating apparent performance ([multi-lead ECG deep learning](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/16/17/2834); [leakage-controlled ECG study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14633)). The direct implication for this thesis is that a naive "4 vessels times N patients equals 4N training instances" framing overstates the effective sample size; the honest accounting stays close to N independent patients, with the multiple vessels functioning as additional correlated channels of one instance rather than additional independent labeled examples. Cross-validation, and any train/test split, must therefore be done at the patient level, with every vessel from a given patient held entirely within one fold, never split across folds, and reported sample sizes for power or EPV-style calculations should use the patient count, not the vessel-instance count. No source found addresses this exact scenario, multiple correlated vessel-level geometric profiles per patient feeding one small classifier, directly; this entire section's quantitative caution (the 80% false-positive figure, the ECG leakage analogy) is extrapolated from adjacent domains by structural analogy, not measured for coronary geometry specifically, and should be presented in the thesis as reasoned extrapolation rather than as an established coronary-specific result.

## Conclusion

The whole-tree expansion does not overturn the RCA-level channel verdict, it stress-tests it, and it survives with amendments rather than a rewrite: diameter is worth adding everywhere it can be measured, angle stays scalar everywhere despite looking anatomically dense on paper, and torsion stays out everywhere because the noise-sensitivity argument is vessel-agnostic while the outcome evidence remains at zero for every vessel checked. What genuinely changes with scope is not the per-channel logic but two things layered on top of it: the LCx introduces a live evidentiary conflict (protective versus null tortuosity-outcome findings) that the RCA's clean absence of evidence never posed, and the left main forces a representational break, from a sequence to a handful of scalars, that the RCA/LAD/LCx feasibility work never had to consider. The most consequential decision in this expansion is not which channels to add to any one vessel; it is the architectural and statistical shift from a single-vessel model to a multi-vessel one, where the naive instinct to treat more vessels as more data is precisely backwards, and where patient-level cross-validation is not a refinement but a precondition for any reported number to mean what it claims to mean.
