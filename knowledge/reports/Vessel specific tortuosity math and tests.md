# One curvature definition fits every coronary

**Use one tortuosity definition for every named vessel, and make it mean absolute curvature κ_a. It is computed from chord turning angles at a fixed chord ℓ = 5 mm on a centerline resampled at 0.25 mm and Gaussian smoothed at σ = 1 mm. Report it per vessel against per-vessel reference distributions, never pooled, with vessel length as a covariate.** On the ImageCAS-X reference centerlines no vessel needs its own formula. The same metrics rank the same way in LAD, LCx and RCA under every perturbation tested, and κ_a keeps its within-vessel ranking across the whole smoothing and chord grid. What differs between vessels is how a metric should be read, not how it should be computed. The main change from the earlier report ("Choose arc/chord plus curvature, not turns") concerns whole-vessel arc/chord TI = L/D − 1. That report made TI the vessel-level anchor and the single metric to name for the Danish outcome analysis. On 150 training scans TI is rank-correlated 0.70 to 0.83 with vessel length on LAD, LCx and RCA, and 82 % to 87 % of its variance is the vessel's large-scale course over the heart. On the RCA it mostly encodes dominance: median TI is 0.29 in left-dominant hearts and 0.81 in right-dominant ones. It loses 29 % to 32 % of its value when 20 mm is cut from the distal end. TI therefore moves to a secondary, length-adjusted "course" variable, and κ_a takes the primary role. The earlier report had named exactly this reversal as its own fallback condition. Q is dropped outright, not demoted. Every empirical number here comes from scripts that live in a session scratchpad under `/tmp`. **Those scripts must be copied into `tortuosity/` with their parameters and rerun before any number enters the thesis.** The selection thresholds must also be committed, with a date, before the rerun.

Notation used below: L is smoothed arc length, D is the end-to-end chord, TI = L/D − 1, κ_a is the mean chord-angle curvature at chord ℓ, κ_r is the RMS curvature, Q = (κ_r/κ_a)², K = κ_a·L is total turning, and TI(Δ) is the mean arc/chord over all sliding windows of arc length Δ. The real-data populations are stated where they are used. "P150" is the first 150 ids of `filelist/train.txt`: LAD, LCx and RCA 150 each, D1 126, OM1 104 and PDA 129 after a 20 mm length floor, with short and absent vessels kept as NaN rows. "P100" is 100 training ids drawn with `numpy.random.default_rng(0)`, giving 300 main vessels with none dropped. No validation or test scan was opened, and the Disease column was never read.

## The mathematics splits tortuosity into two frequency bands

Arc/chord and curvature are two moments of the same object, which explains why they disagree. Let T̄ be the mean unit tangent. Then D = L|T̄|, and the tangent variance V equals 1 − |T̄|² exactly, so **L/D = (1 − V)^(−1/2) and TI ≈ V/2**. This identity was checked to a relative error of 1e-5 on 300 random 3D curves ([math t1][m-t1]). Expanding the tangent deviation in modes shows that TI is half the zeroth moment of the tangent power spectrum, while κ_r² is its second moment. At fixed κ_r, TI grows with the square of the bend wavelength. **TI is therefore dominated by long, gentle bends and κ_r by short, tight ones.** They are tied by sharp inequalities (TI ≤ sec(K/2) − 1, and a Poincaré bound V ≤ L²κ_r²/π²) but by no identity. At fixed total turning, TI differs by a factor of about 3 between a smooth C-bend and a kink ([math t1][m-t1]). A second consequence decides the length question. Under a stationary random-curve model, whole-vessel TI is extensive: its mean rose from 0.095 at 20 mm to 1.33 at 130 mm. κ_a (0.0873 to 0.0874 mm⁻¹) and the sliding-window TI(10 mm) (0.0294 to 0.0295) stayed flat, and dividing TI by L did not fix it ([math t6][m-t6]). The polymer-physics reason is the Kratky-Porod law ⟨D²⟩ = 2PL − 2P²(1 − e^(−L/P)), with persistence length P. It makes TI grow roughly as L/(6P) when L is below P. The derivation does not depend on anatomy, which is why the empirical length confound below should have been expected.

Noise has closed-form laws, and they set the scale. With independent (iid) isotropic jitter σ_n, the turning angle at chord ℓ is Rayleigh-distributed with scale √6·σ_n/ℓ. This gives a κ_a floor of **√(3π)·σ_n/ℓ²** and an additive κ_r² bias of exactly **12σ_n²/ℓ⁴**; Monte Carlo matched both to the fourth decimal ([math t2][m-t2]). The raw polyline inflates length by 2σ_n²/h² at point spacing h. Chord discretisation deflates it by κ_r²ℓ²/24, and Gaussian smoothing of width w deflates it by about (w²/2)κ_r². Only the second-moment bias can be subtracted exactly. First-moment corrections and naive Richardson extrapolation both fail on curved vessels ([math t4][m-t4]). Torsion is worse conditioned by one power of ℓ and one of 1/κ. Its standard deviation scales as σ_n/(κℓ³), and Bullitt's SOAM reads about 2.3 rad per vertex on a straight noisy line whatever the noise level ([math t5][m-t5]). That settles the earlier decision to drop torsion. Four scale rules can be written down: a noise floor below 10 % of the signal, cancellation of the length biases, a mean-squared-error-optimal smoothing width, and resolution of the tightest bend (R = 3 mm, 90°, about 2.4 mm). With coronary values they all land between about 2 and 5 mm. That band supports the 5 mm chord, and supports 3 mm if the centerline noise is small ([math note][n-math]).

How noisy the delivered centerlines are is not settled, and two estimates disagree. The median raw step angle of about 5.8° at 0.45 mm spacing implies iid jitter of only about 0.016 to 0.02 mm; iid jitter of 0.06 mm would give median turns of about 22° ([math t2][m-t2]; [P150 jitter test][pv-jit]). On P100, the median distance from each point to a 1 mm-smoothed curve is 0.054 to 0.062 mm, and raw turning density is 3.6 to 4.1 times κ_a at 5 mm ([P100 analysis][rp-an]). The two readings can both hold only if much of the 0.06 mm residual is smoothing offset (about κw²/2) or spatially correlated error, which does not show up at the scale of one sampling step. Either way, the iid floor for κ_a at ℓ = 5 mm and σ = 1 mm is 2 % to 4 % of the real medians ([P150 noise floor][pv-noise]; [P100 synthetic floor][rp-an]). **White noise is not the risk at 5 mm. Correlated error from segmentation and skeletonisation is, because at these scales it looks like signal.** The math note gives the diagnostic: under white jitter, the variance of the perpendicular second difference is flat across lags 1 to 4, and a rise before the m⁴ signal regime shows correlated error. This has not yet been run on the real centerlines.

## Arc/chord measures course and extent on the trunks

Per vessel, the candidate metrics separate most on TI and least on curvature. At σ = 1 mm and ℓ = 5 mm on P150, median TI is **0.77 for the RCA** against 0.32 (LAD) and 0.29 (LCx), and 0.10 to 0.21 on D1, OM1 and PDA. Median κ_a sits in a narrow band, **0.047 to 0.084 mm⁻¹**, across all six vessels ([P150 analysis, section A][pv-an]). The anatomy literature predicts the TI gap. In 2D angiography, whole-vessel arc/chord has a median of about 2.0 for the RCA against about 1.3 for LAD and LCx, and the authors attribute the RCA value to its C-shaped course ([Zebić Mihić 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/)). So raw whole-vessel L/D can never be pooled across vessel types. The single pooled "main-vessel L/D median 1.45" in the earlier report hides a factor of 2.5 between RCA and LAD, and should not be reported.

The main finding is the length confound. Within each vessel, the table below gives Spearman ρ with smoothed length on P150; an asterisk marks p < 0.01. Whole-vessel TI tracks length on the three trunks, and κ_a does not.

A detrending decomposition shows what TI is picking up. Write 1 + TI = (L/L_b)(L_b/D), where L_b is the length of the same centerline smoothed at σ = 10 mm, a heart-following baseline. **The baseline factor carries 82 % to 87 % of the variance of log(1 + TI) on LAD, LCx and RCA, but only 26 % to 34 % on D1, OM1 and PDA** ([P150 analysis, section F2][pv-an]). On the RCA the chord is almost unrelated to length (ρ = 0.20), because a longer RCA curls round the crux back toward its ostium. The dominance split confirms this directly, although it rests on only 8 left-dominant and 3 codominant cases. Median RCA length is 67 mm in left-dominant against 107 mm in right-dominant hearts, and median RCA TI is 0.29 against 0.81 (p = 0.00033). κ_a does not differ (0.055 against 0.046, p = 0.31) ([P150 dominance][pv-dom]). The extreme cases agree. The lowest-TI RCA (case 266, 52 mm) never reaches the crux, and the highest (case 241, TI 2.53) is a full C-loop that the 10 mm baseline follows closely ([P150 extremes][pv-an]). On the trunks, **whole-vessel TI is a heart-geometry and dominance variable, not a local tortuosity measure.** In an outcome model it would bring in exactly the confounders (heart size, dominance, how far the segmentation reaches) that the thesis must adjust away.

Truncation adds a second failure. Cutting 20 mm from the distal end of P100 vessels changes TI by −32 %, −31 % and −29 % (LAD, LCx, RCA), and its ICC(2,1) falls to **0.60 [0.50, 0.69] on the LAD**, 0.81 on the LCx and 0.83 on the RCA. κ_a at 5 mm moves by −6 %, −3 % and +8 %, with ICC 0.95, 0.88 and 0.93 ([P100 extended analysis, sections 1 and 2][rp-ext]). ICC(3,1) for TI after the cut is 0.83 to 0.95, so most of the loss is systematic shortening rather than reordering. That does not help the comparison that matters: CAS-Net predictions on CGPS will end wherever segmentation reach ends, not where the reference centerline ends. TI stays the most noise-robust metric. Its ICC is 0.98 or higher under 0.5 mm correlated jitter, and its ranking is stable to ρ ≥ 0.985 across smoothing ([P100 analysis][rp-an]; [P150 section B3][pv-an]). The earlier report ranked TI first on noise and never tested truncation. **TI is first on every jitter test and last on every truncation test**, and on these data truncation, not jitter, is the realistic failure.

## Curvature survives every test that arc/chord fails

κ_a passes the tests the earlier report set, with two qualifications. On synthetic curves with known truth, it is monotone in amplitude, wavelength, helix pitch and bend radius at the measured noise, as are TI, κ_r, K and TI(Δ) ([P100 synthetic suite][rp-an]). Its biases are structural rather than noise-driven. The first is aliasing: at ℓ = 5 mm, κ_a falls by 98 % on a 10 mm-wavelength sinusoid, because the chord lands on the same phase every step, and by about 20 % at 15 mm. The second is end effects on short curves with isolated bends, which add 13 % to 32 %. Q and bend counts fail monotonicity in most settings, **including noise-free ones**, which reverses the earlier report's single noise-free check that Q was monotone ([P100 synthetic suite][rp-an]). The earlier report's "κ_a turns over past amplitude ratio 0.2" is also clarified: the analytic truth itself turns over (0.1361 at A/λ 0.2, 0.1338 at 0.3) because L grows faster than K. That is a property of the definition, not a failure of the estimator ([P100 synthetic log][rp-synlog]).

On real centerlines, every metric except bend counts has ICC ≥ 0.99 at the measured iid jitter and under resampling. Measured noise therefore does not separate the candidates, and the harder perturbations do ([P100 analysis][rp-an]). ICC(2,1) at σ = 1 mm, P100, for LAD/LCx/RCA:

κ_a also has the population properties that fairness requires. It is nearly free of length. Its ranking is stable (ρ ≥ 0.97 across σ = 0.5 to 2 mm at fixed ℓ, and ρ ≥ 0.90 across the whole grid except D1 at σ = 0.5, ℓ = 2 at 0.83). It is coherent within a patient, with cross-vessel ρ up to 0.60 (LAD against OM1), while vessel lengths are not (|ρ| ≤ 0.26) ([P150 analysis, sections B2 and G][pv-an]; [P150 cross-vessel][pv-cross]; [P100 extended analysis, section 4][rp-ext]). That coherence fits tortuosity as a patient-level phenotype. It also fits a scan-level factor such as motion or reconstruction, which these data cannot separate. The alternatives either collapse into κ_a or add nothing. Detrended residual length (Rlen at σ = 10 mm) correlates with κ_a at ρ = 0.93 to 0.96. Sliding-window TI(Δ) at 5 to 20 mm correlates at 0.90 to 0.98, K/L at 0.99 to 1.00, and κ_r at 0.97 to 0.99 ([P150 section G][pv-an]; [P100 redundancy][rp-extra]). The noise-floor correction κ_a,corr changes κ_a by 0.2 % at the measured noise ([P100 analysis][rp-an]). Q behaves like measurement noise throughout. Its median is 1.28 to 1.43 on real vessels, against 1.24 to 1.35 on pure jittered lines (4/π ≈ 1.27); its IQR/median is 0.13; and it has no within-patient coherence (|ρ| ≤ 0.16) ([P150 noise floor][pv-noise]; [P150 cross-vessel][pv-cross]).

Two qualifications apply to the recommendation. **κ_a at σ = 1 mm fails a 0.90 gate under 0.5 mm error correlated over 1 mm** (point ICC 0.87/0.76/0.71). It reaches a lower 95 % bound of 0.90 only at σ = 3 mm on LAD and RCA, and not at all on the LCx up to σ = 4 mm ([P100 extended analysis, section 7][rp-ext]). Whether that perturbation is realistic depends on the extractor-to-extractor error spectrum, which nobody has measured yet. The second qualification is that the absolute value of κ_a is defined only at a stated (σ, ℓ). Medians halve between ℓ = 2 and 8 mm (LAD 0.099 to 0.041 mm⁻¹), so κ_a at 5 mm and κ_a at 8 mm are different quantities ([P150 section B][pv-an]). Published curvature values also do not carry over between studies. Biplane end-diastolic mean curvature in normal LAD and RCA is 0.050 to 0.066 mm⁻¹ ([Zhu 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759354/)), while pooled CCTA Menger curvature at 5 mm spacing is 0.081 mm⁻¹ ([van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)).

## Vessels differ in interpretation, not in formula

The anatomy literature makes the strongest case for vessel-specific handling, and it points to two classes, not one method per vessel. The atrioventricular-groove vessels (RCA, LCx) wrap round the base of the heart. Free-wall vessels (LAD, diagonals, obtuse marginals, PDA) run over contracting myocardium. In 3D angiography, groove vessels showed no curvature change from end-diastole to end-systole, while free-wall vessels increased mean curvature by 38 % and had about six times as many systolic flexion points. This rests on 21 arteries and the abstract only ([Liao 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ccd.10106)). Human graders seem to discount the RCA's expected C-curve. Under a "three or more bends of 45° or more" rule, prevalence was 26.9 % in the LCx, 21.1 % in the LAD and 1.4 % in the RCA ([Li 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164184/)), even though the RCA has the highest arc/chord. That implies raters already apply a per-vessel baseline, and an automated method should apply it explicitly. The reproducibility data point the same way. A rule of the form "smallest σ whose ICC lower bound is ≥ 0.90" gives vessel-specific answers only under the harsh 0.5 mm correlated jitter. Those answers are unstable under bootstrap: the LCx choice splits 0.29/0.47/0.19 across σ = 2, 3 and 4 mm. At realistic noise, σ = 0 to 0.5 mm already passes for every vessel ([P100 extended analysis, section 7][rp-ext]). **A vessel-specific scale would itself be a noisy parameter. The data support one pre-declared (σ, ℓ) for all vessels.**

| Vessel | What whole-vessel TI measures | κ_a behaviour | Vessel-specific handling |

The strongest remaining argument for vessel-specific method is the RCA, and it deserves a fair statement. Scale detrending or a surface decomposition could strip out the RCA's imposed C-shape. In the Darboux frame, a curve on a surface has κ² = κ_n² + κ_g², and κ_g, the bending within the surface, is closer to what a grader counts ([Darboux frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darboux_frame)). The anatomy note found no coronary paper that makes this decomposition, which marks a gap a thesis could fill ([anatomy note][n-anat]). Two things argue against making it the primary definition. The baseline scale (10 or 15 mm) has no anatomical anchor, and moving between the two shifts the variance shares by 5 to 10 points. On the side branches the same detrending adds nothing beyond κ_a ([P150 section F2][pv-an]). The RCA is therefore handled with a pre-declared sensitivity analysis, not a separate primary. If the thesis wants a second, large-scale axis, it is whole-vessel TI, treated openly as a course variable: logged, per-vessel standardised, with log L and dominance in the model. Sliding-window TI(20 mm) cannot serve as that axis, because it is 0.94 to 0.97 rank-correlated with κ_a on these vessels ([P100 redundancy][rp-extra]). Note that "TI at 20 mm" means two different things across the notes. The chord-polygon TI(ℓ = 20 mm) in P150 is length-confounded (ρ 0.63 to 0.87), and the sliding window is not.

## A pre-registered protocol makes one definition fair

Fairness here means a selection rule fixed before the values are seen and applied identically to every vessel. It should use only label-free criteria and be confirmed on held-out scans. This is the radiomics pattern: IBSI graded feature reproducibility by the lower 95 % bound of a two-way random, absolute-agreement, single-measure ICC ([IBSI 2020](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/119920642/The_Image_Biomarker_Standardization_Initiative_Standardized_Quantitative_Radiomics_for_High_Throughput_Image_based_Phenotyping.pdf)). Perturbation chains stood in for test-retest scans where none existed ([Zwanenburg 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06719)). Discriminability chooses a pipeline without outcome labels and bounds downstream accuracy ([Bridgeford 2021](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009279)). Three refinements follow from the data. First, compute ICC **within each named vessel**. Pooled ICC counts systematic RCA against LAD differences as signal, which inflates it. Second, "maximise ICC" is a degenerate rule: ICC rises monotonically with σ and ℓ under every jitter family, so the rule always picks the most smoothing on the grid while bias grows. At σ = 2 instead of 1, TI on 8 to 20 mm-wavelength sinusoids loses 28 % to 66 % ([P100 extended analysis, sections 3 and 4][rp-ext]). The rule needs an opposing cap: a synthetic bias tolerance fixed in advance, for example |bias| ≤ 10 % on the λ = 20 mm and bend-radius phantoms, which limits σ to about 1 mm for ℓ = 3 to 5 mm. Third, length should not be removed by construction. Ratio indexing can create spurious associations, and the component belongs in the model as a covariate ([Kronmal 1993](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/2983064)). Tortuosity is not stationary along the vessel: a distal cut lowers κ_a on LAD and LCx and raises it on the RCA, so no normalisation makes a truncated vessel equal a full one.

For population statistics, κ_a should enter a mixed model with vessel as a fixed factor, a tortuosity-by-vessel interaction, the standard covariates (age, sex, hypertension, a size proxy such as total tree length) and patient as a random effect. The global test of the tortuosity terms is then one pre-specified primary test, which handles multiplicity. Descriptive per-vessel z-scores should use a log or rank-inverse-normal transform fixed on training data, because TI has skew of 3 to 7 under the random-curve model and log TI is nearly symmetric ([math t6][m-t6]; [fairness note][n-fair]). Criterion 6 in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md` ("low correlation with length") should be split. Image Quality is pure nuisance, and |ρ| ≤ 0.16 for every metric and vessel on P150 ([P150 dominance and IQ][pv-dom]). Length may carry real biology, so what must be bounded is the estimator's truncation bias, not the observed correlation. Criterion 8 (women higher in healthy CGPS) should become a post-lock confirmatory check, because sex is an outcome-model covariate.

## Conclusion

The question "does each vessel need its own method?" is best answered by separating the estimator from the estimand. The estimator (chord angles on a smoothed curve at declared scales) behaves the same on every vessel, and changing it per vessel would add a noisy tuning parameter while making cross-vessel models impossible. The estimand, what a given number means anatomically, does differ. The same arc/chord value is dominance on the RCA, apical wrap on the LAD, and genuine meander on a diagonal. That is why the shared definition should be the one whose meaning changes least across vessels, which on these data is κ_a. The vessel-specific work then moves into reference distributions, covariates and pre-declared sensitivity analyses, where it can be audited.

Three things now matter more than choosing further metrics. The first is the extractor-to-extractor error spectrum, measured on the 160 test scans once CAS-Net predictions and a centerline extractor exist. It alone decides whether ℓ = 5 or 8 mm and σ = 1 or 2 mm, because correlated error at about 1 mm scale is the one perturbation κ_a fails. The second is reproducibility in the thesis's own sense: copy the scripts from the session scratchpad (`pervessel/`, `repro/` and `math/` under `/tmp/claude-324426/.../95342cdc-.../scratchpad/`) into `tortuosity/`, commit the thresholds and bias cap with a date, and rerun. None of the gates used above was fixed before its numbers were computed. The third follows from the second: confirm on training scans outside both samples used here.
