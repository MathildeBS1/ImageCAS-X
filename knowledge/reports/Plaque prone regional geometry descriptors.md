# Anchor the tree, then measure bifurcations

**The thesis should measure only a few robust, dimensionless descriptors at four anatomically anchored places: the left main bifurcation, the proximal 2 to 17 mm of the LAD, LCx and RCA, the LAD to first diagonal and LCx to first obtuse marginal take-offs, and the crux. What matters is carrying them on a correspondence-consistent node graph, not assembling a large feature set.** At the bifurcations the set is the daughter-to-daughter angle (fixed tangent definition), the Finet ratio (at the left main only) and the daughter diameter ratio. Along the proximal segments it is chord curvature at 5 mm, preferably as the dimensionless curvature ratio κ·r, and radius handled as a ratio or with a size covariate. Every node also carries normalised arc length from the ostium. The literature supports this choice on two counts. First plaque in plaque-free vessels goes mostly to the proximal LAD and to the lateral walls of bifurcations. Low wall shear stress (WSS) is the best longitudinal predictor, and the only geometry-to-WSS correlation in human trees that survived multiple-comparison correction was diameter, not curvature or torsion. Our own tests on 100 ImageCAS-X training scans rule out torsion, the Murray exponent, pointwise taper and Frenet curvature, which are too unstable to carry. They also show that the bifurcation angle's value depends on the tangent definition (up to 19 to 27° within a case), so the definition must be fixed in advance. The advanced mathematics that earns its place is elastic (SRVF) registration of each named vessel to a Karcher template. It supplies the anatomical correspondence the supervisors ask for, as an interpretable warp, and turns each vessel into a fixed-length token sequence. Persistent homology, tree-space metrics and LDDMM remain whole-tree complements or evaluation tools. The binding constraint for the Herlev-Østerbro cohort is not geometry. CAS-Net predictions carry no artery names, so a labelling step comes before every regional descriptor, and it is the first thing to build and validate. Much of the literature below was read only at abstract or snippet level, and every ImageCAS-X number comes from scratch scripts. Both must be cleared before any of it bears weight in the thesis.

Evidence tags carried from the notes: **[FT]** full text read, **[ABS]** abstract or landing page only, **[SNIP]** search-engine summary only, **[PK]** standard result from prior knowledge not re-fetched, **[derivation]** worked out in the notes, **[PROTO]** our own run on ImageCAS-X. Unless stated otherwise, ImageCAS-X numbers are from 100 training scans, drawn as described in the [regional feasibility note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors/regional_feasibility.md). No validation or test scan and no Disease column was opened.

## First plaque lands in the proximal LAD and on bifurcation side walls

Where plaque starts sets which regions deserve a descriptor, and it has the strongest support of anything in the notes. The most relevant cohort is PARADIGM, serial CCTA in 1,343 patients over a median 3.3 years. **Of the 334 patients with no plaque at baseline, 35 % developed new lesions, and in 60 % of them the new lesion was in the LAD.** Patients who already had plaque developed new lesions most often in the RCA (47 %). New lesions were small (median volume 21.8 mm³) and rarely obstructive (0.9 %). The abstract reports no geometric predictors, only baseline plaque, diabetes and BMI [ABS] ([PARADIGM new plaque, EHJ CVI 2026 supplement](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)). The angiographic culprit literature agrees on position. In 208 STEMI patients, occlusions clustered in the proximal third, and occlusion risk fell by 13 % (RCA), 30 % (LAD) and 26 % (LCx) per 10 mm from the ostium [SNIP] ([Wang et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15249505/)). That population is symptomatic and the endpoint is occlusion, not onset. At bifurcations, one multislice CT study put more than 90 % of left main plaques opposite the flow divider, with the carina spared [SNIP] ([PubMed 17028928](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17028928/)), and a separate CCTA series classed 51.3 % of plaques as bifurcation lesions [SNIP] ([Eur Radiol 2008](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00330-008-1223-3)).

Myocardial bridging is the one location claim that the full-text evidence weakens. The tunnelled segment is spared: in 36 patients on 256-slice CCTA, no bridged segment carried plaque [FT] ([PMC10870693](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870693/)). The textbook claim that the segment just proximal to a bridge is worse is contested, however. In the same study, ESS proximal to the bridge (1.2 Pa) did not differ from ESS inside it (0.95 Pa, p = 0.17). An angiographic study of 76 bridge patients and 109 matched controls found *less* proximal and mid LAD atherosclerosis in bridge patients [FT] ([PMC9026775](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9026775/)). Bridging also cannot be detected from a coronary centerline, because its criteria are depth in myocardium and tunnel length [ABS] ([PMC5116748](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5116748/)). It is out of scope unless a whole-heart segmentation is added.

Taken together, **two variables encode most of the known "where": arc length from the ostium, and membership of a bifurcation neighbourhood.** Both are exactly what the supervisors proposed as node features ("arc-length from root", depth) ([from_superviser.tex](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/from_superviser.tex)). The honest gap is that no opened source gives SCCT segment-level frequencies of *first* plaque in initially plaque-free vessels. The PARADIGM "how early" paper likely does ([JCCT 2023](https://www.journalofcardiovascularct.com/article/S1934-5925(23)00426-4/fulltext), 403 on fetch), and it is the first item to read in full.

## Low shear stress drives onset, and radius is its strongest geometric proxy

The serial studies point to haemodynamics and plaque burden, not raw geometry. In PREDICTION, 506 ACS patients had serial IVUS with CFD. Large plaque burden and low local endothelial shear stress independently predicted plaque growth and lumen narrowing over 6 to 10 months [SNIP] ([Stone et al. 2012](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circulationaha.112.096438)). Samady's 20 patients (2,249 segments) separated two mechanisms. Low-WSS segments grew plaque with constrictive remodelling. High-WSS segments showed necrotic core and calcium progression with expansive remodelling [SNIP] ([Samady et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21788584)). High WSS and axial plaque stress matter only once a lesion exists, as in EMERALD's culprit lesions [SNIP] ([Lee et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29550316/)). **At a plaque-free baseline the relevant target is the low and oscillatory WSS mechanism.** The geometric descriptors worth measuring are therefore the ones that plausibly proxy for it: bifurcation angle, daughter-to-parent calibre, local curvature and local expansion.

Human evidence on which of those proxies works is thin, and it points somewhere unexpected. In 39 ASOCA left trees with CFD, **diameter was the only geometry-haemodynamics correlate to survive multiple-comparison correction** (Marginal diameter against average TAESS r = −0.85, adjusted p = 0.0046). Curvature and torsion correlations of about 0.5 to 0.65 did not survive. The Marginal curvature correlation with low-TAESS area was *negative* (r = −0.60), consistent with curvature driving helical flow that raises WSS [FT, preprint] ([Shen et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.06161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)). The same 39 trees showed no bifurcation angle or Finet ratio difference between stenosed and non-stenosed bifurcations (p > 0.057). SB diameter (p = 0.041) and DMV torsion (p = 0.024) did differ, but with only 54 bifurcations this is underpowered, not evidence of absence [FT, preprint] ([Zhang et al. 2023, arXiv 2312.00257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00257)). The bifurcation-angle evidence proper is cross-sectional and symptomatic. LAD-LCx angles were about 94° in diseased and 77° in normal left systems [SNIP] ([PLOS One 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0174352)), and plaque can change the measured angle itself. The earlier tortuosity work added one datum: in 127 CAD-free patients, κ_a explained up to R² 0.23 of low TAWSS and arc/chord was not significant (Kashyap 2022, as recorded in the [earlier report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)).

Whole-vessel "tortuosity protects" findings do not contradict local curvature. In 131 angiography patients, severe tortuosity was more common in non-obstructive than obstructive disease (50.6 % against 14.8 %, OR 7.96), but female sex had OR 17.5 and the groups were 61 % and 24 % female [FT] ([Zebić Mihić et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10534717/)). Those studies score gross angiographic bends, are sex-confounded and measure obstruction rather than onset. Bjørn found tortuosity fragile in his own work, and that fits. The earlier report showed that whole-vessel arc/chord mostly encodes length and dominance: Spearman ρ with length 0.70 to 0.83, and RCA median TI 0.29 in left-dominant against 0.81 in right-dominant hearts ([earlier report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)). **The defensible curvature quantity is therefore local, windowed and dimensionless, not whole-vessel.**

The fluid mechanics gives that quantity a name [derivation]. The curvature ratio δ = κ·r is Dean's small parameter. The Dean number De = Re·√δ needs a flow, and the torsion ratio τ·r inherits torsion's third-derivative noise. The Womersley number is about 1.3 to 2.7 at coronary radii and 1 Hz, so the flow is quasi-steady and pulsatility adds little. These groups add no information beyond κ and r. Their value is interpretability and scale invariance, which answers the supervisors' request to normalise distances directly. A second derivation gives a tree-aware proxy that does add information. Propagate flow from the root with the empirical law Q = 1.43 d^2.55, split it at each bifurcation by (d_sb/d_mb)^2.27, and compute a Poiseuille τ̂ = 4μQ/(πr³) normalised to the root value. Low τ̂ then flags segments wider than their inherited flow warrants. The laws are taken from their use in Shen et al. [FT] ([arXiv 2502.06161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)), and their primary source (van der Giessen 2011) is unverified. A node-local Poiseuille proxy, by contrast, is a monotone function of r and adds nothing. τ̂ carries three times the relative radius error, about 50 % at r = 1.5 mm, so it is an exploratory, segment-averaged feature until checked against CFD or a WSS surrogate on a few ImageCAS-X trees.

## ImageCAS-X keeps angles, Finet and 5 mm curvature, and drops the rest

The regional feasibility runs locate every anchor deterministically from the segment labels, using the repo's `topology.graph.build_tree` and `topology.angles`. What limits them is anatomical availability, not measurement noise. **The LM, LAD-D1 and crux anchors exist in 96 to 98 of 100 scans and the RCA ostium in all 100, but LCx-OM1 in only 80,** because a fifth of trees have no OM1 on the LCx ([analysis.txt §A](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/analysis.txt)). That missingness is plausibly anatomical and should be modelled as a variable, not dropped as NaN. A symmetric ±5 mm window around the LM bifurcation was incomplete in 12 of 98 scans. A 5 mm chord curvature had enough support there in only 60 of 98, which is consistent with a normative LM length of 10.5 ± 5.3 mm. LM windows should therefore be one-sided, on the daughter side.

**The bifurcation angle is measurable everywhere, but its value is set by the tangent definition.** At σ = 1 mm, the six tangent definitions differ within a case by a median of 18.7° at the LM and 27.2° at LAD-D1. A 2 mm secant drifts from 98.6° to 63.2° between σ = 0 and 2 mm. The core-skipping fit `fitcore3` (a line over [r_J, r_J + 3] mm, the default in `topology/angles.py`) moves only between 78.0° and 80.8°. It agrees with a 5 mm secant at ICC 0.95 to 0.97 and gives medians of **LM 80.8°, LAD-D1 61.4°, LCx-OM1 67.1° and crux 64.4°**. Under realistic iid jitter (0.1 mm) every angle has ICC ≥ 0.97. Under the 0.25 mm iid stress the LM holds at 0.81 and the side branches fall to 0.58 to 0.69, recovering to 0.78 to 0.92 at σ = 2 mm ([analysis.txt §B](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/analysis.txt)). The LM median sits inside the 75 to 89° band of CCTA cohorts recorded in the repo's own notes (Temov and Sun 2016, Juan 2017, the Medrano-Gracia atlas). The atlas reports angle B of 89 ± 21° with an intermediate artery and 75 ± 23° without [ABS] ([Medrano-Gracia atlas](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/a-computational-atlas-of-normal-coronary-artery-anatomy)). A ramus flag is therefore mandatory, or a 14° anatomical shift will read as a phenotype. Parent-relative deflections are less stable (ICC 0.60 to 0.79 under the stress), so the daughter-to-daughter angle is primary. The chord-error law [derivation] explains why the angle survives. An endpoint error σ over a chord d gives about √2·σ/d of angular error, roughly 4° at d = 5 mm, well below the 21 to 23° between-subject SD. The small inflow angle (6 to 12°) sits near that noise floor.

**Chord curvature at 5 mm carries over from whole vessels to regional windows; torsion does not.** κ_l5 at σ = 1 mm has ICC 0.97 to 0.99 under realistic jitter in every window, 0.70 to 0.91 under the 0.25 mm iid stress, and 0.86 to 0.98 under ±2 mm window shifts. The Frenet finite-difference curvature collapses to ICC 0.06 to 0.21 under the stress. Torsion reads 7 to 10 times κ_l5, has a jitter ICC of 0.24 to 0.41 even at 0.1 mm noise, and its rank order between σ = 0.5 and 2 mm is uncorrelated (Spearman −0.15 to 0.01) ([analysis.txt §C](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/analysis.txt)). One caution remains. A 5 mm chord inside a 10 to 15 mm window leaves only a few independent chords, so regional κ_l5 is close to a single turning measurement. The earlier report also found that κ_a fails a 0.90 gate under 0.5 mm error correlated over 1 mm (point ICC 0.71 to 0.87), and that perturbation is the realistic threat.

**Radius works as ratios, not as exponents.** Mask EDT and surface-distance radii agree at r = 0.96 over 122,665 points, with EDT reading 0.135 mm larger (0.18 mm below 0.75 mm radius). The two methods share the segmentation, so this tests the algorithm, not segmentation error. The **LM Finet ratio is 0.63 (EDT), with method ICC 0.85 and window ICC 0.90**, close to Finet's 0.678, which is still unverified. The ratio weakens at side branches (0.67 to 0.79) and fails at the crux (0.44). The Murray exponent is unsolvable in 12 to 37 % of bifurcations and has method ICC 0.48 to 0.69. Proximal taper over 20 mm is −0.004 mm/mm, less than a voxel over the window, and positive in a third of LAD and LCx cases ([analysis.txt §D](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/analysis.txt)). The noise derivation agrees: pointwise taper error (about 0.035) exceeds the normal taper (about 0.0125) at a 10 mm baseline [derivation]. EDT is the method to fix for both cohorts, because CGPS has only binary lumens. It must never be mixed with surface distance, since the bias is largest in thin vessels and shifts every ratio.

Size confounding sits in the calibres, not the angles or curvatures. Absolute radii correlate at ρ 0.56 to 0.75 with LM radius. Angles correlate at 0.25 to 0.33 with tree or RCA length, and regional curvature, daughter ratios and taper at |ρ| ≤ 0.25 ([analysis.txt §E](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/regional/analysis.txt)). Kit expects the embeddings to separate on heart size, dominance and branch count first, and this is where that expectation would materialise. The fix is ratios or a size covariate (inter-ostial distance or LM radius), not division, because ratio indexing can create spurious associations (Kronmal 1993, cited in the earlier report). Normalising window *placement* by vessel length would tie it to truncation, the dominant failure in the earlier report. Inter-ostial distance varies only about ±7 %, so fixed-mm windows anchored at landmarks are the safer normalisation. Proportional arc length stays a node feature, not a window rule.

## Elastic registration supplies the correspondence the supervisors asked for

Bjørn's central worry is correspondence: the same token position must mean the same anatomy in every patient ([from_superviser.tex](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/from_superviser.tex)). Most of the advanced shape mathematics in the literature exists to solve correspondence when branches are unlabelled. Feragen's QED and TED on airways [ABS] ([arXiv 1207.5371](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5371)), elastic shape graphs on 92 brain arterial networks [ABS] ([Guo et al., arXiv 2007.04793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04793)) and hierarchical elastic trees with branch birth and death [ABS] ([Wang, Laga, Srivastava, arXiv 2110.08693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08693)) all fall in this group, and all are expensive and give whole-tree distances. ImageCAS-X already labels every centerline point, so **the right construction is a labelled elastic tree.** Each named segment is registered by the square-root velocity function (SRVF), q = β′/√|β′|, to a per-segment Karcher template, with a null curve for absent branches [PK] ([Srivastava et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2010.184)). The warp γ is an explicit, interpretable arc-length correspondence. After warping, every vessel is resampled at M matched positions, which is a fixed-length, correspondence-consistent token sequence. Unit-length scaling removes size by construction, so length becomes an explicit covariate.

The prototype supports feasibility, not yet validity. On 30 ImageCAS-X curves the elastic distance cost about 0.11 s per pair. Warping recovered a synthetic reparameterisation (distance 0.385 to 0.055), and iid 0.05 mm jitter moved distances by a median of 0.016. Error of 0.5 mm correlated over 1 mm moved them by 0.163, which is 0.38 of the median same-type inter-subject distance: the same weakness κ_a has. Separation of vessel types improved only marginally over rotation-only alignment (1-NN 0.93 against 0.90) [PROTO] ([out_M100.txt](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/treemath/out_M100.txt)). The value of warping therefore has to be shown on position-specific tasks. The key validation is cheap on ImageCAS-X: register the LAD and LCx and measure how tightly the labelled D1 and OM1 take-off positions cluster in template coordinates. SRVF also assumes both ends correspond, which truncation violates, so partial or open-end matching needs attention before CAS-Net trees are used. The same framework applied to 1D profiles (the square-root slope function) separates phase from amplitude in κ(s) or r(s) [SNIP] ([Tucker et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1791)). It then supports "this LAD bends more at 30 % of its length than the population", and its generative phase and amplitude models are a published route to the anatomically realistic augmentation Bjørn suggested. The only vascular precedent for this pipeline, AneuRisk65, found weak group shape differences on 65 carotids (permutation p = 0.079) [FT] ([Cheng et al. 2014](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-8/issue-2/Analysis-of-AneuRisk65-data-Internal-carotid-artery-shape-analysis/10.1214/14-EJS938B.pdf)). That is a realistic warning about effect sizes. No coronary SRVF study was found.

The frame choice matters for node features. The Frenet frame breaks at inflection points and torsion is a third derivative. The Bishop (parallel-transport) frame gives a curvature vector (k₁, k₂) with κ² = k₁² + k₂², and torsion appears only as the rotation rate of that vector [PK] ([Bishop 1975](https://doi.org/10.2307/2319846)). It is untested on our data, and it is rotation-covariant up to the initial frame, so for now it is an ablation behind scalar κ. Persistent homology is a whole-tree complement, not a node feature. On 98 brain artery trees, the H1 summary of thickened point samples correlated with age at ρ = 0.61, and still at 0.35 after controlling for total length [FT] ([Bendich et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.6652)). On curated ImageCAS-X trees the H0 barcode is just 4 to 7 branch lengths [PROTO] ([out_tmd.txt](file:///tmp/claude-324426/-zhome-e2-6-224426-project-ImageCAS-X/389c400f-e05f-4871-b8aa-6345347e6d71/scratchpad/treemath/out_tmd.txt)), and on CAS-Net output it would be exposed to spurious spurs. Currents and varifolds give a correspondence-free, topology-tolerant distance [SNIP] ([Pan et al. 2016](https://mlanthology.org/cvprw/2016/pan2016cvprw-currentand/)). They are best used to measure agreement between the reference and CAS-Net-extracted trees, the open error-spectrum question, rather than as descriptors. Whole-tree elastic distances with tangent PCA also give an interpretable baseline embedding. **If a latent-query network does not beat tangent PCA of a labelled elastic tree on the same task, the network is not earning its keep on this data size.**

## The recommended set: a lean node graph plus a few region scalars

The supervisors' ordering fixes the structure. A baseline node representation comes first with no major feature engineering, and each hand-crafted descriptor is then added as an ablation. The descriptors should give the model information it cannot trivially extract from the representation ([from_superviser.tex](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/from_superviser.tex)). The tables below apply that ordering. They are this report's synthesis, and every "keep" rests on the ImageCAS-X stability numbers above, not on any outcome association.

**Node features (one node per 1 mm of arc, or per matched SRVF position).** Every node carries all the baseline features. The ablation features are added one at a time.

| Tier | Feature | Definition | Why | Status |
|-|-|-|-|-|
| Baseline | Position | xyz in a heart-anchored frame (origin at the LCA ostium, axis to the RCA ostium), divided by inter-ostial distance | Supervisor baseline; scale removed | Frame choice untested |
| Baseline | Arc length | s from the ostium, both normalised by named-vessel length and absolute in mm | Encodes the proximal gradient of first plaque | Normalised s is truncation-sensitive; keep both |
| Baseline | Radius | r_EDT, smoothed over ≥ 2 mm, one method in both cohorts | Only WSS correlate that survived correction | Size-confounded; pair with a size covariate |
| Baseline | Topology | Depth (generation), L/R side, region label, template position s* from SRVF | Anatomical identity replaces learned positional encoding | Depends on the labeller |
| Ablation 1 | Curvature ratio | δ = κ_l5·r, κ_l5 as a 5 mm chord at σ = 1 mm, averaged over the window | Dimensionless Dean parameter; low-WSS proxy | κ_l5 stable; δ borderline pointwise, use window average |
| Ablation 2 | Bifurcation proximity | Arc distance to the nearest bifurcation divided by parent radius; side flag (main or side daughter) | Lateral wall at bifurcations is the prime onset site | Cheap; untested |
| Ablation 3 | Local expansion | E = r / median(r over ±5 mm) − 1 | Flags ectatic, low-WSS segments | Untested; at the noise edge |
| Exploratory | Tree-propagated shear | τ̂/τ̂_root from Q = 1.43 d^2.55 and a 2.27 split exponent | Adds flow-split information r alone lacks | Unvalidated; 3× radius error |

**Bifurcation tokens (one per named junction, attached as edge or junction features).**

| Feature | Definition | Where | Status |
|-|-|-|-|
| Daughter angle | fitcore3 at σ = 1 mm (σ = 2 mm as sensitivity) | LM, LAD-D1, LCx-OM1, crux | ICC ≥ 0.97 realistic; side branches weaker under stress |
| Finet ratio | r_0 / (r_1 + r_2), median over [r_J, r_J + 3] mm | LM primary; side branches secondary; not crux | LM ICC 0.85 to 0.90; crux 0.44 |
| Daughter ratio | small over large daughter radius | All four | Size-free; ICC 0.63 to 0.86 |
| Ramus flag | Intermediate artery present | LM | Needed to avoid a 14° shift |
| Out-of-plane angle | PMV to the bifurcation plane | LM | Near the noise floor; exploratory |

**Region scalars for the primary interpretable model.** At the LM these are the daughter angle, Finet ratio, daughter ratio and ramus flag, with LM length as a covariate. In each proximal window (2 to 17 mm from the LM bifurcation for the LAD and LCx, from the ostium for the RCA) they are the mean κ_l5 (or δ) and start radius, the latter adjusted for LM radius or inter-ostial distance. The LAD-D1 and LCx-OM1 angles and daughter ratios complete the set, with OM1 absence recorded as its own variable. The crux angle is kept but read as dominance-entangled (ρ 0.33 with RCA length), because 95 of 100 sampled scans are right-dominant. **Dropped:** torsion, the Murray exponent, pointwise taper, cross-section eccentricity (a 3 mm lumen is about 6 voxels across), Frenet finite-difference curvature, Q, whole-vessel arc/chord as a local descriptor, myocardial bridging, and parent-relative deflections as primaries.

If one primary descriptor must be declared, as the tortuosity analysis design already requires, the evidence favours **the LM daughter-to-daughter angle against new plaque in the LM, proximal LAD and proximal LCx regions**. It has the most geometric literature, the highest stability under stress (ICC 0.81 at 0.43 mm RMS iid), little size confounding, and it targets the site where first plaque concentrates. Regional κ_l5 and the LM Finet ratio are the leading secondaries under Holm or FDR control. This choice should be fixed and dated before any CGPS outcome is seen.

## A ten-year cohort needs labels, reliability and a few hundred patients

The statistical design follows PARADIGM's unit: a region that is plaque-free at baseline, with new plaque at follow-up as the outcome [ABS] ([PARADIGM radiomics, JCCT 2024](https://www.journalofcardiovascularct.com/article/S1934-5925(24)00032-7/abstract)). With two scans about ten years apart there is no event time. A logistic or complementary log-log model with log interval as an offset is more honest than per-segment Cox. The model structure is `newplaque ~ z_desc + region_type + age + sex + htn + smoking + ldl + statin_between + interval + scanner + size + dominance + (1 | patient)`. The descriptor is standardised within region type on reference distributions, and a Mundlak patient mean separates the between-patient contrast the design allows from the within-patient contrast it forbids. GEE gives the population-averaged OR as a sensitivity check. A power chain in the notes [derivation] assumes OR 1.3 per SD, a regional incidence of 20 %, ten regions per patient, ρ = 0.1, covariate R² = 0.2 and reliability λ = 0.7. It gives **about 3,450 regions, or 345 patients**. At 10 % incidence the requirement roughly doubles, and five descriptors under Bonferroni multiply it by about 1.7. The notes also give the regression dilution relation: observed log OR is about λ times the true log OR, which inflates the required sample by 1/λ² [ABS] ([Frost and Thompson 2000](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-985X.00164)). **λ must come from short-interval repeats, not the ten-year pairs**, which confound error with change. Candidates are CAS-Net against expert labels on the ImageCAS-X test split, two segmentations of one CGPS scan, or two reconstructed phases.

Correspondence on CGPS is the upstream risk. Learned labellers reach F1 around 0.95 on main branches [ABS] ([CPR-GCN, arXiv 2003.08560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08560)). On a CNN-tracked graph, per-class F1 ranges from 0.90 for the RCA through 0.74 for the LCx and OM to 0.54 for septals [FT] ([PMC11095121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095121/)). The region scheme should therefore be coarse (LM, proximal, mid and distal LAD, proximal and distal LCx, proximal, mid and distal RCA) with side branches pooled. The labeller should be trained on ImageCAS-X's 800 labelled trees with position-only inputs that match CGPS, and its accuracy reported on a manually labelled CGPS subset. A label-free fallback exists: split at the ostia, follow the largest-radius path, and bin by normalised arc length. For the representation-learning branch, the notes recommend pretraining only on ImageCAS-X and unlabelled CGPS baselines, then feeding region-pooled embeddings into the same mixed model as an ablation. This keeps the few hundred outcome patients out of training. CGPS is 9,533 asymptomatic adults at baseline [ABS] ([Fuchs et al. 2023](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M22-3027)). The size of the serial subset, the scanner generations and the follow-up plaque reading protocol are not public and must be confirmed with the supervisors. ComBat-style harmonisation assumes scanner is not confounded with biology [FT] ([Generalized ComBat 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-08412-9)), so scanner enters as a covariate with harmonisation as a sensitivity analysis.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the effort should go. The descriptor list is nearly settled by stability alone: few candidates survive a 0.25 mm stress test, and those that do (bifurcation angle, Finet at the LM, 5 mm chord curvature, radius ratios) are the ones the plaque-location literature points at. The open problems all sit upstream of the descriptors. They are naming CAS-Net branches on unlabelled CGPS trees, the correlated extractor error that no one has measured yet, and a reliability ratio that the ten-year pairs cannot supply. This vindicates Bjørn's instinct: representation and correspondence carry more of the thesis than feature count. Elastic registration is the one piece of advanced mathematics that serves both the interpretable analysis and the learned model, because it turns anatomical correspondence into an explicit, inspectable warp.

The field also leaves a gap the thesis can fill. No serial study in healthy or plaque-free vessels has tested baseline geometry as a predictor of new plaque. The haemodynamic evidence says radius-driven low shear, not bending, is what geometry should proxy, and the only human evidence on local curvature is sign-ambiguous. A pre-registered test of the LM angle and regional κ·r against ten-year new plaque in an asymptomatic population would be a genuine first, whether its answer is positive or null. A null result would be informative too, provided the reliability correction and power are reported alongside it.
