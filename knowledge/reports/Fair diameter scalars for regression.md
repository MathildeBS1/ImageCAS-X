# Match the domain before choosing the diameter scalar

**Your instinct is right in kind but incomplete: the fair diameter scalar is the mean lumen radius, but computed over exactly the same arc-length domain, with the same bifurcation masking, that the network reads, and that domain should be a fixed-length window from the ostium rather than the whole segmented vessel.** Mean radius mirrors mean absolute curvature (one channel, one summary operator), which keeps the logistic regression symmetric so that the gain from adding diameter can be compared between arms. The notes disagree on whether the primary scalar should be the whole-vessel mean (or its area form, lumen volume over length, V/L) for symmetry, or a proximal-window median for robustness to distal truncation. The disagreement dissolves once one sees that truncation and bifurcation spikes are properties of the *domain and masking*, which must be shared by both arms anyway, not of the *summary operator*. Fix the domain for both models first, then take the mean. A single scalar per channel still does not give the regression the information the network has, so the claim "the network beats regression" also needs an information-matched rung (functional logistic regression on the same profile), otherwise a win only shows that reading a profile beats reading a mean, which is a different result. Minimum radius and percent stenosis should not be the primary scalar: they are the least reproducible diameter measures and they hand the regression lesion information that the curvature arm does not get.

## The curvature precedent defines what "fair" has to mean here

The comparison the thesis is copying is Tello Ayala et al., where a transformer reading per-point RCA curvature reached **AUROC 0.67 (0.65 to 0.69)** against **0.60 (0.58 to 0.63)** for logistic regression on the mean tortuosity score, in 38,691 angiograms; that regression used clinical covariates and **no diameter scalar at all** ([PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)). So there is no published template for the diameter term, and whatever you choose is a design decision you must defend. The defensible principle is the one that makes the curvature contrast interpretable: both arms see one channel, and the only difference is representation (mean versus profile). Carrying that principle into the two-channel arm means one summary per channel, same operator, same domain. The payoff is a clean paired contrast: the increment from adding radius in the regression, AUC(curvature + radius) minus AUC(curvature), set against the same increment for the network, both on identical outer folds.

The methodology literature explains why this matters at 800 scans. In a systematic review of 71 clinical prediction studies, machine learning showed **no C-statistic advantage over logistic regression in low-risk-of-bias comparisons**; the apparent advantage appeared only where the comparison was biased ([Christodoulou et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30763612/)). Deep ECG models beat feature baselines clearly on 21,837 records ([Strodthoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13701)), but on classical tasks and smaller datasets engineered features were as good ([Zvuloni et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37022038/)). ImageCAS-X sits in the second regime, so a handicapped regression would visibly inflate the network's margin, and a small or null margin against a strong regression is a legitimate result that should be framed as such in advance.

## Mean radius is the right operator; V/L is its literature twin

Among size scalars on CCTA, length-normalised lumen volume performs best. In 443 patients with non-obstructive disease, RCA lumen volume divided by RCA length (**V/L**) correlated with distal FFR-CT at **r = 0.61**, against r = 0.36 to 0.37 for single proximal or distal diameters, and predicted FFR-CT ≤ 0.80 with **AUROC 0.88 (0.84 to 0.93)**; V/L (beta 0.48) and proximal diameter (beta 0.09) both survived multivariable adjustment, while distal diameter and raw lumen volume did not ([Tsugu et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10873436/)). V/L is simply the mean cross-sectional area along the centerline, that is, roughly pi times the mean of r squared. It is therefore the same "mean along the vessel" idea as your instinct, in area rather than radius units. Averaged measures are also the reproducible ones: in Circle of Willis centerline graphs, segment-averaged radius from predicted graphs matched the reference with **median relative error below 5% and r above 0.95**, while pointwise curvature was the least reliable feature ([Musio et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13720)).

Between mean radius and mean area (V/L), choose **mean radius as primary** because the network's channel is per-point radius, so the regression then summarises exactly the signal the network reads. With continuous scalars modelled by pre-specified restricted cubic splines (3 to 4 knots), the two are near-monotone transforms of each other and should give nearly identical discrimination; report V/L as a named sensitivity analysis so the result connects to Tsugu. Diameter versus radius is irrelevant: a factor of two rescales the coefficient and changes nothing else.

Mind the outcome mismatch when comparing to Tsugu. Their endpoint is FFR-CT, a functional ischemia measure, whereas the ImageCAS-X label is the Disease column in `Descriptors.xlsx`, an anatomical label. Effect sizes will not transfer.

## Resolving the conflict: truncation is a domain problem, so solve it in the domain

The case for the proximal-window median is real. A whole-vessel mean is biased by distal truncation: a shorter segmented vessel drops its thinnest points and raises the mean, so differences in how far CAS-Net and the reference reach distally become radius differences. Bifurcations inflate an inscribed-sphere radius where the sphere expands into the junction, which is why VMTK splits centerlines into branch and bifurcation groups and pipelines drop the bifurcation tract ([VMTK branch splitting](http://www.vmtk.org/tutorials/BranchSplitting.html); [Musio et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13720)). And calcium blooming produces local dips: in a 3 mm phantom lumen a ring 50% stenosis measured **82.5%** on energy-integrating CT ([PMC9171751](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9171751/)).

The case for the whole-vessel mean is also real, but it rests on symmetry with whatever the network and the curvature scalar see. The mistake would be to fix only the diameter scalar. The network reading a variable-length whole-vessel profile suffers the same truncation (it also sees the sequence length, which the mean does not), and mean absolute curvature over the whole vessel depends on endpoint placement in exactly the same way. A proximal 10 to 20 mm median for diameter paired with a whole-vessel mean for curvature and a whole-vessel profile for the network would give the three quantities three different domains. The regression's diameter term would then see less of the vessel than the network sees, which is unfair in the opposite direction, and the two regression scalars would no longer be comparable to each other.

The resolution is therefore sequential. **First, choose one shared domain for all three quantities**: a fixed arc length from the ostium, in mm, chosen outcome-blind (for example the length reached by nearly all reference and CAS-Net RCAs in the training folds), mirroring the TopCoW fallback of fixed-length sub-segments when landmarks are absent ([Musio et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13720)). Normalised arc length (0 to 1) is the wrong choice because every point moves when the distal end moves. **Second, apply the same bifurcation mask to both arms' inputs**, dropping points within roughly one to two local radii of a branch point. The delivered centerlines carry a per-point `branch_points` flag (alongside `segment_label`, `start_points` and `end_points`), so this needs no new topology work. **Third, take the mean** of radius over the unmasked points, and of absolute curvature over the same points. With truncation removed by the window and bifurcation spikes removed by the mask, the remaining gap between mean and median is blooming dips, which the network also sees. Keeping the mean preserves operator symmetry with mean absolute curvature, and the median becomes a pre-registered sensitivity analysis. The proximal-window median survives in a second role: as the robustness check in the reference-versus-CAS-Net agreement study, where its insensitivity to truncation is exactly what is being tested.

This decision should be made before the outcome is inspected. Choosing among candidate scalars by their association with Disease on the full cohort biases the cross-validated estimate optimistically, even on null data ([Varma and Simon 2006](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Bias-in-error-estimation-when-using-for-model-Varma-Simon/bf75f2555e8485d536ac217068b7963de3d6fa02)). Pre-register the primary scalar, domain and mask in a dated commit; if several diameter scalars are ever entered together, use a ridge or elastic-net penalty tuned in the inner loop only.

## Measurement properties decide which scalars are admissible

The per-point radius in this repository is not VMTK's inscribed-sphere radius. Neither the delivered nor the predicted centerlines carry a radius, so [`topology/radius.py`](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/topology/radius.py) measures both the same way: the Euclidean distance from each centerline point to the nearest background voxel, which over-reads by up to half a voxel, identically for reference and prediction. That is an inscribed-sphere definition quantised to the grid, which tracks the minor axis of an elliptical lumen rather than its area, and in a direct comparison inscribed-sphere radii ran **0.21 to 0.24 mm smaller** (median) than circle-equivalent radii ([Musio et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13720)). At distal RCA radii of 1 to 1.5 mm, a constant offset of that size is 15 to 25% of the quantity. A mean over a long window carries this bias as a nearly constant shift that the regression intercept absorbs; single-point scalars carry it with its full local noise.

That is the measurement case against minimum radius and percent stenosis. Across CCTA versus intravascular imaging, CT **underestimated minimum lumen diameter by 21%** and overestimated diameter stenosis by 39% ([JACC Imaging 2011](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jcmg.2011.03.006)). In 210 segments with FFR, inter-method agreement was highest for minimal lumen area (r = 0.63) and lower for minimal lumen diameter (0.56), percent diameter stenosis (0.49) and percent area stenosis (0.40) ([PMC5052288](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5052288/)). There is also a fairness case: a minimum-type scalar gives the regression direct lesion information that the curvature arm lacks, breaking the "one mean per channel" symmetry. If a lesion-sensitive scalar is wanted, a low percentile (5th or 10th) of radius over the same window is the defensible middle ground, reported as a secondary analysis.

| Candidate | Role | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Mean radius over shared fixed-mm window, bifurcations masked | **Primary** | Same channel, operator and domain as mean abs. curvature and the network input |
| Mean area over the same window (V/L analogue) | Sensitivity | Strongest literature scalar (AUROC 0.88, Tsugu); near-monotone in mean radius |
| Median radius over the same window | Sensitivity | Tests whether blooming and residual spikes drive the result |
| Median radius over proximal 10 to 20 mm | Agreement study, sensitivity | Most truncation-robust; used to validate CAS-Net radii |
| 10th-percentile radius | Secondary | Lesion-sensitive but less noisy than the minimum |
| Minimum radius, percent stenosis, taper slope | Exclude from primary | Least reproducible; asymmetric lesion information; taper depends on both endpoints |

## Covariates and size normalisation must enter both arms identically

Diameter carries body size in a way curvature does not, so size adjustment matters more for this arm. Normalising by a ratio is the wrong default: ratio variables in regression produce spurious correlations, and the recommended fix is to enter the denominator as a covariate ([Kronmal 1993](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/2983064)). Size normalisation is also second-order next to measurement error here: coronary diameter scales with myocardial mass with exponent only about **0.22** ([Choi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7387886/)), so a 50% difference in mass moves diameter by about 9%, comparable to the 8 to 10% CT-versus-IVUS diameter error ([PMC3906085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906085/)). Sex does matter: women have about **9% smaller** coronaries even after BSA indexing ([Dodge et al. 1992](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)).

This raises a practical problem the earlier plan did not notice. `Descriptors.xlsx` has only four columns: Scan ID, Image Quality, Dominance and Disease. **Age and sex are not in it**, so the planned covariate set of age, sex and dominance cannot be built from that file. Either find demographics elsewhere or state that the diameter scalar partly encodes sex and body size in both arms, with dominance as the only shared covariate. Whatever the covariates end up being, they must be the same vector with the same encoding in both arms: extra terms in the regression, late-fused after the pooled sequence embedding in the network. Report a covariate-only floor for both, so the network cannot win simply by modelling covariates nonlinearly. If you ever use a ratio (for example radius divided by the vessel's own proximal reference), apply the identical ratio to the network's per-point radius channel, or the arms see different inputs.

## One scalar per channel is symmetric, not information-matched

The mean-per-channel regression answers "does the profile add beyond its mean". It does not answer "does deep learning beat a linear model given the same information", because the network sees every point. The information-matched comparator is **functional logistic regression**: expand each resampled profile in functional principal components, with the number of components fixed by explained variance inside the training fold, and fit penalised logistic regression on the scores ([logitFD, R Journal 2022](https://rjournal.github.io/articles/RJ-2022-053/)), or penalised functional regression, which was developed on tract profiles very similar to vessel profiles ([Goldsmith et al. 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1198/jcgs.2010.10007)). A small fixed summary set per channel (mean, SD, 10th and 90th percentiles, linear slope) sits between the two and tests whether distributional shape rather than order carries the signal. Generic time-series benchmarks show why this ladder is worth running: on 142 datasets, random-kernel and ensemble methods were best overall and the deep model was best only within its category ([Middlehurst et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029)). If the network beats the mean regression but not the functional one, the gain comes from reading the profile, not from the network. That is still a finding, but it has to be stated precisely.

## Conclusion

The question "which diameter scalar" was really a question about domains. Once the fixed-mm window from the ostium and the bifurcation mask are shared by mean curvature, mean radius and the network input, the symmetric choice (mean) and the robust choice (median) almost coincide, and the remaining difference becomes a sensitivity analysis rather than a design conflict. The same step also fixes a latent unfairness in the curvature arm, whose mean over a variable-length vessel was never truncation-robust either. So adopting the window now changes the curvature-only model too, which argues for settling it before that model is trained. What remains unknown, and is measurable on the 160 test scans, is how much CAS-Net's distal truncation and half-voxel radius quantisation actually move each candidate scalar relative to the reference. Radius agreement should be run on the window mean and on the proximal median before the diameter arm is trained. The absence of age and sex in the delivered metadata is the most consequential open issue, because it decides whether the diameter arm's gain can be separated from body size at all.
