# Merging chain codes and turns for coronaries

**Yes, but only in a narrow sense: what can be merged is Grisan's turn-segmented index and the curve-level Slope Chain Code (SCC) that sits underneath Mateos et al., not Mateos's actual τ3D.** Mateos et al. 2024 measure the folding of a voxelised solid through its axis-aligned slice contours. Applied to a coronary lumen mask, that number would mostly reflect how the heart is oriented in the scanner and how many branches each slice cuts, not the path of the vessel, and the authors themselves say trajectory tortuosity is a different problem ([Mateos 2024, p. 10](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-024-01312-6)). What does transfer is SCC's discretisation: resample each centerline at a constant chord length ℓ and take turning angles, which is rotation invariant by construction on a 3D polyline. Grisan's 2008 index transfers almost unchanged, because arc and chord are defined in any dimension. The one thing that does not transfer is its definition of a "twist" as a change in the sign of curvature. That sign does not exist for a space curve, so it has to be replaced by a 3D inflection rule, and the natural candidate is a flip of the binormal on the SCC chain, following Bullitt's Frenet-normal flip. The merged method is new: none of the three research passes found a peer-reviewed 3D port of Grisan's index. It inherits two risks that matter more for coronaries than for the retina. First, Grisan's index scores zero for any single C-shaped or helical course, and that is normal epicardial anatomy. Second, SCC's sum of turning angles is biased upward by centerline jitter unless ℓ is several millimetres. The method is worth building only as one candidate among several, alongside a threshold-free curvature-density baseline, and it has to pass the synthetic, jitter, extractor-agreement and floor-effect criteria in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md` before it carries any clinical analysis.

A note on evidence levels used throughout. **Cited** means the claim comes from a source that the research notes read in full: the Mateos 2024 and Grisan 2008 PDFs, and the PMC full texts of Kashyap 2022, Kjeldsberg 2021, Bullitt 2008, Ramos 2018, Zebić Mihić 2023 and van Zandwijk 2019. **Unverified** marks claims taken from abstracts, landing pages or search snippets. **Derivation** marks arithmetic or design reasoning by the researchers or by this report, which no source states.

## τ3D is a surface-folding measure; only its SCC core survives

Mateos et al. define τ3D in Eq. 8 as a sum over the X, Y and Z directions. For each direction, the per-slice sums of normalised absolute slope changes a_n, taken along every traced boundary contour, are averaged over the slices in that direction. Each chain element is the turning angle scaled to (−1, 1): **0 is no turn, ±0.5 is a 90° turn, ±1 is a reversal**, and the 2D tortuosity is τ = Σ|a_n| (Eq. 5). Any convex closed contour therefore scores 2, and any convex closed surface scores 6 ([Mateos 2024, Eqs. 4, 5, 8, pp. 3 to 5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-024-01312-6)). The input is a binary solid. There is no skeleton, no 3D curve and no branching concept anywhere in the paper ([Mateos 2024, p. 4, p. 10](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-024-01312-6)). Its only filtering is to downsample each contour by a **fixed factor of 10** and then apply a Kovalevsky digital-straight-segment approximation. The authors call τ3D "extremely sensitive to the definition of chain elements", and they show that the error grows for large spheres because the downsampling factor is fixed ([Mateos 2024, p. 6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-024-01312-6)). The whole validation consists of voxelised spheres of **radius 10 to 80 voxels**, with an error of about ±1 against the reference value 6 for r between 10 and 70, plus a morphological-closing experiment on a single brain. There is no noise, resampling or segmentation-perturbation experiment, and no code release ([Mateos 2024, pp. 5 to 7, Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-024-01312-6)).

Three derivations rule out running Eq. 8 on coronaries. The first is scale. A coronary cross-section at 0.5 mm voxels is 2 to 8 voxels across, and its perimeter of roughly 12 to 25 boundary points would shrink to 1 to 3 vertices after downsampling by 10, far below the smallest validated sphere. The second is rotation. A sphere looks the same from every direction, so the sphere test cannot probe rotation invariance. A tube that curves through fixed slice planes adds extra contour components, each worth about 2, so τ3D depends on vessel orientation and on branch count. The third is a mismatch in the elements themselves. The digital-straight-segment step yields segments of unequal length, while SCC's own derivation fixes the segment length (EF = 1, Eq. 3), which is the likely source of the sensitivity the authors report. The research also found internal errors in the paper that bear on how much weight it can carry. The per-lobe medians for parietal and occipital are swapped between the text and Table 1, the lobe columns are ordered differently in Tables 1 to 3, four pairs of subjects have identical rows in Table 1, and average curvature is described as "equivalent to the geodesic distance", which is dimensionally wrong ([Mateos 2024, pp. 3, 8, 9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-024-01312-6)).

What survives is the parent 2D SCC idea. Since a_n = θ_n/π, **Eq. 5 is the total absolute turning of a polygon divided by π** (derivation). Applied to a 3D centerline polyline resampled at constant chord ℓ, with θ_n = arccos(u_n · u_{n+1}) between consecutive unit chord directions, it becomes a discrete total-absolute-curvature measure that is translation and rotation invariant in 3D. The same group has published a curve-based 3D extension: Bribiesca-Sánchez et al. 2024 add a torsion chain alongside the slope chain and claim invariance to translation, rotation and uniform scaling ([Springer landing page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)). **Unverified:** that paper's exact definitions, including slope scaled to [0, 1], torsion to [0, 0.5], and a tortuosity equal to the sum of both, come only from search snippets, because the full text was paywalled. Parts of the SCC design that rest on Bribiesca 2013 are also known only from its abstract ([ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256822731_A_measure_of_tortuosity_based_on_chain_coding)). One consequence for the thesis gap statement: "the SCC family has no 3D curve measure" would be false.

## Grisan's formula is dimension-free except for its twist detector

Grisan et al. define a tortuosity density, **τ = ((n−1)/n) · (1/L_c) · Σ_i (L_c,si / L_χ,si − 1)**. The vessel is split into n "turn curves", each with constant curvature sign. Each turn contributes its arc/chord excess, the total is divided by the whole vessel's arc length L_c, and the factor (n−1)/n makes a vessel with no sign change score exactly 0 ([Grisan 2008, eq. 19, p. 313](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)). Watch the notation: L_c is the arc length and L_χ is the chord. Straight stretches are split in half, with one half given to each neighbouring turn. On real data the authors fitted a cubic smoothing spline (γ = 0.005, convention undefined). They then split each vessel at the midpoint between crossings of a hysteresis band on signed curvature, not at raw zero crossings, because noise creates "a great number of artificial turn curves" ([Grisan 2008, §IV-B, p. 314](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)). The band value is not given in the text; it reads as about ±0.01 px⁻¹ from Fig. 6. In validation, 30 arteries and 30 veins were traced by hand and ordered by **one** retina specialist. τ reached Spearman **ρ = 0.949 (arteries) and 0.853 (veins)**, only about 0.01 above tc/L_χ (total curvature over chord), and no test of that difference was reported. Under simulated point noise the median ρ was 0.945 and 0.804 ([Grisan 2008, Tables I and II, p. 317](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)). In an independent multi-rater study, inter-rater κ ranged from 0.22 to 0.76, and Grisan's metric was still the best computational metric, with AUC 0.80 ([Ramos 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6245828/)).

The paper is less clean than its reputation. Fig. 11 conflicts with the stated construction of synthetic set 3: the plotted values fit a chain of full semicircles of radius 2, not one semicircle of radius 4 cut into k arcs of constant total length. Fig. 11 also plots τ ≈ 0.031 at one arc, where eq. 19 gives 0. The claim that "six measures increase in all cases" fails for tsc/L_c, and Table II contains a probable misprint (SD 0.380) ([Grisan 2008, pp. 315 to 317](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)). A derivation in the notes matters directly for selection criterion 1. **Under the stated set-3 construction, τ decreases as the number of arcs grows**, from about 0.0088 at k = 2 to about 0.0046 at k = 6. Splitting a fixed total turning into more, flatter arcs shrinks the arc/chord excess, which grows roughly as θ²/24 per turn. Grisan's frequency monotonicity therefore holds only when each added turn keeps its amplitude.

Moving Grisan's index to 3D changes one thing. For a space curve, curvature κ = |r′×r″|/|r′|³ is non-negative and has no sign. A generic 3D curve never reaches κ = 0, so a strict inflection almost never occurs (standard differential geometry; derivation). Grisan's own paper notes that Bullitt's inflection count uses points where "the Frenet normal axis ... changes direction by π radians, that is equivalent to a change in sign of the curvature for planar curves" ([Grisan 2008, §III-A-5, p. 313](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)). Bullitt defines a 3D inflection as a minimum of curvature where the Normal and Binormal flip by close to 180°, detected as large local maxima of ΔN·ΔN ([Bullitt et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2517122/)). **Unverified:** Bullitt 2003's own threshold and smoothing were not retrieved. On a carotid siphon, a bend detector based on rotation of the curvature vector in a parallel-transport frame (Bogunović) gave a consistent 4 bends with CV below 5 %. A detector based on torsion extrema (Piccinelli) gave 6 to 8 bends with CV of 47 to 58 % ([Kjeldsberg 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626959/)). That is the only direct evidence the notes found on which 3D turn definition is stable, and it comes from a different artery.

## The merge: SCC chain as substrate, binormal flip as twist, Grisan as aggregator

The merged method below is the researchers' own design, reconstructed from the two papers. It is not taken from any source. It uses SCC for what SCC does well, a discretisation with a single explicit scale, and Grisan for what Grisan does well, splitting the curve into turns and aggregating them locally.

| Stage | Source idea | What is kept | What must be replaced for 3D CCTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input | Mateos: voxel solid, slice contours | nothing | per-branch LPS-mm centerline polyline, keyed by `segment_label` |
| Discretisation | SCC: constant, straight segments (EF = 1) | constant chord ℓ, vertices on the curve | ℓ in mm (several mm), not a voxel downsampling factor; unequal-length DSS segments dropped |
| Local turn | SCC chain element a_n = θ/π | θ_n = arccos(u_n · u_{n+1}) | signed planar angle becomes an unsigned 3D angle |
| Twist (inflection) | Grisan: sign change of planar κ with hysteresis | hysteresis, midpoint split, halving of straight runs | binormal b_n = u_n × u_{n+1} / |…| flips (b_n · b_m < −c) across a low-θ gap; or a parallel-transport curvature-vector rotation (Bogunović) |
| Per-turn term | Grisan arc/chord excess | L_c,si / L_χ,si − 1 in 3D, unchanged | nothing |
| Aggregate | Grisan eq. 19 | (n−1)/n and 1/L_c | L_c in mm; junction turning angle excluded |

Written out (derivation), the pipeline for each named vessel or segment has six steps: (1) take the branch in walk order from `load_vtk_centerline_branches`; (2) resample at constant chord ℓ; (3) compute θ_n and, wherever θ_n exceeds a small floor, the binormal b_n; (4) mark a twist where the binormal direction reverses between two runs with θ above an upper threshold, separated by a run below a lower threshold, and split at the midpoint of that low run; (5) compute 3D arc and chord for each piece; (6) apply eq. 19. On a planar curve, a sign change of curvature is exactly a binormal flip of π, so this reduces to Grisan's rule in 2D. The binormal is undefined where θ ≈ 0, so the floor in step (3) is required; it is the 3D counterpart of Grisan's hysteresis band. The SCC chain also yields, at no extra cost, the threshold-free companion **τ_SCC / L = Σθ_n / (π L)**. This is a discrete mean absolute curvature, the same family as Bullitt's sum-of-angles metric and Kashyap's κ_a. It is also the 3D analogue of Tello Ayala's 2D score, which is the mean of per-point turning angle divided by π (local note `docs_thesis/papers/telloayala2026tortuosity.md`).

The two outputs should be reported side by side, not blended into one number, because they fail in opposite places (derivation). Grisan's index, computed on the chain, separates one wide arc from several tight bends, since one arc gives n = 1 and τ = 0. It is blind to C-shapes and to helices. A helix has constant κ and torsion, no binormal flip, and scores 0, although it looks highly tortuous. SCC density sees helices and C-shapes. It scores one 180° bend identically whatever its radius, and it saturates in amplitude: for a sinusoid, each half period contributes 2·arctan(2πA/λ), capped at π, so it rises with frequency but flattens with amplitude, a candidate failure of "monotone in amplitude". The research also questions the premise that motivated Grisan's zero floor. Grisan argues that the retina is near-hemispherical, so "the non tortuous paradigm should be the circle arc" ([Grisan 2008, §VI, p. 317](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)). Coronaries run along the epicardium, so constant-sign and helix-like courses are normal anatomy. Whether an RCA shaped like a C should count as non-tortuous is a thesis decision, and the literature does not settle it.

## Noise, not geometry, decides whether the merge works on 0.5 mm centerlines

The coronary evidence base for choosing a metric is thin. The only systematic 3D comparison on CCTA is Kashyap et al. 2022, in 127 patients without CAD. There, average absolute curvature correlated best with low wall shear stress (LMCA R² = 0.196, LAD R² = 0.232), while arc/chord showed no relation (p = 0.86) ([Kashyap 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)). Their criterion was haemodynamic, not reproducibility, and their R² values are small. Zhang et al. 2024 adopted κ_a on ASOCA because of Kashyap ([Zhang 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11416812/)). The only CCTA study that counted inflections did it by chord crossings in a CPR view, not by differential geometry. It reported a mean of **0.63 inflections per segment in end-systole versus 0.51 in end-diastole (p < 0.001)**, and Menger curvature at a 5 mm step of 0.085 versus 0.078 mm⁻¹ ([van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)). Two points follow. With fewer than one inflection per segment on average, many segments will have n = 1 and score Grisan τ = 0, which is a direct floor-effect risk under criterion 5. And the inflection count depends on cardiac phase, which ImageCAS cannot test because it has one phase per scan.

Resampling scale dominates turn-based measures. In the carotid sensitivity study, the bend count **ranged from 3 to 33 depending on centerline resolution**, while smoothing strength moved it only between 6 and 7 ([Kjeldsberg 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626959/)). For the SCC half, an order-of-magnitude derivation shows why ℓ must be large. Take independent vertex jitter σ at spacing ℓ. The second difference has a standard deviation of about √6·σ per component, so the mean spurious turning is about 3σ/ℓ radians per vertex, and the spurious density Σθ/L is about 3σ/ℓ². With σ = 0.25 mm this gives about **0.19 rad/mm at ℓ = 2 mm and about 0.03 rad/mm at ℓ = 5 mm**, against a real signal near 0.08 mm⁻¹ (van Zandwijk's 5 mm Menger curvature). Because SCC sums absolute values, this is a positive bias, not just added variance. Real centerline error is spatially correlated and smaller than independent jitter, so these figures are an upper bound on the effect, not a prediction. For the Grisan half, the same jitter creates spurious binormal flips wherever θ is small, which is why the hysteresis floor is essential. Any value of it in px⁻¹ from the retinal paper does not transfer. The researchers suggest tying ℓ to local lumen radius as one route to anatomical scale invariance, but the radius array in the ImageCAS-X VTK files has not yet been checked, and neither has how junction points are shared between branches (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`). Plaque adds its own risk: a stenosis deflects the centerline locally, and at small ℓ it adds turning and possibly a spurious twist pair, which conflicts with the rule that the measure "must not read high because plaque distorts the centerline" (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`).

Validation therefore has to follow the pre-registered criteria in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`, with thresholds fixed before any data are seen, and it has to apply them to both outputs of the merge. The synthetic suite needs cases that neither parent paper covered: a line (must give 0); Grisan's eq. 20 sinusoid sets, with each planar curve also randomly rotated in 3D; Grisan's set 3 in both the stated and the plotted construction; one wide arc against several tight bends; a helix; and a sinusoid wrapped on a cylinder to mimic an epicardial course. Stability means Spearman ρ of vessel rankings across a sweep of ℓ (for example 1 to 8 mm), of the twist thresholds and of any spline smoothing. Jitter robustness means ICC with about 0.5 mm point perturbation. Extractor agreement means ICC between reference centerlines and CAS-Net-derived ones, which needs a centerline extractor that the repo does not yet have. The floor-effect criterion needs the fraction of vessels with n = 1, computed per named vessel, since RCA and LCx differ in shape. Correlations with length, radius and Image Quality are required, and so is Kendall τ against a blind expert ranking of about 30 vessels. Grisan's own validation leaned on one rater, and Ramos 2018 shows that experts disagree substantially, so a single rater would not suffice here. The final check is the healthy CGPS distribution, with the women-higher direction as a sanity check; the 2D evidence behind that direction comes from Li 2011 ([PLoS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024232)) and Zebić Mihić 2023 ([PMC10795752](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/)). Every number needs its population stated, and short segments (below about 10 to 15 mm, where a smoothing scale of several mm uses up most of the length) should return an explicit NaN row with a reason.

Several claims in this area remain unverified and should not carry weight until a `paper-review` pass: the Bribiesca-Sánchez 2024 torsion chain and its scaling; Bribiesca 2013's constant-length placement and its normalised tortuosity; Bullitt 2003's inflection threshold; Johnson and Dougherty 2007's spline metrics ([Med Eng Phys](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1350453306001652)); Gabrielides and Sapidis 2020's projection-based 3D inflections ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167839620301199)); the 2025 retinal SCC paper; and the Ferrari 2025 HMM coronary abstract, which reports precision 0.87 on only 18 LADs ([EHJ 2025 abstract](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/Supplement_1/ehaf784.136/8312275)). "No 3D Grisan port exists" rests on about three limited search passes, not a systematic review.

## Conclusion

The two papers do not merge symmetrically. Mateos contributes a discretisation principle, not a measure: constant-length chords with turning angle over π, which on a centerline becomes a mean-absolute-curvature estimator with one honest scale parameter, ℓ. Grisan contributes the only structural idea in either paper that coronary metrics currently lack: splitting the path into turns and scoring turns locally. That idea hinges on a twist detector that must be rebuilt in 3D and has no coronary validation. The most defensible thesis position is therefore to treat "3D Grisan on an SCC chain" as a novel candidate, tested against the threshold-free SCC density, which is effectively κ_a and has Kashyap's precedent, and not as a presumed improvement. Grisan's own advantage over tc/L_χ was about 0.01 in ρ on one rater.

The decisive experiment is cheap and comes before any clinical use. Compute the n = 1 fraction and the ℓ-sweep rank stability on the 560 training centerlines. If most LCx and RCA segments collapse to zero, or if the vessel rankings reshuffle between ℓ = 3 and 6 mm, then the turn-based half fails criteria 2 and 5 on ImageCAS-X, whatever its merits in the retina. The C-shape question should also be decided explicitly and written into the thesis, because it is a definitional choice about what coronary tortuosity means, not a property any metric can discover.
