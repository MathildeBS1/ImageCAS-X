**The thesis does not need more descriptor research; it needs its existing results to become reproducible and its Herlev-Østerbro path to become real.** The five earlier reports converged on a small, stable descriptor set (5 mm chord curvature κ_a, a fixed-tangent bifurcation angle, the left main Finet ratio, radius ratios), and every one of them named the same untested bottleneck: agreement between features from reference centerlines and from CAS-Net predictions. Meanwhile, almost every ImageCAS-X number they report lives in session scratchpads under `/tmp` on one login node. The CAS-Net environment that the bottleneck experiment needs is gone (no torch, no weights, no volumes), and Herlev-Østerbro data access, scheduled for weeks 2 to 3, shows no sign of having started. With roughly 12 working weeks to the 22 December submission, and the plan placing all cohort deployment, outlier detection and outcome work in weeks 7 to 14, the ordering above puts irreversible and long-lead items first, then the objective-driven analyses, and pushes the literature's most interesting extensions to last. The ordering follows Kit's instruction to develop descriptors on ImageCAS-X, compute statistics and aim at the Herlev-Østerbro outcome link. It takes Bjørn's representation argument seriously, but as a bounded baseline, not the centre of the thesis.

## Every report agrees on one experiment that has never run

Items 4 and 5 are where the five reports genuinely converge. All five name extractor agreement as the top priority: features from the delivered reference centerlines against features from centerlines extracted from CAS-Net predictions on the 160 test scans, scored by ICC and Bland-Altman ([cross-report synthesis](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports/report_synthesis.md)). The vessel-specific report states that this error spectrum "alone decides whether ℓ = 5 or 8 mm and σ = 1 or 2 mm", because **0.5 mm error correlated over 1 mm is the one perturbation κ_a fails, with point ICC 0.87, 0.76 and 0.71 on LAD, LCx and RCA against a 0.90 gate** ([Vessel specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)). The project plan lists the same measurement as the weeks 3 to 4 deliverable. It is the rare item that the plan, the supervisors' concern about fragility and the literature all want, which is why it outranks every descriptor refinement.

Before it can run, the descriptor definitions must be frozen (item 4). The reports recommend pre-registering thresholds, locking them, and only then opening the Disease column; none of them ever did it, and no threshold carries a commit date. The vessel-specific report asks for a bias cap of 10 % and confirmation on training scans outside both of its samples. P150 (first 150 training ids) and P100 (`default_rng(0)`) overlap, so fewer than 460 unused training scans remain. The earlier `run_merged_tests.py` run appears to have consumed all 560 training scans, which the audit could not rule out ([repo readiness note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports/repo_readiness_gaps.md)). The confirmation set must therefore be listed explicitly, and the tension stated honestly. There is also a quieter integrity issue: older scratchpads (ca2bf90b `disease_compare.py`) and the `tree-extraction` branch (`scripts/tortuosity_disease.py`) show the Disease column was looked at in an earlier session. The pre-registration should record that, not claim a clean blind. Finally, the repo holds two definitions of "the LAD": `tortuosity/vessels.py` takes the longest geodesic path, `topology.graph.Vessel` chains same-named segments. They can end at different points, so P150 and P100 are not strictly comparable. Choose one and record why.

The feature list for item 4 is deliberately short and follows objective 7's wording ("branching pattern, dominance, bifurcation angles and tortuosity") more than the reports' research arc. The regional report supplies the angle, measured with the `fitcore3` tangent (a line over [r_J, r_J + 3] mm, already in `topology/angles.py`). It holds between 78.0° and 80.8° across smoothing where a 2 mm secant drifts from 98.6° to 63.2°, and gives **LM 80.8°, LAD-D1 61.4°, LCx-OM1 67.1°** on 100 training scans ([Plaque prone report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Plaque%20prone%20regional%20geometry%20descriptors.md)). The LM Finet ratio is 0.63 with method ICC 0.85, and it fails at the crux (0.44), so it stays LM-only. Tortuosity is κ_a at σ = 1 mm, ℓ = 5 mm, with arc/chord TI as a length-adjusted secondary. Dominance and branch counts need no new research, only the confound warnings the thesis concepts already record (the right side is annotated more coarsely, and image quality drives branch counts).

## Supervisors split on representation, and the plan settles it

The two supervisors agree on graphs with per-node features, interpretability and normalising away size. They disagree on priority. Bjørn writes that it is "more important to find a strong representation of the CAs than to craft a big set of descriptor features", asks for a tokenised tree with anatomical correspondence and learned latent queries, and calls curvature and tortuosity "a fragile measurement" from his own work. He also warns that transformers need a lot of data. The unsigned third email, most plausibly Kit's, predicts that embeddings will separate on "heart size, dominance, number of branches", says "I don't want you to get bogged down in the details of representation learning for now", and sets "the main goal is the HØ dataset and matching the shape descriptors with outcome" ([supervisor emails](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/from_superviser.tex)). **The formal objectives side with Kit: none of the ten mentions embeddings, graphs or phenotypes, while objective 9 asks for outlier and extreme-value detection of named features, and the plan's risk model example is "top-5% tortuosity"** ([project plan](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/thesis/project_plan.tex)).

Item 8 reconciles the two at low cost. A PCA of arc-length-resampled, labelled segments (node features close to Bjørn's list: position in a heart-anchored frame, radius, normalised arc length, depth, left or right) is the baseline both the week 2 plan ("If an autoencoder cannot beat PCA, the network adds nothing") and the regional report ("If a latent-query network does not beat tangent PCA ... the network is not earning its keep") already name. Its first result is a test, not a nuisance: correlate the leading components with tree length, dominance and branch count. If Kit's prediction holds, that is a finding about what coronary shape variation is, and it tells Bjørn which normalisations a learned model needs. Dominance makes this delicate on ImageCAS-X, since 95 of 100 sampled scans are right-dominant, and RCA arc/chord splits 0.29 against 0.81 by dominance on only 8 left and 3 co-dominant cases ([Vessel specific report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Vessel%20specific%20tortuosity%20math%20and%20tests.md)). A transformer on 800 trees would, in the student's own week 3 words, "largely rediscover PCA".

Item 6 is the piece of supervisor-driven work that the plan also requires. Herlev-Østerbro trees will have no delivered centerlines and no segment names, and CAS-Net output has neither. Every regional descriptor, every named-vessel reference distribution and the plan's "anatomical correspondence between repeat scans of the same participant" (risk 4) depend on naming branches or on a label-free fallback. The regional report ranks the labeller as its priority 2. The readiness audit argues it should be scoped as the central method contribution, because one labeller serves Bjørn's correspondence problem, the repeat-scan correspondence in the plan, and CGPS deployment together. The student's standing default, label-free features on Herlev-Østerbro and named features on ImageCAS-X only, is the fallback if a labeller does not validate on the CAS-Net test trees ([supervisor questions](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/docs_thesis/supervisor_questions.md)).

## Where the literature pulls the thesis off its objectives

The five reports form one chain, and their topic drifted from the objectives in three ways. First, four of five reports are about tortuosity, one of the four feature families objective 7 names, and they spent their effort re-ranking tortuosity formulas: Q went from co-primary to dropped, arc/chord from baseline to Primary 1 to secondary, κ_a from companion to sole primary ([cross-report synthesis](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports/report_synthesis.md)). The Choosing report's own conclusion states the lesson: the question is "which descriptor survives the pipeline", and on that test the simple indices win ([Choosing report](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/reports/Choosing%20a%20coronary%20tortuosity%20descriptor.md)). Further formula work has diminishing returns, especially since the outcome evidence cannot rank metrics: the largest continuous study, 22,334 RCA angiograms, found only **OR 1.05 to 1.09 per SD** for CAD ([PMC13308244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13308244/)).

Second, the regional report moved toward plaque-onset geometry and advanced mathematics. Its location argument is well sourced at abstract level: in PARADIGM, of 334 plaque-free patients, 35 % developed new lesions and 60 % of those were in the LAD ([PARADIGM new plaque, EHJ CVI 2026 supplement](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcimaging/article/27/Supplement_1/jeaf367.317/8446296)). Its haemodynamic argument points away from curvature: in 39 ASOCA trees, diameter was the only geometry to wall shear stress correlate surviving multiple-comparison correction ([Shen et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.06161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06161)). That aligns well with objective 10's plaque-progression outcome, and the proximal-window and bifurcation anchors should shape the Herlev-Østerbro analysis. But its method recommendations drift. SRVF Karcher templates rest on a 30-curve prototype in which about half the dynamic-programming warp steps were rejected and nearest-neighbour vessel-type separation rose only from 0.90 to 0.93; the only vascular precedent found weak group differences on 65 carotids ([Cheng et al. 2014](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-8/issue-2/Analysis-of-AneuRisk65-data-Internal-carotid-artery-shape-analysis/10.1214/14-EJS938B.pdf)). The report's status for κ·r ("borderline pointwise, use window average") has no traceable computation anywhere, even in scratch, so κ·r should be treated as unmeasured ([repo readiness note](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports/repo_readiness_gaps.md)). Its closing claim that the evidence "vindicates Bjørn's instinct" is correct about correspondence and wrong as a prioritisation, because it does not weigh Kit's deferral or the calendar. That is why item 10 defers these.

Third, and most important, **no report addresses objective 9 (outlier and extreme-value detection) or the MACE risk model in objective 10**, and none addresses that ImageCAS-X is clinically referred with CAD in roughly half of its 800 scans. The thesis concepts document is explicit that ImageCAS-X "cannot license a normative description of healthy coronary topology", which is the entire reason the work spans two cohorts. Reference distributions from item 7 are therefore development tools: they show how much each descriptor's distribution moves across Image Quality and dominance strata, a within-ImageCAS proxy for domain shift, and they give the outlier method something to run on before Herlev-Østerbro arrives. Normative ranges proper belong to Herlev-Østerbro. Bjørn and Kit both frame the target partly as phenotypes or clusters; the objectives frame it as distributions and tails. Item 7 serves the objectives, and item 8 gives the phenotype framing a controlled first look.

## The reports disagree on five decisions the thesis must make once

The disagreements are real and must be settled once, in writing, before any outcome data is opened (item 9). **The single primary descriptor is unresolved**: the Choosing report names arc/chord TI plus κ_a, the vessel-specific report names κ_a alone after finding TI rank-correlated 0.70 to 0.83 with vessel length, and the regional report names the LM daughter angle. My reading is that κ_a is the best evidenced tortuosity primary, the LM angle is the best evidenced regional primary, and the thesis should pre-register one per outcome (κ_a for whole-vessel extremes under objective 9, the LM angle for new plaque under objective 10), with a stated multiplicity correction. The length floor differs (about 15, 25 and 20 mm across three reports, while the regional report uses 2 to 17 mm windows), and item 5's extractor error should set it. Radius weighting was dropped by two reports following Hart 1999 and revived as κ·r by the regional report without addressing that objection. The effect-size assumptions for power differ by a factor of three to five in log odds: the Choosing report's OR 1.05 to 1.09 per SD against the regional report's OR 1.3, which yields about 345 patients. The honest position is a sensitivity table over OR in {1.05, 1.1, 1.3} and reliability λ in {0.5, 0.7, 0.9}, which also defines when a null result is informative. And the vessel-specific report is internally inconsistent: its headline says κ_a keeps its ranking across the whole grid, while its notes record a minimum Spearman of 0.86 on LCx in P100, below its own 0.90 bar. The rerun in item 4 should resolve that.

Two further caveats limit what any of the reports licenses. Every ICC they report measures the sensitivity of one reference centerline to synthetic perturbation. None uses real extractor error, cardiac phase or scanner variation, and no expert ranking tested validity ([cross-report synthesis](file:///zhome/e2/6/224426/project/ImageCAS-X/research_notes/Investigation%20priorities%20across%20reports/report_synthesis.md)). And much of the plaque, haemodynamic and SRVF literature was read at abstract or snippet level, including PARADIGM, Stone, Samady, Finet and Fuchs. Item 10 therefore limits literature work to paper-review passes on the sources that will bear thesis claims, starting with the PARADIGM "how early" paper and the Fuchs Herlev-Østerbro outcome cohort ([Fuchs et al. 2023](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M22-3027)).

## Conclusion

Taken together, the five reports settle the descriptor question more firmly than their chain of reversals suggests. Stability alone leaves a short list, and the list matches objective 7. What remains open is upstream and downstream of the descriptors, not inside them. Upstream sits the error that CAS-Net and an unnamed tree introduce, which no one has measured. Downstream sit the two objectives that no report designed: flagging extremes and linking them to outcome in a cohort not yet in hand. The most defensible thesis contribution is therefore not a new tortuosity formula but a characterised measurement chain: a few named features with known error from prediction to feature, which a healthy-population analysis can then trust.

A practical schedule follows from the list. Week 5 carries items 1, 2 and 4 and starts item 3 as submitted jobs. Weeks 6 and 7 carry items 5, 6 and 7, and weeks 7 to 10 revert to the plan's Herlev-Østerbro block, with items 8 and 9 folded in. If access slips past week 7, the fallback thesis is still complete on objectives 5 to 9 with ImageCAS-X, provided items 1 to 7 are done and the outcome analysis exists as a locked, pre-registered design ready to run.
