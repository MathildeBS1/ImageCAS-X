# Pairing curvature moments beats merging turn counts

**Yes, Mateos 2024 and Grisan 2008 can be merged for 3D coronary centerlines, but only as a slope-chain turning density with a Grisan-style turn layer on top. Neither literature nor this report's checks show that merge is the best option available.** The better candidate is a threshold-free pair built from two curvature moments that already have coronary precedent: mean absolute curvature κ_a, the continuous form of the slope-chain density, and a bend-concentration ratio Q = (κ_r/κ_a)², where κ_r is RMS curvature. A turn layer can sit on top of that pair, but only as a challenger, and only if it uses a parallel-transport curvature-vector detector in place of the binormal flip proposed earlier. The pair fixes three of the four known weaknesses of the earlier proposal. It never scores a C-shape or helix as zero. It separates one wide arc from several tight bends of equal total turning, which the slope-chain density cannot do at any chord length. And it keeps rising with amplitude in the range where κ_a turns over and falls, which the Bullitt 2003 table now shows directly. The fourth weakness it cannot fix is the difference between an S-bend and a C-bend: every curvature integral scores them identically, so if the thesis decides that inflections matter, a turn layer is still required. The comparative literature supports reporting separate channels side by side, not a blended number. Every composite that beat a single metric did so by adding non-tortuosity information (caliber, bifurcation angle, haemodynamics). The two retinal studies that pitted tortuosity-only combinations against their best single metric found gains of 0 to 5 points or none. All of this can be validated on the 560 training centerlines without expert labels or CFD, using a synthetic suite, scale and jitter stability, compositional self-consistency, floor fractions and extractor agreement. The one unavoidable limit is physical. Radius-awareness requires a chord or smoothing scale below the bend radius, while noise control requires one of several millimetres. So bends tighter than about 3 to 5 mm cannot be resolved reliably on these centerlines by any metric.

Evidence labels used throughout. **Full text** means the source was read in full by a research pass (PMC HTML, open PDF or open-access publisher page). **Abstract-only** means an abstract, landing page or search snippet. **Fetch-summary** means full text passed through a summarising tool, one step below full text. **Derivation** means reasoning by the researchers or by this report that no source states. **Check** means a quick noise-free numpy computation run while writing this report; the scripts live outside the repository and must be re-implemented in `tortuosity/` before any of their numbers is cited. Both derivations and checks need numerical verification in the thesis synthetic suite.

Two smaller corrections follow from the recovered texts. First, one new note describes SOAM as adding π per inflection on planar curves, but Bullitt's formula explicitly zeroes the torsional angle there. On a planar curve SOAM is therefore just the sum of turning angles per length, and its only extra content is the torsion term. Second, the earlier report derived that the slope-chain density "saturates in amplitude". Bullitt's Table 2 shows the length-normalised sum of angles *falls* as amplitude rises at fixed frequency: sine SOAM went 0.9 → 0.7 → 0.4 for amplitude 10 → 20 → 40, and coil SOAM went 1.3 → 0.6 → 0.3 ([Bullitt 2003, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603/)). That is worse than saturation, and this report's check reproduces it for κ_a (next sections).

## Mateos and Grisan still merge, but only through the chain and a new detector

The answer to the first question is unchanged in outline and changed in detail. Mateos's τ3D is a folding measure of a voxel surface, built from slice contours. It is unusable on coronary lumens, because at 0.5 mm a cross-section is 2 to 8 voxels wide and the measure depends on vessel orientation and on how many branches each slice cuts ([Mateos 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-024-01312-6)). What transfers is its slope chain code (SCC) core. Resample the centerline at a constant chord ℓ and sum the turning angles over π; for a polyline this equals **total absolute curvature divided by π** (derivation, confirmed by the Bribiesca-Sánchez 2024 statement that the 3D slope chain equals the 2D SCC on planar curves). Grisan's density, τ = ((n−1)/n)(1/L)Σ(L_i/C_i − 1), transfers once "change of curvature sign" is replaced by a 3D turn rule ([Grisan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2007.904657)).

Reading Bribiesca-Sánchez 2024 in full changes how much the chain-code side offers. Their 3D-SCC τ = Σ|α_j| + Σ|β_j| is invariant to translation, rotation and uniform scaling. Its validation, however, is three synthetic knots, 90 sperm flagella and two hand-picked comparisons. There is no expert reference, no noise experiment and no statistics, even though the conclusion calls the features "statistically significant". The paper also has internal errors: Table 2 normalises by 100, not by τ_max, and the closed-curve bound is written as (3n−1)/2 where n + n/2 = 3n/2 ([Bribiesca-Sánchez 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)). Three derivations limit its use on coronaries.

First, in their Table 4 two flagella have identical total absolute curvature (κ_ta = 3.25), so their slope sums are also identical (about 1.03). The entire gap between τ = 2.74 and τ = 5.26 therefore comes from the torsion term. Second, that torsion term is ill-conditioned. The discrete binormal N_j = T_{j−1} × T_j has magnitude sin θ_j, so on a nearly straight, jittered polyline its direction is set by noise, and the paper zeroes β only when α is exactly zero. If the dihedral angle is uniform, |β| averages 0.25 per vertex. A jittered straight line would then score a normalised τ_N ≈ 0.17 from torsion alone, against 0.033 to 0.050 for the smoothed flagella. Third, the measure is angle-only and scale-invariant by design, so a 90° bend of radius 2 mm and one of radius 20 mm score the same. **3D-SCC removes Grisan's zero but formalises radius blindness, and its distinctive torsion channel is the least reliable quantity to extract from 0.5 mm centerlines** (derivations).

The paper does contribute one piece that the earlier merge lacked: a published 3D inflection rule. The sign of α_j is kept while the curve keeps curling one way (C) and flipped at an S, with the test made in the frame of the last non-collinear vertex. The same paper supplies a Slope Direction Factor, SDF = Σα/Σ|α|, which is 1 for helix-like or C-like curves ([Bribiesca-Sánchez 2024, Eqs. 2 and 6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-024-01286-9)). Like the earlier binormal flip, though, it is a per-vertex test with no threshold.

The better-evidenced 3D turn rule is the parallel-transport (PT) curvature vector. Bogunović's detector expresses the curvature vector as k₁E₁ + k₂E₂ in a rotation-minimising frame and measures the angle between curvature vectors at consecutive curvature peaks. It had a CV below 5 % across eight segmentations, against 47 % for Piccinelli's torsion extrema ([Kjeldsberg 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626959/)). VMTK already computes the PT normals ([VMTK source](https://github.com/vmtk/vmtk/blob/master/vtkVmtk/ComputationalGeometry/vtkvmtkCenterlineAttributesFilter.cxx)). The researchers' derivation, based on Bishop frame theory, explains the gap. The polar angle of (k₁, k₂) advances at rate τ wherever κ > 0 and jumps by π at a planar inflection. The PT angle between peaks therefore equals the *integrated* torsion between them, obtained from first and second derivatives only. Piccinelli, by contrast, searches for *pointwise* extrema of a third-derivative signal. On a planar curve the PT angle flips by exactly π at a sign change, so it reduces to Grisan's twist. On a helix it accumulates steadily, so splitting whenever it exceeds α cuts a helix into about τL/α bends.

The corrected merge therefore has three changes (derivations). The binormal flip is replaced by a PT-angle split, with curvature peaks above κ_high and boundaries at the lowest-κ point between retained peaks. The factor (n−1)/n is dropped, so a single C-shaped bend scores its own arc/chord excess instead of 0. And every threshold is set in mm or degrees, never in sample counts. morphMan's constants (70, 25 and 5 samples) are the likely reason Piccinelli's results depend so strongly on resolution ([morphMan Piccinelli code](https://github.com/KVSlab/morphMan/blob/master/src/morphman/automated_landmarking/automated_landmarking_piccinelli.py)).

## No single number passes all of criterion 1

Criterion 1 in `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md` asks for five properties: zero on a line, monotone in amplitude and in frequency, invariant to rotation and translation, and able to separate one wide arc from several tight bends. The research on integral and multiscale families, together with this report's checks, shows that every candidate fails at least one of them. The table below summarises the results. The check setup was a planar sinusoid with a 60 mm chord and 0.25 mm chord resampling, with amplitude A from 0.5 to 16 mm at wavelength λ = 10 mm and λ from 30 to 4 mm at A = 2 mm. A second set of 100 mm curves compared one 180° arc of radius 20 mm with three 60° bends of radius 2 mm, bending either the same way or alternating, at ℓ = 1, 3 and 8 mm.

Three findings in this table correct the new research notes as well as the earlier report, and so matter most. First, **κ_a is non-monotone in amplitude.** At fixed wavelength it peaks near A/λ ≈ 0.2, where the maximum slope angle is about 50 to 60°, and then declines. Beyond that point, extra amplitude lengthens the path faster than it adds turning, since turning per half period is capped at π. The earlier report's "saturates" understated this. Bullitt's Table 2 is the published confirmation. The practical question is whether real coronaries sit beyond the peak. Angiographic definitions of severe tortuosity use two consecutive curves of 180° or more ([Zebić Mihić 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10795752/)), which suggests the most tortuous vessels do. There, κ_a would under-rank exactly the vessels the thesis most wants to rank high.

Second, **the two-scale chain code does not fix radius blindness.** One research note proposed turning density at a short and a long chord, (D_s, D_L), as a 3D version of Annunziata's two-scale plane and argued that a long chord "cuts" tight bends. The check contradicts this. For bends that turn one way, the chord directions before and after a bend still differ by the full bend angle, so K/L was 0.032 for both the wide C and the tight bends at ℓ = 1, 3 and 8 mm. Chord length filters oscillations shorter than about 2ℓ, which is a *frequency* filter, not a radius filter. That matches Annunziata's own description of the two scales as "high- and low-frequency turns" ([Annunziata 2016, fetch-summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4794090/)). Only super-linear weighting of curvature (E, Q) separates bends by radius.

Third, **E/L is not the clean fix the integral notes claimed.** Those notes derived that E/L has "no amplitude saturation". Once path length grows with amplitude, E/L plateaus near 2k²/3 (where k = 2π/λ) and dipped slightly in the check. Its radius sensitivity also shrinks quickly as ℓ grows: tight and wide bends differ 7.8-fold at ℓ = 1 but only 1.9-fold at ℓ = 8.

Noise sets the other side of the trade-off. The earlier report's independent-jitter bound gives spurious K/L ≈ 3σ/ℓ²; the integral notes extend it to spurious E/L ≈ 12σ²/ℓ⁴. At σ = 0.25 mm that is about 0.047 mm⁻² at ℓ = 2 mm and 0.0012 mm⁻² at ℓ = 5 mm, against a real κ² near 0.0064 mm⁻² taken from van Zandwijk's 5 mm Menger curvature of about 0.08 mm⁻¹ ([van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)). A single jittered 100 mm line in the check gave the same order of magnitude: K/L 0.17, 0.10 and 0.02 at ℓ = 2, 3 and 5 mm, against predicted values of 0.19, 0.08 and 0.03. Real centerline error is spatially correlated, so these are upper bounds. They still imply that E, and therefore Q, needs ℓ or a smoothing scale of at least about 4 to 5 mm. Radius-awareness, meanwhile, requires ℓ below the bend radius. **Bends tighter than about 3 to 5 mm fall in the gap between these two requirements on 0.5 mm centerlines, for every metric** (derivation). The thesis should state this limit instead of hiding it in a parameter choice. The noise ordering by derivative order is supported wherever it has been measured. In the thoracic aorta, interobserver ICC was 0.97 to 0.98 for arc/chord, 0.82 to 0.84 for fitted-circle curvature and 0.39 to 0.75 for angles ([aorta variability 2018, abstract-only](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29705085/)). **No coronary ICC or CV for any curvature, torsion or bend metric was found.**

The remaining families fail on grounds that no merge repairs. Box-counting fractal dimension needs more decades of scale than a 50 mm segment sampled at 0.5 mm provides. On lung trees it did not track pulmonary pressure, while plain arc/chord did (ρ = 0.60) ([Helmberger 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3909124/)). Torsion shapes helical flow in swine coronaries (r = 0.64) ([De Nisco 2019, abstract-only](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10439-018-02169-x)), but in an idealised coronary model it moved WSS by only 3 %, against 22 % for curvature ([Selvarasu 2012, abstract-only](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4006984)). That supports keeping torsion as a sensitivity analysis, not a primary channel. Elastic shape analysis, functional data analysis and persistent homology produce distances, modes or barcodes, not an index that is zero on a line. They have been applied to brain arteries ([Bendich 2016, abstract-only](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6652); [BAN shape analysis, abstract-only](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04793)) but not to coronary tortuosity. They belong in a later analysis layer, not in the index itself.

## Composites win only when they add something other than tortuosity

The comparative evidence argues against blending tortuosity formulas into one weighted number. Hart's retinal study tested all seven curvature and arc/chord measures combined against the best single one. The combination gained 2.7 points on manual segments (85.6 vs 82.9 %), 0.2 on automatic segments and 5 on manual networks, and it *lost* on automatic networks (90 vs 95 %) ([Hart 1999, full text](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)). A 2025 retinal study crossed eight local indices with five aggregation schemes and found that no global scheme exceeded the best local index. The best pairs reached ρ ≈ 0.89, but the index labels conflict between two fetches and need checking ([PLOS One 2025, fetch-summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)). The combinations that did beat single metrics all added non-tortuosity information:

- vessel type, caliber and location added to Grisan's τ ([Ramos 2019, fetch-summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934469/));
- chord length multiplied by the LM-LAD bifurcation angle (HR 11.36 vs 2.70) ([Moon 2023, abstract-only](https://doi.org/10.21037/qims-23-518));
- curvature combined with CFD-derived haemodynamic quantities for SCAD, AUC 0.95 to 0.97, in a preprint ([Zhang 2026, abstract-only](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.12.26360329));
- a supervised discriminant over many shape measures ([Bullitt 2005, abstract-only](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2517122/)).

ImageCAS-X has no labels to fit weights, so any composite here has to be structural and unweighted.

The evidence also splits cleanly by reference standard, and that is the strongest argument for reporting channels side by side. Against coronary low WSS, curvature integrals beat arc/chord, and κ_a beat RMS curvature in every region ([Kashyap 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)). Against outcomes in 65 Loeys-Dietz carotids, total absolute curvature had the better adjusted odds ratio (2.64 vs 1.88, not significant for arc/chord). It was also far more stable over 8 to 10 years (fold change 0.026 ± 0.298 vs 0.302 ± 0.656), though its raw AUC was marginally lower (0.724 vs 0.737) ([Lee 2024, full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11255721/)). Against expert perception, metrics that count convexity changes won in the retina, with Grisan first, though the best AUC was only 0.80 and inter-rater κ ranged from 0.22 to 0.76 ([Ramos 2018, full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6245828/)). In 3D cerebral MRA, ICM caught meandering vessels and AVM nidi, while SOAM caught tight coils ([Bullitt 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430603/)). Curvature is what haemodynamics and outcomes reward; turn structure is what human perception rewards. **No study merges two tortuosity formulas and shows a gain in 3D**, so the earlier SCC plus Grisan merge and every alternative here are novel and equally unproven.

Haemodynamic references are also weak selection tools on their own terms. κ_a explained at most 23 % of low-WSS variance. Lumen diameter or diameter ratio outweighed curvature in three independent designs ([Johnston 2007, abstract-only](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9155/52/9/013); [Garcha 2025, abstract-only](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11770140/)). The sign of tortuosity's effect on WSS is itself disputed, because helical flow raises WSS ([Vorobtsova 2016, abstract-only](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10439-015-1492-3)). And no 3D CCTA study relating any tortuosity metric to FFR or CT-FFR was found. Radius weighting, the Dean-number argument, has its only direct test in Hart: diameter-multiplied measures "performed slightly worse than their counterparts" ([Hart 1999](https://www.siue.edu/~sumbaug/RetinalProjectPapers/Measurement%20and%20classification%20of%20retinal%20vascular%20tortuosity.pdf)). That result, plus the unchecked radius array in the ImageCAS-X VTK files, argues for leaving lumen radius out of the index and testing it only as a confounder under criterion 6.

## Recommended merge: two curvature moments, one bend layer, one comparator

The recommendation is a ranked shortlist, all computed per named vessel and per segment from `load_vtk_centerline_branches` in walk order. Every candidate returns an explicit NaN row with a reason when the vessel is too short for the chosen scale. With ℓ of 3 to 5 mm, that means below about 15 mm, which excludes Kashyap-length 10 mm pieces. Every element below is a design derivation; no source validates any of them on coronaries.

| Rank | Component | Definition | What it contributes | What it fixes vs the earlier proposal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | **Magnitude: κ_a** | Σθ_n / L at a pre-registered chord ℓ (the SCC density times π), reported with an ℓ sweep | Lowest-order, compositional (Hart), with coronary ([Kashyap](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764056/)) and outcome ([Lee](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11255721/)) precedent; zero on a line; non-zero on C and helix | Retained from the earlier proposal; its failures are now documented, not hidden |
| 1b | **Concentration: Q** | (κ_r / κ_a)² = L·Σ(θ_n²/ℓ) / (Σθ_n)² at the same ℓ; NaN when κ_a is below the noise floor set by the jitter experiment | Separates one wide arc from several tight bends with no threshold or detector; monotone in amplitude and frequency where κ_a declines (check); never 0 on C or helix | Replaces Grisan's gated zero with a floor of 1 on constant curvature; covers the amplitude range where κ_a turns over; restores radius sensitivity down to about ℓ |
| 2 | **Turn layer (challenger)** | PT curvature-vector split (peaks above κ_high, merge unless PT angle exceeds α, boundary at the κ minimum), then Σ(L_b/C_b − 1)/L with no (n−1)/n; plus bends of 45° or more per 100 mm, for comparison with angiography only | The only channel that separates S from C (check: κ_a, E and Q are identical for alternating and same-sign bends); splits helices into τL/α bends | Swaps the per-sample binormal flip (torsion family, 47 % CV evidence) for the PT angle (CV below 5 %); removes the n = 1 zero; thresholds in mm and degrees |
| 3 | **Comparators** | Earlier SCC × binormal-flip Grisan; arc/chord (VMTK L/D − 1); spline κ_a | Continuity with the earlier report and with the clinical literature | Keeps the earlier proposal testable instead of discarding it unexamined |
| Sensitivity only | 3D-SCC torsion Σ\|β\|/L; Bullitt SOAM | As published | Tests whether out-of-plane winding survives noise | A documented rejection on reliability grounds is itself a thesis result, since 3D-SCC has never been tested under noise |

The pair (κ_a, Q) is ranked first for three reasons. It needs no detector and no threshold beyond ℓ. Both parts are functions of Kashyap's κ_a and κ_r, so a reviewer sees continuity with the only large 3D coronary comparison. And it covers the failure modes of the earlier twin outputs:

- **Helix and C-shape.** Both channels are non-zero on them.
- **Radius blindness.** Q carries radius information.
- **Amplitude decline.** Q keeps rising where κ_a falls.

What the pair cannot see is inflection. Whether an S-bend should outrank a C-bend of the same curvature is the definitional question the earlier report flagged. The literature does not settle it: perception studies reward it, haemodynamic studies do not test it. Hence the rank-2 turn layer. It should be kept only if it passes criteria 2, 3 and 5 **and** carries information beyond the pair, for example a low partial Spearman with Q given κ_a, or separation of S from C on the synthetic suite that the pair provably lacks.

Three known risks travel with the recommendation.

- **Q grows with straight padding.** A vessel with the same bends plus a longer straight run has a higher Q, because Q ≈ L divided by the bent length. The length correlation in criterion 6 therefore has to be checked per named vessel, not assumed away (derivation).
- **Plaque can push Q up.** A stenosis deflects the centerline locally, which concentrates curvature, so plaque may raise Q more than κ_a. That would break the rule that the index "must not read high because plaque distorts the centerline" (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`). A larger ℓ reduces the effect, at the cost of radius resolution (derivation).
- **ImageCAS cannot test cardiac phase.** Inflection counts differ between systole and diastole (0.63 vs 0.51 per segment) ([van Zandwijk 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7165136/)), and each ImageCAS scan has one phase. The turn layer is the component most exposed to this.

## A label-free validation ladder on 560 training centerlines

Everything below uses reference centerlines and `Descriptors.xlsx` strata other than Disease. None of it needs expert labels or CFD. Pass thresholds are fixed before any real centerline is scored, as `tortuosity/CLAUDE.md` requires. The threshold values suggested below are this report's proposals, not sourced. The development population is the 560 training cases per named vessel (LM, LAD, LCx, RCA). The 160 test cases are reserved for extractor agreement, and every result states its case and vessel counts.

**Step 0: read the files before designing.** Before any code is written, read one VTK file to confirm whether it has a radius array and how branches share junction points. Both are still unchecked (`tortuosity/CLAUDE.md`).

**Step 1: synthetic suite (criterion 1).** Run every candidate on a fixed set of curves, each also applied after random 3D rotation and translation:

- a straight line;
- arcs of fixed angle across a radius sweep;
- one 180° arc of radius 20 mm against three 60° bends of radius 2 mm, with the bends both same-sign and alternating;
- sinusoid amplitude and frequency sweeps at a fixed chord, recording where each metric turns over;
- a helix with a pitch sweep;
- a sinusoid wrapped on a cylinder of epicardial radius;
- Grisan's set 3, in both the stated and the plotted construction.

The pass rules are: exactly 0 on the line for κ_a and the turn layer; invariance to numerical tolerance; and a documented monotone range, not a blanket claim. This step re-implements this report's checks inside `tortuosity/` and either confirms or overturns them.

**Step 2: noise calibration on synthetics.** Add independent and spatially correlated jitter at σ of 0.1 to 0.5 mm and measure the bias of κ_a, E and Q against ℓ. Pre-register the ℓ at which bias falls below a set fraction of a realistic κ (0.08 mm⁻¹ at 5 mm from van Zandwijk), for example 20 %. This fixes ℓ and Q's NaN floor before any real data are seen.

**Step 3: rank stability (criterion 2).** Compute Spearman ρ of vessel rankings between adjacent ℓ values from 2 to 8 mm, and across the smoothing sweep, on the training split. A suggested bar is ρ ≥ 0.9. Plot the tortuosity-versus-ℓ curves instead of hiding them.

**Step 4: jitter ICC (criterion 3).** Perturb the real training centerlines at about 0.5 mm with correlated noise, then report ICC with a bootstrap confidence interval against the between-vessel spread.

**Step 5: compositional self-consistency.** Score each whole vessel against the length-weighted aggregate of its segments, using Spearman, in the manner of Ramírez 2025 ([PLOS One 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331121/)). This is the one reference-free test aimed at the bend-aggregation step the turn layer introduces. κ_a is compositional by construction (Hart), so this step mainly tests Q and the turn layer.

**Step 6: floor and ceiling (criterion 5).** Report, per named vessel, the fraction of vessels at 0 (turn layer, earlier Grisan), at Q ≈ 1, or NaN, with reasons. RCA and LCx are the expected problem cases, because van Zandwijk's mean of 0.51 to 0.63 inflections per segment implies many single-turn segments.

**Step 7: confounds (criterion 6).** Report Spearman with vessel length, with radius if the array exists, and a shift test across Image Quality strata for both the values and their ICCs. Use Dominance only as a check that length normalisation is working, since no literature gives it an expected direction.

**Step 8: extractor agreement (criterion 4).** On the 160 test cases, compare reference centerlines with centerlines extracted from CAS-Net predictions. The repository does not yet have that extractor. Report ICC and Bland-Altman, not correlation alone: automated iliac arc/chord showed r = 0.95 but ICC 0.73 against manual measurement ([Barb 2026, abstract-only](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrad.2025.112609)).

**Step 9: decision rule, then lock.** Prefer (κ_a, Q) unless the turn layer passes Steps 3 to 8 and adds independent information. Criterion 7 (blind ranking of about 30 vessels) stays optional and needs at least two raters with their own κ reported, given retinal inter-rater κ as low as 0.22. It never serves to fit weights. Only after the definition is locked are `Disease` and the CGPS healthy distribution opened, with women higher as the sanity check. A reliability-first ranking without labels has precedent: a retinal curvature index was chosen on test-retest Spearman of 0.87 to 0.92 ([VCI study, fetch-summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11500049/)), and Kjeldsberg ranked bend detectors by CV.

## Conclusion

The merge question has changed shape. Mateos contributes a discretisation, Grisan contributes the idea of turns, and the merge between them is sound once the twist detector becomes a parallel-transport angle. What the new evidence adds is that the more important weaknesses were on the curvature side. The slope-chain density falls with amplitude past a moderate bend. It is blind to bend radius at every chord length, so the two-scale fix proposed in the notes does not work. And no curvature integral can tell an S from a C. Q, the ratio of Kashyap's two curvature moments, repairs the first two with no new parameter. That leaves inflection as the only thing a turn layer uniquely buys. The thesis can therefore turn the C-versus-S decision into a measurable question: does the turn layer carry information beyond (κ_a, Q) that survives noise?

The deeper constraint is resolution, not method choice. On 0.5 mm reference centerlines, the scale needed to suppress jitter overlaps the radius of the tightest coronary bends, so every candidate here, merged or not, loses the tightest bends to noise or to smoothing. Stating that limit with numbers from Step 2, and showing that the chosen ℓ keeps vessel rankings stable, is a stronger thesis contribution than any new formula. It would also produce the first coronary reproducibility figures of the kind criteria 3 and 4 require, which the literature currently lacks for every metric discussed here.
