\section{Reliability the Geometric Features}
\label{sec:method-validity}
\label{sec:method-medis}

The three geometric features were computed directly from the centerlines and lumen surfaces predicted by the in-house tracer (Section~\ref{sec:method-features}).
Each feature was designed to be robust to small errors in these predictions, but a large segmentation error still carries through.
How large the errors are is unknown.
No ground truth existed for the Herlev--{\O}sterbro scans, so the usual comparison by the Dice similarity coefficient or the Hausdorff distance could not be made.
In addition, the tracer was trained on CCTA data from other scanners, so the Herlev--{\O}sterbro scans were out of distribution and larger errors are more likely.
The reliability of the features could therefore only be estimated indirectly, first by comparing them with a second extraction of the same scans, and then by checking whether the features differ between groups of participants whose characteristics are known to affect the geometry that the features describe.


\subsection{Comparison with Medis}
\label{sec:method-medis-pairs}
\label{sec:method-medis-agreement}

For part of the Herlev--{\O}sterbro dataset, centerlines and lumen contours had already been extracted from the volumes with the commercial software Medis (Section~\ref{sec:data-medis}).
The extraction was automatic, and a clinician afterwards went in and corrected it, but only around the segments with plaque.
The rest of each vessel was left uncorrected, so the Medis extraction cannot be treated as ground truth.
It does, however, give a second extraction of the same vessels from the same images, against which the tracer can be compared.
This comparison was only made on the three geometric features, since these are what the models use.

The number of participants available for comparison differed between the features.
The Medis centerlines cover the whole coronary tree, so the angle and the curvature could be compared in most participants.
Medis contours, in contrast, exist only for segments with plaque, which restricted the asymmetry to participants with plaque at the left main bifurcation.

For each pair, the Medis value was subtracted from the tracer value, and these differences were summarised by their mean and standard deviation (SD).
Whether this disagreement is a concern depends on how much the feature varies between participants, and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to weigh the two against each other.
It is the proportion of the total variance that lies between participants, and it equals 1 when the two extractions differ only by a constant.
The consistency form of the ICC was used, as a constant offset does not change a hazard ratio per SD (Appendix~\ref{app:icc}).
As neither extraction is known to be correct, a difference between them shows only that they disagree, not which of them is wrong, or whether both are.
Close agreement is nonetheless informative.
Medis and the in-house tracer were developed independently, so it is less likely that they agree only because both are wrong in the same way.
Errors that arise from the images themselves are a different matter.
Motion artefacts or limited resolution in narrow arteries would affect both extractions.


\subsection{Known Groups}
\label{sec:method-validity-groups}
\label{sec:method-validity-dominance}

The second indirect estimate of the reliability of the features uses known groups.
Some characteristics of a participant are known to change the coronary geometry in predictable ways, and these changes can be tied directly to the features.
If a feature differs in the expected direction between participants who differ in such a characteristic, this is a good indication that it captures the geometry it is intended to describe.

For the LAD/LCx asymmetry, $a$ was plotted by dominance group.
In left-dominant hearts the LCx is larger on average (Section~\ref{sec:dominance}), so if the asymmetry captures the relative size of the two daughters, $a$ should be higher in left-dominant than in right-dominant hearts.
