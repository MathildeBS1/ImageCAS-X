Choosing the outcome: what the CGPS data supports

Aggregate results from cgps_stats.py on 2,116 patients with a baseline and a follow-up CT. Counts below 5 are suppressed in the source report. Hover any bar for exact counts; every chart has a table view.

The five plaque trajectories depend on which patients you count

Among patients whose baseline segments were read, most plaque grows (54–57% "plaque → more"). The 1,094 without baseline reads have SIS = 0, so they can only land in the two "no plaque" groups. Mixing them in (bottom bar) halves "plaque → more" and inflates "no plaque → plaque".

The "not read" bar is derived from the worst follow-up stenosis of those patients (no plaque vs any plaque); 8 have no follow-up grade. Extent = number of segments with plaque; burden = summed stenosis grades.

Five trajectories at each anatomical level

The finer the unit, the more of it stays plaque-free. A whole tree almost always changes; a single segment rarely does. Read patients only, burden-defined (more / less = summed stenosis grade change).

Units: segments (17 per patient), positions (proximal / mid / distal / side branches), vessels (LM, LAD, CX, RCA), whole tree (1 per patient). At segment level, units in the same patient are correlated (ICC 0.1–0.3), so the effective sample is roughly a third of the unit count.

Candidate outcomes compared (whole-tree level)

A usable outcome needs enough patients, balanced events and far more increases than decreases. Calcium progression (Hokanson) is the clearest signal; summed stenosis +2 is the best plaque outcome. High-risk features are mostly reader noise.

Increase : decrease = events divided by the opposite change (e.g. plaque or stenosis going down), which is mostly reader variation. Log scale; the line at 1 means as many decreases as increases. Clinical events (heart attack, revascularisation, stent, bypass) are left out: only 10 in 11 years.

The signal gets cleaner going up the tree

Increase : decrease ratio for four plaque outcomes, from single segments to the whole tree. Most outcomes are least noisy at whole-tree level; "new plaque in a plaque-free segment" is the exception and is noisy everywhere.

Where stenosis burden increases

Share of segments whose stenosis grade rose, read patients only. Progression concentrates in the proximal and mid LAD, the mid and proximal RCA, and the proximal CX; distal segments and side branches change least.

Missing baseline data

About half the cohort lacks baseline segment reads, history, BMI and HbA1c, possibly the same patients. Everything else is nearly complete: 1,974 patients have all predictors below the 20% line.
