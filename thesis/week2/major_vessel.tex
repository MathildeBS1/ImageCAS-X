\subsection{Scope: focusing for a start on the left main, LAD and LCX}

As a start I'm narrowing the vessels whose topology I measure and analyze to three: the \textbf{left main
artery (LM)}, the \textbf{left anterior descending artery (LAD)} and the \textbf{left circumflex
artery (LCX)}, rather than the coronary tree in full.

Three things point the same way. The LM is the sole conduit to both the LAD and the LCX, so a lesion
there threatens both territories at once rather than one branch's alone. The LAD supplies the
largest single territory of any coronary vessel and carries the heaviest documented plaque burden of
any one vessel: three-vessel optical coherence tomography of 131 patients found rupture-prone
thin-cap fibroatheroma in 18.9\% of 534 plaques, clustering most in the proximal LAD, with smaller
peaks in the right coronary artery (RCA) and the LCX \cite{araki2020plaques}. The LCX is the LM's
other branch and shares the LM bifurcation, the single geometric feature the disease-prediction
literature returns to most often.

The rest of the tree is not disease-free, and I may return to including more of it later.
