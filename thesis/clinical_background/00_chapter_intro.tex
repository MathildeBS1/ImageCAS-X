\chapter{Clinical Background}
\label{ch:clinical}

The coronary arteries supply the heart muscle itself, and the shape of the tree they form is what
this thesis sets out to measure.
Before such a measurement means anything, four things have to be in place: what a normal coronary
tree looks like, how much it varies between healthy people, what computed tomography can and cannot
resolve of it, and why its shape should have any bearing on disease at all.
The last of these is the one that turns a geometric description into a clinical quantity, and it is
the reason the rest of the chapter is worth reading.

The chapter follows that order.
Sections~\ref{sec:anatomy} and~\ref{sec:variants} describe the normal arterial tree, coronary
dominance, and the variants that change how the tree is connected; Sections~\ref{sec:cad}
and~\ref{sec:ccta} cover the disease and the imaging used to see it; and
Sections~\ref{sec:hemodynamics} and~\ref{sec:geometry} set out the hemodynamic mechanism that links
the shape of a vessel to where disease forms in it.
No cardiology background is assumed, and terms are defined where they first appear.
