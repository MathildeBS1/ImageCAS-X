\chapter{Segmentation Pipeline}
\label{ch:pipeline}

This chapter walks through the segmentation pipeline for \textbf{CAS-Net} \cite{dong2023casnet},
the model the topology pipeline in later chapters is built on. CAS-Net is one of several methods
supported by a shared benchmark codebase, forked from the one released with ImageCAS-X
\cite{bransby2026imagecasx}; comparing those methods is a separate objective of this thesis, so
they are disregarded here and only CAS-Net is followed, as the best-performing method on the
ImageCAS-X benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:pipeline-benchmark}). Predictions are scored against the
\textbf{ground truth (GT)} mask with the overlap-based \textbf{Dice similarity coefficient (DSC)}
and the boundary-based \textbf{95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95)}, both defined in
Section~\ref{sec:pipeline-metrics}.

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline-overview} gives the shape of the whole pipeline. A scan and its GT mask
are prepared once, offline (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline-data}), and then flow down two paths that
share the same trained network: \emph{training} (Sections~\ref{sec:pipeline-architecture}--
\ref{sec:pipeline-training}) repeatedly shows the network patches and adjusts its weights, and
\emph{inference} (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline-inference}) runs the finished network over a whole,
unseen scan. Section~\ref{sec:pipeline-metrics} covers how the resulting predictions are scored.

Every stage is driven by one shared, config-driven codebase, so the same preprocessing, training
and evaluation rules apply throughout and a new method or setting only needs a config change, not
a rewrite of the pipeline scripts.
