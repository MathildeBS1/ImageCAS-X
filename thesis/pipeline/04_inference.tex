\section{Inference}
\label{sec:pipeline-inference}

Inference loads the trained checkpoint and runs it over the held-out test scans, producing one
saved segmentation per scan. Training only ever showed the network $128\times160\times160$ crops,
so a whole test volume, commonly over $400^3$ voxels, cannot be consumed in one pass.

The scan is instead cut into overlapping windows the size of a training crop, each run through the
network separately and blended back together, following standard practice for patch-trained 3D
segmentation \cite{isensee2021nnunet}: windows overlap by half their size, and since a window's
prediction is least reliable near its own edges, each window is weighted by
\[
  w(\mathbf{d}) = \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k \in \{x,y,z\}} (d_k / \sigma_k)^2\right),
  \qquad \sigma_k = 0.125\,p_k,
\]
where $\mathbf{d}$ is a voxel's offset from its window's centre and $p_k$ the window's size along
axis $k$: weight 1 at the centre, close to 0 near the edge, so a voxel covered by several windows
is dominated by whichever has it nearest to that window's own centre.

This tiled prediction is repeated four times per scan, on the unflipped volume and on it flipped
left--right, front--back, and both, never the head-to-foot axis, since that is not an
anatomically valid orientation. Each result is flipped back and the four are averaged, since a
scan and its mirror image do not predict identically even though anatomically they should, at
roughly four times the inference compute.

The averaged probabilities are resampled from the internal 0.5\,mm grid to the scan's native
resolution \emph{before} any thresholding, so the hard decision is made at full original
resolution rather than on an already-thresholded mask, which would alias fine vessel boundaries.
\textbf{argmax} then assigns each voxel to whichever of the two channels (background, lumen)
scores higher, and \textbf{small-component removal} discards any connected component under 100
voxels as noise, keeping every larger one, so the left and right coronary trees survive as
separate components. The result is written to disk, one file per scan, for evaluation
(Section~\ref{sec:pipeline-metrics}) to read.
