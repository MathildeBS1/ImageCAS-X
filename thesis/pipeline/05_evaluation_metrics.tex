\section{Evaluation metrics}
\label{sec:pipeline-metrics}

Evaluation never runs the network: it reads the saved predictions and the matching GT masks and
centerlines, and scores one against the other. The two GT artifacts serve different metrics: the
GT mask, a full 3D volume label, is what Dice, HD95 and the Betti numbers below are scored
against, while the GT centerline, a separately annotated 1D path down the middle of the vessel, is
what the centerline metrics are scored against -- CAS-Net itself is never trained to produce a
centerline, only the lumen mask (Sections~\ref{sec:pipeline-architecture}--
\ref{sec:pipeline-training}); the ``predicted'' centerline used below is instead extracted
algorithmically from the predicted mask at evaluation time, by \textbf{skeletonisation}: a
morphological thinning of the binary mask down to a 1-voxel-wide skeleton that preserves its
connectivity. No single number fully captures what a
good segmentation of a thin, branching structure means, so the benchmark reports several metric
families together \cite{taha2015metrics}, each computed at the scan's original resolution so
distances and volumes are in true physical units.

\textbf{Dice} (Equation~\ref{eq:pipeline-dice}) answers how much of the vessel, by volume, was
found, and is the headline number in Table~\ref{tab:pipeline-benchmark}. \textbf{HD95}, the
95th-percentile Hausdorff distance between the predicted and GT surfaces, complements it as a
worst-case, boundary-based measure: for every point on one surface, the distance to the nearest
point on the other is found, and the 95th percentile of these distances is reported -- catching a
localised error that a volume-averaged score like Dice can dilute away.

\textbf{Betti numbers} count topological errors that Dice and HD95 are largely blind to: $\beta_0$
is the number of separate connected components and $\beta_1$ the number of loops. The benchmark
reports the absolute error in each against the GT, so a mask that is broken into fragments or
contains a spurious loop between two branches is penalised even if its overlap and boundary
distance both look fine.

Three further metrics target the vessel's 1D \textbf{centerline} rather than its full volume:
\textbf{clDice} \cite{shit2021cldice} checks how much of each mask's skeleton falls inside the
other, making it far more sensitive than Dice to a connectivity-breaking gap; \textbf{centerline
mean distance} and \textbf{centerline HD95} compare the predicted skeleton directly against the
GT's annotated centerline points, the same worst-case logic as HD95 applied in 1D. Predicted and
GT lumen volumes are also reported directly, alongside their absolute and signed difference.

Beyond one number per scan, every metric is also broken down by scan-level subgroup (image
quality, dominance, disease status) and by location along the vessel -- a local Dice computed in
an 8\,mm cube around points sampled along the GT centerline, grouped by coronary segment,
distance from the ostium, local diameter and local HU attenuation -- so a result can be read as,
for instance, holding up proximally but degrading distally, tracking the vessel's shrinking
diameter.
