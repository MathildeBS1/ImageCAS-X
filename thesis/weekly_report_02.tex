\section{Week 2: 7--11 September 2026}

\sectionprogress

\textbf{Hemodynamics.} I read up on wall shear stress (WSS) in particular, and on fractional flow
reserve (FFR). It is well documented that regions of low or oscillatory WSS -- at bifurcations and
on the inner wall of bends -- are prone to plaque development \cite{chatzizisis2007ess,
asakura1990flow}. Far fewer studies relate the geometry of the coronary tree to its hemodynamics in
healthy arteries, and the role of flow in the onset of plaque remains underexplored for lack of
follow-up that starts from a healthy state \cite{zhang2024curvature}.

\textbf{Healthy population.} Most studies of coronary geometry are carried out in patients with
disease and ask where in the tree plaque appears, a question that requires plaque to be present. A
more interesting question for this thesis is whether the topology of the tree carries any signs of
risk \emph{before} disease develops. Phillip mentioned that the Copenhagen General Population Study
(CGPS; Herlev--{\O}sterbro) includes participants who were scanned twice, about ten years apart:
some had healthy trees at the first scan and had developed CAD by the second, while others remained
unchanged. Comparing these two groups could reveal geometric patterns that precede disease.

\textbf{Scope narrowed.} Rather than analyzing the whole coronary tree, I will begin the topology and
feature-extraction work with three vessels -- the left main (LM), left anterior descending (LAD) and
left circumflex (LCX) arteries -- for the reasons given in \Cref{sec:week2-major-vessel}.

\textbf{Features, mostly tortuosity.} Bifurcation angle is the one coronary feature with a
standardized definition, although the same angle can hide very different branch diameters.
Tortuosity is the opposite case. Its dominant measure, the tortuosity index $\tau = L/D$, cannot
tell apart vessels that bend in very different ways (\Cref{fig:week2-tortuosity-ambiguity}), and in
127 patients without CAD it showed no relation to low wall shear stress, whereas curvature did
\cite{kashyap2022tortuosity}. I wrote a first implementation that computes, for each vessel's
centerline, the index together with curvature, turning angle per mm, inflections, torsion,
non-planarity and lumen radius. Everything except the index depends on how much the centerline is
smoothed before the derivatives are taken, so the measures that best reflect the flow are also the
hardest to make reproducible.

\textbf{CAS-Net pipeline.} I went through the methods used in CAS-Net \cite{dong2023casnet} in more
depth, at a high level: data preparation and preprocessing, network architecture, training, inference
and evaluation metrics. As discussed, I will train CAS-Net from scratch. My contribution to improving
the segmentation for topological feature extraction will most likely focus on post-processing, since
the overlap with the reference annotations is already high, whereas the topology is not
\cite{bransby2026imagecasx}.

\subsection{Next week}

Next week has two tracks: training CAS-Net from scratch, and turning the tortuosity work from a
script into a method.

\textbf{Training CAS-Net from scratch.} First I will run inference and evaluation with the delivered
weights. That tests my environment in hours rather than weeks and gives the reference numbers my own
run will be judged against. A five-epoch timing run then measures the time per iteration and the
memory use. From that I can size the full run, whose published schedule of 1000 epochs does not
fit in one 72-hour job and has to be chained, or, depending on the answer to the question below,
continue from the pretrained weights instead.

\textbf{Tortuosity: describing a vessel's shape with numbers.} The goal is a description of each
vessel that tells apart the three curves in \Cref{fig:week2-tortuosity-ambiguity}, which the index
cannot. I see two routes, and I want to try both on the LM, LAD and LCX.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hand-crafted descriptors.} I choose the numbers, each with a geometric meaning, as
    the current script does. Two extensions. Keep curvature as a profile along the vessel rather
    than averaging it into one number, so that where a bend sits is not lost. And read every bend
    and angle against the vessel's thickness: the same curvature disturbs the flow more in a thick
    vessel than in a thin one, and the same bifurcation angle means something different when a
    thick vessel splits into two thin branches than when it splits into one thick and one thin
    (\Cref{fig:week2-bifurcation-angle}).

  \item \textbf{Learned representations.} A network finds the structure instead. An encoder, for
    example an autoencoder, trained to compress each vessel (its centerline, or the segmentation
    around it) into a short vector of numbers, an \emph{embedding}, and to reconstruct it again, has
    to keep whatever varies most between vessels. Whether that includes tortuosity is something to
    check, not assume.

  \item \textbf{PCA as the baseline.} Principal component analysis on the vessel shapes is the
    linear version of the same idea: resample every centerline to the same number of points from
    the ostium, align them, and find the few directions in which the population varies most. This
    is a statistical shape model, which the field's review recommends for combining many shape
    factors \cite{shen2026geometry} and which has been applied to the left main bifurcation
    \cite{medranogracia2017bifurcation}. If an autoencoder cannot beat PCA, the network adds
    nothing.

  \item \textbf{t-SNE to look at the result.} Projecting the descriptor or embedding vectors to two
    dimensions and coloring them by vessel, by tortuosity index and by dominance shows what a
    representation has picked up: if the LAD and LCX separate it carries anatomy, and if vessels with
    the same index spread out it carries more than the index. t-SNE is for looking, not measuring,
    since distances between its clusters are not meaningful.
\end{itemize}

The two routes check each other. A learned dimension that correlates with curvature confirms a
hand-crafted descriptor; one that correlates with none of them is structure I did not think to
measure. I will discuss these directions with Bj{\o}rn.
