\subsection{Turning-Angle Curvature}
\label{sec:method-curvature}

The centerline points of each artery, ordered from the ostium outward and given in millimeters, were first respaced into chords of equal length.
Starting at the ostium, each new vertex $\mathbf{v}_j$ was placed on the centerline at a straight-line distance of exactly $\ell = 5$\,mm from the previous vertex, and a distal remainder shorter than $\ell$ was discarded.
The original points are irregularly spaced, and the respacing makes every turning angle span the same distance along the vessel.
As described in Section~\ref{sec:tech-curvature}, the false turning per millimeter caused by noise in the point positions grows as $1/\ell^2$, so the chord length sets a trade-off between noise and spatial resolution.
In the 560 training RCAs of ImageCAS-X, the median mean curvature fell by 41\% from $\ell = 2$\,mm to $\ell = 5$\,mm but by only a further 13\% from 5 to 8\,mm, indicating that noise dominates below 5\,mm.
The chord was therefore set to 5\,mm, the value adopted for coronary curvature on CCTA by van Zandwijk et al.\ \cite{vanzandwijk2019curvature}, at the cost of not resolving bends shorter than a few millimeters.
No smoothing was applied beyond the respacing.

At each interior vertex, the turning angle was computed from the chords arriving at and leaving the vertex:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_j = \operatorname{atan2}\bigl(\lVert \mathbf{a}_j \times \mathbf{b}_j \rVert,\; \mathbf{a}_j \cdot \mathbf{b}_j\bigr),
  \qquad j = 1,\dots,n,
  \label{eq:method-turn}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{a}_j = \mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_{j-1}$ is the arriving chord, $\mathbf{b}_j = \mathbf{v}_{j+1} - \mathbf{v}_j$ the leaving chord, and $n$ the number of interior vertices.
The two-argument arctangent gives the same angle as the arccosine of the normalized dot product, but remains accurate for nearly straight chords, where the cosine is close to 1 and changes little with the angle.
Each bend of a 3D vessel lies in its own plane, so a turn has no left or right, and $\theta_j$ is unsigned and lies between $0$ and $\pi$.

Two curvature descriptors were derived from the turning angles of each artery.
The curvature profile is the sequence
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_j = \frac{\theta_j}{\ell}, \qquad j = 1,\dots,n,
  \label{eq:method-profile}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_j$ is the turning per millimeter at vertex $\mathbf{v}_j$, in rad/mm, ordered from the ostium outward.
The profile retains where along the artery each bend lies, which matters since vulnerable plaques cluster in the proximal segments of all three arteries \cite{araki2020plaques}.
The mean curvature is the average of the profile,
\begin{equation}
  T_\ell = \frac{1}{n\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{n} \theta_j ,
  \label{eq:method-tl}
\end{equation}
where $n\ell$ is the length of vessel covered by the turning angles, so that $T_\ell$ is the mean absolute curvature of the artery at scale $\ell$, in rad/mm.
Mean curvature was preferred to the tortuosity index as the summary, as it was the measure most closely related to low wall shear stress in patients without coronary artery disease, whereas the tortuosity index showed no correlation \cite{kashyap2022tortuosity}.
An artery with fewer than two turning angles at $\ell = 5$\,mm was recorded as missing.

The descriptors were adapted from the turning-angle score of Tello Ayala et al.\ \cite{telloayala2026tortuosity}, with three modifications.
Their score was computed on 2D skeletons of invasive angiograms, whereas the centerlines here are 3D.
Their angles were measured between consecutive skeleton points, whereas here every angle spans a fixed 5\,mm chord.
Their angles were averaged over the points and divided by $\pi$, whereas here the total turning was divided by the length covered, so that $T_\ell$ retains its unit of rad/mm.
