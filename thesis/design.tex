\section{What this thesis measures, and in whom}
\label{sec:design}

Two choices separate this work from the literature it draws on, and each needs justifying.

The first is the whole tree rather than a single junction.
Coronary geometry is almost always measured at the left main bifurcation, and the disease is not
confined there: mapping all three coronary arteries by optical coherence tomography in 131 patients,
Araki et al.\ found the rupture-prone plaque phenotype clustered proximally in each of them
\cite{araki2020plaques}.
More decisively, most of what this thesis measures cannot be defined on a bifurcation at all.
Dominance is a statement about which arterial system crosses the crux on the underside of the heart
(Section~\ref{sec:dominance}), and a branch count needs something to count over.
Measuring at the junction does not approximate the tree-scale question but discards it.

The value of such a description is established in another vascular bed.
Rudnicka et al.\ measured vessel width and tortuosity across the retinal vascular tree in 88,052 UK
Biobank participants and predicted circulatory mortality, myocardial infarction and stroke
\cite{rudnicka2022quartz}; the largest published analysis of a whole coronary tree covers 39
patients \cite{shen2026geometry}.

The second choice is the population.
Calling one tree unusual requires a reference distribution, and the reference decides what the word
means: measured in patients, unusual means unlike a patient.
A clinically referred cohort cannot supply one, because disease alters the geometry before it
becomes visible.
Examining 136 hearts at autopsy, Glagov et al.\ found the vessel enlarges outward as plaque
accumulates, so the lumen does not narrow measurably until a lesion occupies about 40\% of it
\cite{glagov1987remodeling} --- an artery called normal in such a cohort may already have
remodeled.

No single dataset meets both requirements.
ImageCAS-X carries the annotation needed to build the construction and measure how reproducible it
is \cite{bransby2026imagecasx}; the Copenhagen General Population Study supplies participants not
selected for disease, repeat scans of the same people, and adjudicated outcomes
\cite{fuchs2023cgps}.
